What is Reality2?

Reality2 is a decentralised protocol for devices that cooperate over changing connections. Small sensors, phones and servers can take part in a shared mesh, while cryptographic trust groups govern membership and access. The design starts with a simple fact: devices move, radios sleep, and messages get lost.

Your Own Trust Group

Membership is established through cryptographic identity and deliberate enrolment. A device stays a member when it goes offline. Sensitive actions require the appropriate authority.

Small Devices Welcome

The protocol is designed for constrained sensors as well as larger computers. Each device declares its facilities; cooperation does not assume that every peer has the same power, storage or connectivity.

Connections Come and Go

Different communication media share a common protocol. Devices use local observations to choose where to forward messages, while allowing for loss, sleeping neighbours and paths that disappear.

No Cloud Dependency

Devices can relay messages for groups they do not belong to. Carrying a message grants no membership or authority, and relays do not need the keys to read encrypted payloads. The design requires no central service.

Technology that stays calm - working quietly in the background, only stepping forward when it genuinely needs your attention. Technology that adapts - finding new paths when old ones fail, strengthening connections that matter, letting go of ones that don't. Digital sovereignty - real control over your own identity, data, and relationships - made practical.

People and Planet

Reality2 isn't only about protecting your data. It's about technology in symbiosis with the world it lives in - distributed intelligence that serves people and the natural world, instead of extracting from them.

Symbiosis, not extraction

The mesh exists because your devices create it - not because a company monetises it. Intelligence lives at the edge, where things actually happen: sensing, deciding and acting locally. Your data has value, and you decide whether and how it's shared. Nothing is harvested by default.

Sovereignty for people and nature

A person wearing a watch, ring and phone forms a sovereign trust group. Sensors across a river catchment can form one too, governed by the community that looks after it. Reality2 extends digital sovereignty beyond people to the living world - rivers, forests and mountains - cared for through the people and groups who act as their guardians.

On-grid and off

Distributed senses and intelligence carried over BLE, WiFi and LoRa - working whether or not there's internet. When a storm takes the cell towers down, the mesh is designed to keep watching the river and keep people connected. Resilience is the point.

Sovereignty isn't a setting you switch on. It's built into the architecture - so the people and communities who are guardians of their own data, and of the rivers, forests and mountains they care for, stay in control by design.

Where R2 Fits

The design connects small, local interactions with longer-lived networks. These are illustrative uses, not a catalogue of available apps.

Everyday Cooperation

A presenter and a room's display can agree on a temporary relationship, limited to the task at hand. Devices can cooperate without handing over control of their whole group.

People on the Move

In a crowd, devices encounter new neighbours and lose old ones. The mesh is designed around those brief encounters; membership and permissions remain separate from who happens to be nearby.

For Developers

The R2 Standard is the source of truth for the protocol. Development is organised around explicit layer contracts and evidence of conformance. Earlier apps and implementation details should not be used as the current specification.

One Layered Design

The design separates transport, discovery, routing and framing from trust, management and applications. Implementations meet the contracts at each boundary.

Evidence Matters

Requirements, byte-level formats and test vectors make behaviour checkable. A successful demo alone does not establish conformance across the protocol.
