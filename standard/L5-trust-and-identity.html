Introduction

Everything below this layer works for everyone. A relay carries any group's frames, a beacon announces to anyone listening, and nothing below the boundary can tell a member from a stranger. Layer 5 is where that stops being true — where a hive holds keys, belongs to something, and decides whether a frame that arrived is a frame that gets acted on.

The unit of belonging is the trust group: a flat set of hives sharing a cryptographic identity. Flat is a design position, not a simplification — there are no groups of groups, no hierarchy, and no administrator standing above the members. Where two groups need to interact, they do it by explicit, revocable agreement between equals, and nothing is implied by resemblance, proximity or common ownership.

Four commitments organise everything below.

There is no ungrouped state. A hive belongs to a trust group from its first boot — at minimum a group containing only itself, with real keys it generated. "No group yet" does not exist as a running state, and nothing in this document has to handle it. What distinguishes an unclaimed device from an owned one is not the presence of keys but a claim state, and the transition between the two is guarded physically.

One group at a time. A hive belongs to exactly one trust group at any instant. A device holding the keys of two security domains is a standing leak between them, so simultaneous membership does not exist here — groups interact through entanglement, never through shared members. Changing group is identity replacement: the old persona is destroyed, and what joins the new group is, observably, a different hive.

Delivery is gated, and the gate fails closed. A frame addressed to a group is acted on only after its integrity tag verifies against a key the receiver actually holds. No key, no delivery — not "no key, deliver cautiously". A gate that fails open is worse than no gate, because everything downstream believes the gate was passed.

Membership is deliberate, in both directions. A hive joins a group through a ceremony involving a person, and leaves it by an explicit act — its own or the group's. Silence is never departure: a member asleep for a season is still a member, and the group's memory of who belongs does not decay. What decays is reachability, which is a routing fact and is kept strictly apart.

1 Scope

This document specifies the trust group: its identity, its keys, its membership, and its lifecycle.

It specifies the claim state by which a device is unclaimed or owned, and the transitions between them.

It specifies member certificates, their issuance, renewal and revocation, and how revocation reaches members that were absent when it happened.

It specifies the delivery gate by which a received frame is accepted, and what acceptance does and does not establish.

It specifies the relationship between groups: entanglement, its conditions, its levels, and its severance.

It specifies the development trust group and its admission rule.

It establishes what conformance at this layer consists of, and identifies what is not decided.

It does not specify the enrolment ceremony's transport and user interaction, which belong to a companion document of this layer. It does not specify frame layout, which is Layer 4, or what an application does with a delivered frame, which is Layer 7.

3 Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the terms and definitions given in the documents listed in Clause 2 apply, together with the following.

3.1 trust group

(defined in 01-terminology.md 3.8)

3.2 persona

hive's membership of one trust group, together with the identity and key material that membership gives it

3.3 group keypair

signature keypair whose public half is the trust group's identity and whose secret half signs its certificates

3.4 key holder

member holding the group's secret key

3.5 member keypair

signature keypair generated by a hive for one persona, whose public half is the subject of its certificate

3.6 claim state

persisted state of a device: OPEN, meaning claimable, or OWNER, meaning claimed

3.7 delivery gate

check a receiver applies to a group-addressed frame before anything above this layer sees it

3.8 entanglement

explicit, conditional agreement between two trust groups by which frames of one are accepted by the other, standing as long as its conditions are met

3.9 re-persona

replacement of a hive's persona: destruction of the current one and enrolment into another group

4 The trust group

4.1 Shape

4.1.1 A trust group shall be a flat set of hives. Groups shall not contain groups, and no hierarchy of groups shall be expressed at this layer.

4.1.2 Authenticated interaction between groups shall occur only through entanglement (Clause 10), which also admits the unauthenticated level of its ladder.

Note 1 to entry: Flatness is what keeps authority where the members are. A hierarchy would put someone above the group — and every mechanism in this document assumes there is no such party to appeal to, defer to, or be compromised through.

4.2 Identity

4.2.1 A trust group shall have exactly one group keypair (3.3). The public half shall be the group's identity.

4.2.2 The group's identifier shall be derived from its public key, so that generating the keypair determines the identifier.

4.2.3 Every structure at this layer carrying a key, signature or ciphertext shall carry an algorithm identifier. An implementation shall support the required algorithms and shall reject, without acting on, any structure whose algorithm identifier it does not recognise.

4.2.4 The required signature algorithm is Ed25519; the required key-establishment algorithm is X25519. Identifier values, key sizes and reserved codes are given in the test vectors of this layer.

Note 0 to entry: Those vectors do not yet exist — 14.3 records the debt, and until they land, the algorithm identifier values 4.2.3 requires cannot be obtained from this standard at all, so no two implementations can agree on them. The purpose strings of the derivation are owed with them (FORMATS.md, Clause 4a). (Debt made explicit 2026-08-01, matching Layer 4's own statement at its 12.3 — an unstated debt reads as an oversight, a stated one as work.)

Note 1 to entry: 4.2.2 removes a whole class of mistake: there is no separate act of naming a group, so no two groups can claim one name, and no registry adjudicates. The identifier is checkable by anyone holding the public key.

Note 2 to entry: 4.2.3 is the agility seam. Algorithms will be replaced within the life of this standard, and the identifier is what lets new ones arrive without a protocol break — silent rejection of the unrecognised is what makes a future algorithm safe to introduce.

4.3 Every hive is always in a group

4.3.1 On first boot, a hive shall create a trust group containing only itself: it shall generate a group keypair, hold the secret half, and derive the group identifier from the public half.

4.3.1a A hive shall be able to report whether the persona it holds was created during the current boot or loaded from storage, and shall not report the two alike.

Note 1 to entry: An identity store that returns a valid key either way lets a device silently become a different device. Measured in an implementation: the store answered with 32 valid bytes whether it had loaded an established identity or minted one seconds earlier, so a failure to read existing material was indistinguishable from correct operation — the hive comes up healthy, its peers see a stranger, and nothing anywhere reports it. Every peer's view of the group changes and the device itself is the only party that could have noticed.

Note 2 to entry: This asks for less than a provenance record and deliberately so. The platform-written record of Layer 6, 5.4.2a at least carries a sequence that encodes a generation; an identity store commonly carries no marker at all — no counter, no timestamp — so nothing distinguishes an identity five seconds old from one six months old, and a full provenance scheme would be a larger change than the evidence supports. Distinguishing minted now from loaded is enough to make the silent case audible, and it is answerable by any platform, since a hive that has just created a persona knows it did. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), finding SS138. This is 01-terminology.md 5.4 at the identity layer: the key material is valid, and valid says nothing about whether it is the identity this device had yesterday.)

4.3.2 A hive shall be a member of exactly one trust group at any instant.

4.3.3 There shall be no ungrouped, keyless or persona-less running state.

4.3.4 A hive leaving a group without joining another shall enter a fresh group of one, with a newly generated keypair and therefore a new group identifier.

4.3.3a A hive reporting its trust group, its keys or its persona shall not present the absence of one as a legitimate value.

Note 0 to entry: 4.3.3 decides what a report showing no group means, and the answer is not blank. A hive that cannot produce a trust group is not a hive with a field left unset — by 01-terminology.md 3.2 it is not a hive, and by 4.3.3 it is not a running state this standard admits. So none, unset or an empty field is the wrong report: each reads as nothing here yet when what has been observed is a fault. 01-terminology.md 5.3's third answer applies — I cannot tell is distinct and conformant — but it is distinct from there is none, and both are distinct from a value. The same reading follows for keys and personas, which 4.3.3 names in the same breath. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), after a panel specification was written to display an unset trust group as an ordinary field; finding SS202. 4.3.3 was already sufficient about the state — what was missing was a clause about the report, and anyone reaching either.)

Note 1 to entry: The group-of-one is not a placeholder. Its keys are real, its structure is ordinary, and the delivery gate treats it as any other group — a frame addressed to it under its own key verifies and is delivered; every other group's frames fail closed exactly as they would anywhere. "Create a group" and "boot a fresh hive" are the same key-level act.

Note 2 to entry: 4.3.2 is the leak rule. A hive inside two groups is a bridge neither group agreed to — its compromise is both groups' compromise, and its ordinary operation moves information across a boundary that entanglement exists to make explicit. Where a person or operator legitimately manages many groups, what they hold is custody of keys for provisioning others — their own hive still has exactly one persona.

4.3.5 One device may host more than one hive concurrently. Where it does:

a) each hosted hive shall hold its persona, keys and state in an environment isolated from every other hosted hive's; and

b) co-hosted hives shall have no communication path with one another other than the transient network: each is, to every other, an ordinary peer — discovered by its beacon, reached by frames, gated by its gate.

Note 3 to entry: 4.3.5 is the dual of the complex hive. A complex hive is several devices being one hive; this is one device being several hives — a PC running a work persona and a home persona side by side, each sandboxed, each in exactly one group (4.3.2 applies per hive, and is not bent by co-residence). The b) rule is what keeps co-residence from becoming the bridge Note 2 forbids: two personas that could whisper through shared memory would link their groups through a channel no gate sees. Through the network, their traffic crosses the same boundaries as anyone else's — the sandbox wall does for one device what the trust boundary does for the mesh.

Note 4 to entry: The normal case remains one device, one hive — most devices are capable of running exactly one, and nothing here obliges more. Co-hosting is a capability of larger platforms; the simple form of many-personas on a small device is dormancy — personas held sealed and activated one at a time (L5C-management-wallet.md, 5.1 e), never two running at once.

4.4 Claim state — the physical guard

4.4.1 Every persona shall carry a persisted claim state (3.6): OPEN or OWNER.

4.4.2 Claim state shall be OPEN only at first boot or after a physical reset. A claim — enrolment of an OPEN device into an owner's group — shall proceed only from OPEN, checked both before the request is emitted and again before installation, and the installation shall set OWNER in the same atomic commit that installs the persona.

4.4.3 The transition OWNER to OPEN shall be possible only by local physical action on the device. There shall be no network-invocable path to OPEN — not even an authenticated one.

4.4.4 Leaving a group shall not set OPEN. The departing hive enters a closed group of one (4.3.4) and remains unclaimable until physically reset.

4.4.5 A firmware update shall preserve the persona and its claim state.

4.4.6 A missing or unreadable claim state shall be treated at boot as not-OWNER and not claimable over the network, recoverable only by local action.

4.4.6a A hive should report a missing claim state distinguishably from an unreadable one.

Note 0 to entry: 4.4.6 folds the two for behaviour, correctly, and 4.4.6a refuses to fold them for reporting. Neither is evidence of OPEN, so neither is claimable and the claim logic treats them identically — but an unprovisioned device and one whose storage faulted are entirely different facts to whoever investigates, with different fixes. This is Layer 6, 5.5.4's rule one layer down, and 01-terminology.md 5.3's test applied to a report rather than to a decision: indistinguishable to the machinery and indistinguishable to whoever acts on it are different questions.

Note 0a to entry: The distinction is destroyed at the point of reading, not downstream, and that is where it has to be defended. An implementation reported the root cause as its own input type: a claim state read as an optional value has one empty case, and there are two ways to have no value — so the conflation happens before any later code could possibly recover it. No amount of care downstream restores a distinction the input type has already erased. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), from the lane that found it in its own reader.)

Note 1 to entry: 4.4.3 is the clause that closes remote takeover, and its absolutism is the point. Any network path to OPEN — however authenticated — is a remote reset-then-claim chain: whoever can invoke it can make the device claimable and then claim it. Requiring the hand that resets to be at the device makes stealing a hive cost physical presence, which is the one cost this standard's threat model consistently relies on.

Note 2 to entry: 4.4.4 closes the same hole from the other side. If leaving made a device claimable, then anyone who could induce a leave — a compromised key holder, a forged eviction — could open it remotely. A departed hive is sovereign, not available.

Verification: attempt the OWNER-to-OPEN transition by every network surface the implementation exposes, authenticated and not. All fail. The vacuity guard is the local path: the physical action succeeds, or the test proved only that nothing works.

5 Identity and keys

5.1 The identity split

5.1.1 A hive shall generate a fresh member keypair (3.5) for each persona. The public half is the persona's identity within the group and its certificate subject.

5.1.1a A persona identity shall be unique among personas. A hive shall present as its own only a persona identity it generated under 5.1.1 and has held without interruption since.

Note 1 to entry: The obligation is stated directly, because the corpus does not define a persona as a composition of identifiers and this clause must not be the first place that says it does. 3.2 defines a persona as a hive's membership of one trust group together with the identity and key material that membership gives it — membership and material, not a pair of identifiers. What composes a group half with a hive half is the WIRE ENCODING of an extended identity (FORMATS.md 3.3, Layer 4, 6.1.3), which is a projection of the persona and not its definition. So uniqueness does not derive from a composition and is required here on its own terms.

Note 1a to entry: What the persona's identity IS, and what it is not, because the two are deliberately separated. 5.1.1 makes the persona's identity the member keypair's public half. Layer 0, 4.3.1 separately requires every device to have an identity unique among devices that persists across loss of power — and 5.1.2 forbids that device identity from being transmitted on the mesh, from being a certificate subject, and from being derivable from any persona identity. So the hive half on the wire is a projection of the PERSONA's key, never of the device identity — the two obligations govern different objects, and satisfying one says nothing about the other. (Recorded because a natural reading joins them and the clauses forbid it.)

Note 2 to entry: The sharper reading of a duplicate, and the reason the clause is worth having: two devices presenting one persona means one of the two halves was not what it claimed. Either a device identity was not unique or did not persist — Layer 0, 4.3.1 — or a persona was adopted rather than generated, which the second sentence forbids. A duplicated persona is therefore never a normal condition and never an accident of the wire.

Note 3 to entry: This is not in conflict with FORMATS.md 3.6, which governs a different object, and that document already draws the line. 3.6's uniqueness is not guaranteed applies to the four-byte truncated wire identity, where a collision is a property of a hash and nobody is at fault — such collisions are survived. Its own closing sentence separates the two: the identity itself (the public key) is what is unique with overwhelming probability. 5.1.1a makes that an obligation on the persona key rather than an expectation about randomness: what 3.6 says will almost certainly hold, this clause says shall hold, and a breach means something went wrong rather than something was unlucky.

Note 4 to entry: What this clause does NOT say, stated because the gap is deliberate: it places no duty on a receiver. Nothing here requires a hive observing one identity from two sources to detect, report or act on it, and the two mechanisms such a duplicate would disturb — the per-origin high-water mark of 7.4.3 b) and Layer 3's duplicate suppression — remain documented as anti-replay and as a member's misbehaviour respectively. Whether a receiver owes anything is a separate question and is not answered here. (Added 2026-08-05 on Roy's ruling. RATIFIED by Roy 2026-08-05, d273 — 5.1.1a and its notes are normative text and their wording is his to change. Finding SS284.)

5.1.2 A hive's durable device identity (Layer 0, 4.3) shall never be transmitted on the mesh, shall never be a certificate subject, and shall never be derivable from any persona identity.

5.1.3 The identities of one hive's successive personas shall not be derivable from one another or from anything the hive transmits.

5.1.4 On re-persona (3.9), a hive shall destroy the member keypair, the derived keys and the beacon identifier of the old persona, and shall generate all three afresh.

Note 1 to entry: The split in 5.1.2 is the standard's deepest privacy commitment: what the mesh sees is a persona; what the owner holds is a device. The durable identity exists for exactly one audience — the owner, off-mesh, for update targeting and custody — and keeping it off the air is what makes 5.1.3 achievable at all.

Note 2 to entry: 5.1.4's third item is easy to miss and voids the other two if missed. The beacon identifier is stable through a persona's life (Layer 2), and canon holds that a persona change is observationally a different hive. A beacon identifier carried across re-persona links the two personas to any observer with a log — the one identifier that survived is the one identifier that was broadcast in clear the whole time.

5.2 One derivation, many keys

5.2.1 Every key derived at this layer shall use one derivation: HKDF-SHA256, 32-byte output, the relevant secret as input keying material, the relevant public binding as salt, and a domain-separating purpose string as info.

5.2.2 The group's payload-protection key and its integrity key shall be derived from the group secret by that template, under distinct purpose strings.

5.2.3 No two derivations shall share a purpose string.

Note 1 to entry: One template is a rule about the future as much as the present. The next key this layer needs will be derived the same way with a new purpose string — and because purpose strings are disjoint, no new derivation can collide with an existing one by accident. A second derivation scheme is how that guarantee is lost.

5.3 Custody of key material

5.3.1 The group secret key shall exist only on key holders (3.4), stored in the most protected facility the platform provides.

5.3.1a Where the platform provides no facility meeting 5.3.2, the group secret key shall live in volatile memory only.

5.3.1b 5.3.1 shall not be read as permitting a key to rest unsealed in the most protected facility a platform happens to provide.

Note 0 to entry: 5.3.1 states a RELATIVE obligation and 5.3.2 an ABSOLUTE one, and on a platform with no sealing facility they contradict. The most protected facility the platform provides is satisfied by plaintext flash where that is all there is; plaintext key material at rest is forbidden in every case is not. Two adjacent clauses cannot both hold there, and an implementer resolving it privately will resolve it the cheap way. 5.3.1a says which governs: the absolute one. Raised by a lane whose platform turned out to have a qualifying facility — so this defect did not bite it and would have waited for a platform that has nothing. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), finding SS217.)

5.3.2 Derived keys shall be persisted only sealed, such that what rests in storage is unreadable without a hardware-rooted secret of the device. Where the platform provides no such facility, derived keys shall live in volatile memory only and be re-obtained on next contact with the group. Plaintext key material at rest is forbidden in every case.

Note 0 to entry: The verify of Note 1 below is verification under the group's symmetric integrity key, and it establishes nothing about which peer sent a frame. 7.2.2 states the bound — passage of the gate does not establish which member sent a frame — and 7.2 Note 1 states the mechanism: every member of a group can tag any frame of it. The pointer is here because the collapse has been made: two independent readers took unable to verify as unable to recognise a specific peer, on the same day and in the same direction. Losing keys costs a hive the ability to verify frames of its group at all; it costs nothing that was ever per-peer, because nothing here was. (Added 2026-08-04, PROVISIONAL(standard), finding SS277.)

Note 1 to entry: The volatile-only rule cannot be absolute, and the reason is the member that is not a key holder: it has no group secret to re-derive from (5.2 derives from what it does not hold), so an absolute rule would leave a field sensor that rebooted alone deaf to its own group — unable to verify, tag or decrypt — until it next met a key holder, which may be days. Sealed persistence under a hardware root is the ratified resolution (corpus key-custody contract, 2026-06-23): the requirement is stated as a property — unreadable without the hardware-rooted secret — never as a named silicon feature, and what a given platform's sealing facility is, and how its presence is declared and checked, remains the Layer 0 gap (L0 10.4) this clause inherits.

5.3.3 A member secret key shall never leave the hive that generated it.

5.3.4 An implementation shall erase secret key material when it leaves use, by a means the toolchain cannot elide; shall not make implicit copies of it; and shall redact it from every diagnostic, log, error and serialised representation.

Note 1 to entry: 5.3.4's third item is the one that catches real systems. A key printed into a crash report has been persisted as surely as one written to disk — the rule against persistence is only as strong as the discipline of every path that formats a structure containing a key.

Verification: by inspection of types — key-bearing types are non-copyable and erase on release — and by asserting the redacted form of every key-bearing type's diagnostic output.

6 Certificates

6.1 What a certificate is

6.1.1 A member's certificate shall bind the member keypair's public half to the group, signed by the group secret key.

6.1.2 A hive is a member of a group if and only if it holds a valid, unrevoked certificate of that group.

6.1.3 A certificate shall name the key epoch (Clause 8) at which it was issued, and shall be current only while that epoch is within the group's stated acceptance depth of the current epoch and its subject is not revoked.

6.1.4 Certificates shall be re-issued in the background: at rotation for members present, and on next contact with a key holder for members that were not.

6.1.5 A member absent through any number of rotations remains a member unless revoked. On return it walks the retained chain forward (8.4), or receives current material directly at re-issue; a key holder shall re-issue against the member keypair and any authentic prior certificate of that subject, however old, unless the subject is in the revocation set. Absence shall never be grounds for refusal.

Note 1 to entry: Rotation cadence and acceptance depth together are the quiet half of revocation. A revocation that fails to reach a member is bounded by cadence × depth — the certificate it would have revoked falls out of currency when its epoch does — which is the bound certificate expiry used to provide, now measured in rotations instead of calendar time.

Note 2 to entry: 6.1.3 replaced this standard's one absolute-time judgement (ruled 2026-08-01, CLOSED-QUESTIONS.md C8). Currency is now a set-membership question over epochs — does this certificate chain to an epoch I still accept? — answerable by any platform from material it already holds, with nothing to reconstruct after power loss. Every time judgement in this standard is now an interval, and the intervals that matter here (the rotation cadence) are measured by key holders, the clocked and powered end.

Note 3 to entry: 6.1.5 is membership never decays made mechanical, and it is the returning device's whole story. A sensor dark for a season comes back holding its member keypair (which never rotates) and a stale-epoch certificate: stale currency is not lost membership. Its certificate is no longer money, but it is still evidence — an authentic, revocation-checked claim on re-issue. Where the retained chain no longer reaches back to its epoch, the revocation set does (6.2.1, append-only and never pruned): the set is the permanent summary of every eviction the walker missed.

6.2 Revocation

6.2.1 A group shall maintain a revocation set: append-only, grow-only, never pruned.

6.2.2 Each entry shall name the revoked certificate subject; the key epoch (Clause 8) current when it was issued, together with a sequence number within that epoch; a reason drawn from 6.2.2a; and shall be signed by the group secret key. An entry may also carry an absolute time, which is advisory only and shall not be relied on by any decision.

6.2.2a The reason shall be one of: compromise — the subject's key material is believed exposed; eviction — the subject is removed from the group by decision; retirement — the subject has left or been decommissioned without fault; or superseded — the certificate is replaced by a later one for the same subject.

Note 1 to entry: The ordering is (epoch, sequence), not a clock, for the reason the whole standard gives: time is a local ruler, not a shared fact. The epoch is already carried by every certificate (6.1.3) and already increments on every rotation (8.5), so the ordering a revocation set needs exists without inventing anything. An absolute time is permitted because an operator reading a revocation set wants to know when, and forbidden from being load-bearing because two groups' clocks are not comparable and nothing in this standard makes them so. The reason set is enumerated here because 8.2 grades the grace period by it — compromise gets zero grace, and a reason the receiver cannot interpret cannot be graded. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor).)

6.2.2b A revocation entry shall be admitted to the revocation set only after its signature has been verified against the group's public key.

Note 1 to entry: 6.2.1 and 6.2.2 are each correct and dangerous together: the set is append-only and never pruned, so an entry admitted before its signature is checked is permanent, and a single forged entry evicts an arbitrary member irreversibly — with 6.2.4's binding of revocations into rotations giving the forgery reach. This is the ordering rule of 01-terminology.md 5.2, and it is worse here than at 7.4.3a because no epoch lapses it. An implementation had exactly this defect, found by audit rather than by test, for the usual reason: no legitimate exchange distinguishes the two orderings. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor).)

6.2.3 On learning of a revocation, a member shall reject the revoked certificate immediately.

6.2.4 A revocation that evicts a member shall be bound into the key rotation it triggers (Clause 8), such that a member cannot accept the rotation without also receiving the eviction.

6.2.4a The binding shall be a signature by the group secret key over the rotation link, covering the epoch it rotates to and the link's cause, where the cause states either that the rotation is routine or that it carries an eviction, and in the latter case commits to the revoked subject and the reason of 6.2.2a. A member shall reject a link whose signature does not verify.

Note 1 to entry: 6.2.4 states a requirement that cannot be met without 6.2.4a. As first written it said what must be true — the eviction cannot be separated from the rotation — without saying what makes that so, and nothing else in this document said what makes a rotation link authentic at all. A link presented as routine, with the eviction simply omitted, was therefore indistinguishable from a genuine routine rotation: a returning member walks forward, arrives at the current epoch holding current keys, and never learns the eviction happened. That is precisely the outcome 6.2.4 exists to prevent, and no implementation could detect it.

Note 2 to entry: The signature covers the cause, not merely the eviction, and the difference is the whole mechanism. Signing only an eviction leaves a routine claim unauthenticated, so stripping the eviction still yields a link that verifies as routine. Signing the cause makes a routine link and an eviction-carrying link different signed objects, so omission changes the signed bytes and fails. It also gives 8.4's walk-forward something to verify per link, which it otherwise lacks.

Note 3 to entry: Both omissions were measured as rewarding, which is why the reason is inside the commitment and not merely alongside it. Dropping the entry keeps an evicted member alive on every hive that walks the chain. Dropping the reason turns a compromise eviction's mandatory zero grace (8.2) into the deployment's full grace — and Clause 8's own note says grace on a compromise eviction is a window in which the rotation has changed nothing. One omitted field converts the most urgent action in this standard into a no-op while the hive believes it has rotated. (Added 2026-08-02 from an implementation that could not close this in code and said so; PROVISIONAL(supervisor).)

Note 1 to entry: 6.2.4 is how revocation reaches the absent. Propagating revocations is best-effort and proves nothing about who received them — what an absent member cannot skip is the key rotation it has to accept to keep functioning, so the eviction rides inside the thing its acceptance is conditioned on. The set is the record; the rotation is the delivery.

Note 2 to entry: The bound this achieves is honest and partial: a member that stays absent learns nothing until it next meets the group, and its staleness is unbounded and unobservable meanwhile. That residual is stated rather than engineered away, because a monotonic mechanism cannot express "revoked at the current epoch" — only the currency bound of cadence × depth (6.1 Note 1) limits that case.

7 The delivery gate

7.1 The gate

7.1.1 A receiver shall apply the delivery gate to every frame carrying a group integrity tag before anything above this layer sees it.

7.1.2 The gate shall be, in order:

a) not addressed to me — neither to my group, nor to my hive, nor to all hives (Layer 4, 6.1) → relay only, never deliver locally;

b) addressed to me → verify the tag against the group's currently valid integrity keys — the current key, and the immediately prior key only within its grace period; on success, deliver as intra-group;

c) on failure of b), where live entanglements exist, trial-verify the tag against each live entanglement's keys; on a match, deliver as a crossing of that entanglement, subject to its scope and level;

d) nothing verifies → drop.

7.1.3 A receiver holding no usable key for a group-addressed frame shall not deliver it. Failing open shall not be a configuration, a fallback, or a migration default.

Note 1 to entry: 7.1.2 a) says addressed to me, not addressed to my group, because Layer 4 addresses a frame to all hives, to one group, or to one hive (6.1.1) — and a frame addressed to this hive's half is one the layers below deliver upward (Layer 3, 8.1 c). Read as my group alone, unicast-to-hive and broadcast would both fall to relay-only and never be delivered by anyone, which is not the design. The tag is still verified under group or entanglement keys in b) and c); what a) decides is whose frame this is, not which key opens it. (Corrected 2026-08-01 — PROVISIONAL(supervisor), pending Roy: two independent implementation readings hit the literal text and reached this same conclusion.)

Note 2 to entry: A grace period does not survive a restart. 7.1.2 b) accepts the immediately prior key only within its grace period; a grace deadline is an instant on the local monotonic ruler; and Layer 0, 5.2 forbids assuming that an interval spanning a power cycle is measurable. A deadline recorded before a restart therefore means nothing after it, and the only reading consistent with 7.1.3 is that no prior key is in grace after a restart until the group re-supplies material. An implementer who persists the deadline and trusts it gets a silently over-long grace window — an error in exactly the direction this clause exists to prevent. (Stated 2026-08-01 from implementation evidence; it follows from two clauses and was written in neither.)

7.1.3a Where the computation of the authenticated span and the verification of the tag are performed by separately implemented components — as Layer 0, 6.1 requires — the interface between them shall carry the frame, or bytes the verifying component can itself establish to be the span of Layer 4, 10.2.1. A caller-chosen byte sequence shall not be accepted as the span.

Note 1 to entry: A verifier verifies a tag over whatever bytes it is given, and every byte sequence is a valid one. A caller passing a shorter span — omitting the target, say — obtains a tag that verifies while saying nothing about the omitted field, which an attacker may then alter freely. Nothing in such an interface refuses it, and no test of either side finds it: both components are correct, and the agreement between them is the whole of the guarantee.

Note 2 to entry: This is a seam that Layer 0, 6.1 creates rather than one an implementer chose. That clause forbids Layer 1–4 code from touching trust material, so the span is computed where the frame is understood and the tag is verified where the key is held, and the two ends cannot be one function. An isolation requirement does not remove the coupling — it converts a shared function into an agreement between parties, which is no longer enforced by a compiler and is enforced instead by whoever reads both sides. 01-terminology.md 5.4b applies at this seam by the same reasoning as at a durable record.

Note 3 to entry: Carrying the frame is the stronger of the two permitted forms, because it makes a wrong span unrepresentable rather than discouraged; the second form exists for implementations whose layering genuinely prevents it. Both are conformant, and the asymmetry is doing the work rather than a prohibition: the escape hatch exists but is visibly the lesser option, so choosing it is a decision someone has to defend rather than a default they drift into. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), finding SS148, from an implementation that found the seam by applying a general rule to code it had written hours earlier.)

7.1.4 The gate shall be identical on every platform class and every bearer. There shall be no local-network, same-site or debug exemption in a production image.

Note 1 to entry: 7.1.2 a) is the boundary made operational: relay is below the gate, and the gate governs delivery only. A hive forwards frames it could never verify, and that is correct — the gate's question is never "may this frame exist" but "may this frame act here".

Note 2 to entry: The trial-verify in c) is deliberate. The frame does not say which key signed it, because a claim in the frame would still have to be checked by trying the key — the flag would add bytes and remove nothing (Layer 4, 10.3). Entanglements are few, so the trial is cheap; a forged frame fails every key a no-key forger could not hold.

Note 3 to entry: 7.1.3's phrasing is from a real failure: a gate that delivered unverified when no keys were loaded, in a build whose key loader never ran in production — an inactive gate that reported itself as a gate. Everything downstream believed the frames were verified. No gate at all would have been safer, because nothing would have claimed otherwise.

Verification: present a group-addressed frame to a receiver holding no key for it. The frame relays or drops, and nothing above Layer 5 observes it. The vacuity guard: the same frame, with the key loaded, delivers.

7.2 What passing the gate establishes — and does not

7.2.1 A frame passing the gate under a group key establishes one thing: the content was tagged by a holder of an accepted key of that group.

7.2.2 A receiver shall not treat passage as establishing which member sent the frame, that the sender currently holds a valid certificate, that the frame is fresh, or that no key holder altered it in flight.

7.2.3 An operation whose effect depends on which member asked, or on when, shall require evidence beyond the gate: a signature attributable to a member, freshness evidence, or both. The gate is a floor, never the authorisation.

Note 1 to entry: The group integrity key is symmetric and group-wide — every member can tag any frame, and a captured frame replays with a valid tag. 7.2 exists because the natural reading of "verified" is all the things 7.2.2 lists, and every one of them is false. The layer that acts on a frame decides how much more than the floor it needs, and Clause 13 records what this document has not yet given it.

Note 2 to entry: The natural misreading is specific, and it survives knowing 7.2.1. A frame's origin sits inside Layer 4's authenticated span, so an implementer reads that the tag covers the origin — which it does — and concludes that the origin is authenticated, which it is not. The span makes the origin unforgeable by an outsider; the group key makes it freely forgeable by a member. The concrete consequence is at Layer 3: duplicate suppression keys on (origin, message identifier), so a member can pre-claim another member's pairs and have every relay drop that member's genuine frames — one member silencing another, with valid tags throughout. Layer 3, 5.3.4 bounds it and Layer 3, 10.5 records what remains. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), after an implementer swept for artefacts trusted because they validate and asked whether per-member attribution was ever meant to be authenticated. It is not — 7.2.2 says so, and 7.2.3 says what to use instead.)

7.3 Confidentiality — the classification names the key

7.3.1 The payload of an intra-group frame shall be encrypted under the group's payload-protection key (5.2.2), unless the event is public by design; the payload of an entangled crossing shall be encrypted under that entanglement's encryption key, at its level.

7.3.2 A frame at the unauthenticated level carries its payload in clear: no shared key exists at that level, and none is negotiated.

7.3.3 No frame shall carry a key identifier, cipher negotiation, or any statement of which key protects it. The delivery gate's classification names the decryption key: a frame that verified as own-group decrypts under the group's key; a frame that verified as a crossing decrypts under the matched entanglement's key.

7.3.4 The fields a relay reads — the event identifier hash, the target, the hop limit, the budget, the route record — are not encrypted, on any frame.

Note 1 to entry: 7.3.3 is why two sentants never negotiate how to understand each other. The act that authorises a frame is the act that names its key — membership distributed the group key at join; establishment distributed the entanglement's at agreement — so by the time a payload is decrypted, the question "which key?" was already answered by the verification that admitted it. A key identifier in the frame would answer a question nobody still has, and advertise key structure to every carrier.

Note 2 to entry: 7.3.4 states the honest boundary of confidentiality. A carrier sees that traffic exists, when, how much, between which neighbours, and what event hash it names — never what the payload says. The envelope is the price of being routable by devices that are deliberately not trusted; the non-aggregation position (Clause 12) is what bounds the price.

Note 3 to entry: The cleartext of 7.3.2 is not a weakness of the unauthenticated level; it is its meaning. That level carries public speech — and anything confidential requires at least an entanglement, which is the incentive pointing exactly where the design wants it.

7.4 Member-attributable evidence

7.4.1 Evidence attributable to a member shall be a signature by that member's member key (5.2) over the operation or claim together with a freshness token (7.4.3), accompanied by the member's certificate (Clause 6).

7.4.2 A verifier shall verify the chain against the issuing group's public key alone — the certificate binds the member key to the group; the signature binds the claim to the member key — and shall not require the group's membership roster.

7.4.3 The freshness token shall be one of:

a) for an interactive exchange, a nonce issued by the verifier for this exchange; or

b) otherwise, a monotonic (epoch, sequence) token, against which the verifier shall keep a per-origin high-water mark and shall reject evidence whose token does not exceed the last accepted.

Neither form shall require absolute time.

7.4.3a A verifier shall not advance the high-water mark on evidence whose signature it has not verified. The mark is advanced by evidence, and a token is evidence only once signed: the certificate chain and the member signature are verified first, and the mark is advanced last.

Note 1 to entry: Without this ordering the anti-replay mechanism becomes a lockout, and the attack costs nothing. Certificates are public — 6.1.4 re-issues them in the background and 7.4.1 has them accompany every piece of evidence — so any party who has ever seen member M's certificate can present it with a junk signature and a far-future sequence. A verifier that recorded the token before verifying it then rejects M's own genuine evidence at every sequence below that value, until the epoch changes. M is locked out; the verifier believes it is enforcing anti-replay correctly; and M's frames pass the delivery gate the whole time, so nothing looks wrong at any layer.

Note 2 to entry: A stateless pre-filter is permitted and sensible — rejecting an obviously stale token before doing curve arithmetic costs an attacker nothing and saves a verifier real work. What 7.4.3a forbids is the state change, not the early rejection.

Note 3 to entry: The dangerous ordering and the correct one are indistinguishable in a passing test suite: every legitimate exchange behaves identically under both, and only an adversary tells them apart. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), from implementation evidence — the finding is core's, and the ordering was not stated anywhere.)

7.4.4 A verifier may accept, and a member may produce, a membership proof that does not reveal which member signed, only where the entanglement between the groups carries a condition explicitly granting that verifier the member-key set the proof is verified against. Where no such condition stands, the anonymous form is not offered.

Note 1 to entry: 7.4.2 is the load-bearing choice: the evidence carries its own chain, so the verifier needs nothing it does not already hold — its own group's public key, or an entangled group's, learned at establishment. The alternative — proofs verified against an enumerated member set — requires distributing the roster to every verifier, which cross-group is a disclosure of exactly what 12.5 keeps unenumerable. Revocation rides the machinery already here: rotation on eviction (Clause 8) retires the certificate with the key that signed it.

Note 2 to entry: What the default form reveals is which member key signed — a per-group pseudonym, derived independently of every other identifier the device has (5.1, 5.2), and meaningless outside the group's own scope. For the operations that need this evidence — authority, manifests, attestations — the attribution is the point.

Note 3 to entry: 7.4.4 prices the anonymity trade rather than resolving it by fiat. Anonymity toward a verifier costs the roster's disclosure to that verifier, and signature work that grows with the group — so the group that wants the anonymous form pays for it, in a condition declared in its entanglement's terms, and a platform that cannot afford the computation simply does not offer the grade.

Note 4 to entry: One scheme, every consumer: member-specific authority (7.2.3), update manifests (Layer 6), attestations at their ruled shape — level, time, signature — and anti-replay, which is 7.4.3 doing its work rather than a separate mechanism. Byte formats for the evidence, the certificate and the token are the companion document's.

Verification: present a verifier with valid evidence; the same evidence replayed (token not exceeding the high-water mark); evidence whose certificate names an epoch outside the group's acceptance depth (6.1.3); and a bare signature without its certificate. It accepts the first and rejects the other three — and the anonymous form, offered without a standing roster-grant condition, is refused.

8 Rotation

8.1 A group shall be able to rotate its derived keys, and shall rotate on every eviction.

8.2 A rotation shall carry a grace period for the immediately prior key, graded by reason: an eviction for compromise shall have zero grace.

8.3 A rotation triggered by eviction shall bind the eviction as 6.2.4 requires.

8.4 A member absent through one or more rotations shall be able to walk forward through the retained chain when it returns, receiving each rotation's bound evictions as it goes. A member shall verify each link of that chain before absorbing the evictions bound into it, and shall stop at the first link that does not verify.

Note 0 to entry: The walk is the one path by which a member accepts evictions it did not witness, and the links arrive over the network. Absorbing them before verification is 6.2.2b's defect reached by a different route — and a worse one, because a single forged link both evicts an arbitrary member and carries the walk forward past the point where anything true could still be learned. Stopping at the first bad link, rather than skipping it, is what keeps a partial walk honest: a member that cannot verify link n knows only what it knew at n−1. (Added 2026-08-02; found by re-reading the neighbours of 6.2.2b after fixing it. PROVISIONAL(supervisor).)

8.5 Each rotation shall increment the group's key epoch, and the epoch shall be named in every certificate issued (6.1.3).

8.6 A group's policy shall state a maximum interval between rotations, and its key holders shall rotate at least that often, measured on their own rulers; the policy shall likewise state the acceptance depth of 6.1.3.

Note 1 to entry: Grace exists for the honest member that was briefly asleep during an ordinary rotation, and 8.2's zero-grace case exists because the compromised key is the thing the rotation exists to retire — a grace period on it is a window in which the rotation has changed nothing.

Note 2 to entry: 8.6 exists because rotation now carries the function certificate expiry used to carry (6.1 Note 1): a group that never rotated would never expire anything, and a missed revocation's window would be unbounded. The obligation is normative; the two numbers — cadence and depth — are the group's own, chosen for its purpose the way every graded value in this standard is, and measured at the clocked, powered end.

For this artefact a typo and a different group are indistinguishable.

Note 2c to entry: What this lane verified and what it did not, because on this artefact the distinction is the point. Note 2d to entry: Two excluded cases, because they fail at different gates and only one is cheap to catch. 10 Entanglement

10.1 The ladder of authentication

10.1.1 Communication between hives of different trust groups shall be possible at every level of the following ladder, and the receiving side decides, per operation, what level that operation requires.

Level Evidence the receiver holds What may happen
same-group the frame verifies under the receiver's own group key full intra-group delivery
entangled, by grade the frame verifies under a live entanglement's keys delivery within that entanglement's scope and grade
unauthenticated the frame carries no group tag delivery as unauthenticated; the receiving application's own filtering (Layer 7) is the only gate, and no group-addressed claim was made

10.1.2 A frame carrying a group tag that verifies under nothing shall be dropped (7.1.2 d); a frame carrying no tag is not that case, and may be delivered as unauthenticated.

Note 1 to entry: The ladder governs delivery — what a frame may do above the trust boundary. It does not govern communication as such, because below the boundary, devices of any and every group are already communicating: relaying one another's frames, hearing one another's beacons, forming one another's neighbour entries. That is the shared mesh (Layers 0 to 4), it exists before and beneath any entanglement, and neither entanglement nor its absence touches it. Entanglement is a relationship between trust groups; the mesh is a relationship between devices, and every device is in it.

Note 2 to entry: The unauthenticated level is why hives that share no group and no entanglement can still exchange application traffic. An unauthenticated frame claims nothing and gets nothing — it reaches the sentant firewall (Layer 7, 9.1), where no handler means no effect — but a public reading, a discovery exchange, a first hello all live at this level, and requiring entanglement for them would make every first contact impossible. What distinguishes it from the failed verification of 7.1.2 d) is the claim: a frame that claimed a group and failed its proof is a forgery; a frame that claimed nothing is a stranger, and strangers are legal.

10.2 What an entanglement is

10.2.1 An entanglement shall be an explicit agreement between two trust groups, holding its own derived keys under the one derivation of 5.2, carrying:

a) a scope — what may cross, in which direction;

b) a level of authentication — the grade at which crossings are trusted; and

c) its conditions — the stated terms on which the entanglement stands.

10.2.2 An entanglement may be short-lived or long-lived; its intended lifetime is one of its conditions.

10.2.3 An entanglement shall persist exactly as long as its conditions are met.

Note 1 to entry: The companion is L5A-entanglement.md, and it is not optional reading for an implementer of this clause: establishment and its sequence are its Clause 5, the grades of authentication its 5.3, the expression and evaluation of conditions its Clause 6, and the ways an entanglement ends its Clause 8. A reader of this document alone has the model but not the mechanism. (Cross-reference made explicit 2026-08-01.)

10.3 Breach

10.3.1 Where either side breaches the conditions, the other side shall either sever the entanglement or demote it — move it down a level of authentication — and which response applies to which breach is itself one of the conditions.

10.3.2 Severing shall stop crossings at once: frames verifying only under the severed entanglement's keys fail the gate from that moment. Demotion shall re-scope crossings to the lower level at once.

10.3.3 Either side may sever at any time, without the other's consent, conditions aside.

Note 1 to entry: The conditions are the entanglement. Two groups are not entangled because a ceremony happened once; they are entangled as long as the stated terms hold — which makes an entanglement a standing claim, tested by conduct, in exactly the sense the rest of this standard tests claims: never proven, only un-breached so far. Demotion is that epistemology given a mechanism — a breach that warrants less trust rather than none moves the relationship down the 10.1 ladder instead of off it.

Note 2 to entry: 10.3.3 preserves sovereignty over and above the conditions: no group is ever contractually locked into trusting another. The conditions govern what counts as a breach; they never oblige a group to keep a door open.

10.4 Crossings

10.4.1 A frame crossing an entanglement shall pass the delivery gate as 7.1.2 c), and its acceptance establishes which entanglement it crossed — never which member of the entangled group sent it.

Note 1 to entry: This clause states the contract; establishment, the grades themselves, condition expression, and lifecycle are the entanglement companion's (L5A-entanglement.md) — the frame here is the decided part, and the companion fills it.

11 What this layer provides and requires

11.1 This layer shall provide to the layers above:

a) delivered frames, each marked intra-group, a crossing of a named entanglement, or unauthenticated;

b) the hive's persona: its group, its member identity, its claim state; and

c) the association between beacon identifiers and known peers (Layer 2, 5.4.3), learned here and only here.

11.2 This layer shall require of the layers below: delivery of frames as Layer 3 provides them, the integrity tag mechanics of Layer 4, and the key-material facilities of Layer 0.

11.3 Nothing below this layer shall hold, read or depend on trust material. (Layer 0, Clause 6.)

12 Security considerations

12.1 The group's symmetric keys are held by every member. The capture of any one member exposes what those keys protect — past traffic it recorded, and the ability to tag frames — until rotation. Membership is the security perimeter, and the perimeter is as strong as its most exposed member.

12.2 The gate authenticates groups, not members (7.2). Everything member-specific rides above it.

12.3 Replay of captured frames verifies (Layer 4, 10.2 Note 3). Idempotent effects are the systemic defence; traffic that is not idempotent carries the freshness token of 7.4.3, and its replay fails at the verifier's high-water mark.

12.4 Physical possession of an OPEN device is ownership of it, by design (4.4). Physical possession of an OWNER device yields its persona's material only as well as the platform protects it — and Layer 0 records that protection as a stated gap.

12.5 What this layer never does: no central authority exists to compromise, no registry maps groups to devices, and no party outside a group can enumerate its members from protocol traffic.

Note 0 to entry: 12.5's third claim is stated without a bound and is not true without one. Layer 4, 6.1.3 puts a group half and a hive half in the extended target, both four bytes on the wire; 7.3.4 leaves the target unencrypted on every frame; and the route record carries hive identities the same way. So a party in range that neither relays nor joins can read (group, hive) pairs directly off passing frames — which is enumeration of that group's transmitting members, to the resolution of a truncated hash. What is true is the bounded version: no party can enumerate members it never hears, no registry maps groups to devices, and the identities recovered are truncated hashes rather than identities (FORMATS.md 3.6 declines to guarantee their uniqueness). The unqualified sentence claims more than the wire permits. AND THE CONSEQUENCE REACHES BACK: Note 2 to 7.3.4 names the non-aggregation position (Clause 12) as what bounds the price of the unencrypted envelope — so an over-broad 12.5 is a bound that bounds nothing, at the one place the standard admits its envelope is readable. (Added 2026-08-04, PROVISIONAL(standard), finding SS282, reached from android's question whether Clause 12 shares 7.3.4 Note 2's carrier-scoping. It does not share that defect; it has a larger one.)

13 What is not decided at this layer

This clause states gaps and contains no requirements.

13.1 The enrolment ceremony — drafted (2026-08-01). The ceremony between the states this document defines is now L5B-enrolment.md: invitation, commit-then-reveal exchange, role-graded verification, atomic install, forward-only re-claim. What remains owed from this gap is its byte formats (with the formats companion) and the management wallet (13.7).

13.2 Certificate time — settled (2026-08-01). Certificate validity was this standard's one absolute-time judgement, unanswerable after restart on platforms this standard permits. Ruled: epoch-anchored currency (6.1.3), with obligatory rotation cadence (8.6) carrying expiry's function and the return path stated (6.1.5). No clause of this standard now requires absolute time. (The ruling: CLOSED-QUESTIONS.md C8; this was the surviving half of the question the dropped rotating identifier closed.)

13.3 Protecting persona material against physical capture of an OWNER device. Layer 0 requires retention and records protection as a gap (L0 10.4). The consequence at this layer is 12.1: a captured member is a group exposure. Hardware-anchored protection, scoped member keys for exposed roles, or acceptance — undecided, and the decision shapes what a field deployment may store on its most exposed devices.

13.4 Anti-replay for non-idempotent operations — settled (2026-08-01). The freshness token of 7.4.3 closes this: interactive exchanges carry the verifier's nonce, everything else a monotonic token gated by a per-origin high-water mark, and neither requires absolute time. What remains owed is the token's byte format, with the companion.

13.5 Member-attributable evidence — the scheme is settled (2026-08-01); the formats are owed. 7.4 defines the shape: a certificate-carrying member signature verified against the group public key alone, with the anonymous form gated behind an explicit roster-disclosure condition — which resolves the corpus's member-set conflict by pricing it into the entanglement's terms. Byte formats for evidence, certificate and token are the companion document's. (The ruling: CLOSED-QUESTIONS.md C7.)

13.6 Aggregate rate limiting. Below the boundary, per-origin limits bound nuisance (Layer 3, 5.7); nothing bounds a determined key-holding origin across the mesh. Whether group-level policy should, and how it would be expressed, is undecided.

13.7 Custody and the management wallet — drafted (2026-08-01). The wallet's rules are now L5C-management-wallet.md: custody kept out of membership, storing-vs-acting gated on the owner's attested level, custody removable only by rotation, backups counted as custodians, capture priced as every held group's compromise. What remains owed from this gap is byte formats and the splitting scheme, with the formats companion.

13.8 The public-by-design exemption of 7.3.1 — not decided, and not this layer's to decide. 7.3.1 exempts an event that is public by design from intra-group payload encryption and gives no criterion and no declaration mechanism. It cannot be given one here. 7.3.3 forbids a frame carrying any statement of which key protects it, and no key protects this is such a statement — so a receiver, told by its own classification to decrypt under the group key, cannot distinguish an exempt frame from an encrypted one by looking at the frame. The property must belong to the event's definition, known to both sides before the frame exists, which places it at Layer 7 (10.6). Until it is decided, the fail-closed reading governs: encrypt every intra-group payload and take no exemption, because 7.3.1's default is encryption and the exemption is the undefined branch. (Added 2026-08-04, PROVISIONAL(standard), finding SS44 — registered before this date, promoted when it began gating an implementation, and reached for by two lanes wiring 7.3.)

13.9 What a non-key-holder member holds — the material is entailed and no clause moves it. 5.2.2 requires the payload-protection and integrity keys, 7.3.1 and 7.4 require a member to use them, and 5.3.1 confines the group secret they derive from to key holders — so a member that is not a key holder must receive them. No clause states that it does. The enrolment companion's install step returns the group material the role carries (L5B 6.1.1 e) without defining the phrase, and 5.3.2's re-obtained on next contact with the group names no path. Note 1 to 7.3.3's membership distributed the group key at join is a note, and states the intent rather than the requirement. (Added 2026-08-04, PROVISIONAL(standard), finding SS271; stated fully in L5B 10.6.)

13.10 Clause 9 names an admission and specifies none — and the consequence lands on the certificate. L5B 4.5 makes the development group's published-credential admission (Clause 9) the one path that replaces the ceremony. Clause 9 gives key material (9.1a–9.1h), an admission rule (9.2) and production-side inertness (9.3), and no procedural clause at all. The consequence is specific rather than general: 6.1.2 makes a hive a member if and only if it holds a valid, unrevoked certificate; 6.1.1 requires that certificate to be signed by the group secret key; and the only issuance site the corpus describes is the ceremony's install step (L5B 6.1.1 e)), which this path replaces. So a development-mode device can hold this group's persona and still not be a member by 6.1.2, and Clause 9's stated purpose — benches interoperating without each minting its own group — does not complete. Nothing in this corpus says a device issues its own certificate; that reading is an inference, and it is not written here. And the text points away from it rather than merely failing to support it: 6.1.1 requires the signature of the group secret key, so whatever holds that key and can sign is a candidate issuer — which for a published-credential group is not necessarily the joining device. What 4.5 removes is the issuer, not the issuance. (Added 2026-08-04, PROVISIONAL(standard), finding SS266, sharpened by android, which reached the gap from the implementing side while migrating a member keypair. Whether this path is a second route or a variation within the ceremony is the companion question, SS267.)

13.12 What a development-mode device holds, and how it holds it. Clause 9 gives the development group's material, its admission rule and its production-side inertness, and says nothing about what a device on that group holds at run time or where it keeps it. Two silences follow, and a working bench network depends on both. First, the gate does not require membership. 7.1.2 b) verifies against the group's currently valid integrity keys — a key the receiver holds — while 6.1.2 makes membership turn on a certificate, and no clause requires the key to have arrived through the membership. A hive holding the key and no certificate therefore passes the gate and is not a member. Second, custody had no stated exemption here — CLOSED 2026-08-05 by 9.1i and 9.1j, ratified by Roy as d255. It read that 5.3.2 forbids plaintext key material at rest in every case while Clause 9 stated exceptions where it had them and none against Clause 5. 9.1i is now that exception and 9.1j is its fence, and 9.3a closes the production side. The first silence is NOT closed and is the one that matters now: nothing still requires the key to have arrived through a membership, so a hive holding it is not thereby a member. This half of the entry stays open, and who issues a development device's certificate remains SS266.

(Half-closure recorded here rather than by deleting the entry: a gap clause that is silently edited when part of it is answered leaves a reader unable to tell which part moved. This paragraph is not ratified text — the ratification is 9.1i, 9.1j and 9.3a — and updating a what is not decided clause when something becomes decided is bookkeeping, not an amendment to what Roy signed.) (Added 2026-08-04, PROVISIONAL(standard), finding SS276; adjacent to SS266, which asks who issues the certificate this silence makes unnecessary.)

13.11 The means by which a GROUP_MGMT frame's authenticity is established. Layer 4 states twice that this layer supplies it — 10.1.4 for its authenticity, Note 2 to 8.3 for its origin — and this document names no such means. The type does not appear anywhere in it. The second forward is the sharper problem: 7.2.2 states that passing the gate does not establish which member sent a frame, and 7.2.3 requires evidence beyond the gate for any operation turning on which member — so Layer 4 forwards to this layer the very property this layer declines to establish. And 7.2.3 does not close the gap by analogy, because its subject is a frame that passed the gate: it governs a floor that is insufficient, while an untagged frame has no floor at all, and 10.1.2 permits such a frame to be delivered as unauthenticated without stating any obligation on what receives it. What is owed is a receiver obligation — whether one exists, and what evidence discharges it. Any answer is constrained by carriage: Layer 4, 5.3 makes the payload opaque at that layer and its Note 2 to 8.3 makes carriage one hop with no route record, so member-attributable evidence (7.4) could only ride inside a payload Layer 4 must not parse. The hazard is armed rather than theoretical: an implementation that has not yet written a group-management consumer is safe by sequencing, not by any guard, and a clean delivery reads as permission. (Added 2026-08-04, PROVISIONAL(standard), finding SS274, raised by core as CORE-17 and confirmed here with an independent control. Created by settling SS31 — the ruling that GROUP_MGMT is never tagged is right, and it named a destination that is empty.)

14 Conformance

Every requirement of this document is demonstrated by conformance evidence of one of the three classes of 01-terminology.md, 3.10 — behavioural, by construction, or by declaration. A requirement whose evidence is by construction is not thereby optional: the demonstration is an inspection a third party can perform without the implementer's cooperation, and its absence is non-conformance exactly as a failed behavioural test is. Behavioural evidence for any requirement of this document carries the control 01-terminology.md 4.2 to 4.5 requires — the same procedure run against a case the requirement excludes, giving a distinguishable non-satisfying result attributable to the requirement under test; a demonstration that could not have come out any other way is not evidence. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor); the control sentence added the same day, finding SS183.)

(Added 2026-08-01 — PROVISIONAL(supervisor), pending Roy. Clause 1 has always asserted that this document establishes what conformance at this layer consists of, and no such clause existed; every sibling part has one. It is numbered 14 rather than inserted before Security considerations because clauses 12 and 13 are cited from other documents and from the register, and renumbering them would break those citations. Placement is Roy's to correct at ratification.)

14.1 An implementation conforms to this document where it:

a) holds exactly one group keypair per group and derives the group identifier from its public half (4.2), and maintains the one-group-per-hive and no-nesting rules of 4.1 and 4.3;

b) carries an algorithm identifier on every key, signature and ciphertext structure, and rejects unrecognised identifiers without acting on them (4.2.3);

c) maintains persona and claim state as Clause 4.4 requires, including the atomic OWNER-setting commit, the physical-action-only path to OPEN, and preservation across firmware update;

d) generates and protects key material as Clause 5 requires — fresh member keypair per persona, the single derivation of 5.2 under distinct purpose strings, and the storage, non-transmission and erasure rules of 5.3;

e) issues, re-issues and revokes certificates as Clause 6 requires, including background re-issue and the append-only revocation set;

f) applies the delivery gate of 7.1 in the stated order, on every platform class and every bearer, with no exemption in a production image, and never treats gate passage as member attribution (7.2);

g) encrypts payloads as 7.3 requires and carries no key identifier or cipher negotiation on any frame;

h) constructs and verifies member-attributable evidence as 7.4 requires, including the freshness token and the per-origin high-water mark; and

i) rotates keys as Clause 8 requires, binding evictions into the rotation that follows them.

14.2 Conformance to 7.1.2 shall be demonstrated with a frame addressed to the hive rather than to its group, and with a broadcast frame: each is delivered when its tag verifies, and neither falls to relay-only.

14.3 Conformance to 4.2.3 and 5.2 cannot presently be demonstrated: the test vectors this document's 4.2.4 sources identifier values, key sizes and reserved codes from do not exist, and the derivation's purpose strings are drafted but unratified (FORMATS.md 4a). Producing both is owed work, and until it lands two implementations can each believe themselves conformant with no way to prove it to each other.
