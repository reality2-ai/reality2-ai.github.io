Reality2 — Layer 1 binding: Bluetooth Low Energy (ble, ordinal 0)

Normative document

About this reading edition

The numbered requirements and their provisional markers are retained from the cited source revision. Selected non-normative source notes, editorial attributions and internal working details are omitted. Clause numbers are unchanged; references to omitted notes may remain. The overview and worked examples are informative. Navigation and reading aids are not requirements.

Version: 0.9.0 · Date: 2026-09-03 · Status: PROVISIONAL(standard) · Scope: this bearer only

⚠ This is a binding document under Layer 1, Clause 11. It states what that clause defers to a binding and restates no requirement of Layer 1 (11.2). Where it appears to repeat a rule, it is citing the clause that governs and saying what this medium does about it.

Verbal forms. shall = requirement · should = recommendation · may = permission · can = possibility and capability.

1 Scope, and what is deliberately not here

1.1 This document binds Bluetooth Low Energy to bearer ordinal 0 (ble), whose registry row Layer 1, 8.2.1 fixes: reach proximity, wire tier compact, largest payload 200 B, relative cost 1.

1.2 It states items b) in Clause 2, c) in Clause 3, d) in Clause 4, e) in 5.1, j) in Clause 6, f) in 6a, g) in 6b, h) in 6c and i) in 6d. Item a) is fixed by the registry row. All ten items are stated, and none by a pointer. Layer 1, 11.1a — the role-concurrency obligation, which applies to a medium with a central and a peripheral and therefore to this one — is stated in 2a, and its conformance test is Layer 1, 12.4.

1.2a This document does not share its ordinal with another binding, so 11.1 a)'s second half — which properties make two bindings indistinguishable above Layer 1 — has no subject here. Stated rather than omitted: 8.2.4 permits the sharing, so its absence is a fact about this binding and not about the registry.

1.3 ⚠ This is the second binding in the corpus, and the first whose medium holds connections. ESP-NOW reaches its peers by a local table entry that the far end never participates in, which Layer 1, 10 Note 3 rules is not a connection for that clause. A BLE link is bilateral, is lost by elapsed silence, and is re-established after loss — so Clause 10 applies here in full, for the first time in this corpus, and 6d states it. One consequence follows in 6d: SS2 acquires its first real instance. Note that Clause 7's profile is a closed list of eight parameters with no connection block — which is precisely what SS2 is about, and why 6d states the two values rather than pointing at a field.

1.4 Out of scope, named rather than left silent:

  • Bluetooth BR/EDR (classic). This binding is Low Energy only. BR/EDR is a different medium with a different framing and holds no ordinal in the registry.
  • Legacy advertising. 5.2.3 states the arithmetic that excludes it and the version floor that follows. It is excluded by measurement rather than by preference.
  • Bonding, pairing and the Security Manager. BLE's own key agreement is not used by this binding and is neither required nor forbidden: everything this standard protects is protected end-to-end above Layer 4, and a link-layer key would authenticate a radio where Layer 5 authenticates a hive. See 4.5.
  • The class-hash algorithm. As for the ESP-NOW binding, it is a stated dependency of the announcement and not a choice made here (SS1).
  • The bridge of a complex hive. 01-terminology.md 4.1's bridge is not a bearer and takes no ordinal; a hive whose parts are joined over BLE is joined by a bridge, and this document does not govern it (SS385).
  • Every other bearer, on the ordinary per-bearer rule. L1-BINDING-LORA.md was written the same day and binds ordinal 2; the two share an announcement body in its first three elements and nothing else.

2 b) How a frame is placed in a transport unit

2.1 A frame shall be carried as the value of a single GATT characteristic operation, one frame per operation, with no wrapper, prefix or padding added by this binding.

2.2 A hive shall expose the Reality2 transport service, whose UUID 3.1 fixes, containing exactly two characteristics:

Characteristic UUID Properties Direction
inbound 52324e54-0002-4000-8000-52324e540001 Write Without Response to the hive exposing the service
outbound 52324e54-0003-4000-8000-52324e540001 Notify from the hive exposing the service

2.3 A hive shall be able to act as GATT server and as GATT client, and shall not require a peer to take a particular one of those roles before frames can be exchanged in either direction. Layer 1, 3.1 makes a bearer symmetric between hives; BLE is not symmetric between a central and a peripheral, and 2.3 is what stops that asymmetry reaching upward.

2.4 Transport units are delimited by the medium and this binding adds nothing: the ATT operation carries its own length, and the second half of 11.1 b) — how transport units are delimited where the medium does not delimit them — has no subject here.

2.5 A hive shall not reassemble a frame from more than one characteristic operation. A frame that does not fit one operation is not carried (5.1.3).

Note 1 to entry: GATT rather than an L2CAP credit-based channel, and the reason is availability rather than fitness. A credit-based channel is the better mechanism on its merits — it segments, it flow-controls, and it carries a service data unit larger than any frame this bearer admits. It is also unavailable or awkward on platforms this bearer exists to reach. A binding that two implementations cannot both satisfy has not bound anything, so the common mechanism is chosen and the better one is recorded here as what a later revision would take if the ground changed.

Note 2 to entry: 2.5 is a refusal to invent fragmentation. Layer 4, 6.2 forbids any layer to fragment, and a binding that reassembled across operations would be fragmenting under another name — the frame would arrive whole, and the fact that it did would be this document's doing rather than the sender's.

2a The role-concurrency obligation (Layer 1, 11.1a)

2a.1 A conforming hive shall support the central and peripheral roles at the same time: it shall advertise and accept an inbound connection while an outbound connection attempt of its own is in progress.

2a.2 Where two hives attempt to reach each other at the same moment, the yield rule shall be: the hive whose current medium address (4.1) is numerically lower, compared as a big-endian 48-bit unsigned integer, abandons its outbound attempt and accepts the inbound one. The other shall continue its attempt.

2a.3 A hive shall not refuse an inbound connection on the ground that an outbound attempt is in progress.

Note 2 to entry: The tie-break is on the medium address rather than on any hive identifier, and that is deliberate. The address is the only value both sides hold before a connection exists — a canonical hive identifier is learned from traffic (4.4), and there is no traffic yet. A rule that needed the identifier could not be applied at the moment it is needed.

Note 3 to entry: The addresses rotate (4.3), so the rule does not systematically favour any device. Two hives that collide repeatedly will not keep resolving the same way, which is what stops a low-addressed hive from being permanently the one that yields.

3 c) The discovery marker

3.1 The discovery marker Layer 1, 4.7.1 requires shall be the 128-bit service UUID

52324e54-0001-4000-8000-52324e540001

carried in the advertising data as a Service Data — 128-bit UUID structure (AD type 0x21), whose data field is the announcement body of 5.3.

3.1a The marker's octets shall be carried least-significant octet first, as this medium carries every UUID in advertising data; the first octet on air shall be 0x01 and the last 0x52. PROVISIONAL(r2, SS394).

Note 0a to entry: A value printed in one order and transmitted in another needs saying even where the convention is well known, because the convention belongs to the medium and the reader is arriving from this document. No byte on the wire changes: 3.1a states what a conformant implementation was already doing, and names what a mistaken one does instead.

3.2 The marker shall be present in every announcement, and a receiver shall be able to reach it without connecting, without a scan request and without parsing any frame. This is what makes Layer 2, 7.1's first filter step free: the comparison is one UUID against an advertisement the scanner already has in hand.

3.3 The marker shall not appear inside a frame (Layer 1, 4.7.2). It is a property of the medium framing, and this medium has framing to put it in.

3.4 ⚠ Which reading of medium framing this binding takes, because Layer 1, 13.2 records that the term is used normatively and defined nowhere, and that the two readings differ by exactly the advertised maximum transport unit payload (SS272). This binding takes the first reading: the marker sits in the medium's own framing, outside the payload a frame occupies. It is an advertising structure; a frame is the value of a characteristic operation on a connection; the two never share a byte. So the 200 B of 5.1.1 is unaffected by the marker's sixteen octets, and this bearer is one where the ambiguity has no consequence.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 2 to entry: It is deliberately outside the Bluetooth SIG base range. The range 0000xxxx-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB is SIG-allocated space for 16-bit and 32-bit UUIDs, and a value inside it would be claiming an allocation this project does not hold. A 128-bit UUID needs no allocation from anybody, which is why it is the marker rather than the cheaper 16-bit form.

Note 3 to entry: The marker costs sixteen octets and that is the whole reason 5.2.3 excludes legacy advertising. A 16-bit UUID would cost two, and is unavailable. The cost is paid once per advertisement and buys a filter that never needs a connection.

4 d) Medium address to canonical hive identifier

4.1 The medium address is the 48-bit BLE device address together with its address type.

4.2 A hive shall use a non-resolvable private address, and shall not use a public address or a random static address.

4.3 A hive shall change that address at intervals not exceeding a stated maximum, and shall change it whenever the persona changes (Layer 5, 5.1.4).

4.4 A bearer shall associate a medium address with the canonical hive identifier Layer 1, 3.6 defines, and shall report that identifier upward as 4.5.1 requires; the association shall be learned from received traffic and shall not be computed from the address.

4.5 A bearer shall discard the association when the medium address changes, and shall not treat two addresses as one peer on any evidence this layer holds.

Note 2 to entry: 4.5 is the cost, and it is accepted. Discarding the association on every address change means a peer that rotates becomes, to Layer 1, a new peer — its neighbour entry ages out and is rebuilt, and its link quality is measured again from nothing. Unlinkability and continuity are the same knob turned in opposite directions, and this document turns it toward unlinkability because Layer 5 already provides continuity where it matters and this layer cannot provide privacy at all if the address persists.

Note 3 to entry: BLE's own pairing is not what makes a peer known here, and using it would create a second identity system underneath this one. A bonded peer is a radio that a controller recognises; a canonical hive identifier names a hive. The two would drift the moment a hive re-personas without re-pairing, or a peripheral changed hands — and 4.4's learned association has no such failure because it holds only what the traffic said.

5 The announcement, and e) the payload it must fit in

5.1 Maximum transport unit payload

5.1.1 The maximum transport unit payload is 200 octets, fixed by the registry row (Layer 1, 8.2.1). Nothing in this medium varies it, so 11.1 e)'s second half has no subject.

5.1.2 A hive shall negotiate an ATT maximum transmission unit of at least 203 octets before carrying a frame, and shall enable the link-layer data length extension required to reach it.

5.1.3 Where the negotiated maximum transmission unit is below 203 octets, the bearer shall report restricted (6b.2) and shall not carry a frame on that connection.

Note 1 to entry: 203 is 200 plus the three-octet ATT operation header, and the arithmetic is stated because getting it wrong produces the worst failure this medium offers: a frame truncated by the controller, delivered as a short write, and parsed as a malformed frame by a layer that has no way to know the medium did it.

Note 2 to entry: 5.1.3 is the honest disposal of a negotiation that can fail. The payload is fixed by the registry and the medium cannot vary it — but a connection can fail to reach it, which is a property of the link and not of the bearer. Reporting restricted says the bearer is in service and this peer cannot be reached at full size, which is exactly what happened.

5.2 The announcement is an advertisement

5.2.1 The announcement shall be a BLE advertisement carrying the Service Data structure of 3.1, and shall not be a frame. This is where this binding differs most from the ESP-NOW one, whose announcement is a frame: an advertisement is not addressed, is not acknowledged and cannot be relayed, and Layer 2's beacon needs none of those.

5.2.2 A hive shall advertise while any BLE bearer is in service (Layer 1, 3.5a) and that bearer participates in discovery.

5.2.3 A hive shall use extended advertising, and therefore shall implement Bluetooth 5.0 or later.

5.2.4 The advertisement shall not carry the hive's canonical identifier, any value derived from the hive's trust group, or any identifier of the hive other than the beacon identifier of 5.3.2 (Layer 2, 5.3).

Note 1 to entry: 5.2.3 is arithmetic, not preference, and here it is. Legacy advertising carries 31 octets. The Service Data structure costs one octet of length, one of type and sixteen of UUID before any body at all — eighteen — and the mandatory body of 5.3 is fifteen. Thirty-three into thirty-one does not go. A connectable LE advertisement also carries the three-octet Flags structure, so the complete advertising-data sequence is thirty-six octets; the Service Data structure is still the binding's marker and has its own stated length. The alternatives were each rejected for a stated reason: a 16-bit UUID is SIG-allocated space this project does not hold; a scan response costs the scanner an active scan and so breaks the free first filter of Layer 2, 7.1; and manufacturer-specific data needs a company identifier this project does not hold. Extended advertising carries 254 octets and the complete advertising-data sequence occupies 36 of them.

Note 2 to entry: The version floor is a real exclusion and is named as one. A Bluetooth 4.x device cannot satisfy this binding. That is a narrower bearer than the medium could support, and it is the price of a marker that needs no allocation and a body that is the same on every bearer.

5.3 The announcement body

5.3.1 The body shall be a sequence of elements, each type (1 octet) || length (1 octet) || value (length octets), in ascending type order, with no element repeated.

5.3.2 The elements shall be:

Type Length Value Required by
0x01 4 beacon identifier L2 5.1 b), 5.4.1
0x02 4 class hash L2 5.1 c)
0x03 1 build-mode declaration L2 5.4a.1 — see Clause 6

5.3.3 An element whose type is not in 5.3.2 shall be skipped and its length honoured.

5.3.4 A body whose final element is truncated shall be treated as unreadable from that element onward, and shall not be treated as though the element were absent.

5.3.4a The body's extent shall be the length the Service Data structure of 5.2.1 declares, less its AD type and UUID octets, and a body shorter than that shall be treated as unreadable throughout — not as a shorter body that is well-formed to its end. PROVISIONAL(r2, SS390).

Note 0 to entry: 5.3.4 protects a cut INSIDE an element and 5.3.4a protects a cut AT a boundary, and without the second the first reads as covering both. A body cut at an element boundary is byte-for-byte a shorter, complete, well-formed body: nothing in it is truncated, so 5.3.4 has no subject, and 6.3's well-formed to its end, element 0x03 absent applies in terms — the verdict is production, which 6.4 forbids a scanner to reach from unknown. ‼ And the element at risk is the one that matters by construction rather than by bad luck: 5.3.1 fixes ascending type order and the build-mode declaration is 0x03, the last of the three required elements, so it is the first thing any truncation removes. Measured 2026-08-18 on a development body of fifteen octets: truncated to twelve — the boundary after the class hash — a decoder reports no truncation and concludes production.

5.3.5 The beacon identifier shall be the value Layer 2, 5.4.1 requires, and shall not be derived from any other identifier the hive holds.

Note 1 to entry: This body is byte-for-byte the body of the ESP-NOW binding, and that is deliberate. The announcement body answers Layer 2, which knows nothing about media; only its carriage is a property of a medium. Two bindings that encoded the same three values differently would oblige every implementation to hold two decoders for one question, and the second decoder would be the one with the bug in it. Where a later binding cannot carry this body, the honest move is 11.1 j)'s stated-nil form and not a second layout.

Note 2 to entry: 5.3.5 drops one word the ESP-NOW binding carries, and the omission is deliberate. That binding says the identifier is generated randomly by the hive or installed at manufacture, and stable. Stable is true within a persona and false across one — Layer 5, 5.1.4 obliges a fresh beacon identifier on re-persona — and on a bearer whose address also rotates (4.3), a reader who took stable at face value would defeat both. The clause it cites is unchanged; what is removed is a gloss that was never true of the whole lifetime.

6 j) The build-mode declaration

6.1 The build-mode declaration Layer 2, 5.4a.1 requires shall be element type 0x03 of 5.3.2, one octet, with the values:

Value Meaning
0x01 development

6.2 A production hive shall not emit element 0x03 at all.

6.3 A receiver that finds no element 0x03 shall treat the declaration as absent; one that finds a value other than 0x01 shall treat it as unreadable; and neither shall be treated as production declared.

Note 1 to entry: There is no value meaning production, and that is Layer 2, 5.4a.2 rather than an economy. A production beacon may carry no development-related bytes, so production is encodable only as the absence of the element — which is why 5.3.4's truncation rule exists and why 6.3 keeps absent and unreadable apart. A scanner that collapsed them would report a cut-off advertisement as a production hive.

6a f) How the quality value is derived

6a.1 The quality value of Layer 1, 5.3.1 shall be derived from the received signal strength indication of the advertisement or connection event that carried the most recent traffic from that peer.

6a.2 The derivation shall map a stated floor to 0.0 and a stated ceiling to 1.0, shall clamp outside them, and shall be monotonic in signal strength.

6a.3 The floor and the ceiling shall be stated by the implementation. This document fixes neither.

6a.4 The value shall be held per medium address, and shall be discarded with the association when the address changes (4.5).

Note 1 to entry: 6a.3 refuses to pin constants for the same reason the ESP-NOW binding does — the usable range is a property of an antenna, a package and an enclosure, and a number fixed here would be wrong for most of them. What is fixed is the shape: floor, ceiling, clamped, monotonic, so that two implementations disagree about calibration and not about meaning.

Note 2 to entry: 6a.4 is 4.5's consequence and is stated because it is easy to miss. A quality value that outlived the address it was measured on would be a measurement of one radio attributed to another.

6a-bis B5, the fade rate, and what it is set against

6a.5 The bearer profile shall state B5 fade rate, and that value shall exceed the longest advertising interval the hive can adopt (Layer 2, 6.3.1).

6a.6 A hive shall declare the range of advertising intervals it can adopt (Layer 2, 6.1.3), and shall not apply this bearer's fade behaviour to any other bearer (Layer 2, 6.3.3).

Note 1 to entry: B5 is declared here and constrained there, which is the division Layer 1, 7.1 Note 2 sets out: the value is not a property of the medium, it is set against how often this bearer announces presence, and Layer 2 states what it has to exceed. A fade shorter than the advertising interval declares a present hive receding once per interval, for ever.

6a-ter Platform binding profile

6a-ter.1 A platform that declares this binding in P3 of Layer 0, 8.2.1 shall carry the P3 binding-parameter declaration Layer 0, 8.2.1a requires. Its schema identifier shall be ai.reality2.binding.ble and its version shall be 1.

6a-ter.2 Version 1's canonical parameter value shall be UTF-8 text containing exactly these lower-case ASCII name=value lines, in this order, separated by a single line feed and with no leading, trailing, or other whitespace:

quality-floor-dbm=<signed decimal integer>
quality-ceiling-dbm=<signed decimal integer>
advertising-interval-min-s=<unsigned decimal integer>
advertising-interval-max-s=<unsigned decimal integer>
fade-s=<unsigned decimal integer>
connection-interval-ms=<unsigned decimal integer>
supervision-timeout-ms=<unsigned decimal integer>
max-reattempt-interval-s=<unsigned decimal integer>
connection-limit=<unsigned decimal integer>
address-rotation-max-s=<unsigned decimal integer>

A decimal zero is written 0; a non-zero decimal has no leading zero; and a signed negative value has - followed by a non-zero magnitude. These rules make one value have one representation rather than merely making the field readable.

6a-ter.3 The values in 6a-ter.2 shall be the values the platform uses for 4.3, 6a.3, 6a.5–6a.6, 6d.4–6d.6. The advertising interval minimum shall be non-zero and shall not exceed its maximum; the fade, connection, supervision, connection-limit, and address-rotation constraints elsewhere in this binding still apply.

6a-ter.3a A stated connection interval and supervision timeout shall be exactly representable in the Bluetooth LE link-layer parameter units. A platform shall not round a declared value when applying it to the controller.

6b g) The conditions producing each state

6b.1 available. The controller is initialised and powered, advertising and scanning are permitted, and at least one connection slot is free.

6b.2 restricted. All connection slots are in use; or a connection to the peer exists but its negotiated maximum transmission unit is below 203 octets (5.1.3). In both cases the bearer is in service and some peer cannot be reached, which is what restricted means.

6b.3 unavailable. The controller is powered down, the host has disabled the radio, or the platform has withdrawn it. An elapse does not produce this state and neither does a peer: it is the local radio being absent.

6b.4 failed. The host-controller transport has errored, the controller has reset unexpectedly, or an operation has returned an error the host cannot attribute to a peer.

6b.5 All four states of Layer 1, 5.2 can be produced on this medium. 11.1 g)'s stated-nil form — a state the medium cannot reach, and what prevents it — has no subject here.

Note 2 to entry: restricted has two conditions and they are not the same kind. Slot exhaustion is a property of the local controller; an undersized maximum transmission unit is a property of one link. Both make a peer unreachable while the bearer works, which is the state's definition, and an implementation that reported the second as failed would be telling a layer above that the radio is broken when a single negotiation was.

6c h) Duty cycle and airtime budget

6c.1 Layer 1, 9.1 does not apply to this bearer. The 2.4 GHz band this medium occupies is not governed by a duty cycle or an airtime budget in the regulatory regimes this document contemplates; what governs it is transmit power, which is a platform property declared at Layer 0 and not a bearer obligation.

6c.2 The bearer profile shall state B8 regulated as false.

Note 1 to entry: This is a claim about regulation and is the kind that ages. It is stated as this document's claim rather than as a fact of physics, and a deployment in a regime that does budget airtime in this band would need a revision. The contrast with the LoRa bearer is the point: same registry, same clause, opposite answer, and the answer belongs in the binding because that is where the medium is known.

6d i) The connection lifecycle and the silence period

6d.1 BLE requires a connection before a frame can be carried, so Layer 1, Clause 10 applies in full. A link is bilateral, the far end participates in establishing it, it is lost by elapsed silence, and it is re-established after loss — the tests Clause 10 Note 3 sets for whether a precondition is a connection.

6d.2 The interval at which the bearer expects to hear from a peer shall be the connection interval in force on that link.

6d.3 The silence period of Layer 1, 10.2 shall be the link supervision timeout, and shall be at least three times the connection interval (Layer 1, 10.3).

6d.4 A bearer shall declare the connection interval and the supervision timeout it uses, and shall report a peer unreachable when the supervision timeout elapses.

6d.5 A bearer should reattempt a lost connection at an interval that increases to a stated maximum, and should continue reattempting for as long as the bearer is in service (Layer 1, 10.4).

6d.6 The number of connections a hive can hold at once shall be stated by the implementation, and exhausting it shall produce restricted (6b.2).

Note 2 to entry: The factor of three is satisfied by construction on this medium and that is a coincidence worth naming rather than relying on. Bluetooth's own rules already require a supervision timeout greater than twice the effective connection interval, so an implementation that merely obeys the medium is close to obeying 10.3 — but "close to" is not "obeys", the two rules count different things, and a binding that leaned on the coincidence would be conformant by luck.

Note 3 to entry: 6d.6 is where 11.1 i)'s last question is answered — how a precondition comes to be met. A connection is established by either hive initiating to an address it has seen advertising; no ceremony, no key and no prior relationship is required, because everything that depends on knowing who the peer is happens above Layer 4. A hive that required pairing before carriage would have made a radio's identity a precondition for a hive's traffic.

7 What this closes, what it narrows, and what it leaves open

7.1 Closes. Bearer ordinal 0 now has a binding document, so Layer 1, 12.1 g) can be met for it. SS264 counted six bindings owed against one written; five remain.

7.2 Closes for this bearer only. SS384 — a medium address that outlives the persona it served. 4.2 and 4.3 make the address rotate with the persona, and 4.5 makes the association follow it. This is a disposal on one bearer and not a corpus answer: every other binding still owes the question, and SS354's proposed tenth item for 11.1 is what would oblige them to answer it.

7.3 Narrows. SS2 — its practical half is discharged in 6d and its clause-level half is untouched (6d Note 1).

7.4 Leaves open.

  • The marker UUID is PROVISIONAL(supervisor) (3.1 Note 1). It is a permanent allocation and Roy's word is what fixes it.
  • The class-hash algorithm (SS1), inherited and not introduced here.
  • Whether the 4.x exclusion is acceptable (5.2.3 Note 2). It is a deliberate narrowing and a deployment may find it too narrow.
  • ⚠ The duty-class declaration of Layer 2, 5.4b is carried by no element of 5.3.2, and neither is it carried by the ESP-NOW binding. Layer 2, 5.2 obliges a beacon emitted by a hive receptive only at intervals to carry it. So a duty-cycled hive cannot conformantly announce on either bound bearer in this corpus — the body has three elements and none is the duty class. This is inherited rather than introduced here: the body is deliberately the same as the ESP-NOW binding's (5.3 Note 1), so the omission is the same omission, and fixing it in one document alone would break the property that makes the body worth sharing. It is raised rather than patched, because a fourth element type is a change to both bindings and to Layer 2's expectations of all of them.
  • No vector covers any of this. SS383 records that a normative obligation about bytes with no vector is outside the conformance contract by construction, and every byte layout in Clause 3, 5.3 and 6 is in that position. The announcement body is the same as the ESP-NOW binding's, so a vector written for one would serve both, which is an argument for writing it once.
