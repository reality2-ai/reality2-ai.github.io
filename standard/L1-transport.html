Reality2 Standard — Layer 1: Transport

Normative document

About this reading edition

The overview and worked examples are informative. Navigation and reading aids are not requirements.

Version: 0.9.0 · Date: 2026-09-11 · Status: Draft

What "Draft" names here is the editorial maturity of the text, and nothing else. It is not a licence to deviate: every requirement here is normative now and the implementing lanes build from it. The only text open to replacement is a value marked PROVISIONAL(...) at its own clause.

Verbal forms. shall = requirement · should = recommendation · may = permission · can = possibility and capability.

Introduction

A Reality2 hive does not have a network connection. It has as many as its hardware provides, running at the same time, and it does not choose between them at this layer.

Layer 1 is where each medium is made to look like every other one. A radio that broadcasts to whoever is listening, a wired link between two processors in the same enclosure, a socket to a machine on another continent — above this layer they are the same thing: something that carries a frame to a peer, sometimes.

That word sometimes is the layer's whole difficulty. Layer 1 promises nothing about delivery. A frame handed to a bearer may arrive, may not, and no one is told which. Everything Reality2 does about that — retrying, choosing a different bearer, holding a frame for a sleeping peer — happens at Layer 3, and can only happen there if every bearer fails in the same shape.

So this document specifies one contract and requires every bearer to satisfy it. The contract is deliberately thin, because a rule that only some media can honour would either exclude those media or be quietly ignored by them, and both outcomes end in a mesh whose behaviour depends on which radio a frame happened to take.

Three things follow, and they are worth having in mind before the clauses.

A bearer carries frames; it does not read them. The bytes handed down are the bytes transmitted. A bearer that inspects or rewrites a frame has coupled itself to a format it does not own, and will break when that format changes beneath it.

A bearer reports peers by the identity the rest of the system uses, never by the address it happens to know them by. This is the single rule in this document most likely to be got wrong, and 4.5 explains why the failure is silent.

Adding a bearer changes nothing above Layer 1. If supporting a new medium requires an edit to routing or to the wire format, the contract has been broken rather than extended.

1 Scope

This document specifies the contract that every Reality2 transport bearer satisfies, whatever medium it uses.

It specifies what a bearer reports to the layers above it, and what those layers may ask of a bearer.

It defines the bearer registry, and the identifiers by which a bearer is named on the wire.

It specifies the parameters by which a bearer declares itself, and what a document specifying an individual bearer contains.

It establishes what conformance at this layer consists of.

It identifies what is not decided at this layer.

It does not specify any individual medium — modulation, channel plans, association procedures, socket options and their like belong to the binding documents described in Clause 11. It does not specify frame contents, which are Layer 4, or the choice of which bearer carries a given frame, which is Layer 3.

3 Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the terms and definitions given in the documents listed in Clause 2 apply, together with the following.

3.1 bearer

means of carrying frames between hives over one medium, satisfying the contract in Clause 4

Note 1 to entry: A hive commonly runs several bearers at once. They do not coordinate with one another at this layer.

3.2 binding

part of an implementation that adapts frames to one medium

Note 1 to entry: A bearer is the contract; a binding is one realisation of it. Two bindings for the same medium can differ entirely in construction and remain interchangeable above Layer 1.

3.3 transport unit

quantity of data the medium delivers or fails to deliver as a whole

Note 1 to entry: A radio frame, a datagram and a length-delimited segment of a stream are each a transport unit.

3.4 bearer ordinal

number identifying a bearer, appearing on the wire and in the neighbour table

3.5 bearer profile

set of parameter values a bearer declares, as specified in Clause 7

3.5a bearer in service

bearer a hive provides and has not disabled, whatever state (5.2) it is currently in

Note 1 to entry: A bearer in service can be temporarily unable to carry frames. Being in service is a fact about the hive's configuration; state is a fact about the moment. The layers above use the first to decide what a hive is, and the second to decide what it can do now.

3.6 canonical hive identifier

identifier of a hive that is the same whichever bearer it is observed on

3.7 maximum transport unit payload

largest frame a bearer can carry in one transport unit at a given moment

3.8 broadcast

send directed at every hive that can hear it, rather than at a named one

4 The bearer contract

4.1 A bearer shall satisfy every requirement in this clause. A means of carrying frames that does not is not a bearer, and shall not be presented as one.

4.2 Frames are carried unaltered

4.2.1 A bearer shall deliver upward exactly the bytes that were handed down at the peer, unaltered.

4.2.2 A bearer may wrap a frame for its medium — a length prefix, a header, a radio preamble — and shall remove that wrapping before delivering the frame upward.

4.2.3 A bearer shall not interpret the contents of a frame.

Note 1 to entry: 4.2.3 constrains the implementer, not the hardware. A bearer that reads a frame to make a decision has bound itself to a format specified at another layer and maintained by other people. The next change to that format will not be accompanied by a warning to anyone working on bearers.

Verification of 4.2.3: transmit frames of every permitted length, including the largest the bearer declares and a single byte, and compare the delivered bytes with the bytes sent.

4.3 One frame per transport unit

4.3.1 Where a medium provides transport unit boundaries, a bearer shall carry exactly one frame per transport unit.

4.3.2 Where a medium provides a byte stream and no boundaries, a bearer shall delimit frames, and shall reject a claimed length exceeding its declared maximum without consuming further input as though it were payload.

Note 1 to entry: 4.3.2's second half is the whole of the rule. A delimiter that trusts a length field will follow a corrupt one into the next frame and every frame after it, and a stream bearer that has lost delimitation does not recover on its own.

Verification of 4.3.2: supply a stream containing a length field larger than the declared maximum, followed by a valid frame. A conforming bearer rejects the first and delivers the second.

4.4 Delivery is not promised

4.4.1 A bearer shall not be required to deliver a frame.

4.4.2 A bearer shall not acknowledge a frame at this layer, and shall not retransmit one on its own initiative.

4.4.3 A bearer whose medium performs its own acknowledgement or retransmission shall not expose either upward.

Note 1 to entry: 4.4.3 is not a preference for unreliability. A bearer that is reliable where others are not produces a mesh whose behaviour depends on which medium a frame happened to take, which is the property Layer 3 exists to remove. A medium that retransmits internally may do so; what it may not do is let anything above notice.

Note 2 to entry: Where delivery has to be assured, that is done above this layer and end-to-end, by a party that can tell whether the thing it wanted actually happened. A bearer only ever knows that it transmitted.

4.4.4 A bearer may refuse peers or connections to protect its own resources — on a medium crossing networks belonging to others, for example. No layer shall treat such refusal or acceptance as authenticating anything: frame integrity and trust are established above Layer 4, end to end, and a bearer that screens its peers has protected its own resources, never the frames it carries.

4.5 Peers are reported by their canonical hive identifier

4.5.1 When a bearer delivers a frame upward, it shall report the sender's canonical hive identifier (3.6).

4.5.2 A bearer shall not report an identifier derived from a medium address where the canonical identifier is known.

4.5.3 Where no canonical identifier is yet known for a medium address, a bearer may report one derived from that address, and shall replace it with the canonical identifier as soon as one becomes known for that address.

Note 1 to entry: This is the rule in this document most likely to be implemented wrongly, and its failure is silent. A bearer naturally knows its peers by medium address — a radio address, an address and port — and reporting that is the obvious thing to do. Nothing rejects it. Nothing logs it.

Note 2 to entry: What happens instead is that a hive reachable on three bearers becomes three neighbours. Each entry accumulates its own history, each is judged separately on how well it is working, and the layers above never learn that the three are one hive. Every decision that depends on knowing a peer as one peer — how confident a path is, whether a frame has been seen before, which of several bearers to prefer for a destination — is made on a third of the evidence. The system does not fail; it performs worse than it should, for a reason that is invisible from any layer that could detect it.

Verification of 4.5.3: bring up one hive reachable on two bearers at once. It appears in the neighbour table as one entry carrying two bearers, never as two entries.

4.6 Broadcast and addressed send

4.6.1 A bearer other than a receive-only ingress shall support sending a frame to one named hive.

4.6.2 A bearer that participates in discovery shall support broadcast (3.8).

4.6.2a A bearer that does not support broadcast shall not participate in discovery, and its binding document shall state that it does not.

4.6.3 A receive-only ingress shall not support addressed or broadcast send, shall report the receive-only state of 5.2.1, and shall not participate in discovery. It may deliver received frames and their per-arrival quality upward.

4.6.3a Layer 1 shall expose a receive-only ingress through a reception contract separate from the output-bearer contract; that ingress is not a value of the latter.

Note 0 to entry: Receive-only is structural, not an unavailable sender awaiting recovery. It describes an assembly which deliberately omitted an output path, such as a passive radio observer. Treating it as unavailable tells a caller that waiting can make it routable; treating it as failed tells an operator to repair a capability that was never present. Neither is true. Its received frames still reach the ordinary Layer 2 and Layer 3 receive path: reception and local emission are separate facts.

Note 1 to entry: A bearer whose medium has no native broadcast satisfies 4.6.2 by sending to each peer it knows of. That is more expensive and is permitted.

Note 1a to entry: The broadcast requirement is a discovery requirement, and a bearer that does not participate in discovery does not need it. This clause read as an unqualified obligation to support broadcast until 2026-08-09, and the ground its own Note gave was discovery and only discovery — the layers above use broadcast to find peers they do not yet know. So the relaxation follows from that Note's reasoning rather than against it: what read as one requirement was two fused together, and 4.6.2a separates them. A requirement is not being weakened for convenience; a reason is being applied to its own scope.

Note 1b to entry: The case is a bearer whose peers are already members. Roy, 2026-08-09: some of these may be used for comms inside TGs where they keep track of each other rather than discovering — a class-A uplink, a cellular link, a connection to a known server. Such a bearer never needs to find anybody, and requiring broadcast excluded it from the network on the strength of a capability it would never use.

Note 1c to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(standard).

4.7 The discovery marker belongs to the medium

4.7.1 A bearer shall carry a marker in its medium framing by which a receiver can distinguish Reality2 traffic from other traffic on the same medium.

4.7.2 The marker shall not be placed inside the frame.

Note 1 to entry: This is the whole of Layer 1's part in discovery. What is advertised, and what a receiver does with it, is Layer 2. The marker only answers is this ours? — cheaply, before anything is parsed, on a medium that carries other people's traffic too.

Note 2 to entry: 4.7.2 follows from 4.2.1. A marker inside the frame would be bytes the bearer added, and there are none.

4.8 No bearer is privileged

4.8.1 Supporting an additional bearer shall not require any change at Layer 3 or above.

4.8.2 Layer 1's binding-configuration operation shall accept a versioned, opaque binding-defined parameter representation.

4.8.3 A binding shall either apply a configuration accepted by that operation or return a defined refusal.

Note 2 to entry: The representation is deliberately owned by the binding rather than by a medium-specific type leaking upward. Its version distinguishes a representation whose meaning has changed; its contents are opaque to the consumer. unsupported, malformed, unavailable and failed are distinct refusals: a fixed-at-construction binding may honestly decline a runtime update, but it may not report that it applied one. This is the portable Layer 1 form of the per-binding configuration operation required by Layer 0, 5.1.2.

Note 1 to entry: 4.8.1 is testable in the only way that matters: add a bearer and see what else has to change. If routing or the wire format has to be edited, the contract has been broken rather than extended, and the correct response is to find which requirement in this clause the new bearer does not satisfy.

4.9 Provision is not policy

4.9.1 A deployable image — a production image (Layer 6, 6.2.1), as distinct from a development image — shall include a binding for every bearer the target hardware provides; only a bearer whose hardware is absent from the target may be omitted.

4.9.2 Frugality shall be exercised at runtime — by disabling a bearer — and never by omitting its binding at build time.

4.9.3 Disabling a bearer to save power shall remove power from its radio where the hardware permits.

4.9.4 4.9.1 obliges bindings for the bearers the target hardware provides. A bearer not carried by a radio of its own is not obliged by this clause — whether it rides an operating system's networking, or an IP stack linked into the image itself.

Note 0 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 2 to entry: Development images are exempt (4.9.1), because bringing a board up one bearer at a time is how a board is brought up at all — and a development image announces itself as one (Layer 2, 5.4a), so nothing it omits can be mistaken for a production device's capability. The boundary 4.9.4 draws is the same one: the clause is about hardware a deployment paid for and cannot use, not about every transport a platform could conceivably speak.

5 What a bearer reports upward

5.1 A bearer shall report the following, and shall keep each current.

Note 1 to entry: Addressable is what this bearer can send to — an open connection, an entry in its own address table, a peer within range. It is not the same question as which peers have been heard announcing themselves, which is Layer 2's and is answered separately. A peer can be addressable without having been heard from recently, and can have been heard from without being addressable. Neither record substitutes for the other, and a bearer that reports one in place of the other has answered a question nobody asked.

Reported Meaning
Bearer ordinal which bearer this is (Clause 8)
State one of the four states in 5.2
Maximum transport unit payload the largest frame it can carry now (3.7)
Link quality, per peer 5.3
Addressable peers which hives this bearer can presently send to

5.2 State

5.2.0 A bearer state is a property of (hive, bearer), not of the device. Where one physical medium carries the bearers of more than one hive, each hive holds its own state for it, and those states are independent. A conformance claim about a bearer shall be scoped to the hive making it.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

5.2.1 A bearer shall report one of the following states.

State Meaning
available can carry frames to peers it knows and to peers it does not yet know
restricted can carry frames to peers it already knows, and cannot take on new ones
unavailable cannot carry frames now, and expects to be able to later
failed cannot carry frames, and needs intervention or recovery before it can
receive-only can deliver received frames but has no output path in this assembly

5.2.2 A bearer that cannot send shall report unavailable, failed, or receive-only rather than accepting a frame and discarding it.

Note 1 to entry: restricted exists because several media have a hard limit on simultaneous peers. Without a state for it, such a bearer has to either refuse frames to peers it is perfectly able to reach, or accept frames to peers it cannot reach at all. Both are worse than saying which case it is in.

Note 2 to entry: The distinction between unavailable and failed is whether waiting will help. It is stated as a requirement because the two look identical from outside and lead to opposite responses — one is waited out, the other is acted on.

Note 2a to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(standard).

Verification of 5.2.2: exhaust the bearer's peer capacity and read its state; it reports restricted, not available and not failed.

Verification of 5.2.1: for each state the bearer's binding document declares reachable (11.1 g), bring about the condition it names and read the state. A bearer that reports one state throughout, while its binding names conditions for others, has not been shown to report state at all — 01-terminology.md 4.5: a demonstration that could not have come out any other way is not evidence. Take at least one of the readings without a send attempt, against a condition that changes the bearer's ability while nothing is being sent: a state recomputed only on transmit is a record of the last send rather than of the bearer, and 12.1 b) requires each report to be kept current rather than merely to have been true once. (Added 2026-08-14, PROVISIONAL(standard), SS359 g), from a measurement of the corpus's only bound bearer: two of the four states produced by no site, the other two set on the synchronous return of the send call alone, on a link whose traffic is predominantly inbound.)

5.3 Link quality

5.3.1 A bearer shall report, for each peer it can reach, a quality value between 0 and 1 inclusive, where 0 is unusable and 1 is as good as that bearer gets.

5.3.2 A bearer should report the medium-specific measurements the quality value was derived from, where it has them.

Note 1 to entry: Some platforms supply reception-side evidence only. A radio driver may be unable to report a transmission's outcome without blocking, and blocking a send to obtain that evidence is already forbidden by 4.4.3 — the completion signal is the medium's own acknowledgement. Quality derived from reception alone is therefore conformant; what is not conformant is waiting on that signal to improve it.

5.3.3 A bearer shall report quality 0, or report the peer as unreachable, for a peer it can no longer reach.

Note 1 to entry: The normalised value is required and the raw measurements are not, because the layers above compare bearers with one another. A signal strength in dBm and a round-trip time in milliseconds cannot be compared; two numbers between 0 and 1 can. The raw measurements are worth reporting for diagnosis, and are worth nothing for the decision the value is used to make.

Note 2 to entry: Two bearers reporting the same quality value are not claiming the same physical conditions. Each value is relative to what that bearer can achieve, which is what makes them comparable at all.

Note 3 to entry: The value is never compared between hives, and that is why a binding fixes no absolute bounds. Layer 3 keeps the link axis as its own record of its own receptions and decides its own forwarding with it; no clause anywhere compares one hive's quality figure with another's. So two implementations of one bearer choosing different physical bounds are both conformant — which is 5.3.1's as good as that bearer gets read as it is written, and is the reason a binding document states the derivation (11.1 f) rather than pinning constants.

Verification of 5.3.1: read the value under two link conditions separated widely enough that the medium's own measurement differs, and show that the reported values differ. A value that does not move is not evidence that quality is being reported — 01-terminology.md 4.5: a demonstration that could not have come out any other way is not evidence, and this clause's failure mode is a constant. (Added 2026-08-14, PROVISIONAL(standard), SS359, from an implementation whose quality was saturated at 1.0 at every signal level it had ever seen and which satisfied 12.1 b) throughout — the value was reported, and it was current.)

5.3.4 A bearer may report a local estimate of relative transmission speed for a particular peer, separately from quality and relative sending cost. Such an estimate should be comparable with the estimates of other bindings on the same hive, independent of the size of the frame currently being routed, and accompanied by its basis: measured, derived from configuration, or a provisional local preference. An absent estimate should remain distinguishable from a measured slow link.

Note 4 to entry: This is an optional local input to Layer 3, 6.2.4, not a new on-air field, registry ranking or delivery guarantee. A coarse preference can be useful before calibration; it is not measured throughput. In particular, an IP bearer does not identify the speed of its underlying access link. The binding owns this estimate so that routing can compare bindings without interpreting a medium-specific configuration (4.8.1 and 7.2).

6 What may be asked of a bearer

6.1 A bearer other than a receive-only ingress shall accept a request to send one frame to one target, where the target is a canonical hive identifier (3.6) or the broadcast target.

6.2 A bearer shall not require the layers above it to know anything about its medium in order to make that request.

6.3 A bearer shall reject a frame larger than its currently declared maximum transport unit payload, and shall not fragment it.

Note 1 to entry: 6.3 assigns fragmentation to whoever knows what the frame means. A bearer splitting a frame it is required by 4.2.3 not to read would be dividing something whose structure it cannot see, and reassembly would then be a second place where the format is known.

7 The bearer profile

7.1 A bearer shall declare a profile stating a value for each of the following.

# Parameter Meaning
B1 Ordinal its entry in the registry (Clause 8)
B2 Maximum transport unit payload largest frame in one transport unit; for a receive-only ingress, the largest received frame it can deliver upward; may vary with link mode, in which case the profile states the range and what varies it
B3 Wire tier the fixed ordinary-frame encoding of this binding instance; the registry gives the default, with the explicit UDP mode of 8.2.5 as an exception; crossing between different tiers is specified at Layer 4
B4 Quality metric what 5.3.1's value is derived from
B5 Fade rate or passive-observation retention how quickly a peer not heard from on this bearer should be treated as receding; for a receive-only ingress, the local period for which a passive observation remains visible
B6 Relative cost the cost of sending on this bearer, relative to other bearers on the same hive
B7 Reach the distance class this bearer covers: proximity, local, long-range, global, or point-to-point
B8 Regulated whether transmission on this medium is limited by a duty cycle or airtime budget (Clause 9)
B9 Receptivity and rendezvous PROVISIONAL(r2-codex, SS533): whether the bearer is continuously receptive or receptive only in intervals; an intermittent value states its receptive-window duration, its inclusive range from one window closing to the next opening, its inclusive per-frame range from eligible retained work to a transmission opportunity, and whether the deployment relies on autonomous overlap or a continuously receptive counterpart

7.2 A bearer profile shall be readable by the layers above without knowledge of the medium.

Note 1 to entry: B5 is why silence means different things on different media. A bearer that speaks every few seconds and a bearer that speaks every few minutes cannot share one definition of "not heard from lately" — applying the fast bearer's patience to the slow one declares working links dead, and applying the slow one's to the fast leaves dead links in the table long after they are gone.

Note 2 to entry: B5's value is not a property of the medium and is not set here. It is set against how often the bearer is used to announce presence, which is specified at Layer 2. A bearer declares the value; Layer 2 states what the value has to exceed.

Note 2a to entry: A passive-observation retention is not a disguised fade. A receive-only ingress cannot establish a remote sender's beacon cadence or legal budget merely by hearing it. Its bounded local retention makes a past observation legible without claiming that its expiry means the peer left, receded, or became unreachable. Layer 2 records that distinction and Layer 3 never selects such an ingress as a next hop.

Note 3 to entry: B7 exists so that the layers above can choose a bearer by whether it can reach the destination at all, before considering what it costs. The classes are ordered: proximity, local, long-range, global. Point-to-point is deliberately outside that order — a wired link between two processors reaches exactly one peer and no distance.

Note 4 to entry: B7 classifies the medium's distance bound. It never promises that any particular destination is reachable. A global-reach bearer on an isolated network — a udp binding on a LAN with no route out — reaches only that network's peers, and nothing is wrong: the class says how far the medium can carry, and the only ground truth about reaching a given destination is the addressable-peers report (5.1), which Layer 3's viability rule consumes. Reach class filters candidates for destinations not yet located; addressability decides delivery.

8 The bearer registry

8.1 What binds

8.1.1 The bearer ordinal and its bit are normative. The name is a label.

8.1.2 An implementation shall interoperate with an implementation using a different label for the same ordinal.

Note 1 to entry: Names change and ordinals cannot. Anything comparing bearers across two implementations compares ordinals.

8.2 Registry

8.2.1 The bearer ordinals are as follows.

Ordinal Bit Label Reach Wire tier Largest payload Relative cost
0 0x01 ble proximity compact 200 B 1
1 0x02 — — — — —
2 0x04 lora long-range compact 222 B 5
3 0x08 tcp global extended 65 535 B 8
4 0x10 usb point-to-point compact 255 B 0
5 0x20 wifi-mesh local compact 250 B 9
6 0x40 udp global extended 65 535 B 8

8.2.2 The bearer-set field is seven bits wide: the mask 0x7F. The set of assignable bearers is 0x7D while ordinal 1 remains reserved (8.3.1) — a mask consumer accepting bit 0x02 as live has accepted a bearer that cannot exist.

8.2.3 A bearer shall not use an ordinal not in this registry.

Note 1 to entry: usb has relative cost 0 because a hive on the far end of that link is powered through it. Cost here is energy spent by the sender, not difficulty.

Note 2 to entry: wifi-mesh carries the compact tier although it uses the same radio band as bearers that carry the extended tier. The default tier follows the ordinal, never the band — the bearer uses the medium without its addressing or association, so nothing about the two cases is shared beyond the frequency. The explicit UDP mode of 8.2.5 changes the binding instance's declared tier, not the radio-band interpretation or the registry default.

Note 3 to entry: Ordinal 3 is a reliable ordered stream over IP. More than one medium presents itself that way — a socket carrying length-delimited frames, and a message-oriented protocol layered on such a socket — and 8.2.4 permits both to use the ordinal.

8.2.4 More than one binding may use the same ordinal, provided the bindings declare the same profile (7.1), except for the UDP mode-specific B2 and B3 values permitted by 8.2.5. This exception assigns no new ordinal and permits no other profile difference between bindings sharing an ordinal.

Note 1 to entry: The condition was previously stated only as indistinguishable above Layer 1, with its content in this Note — and a Note carries no requirement, so an implementer bound by 8.2.4 had no normative test. 7.1's profile is that test: B1 to B9 are everything a bearer declares upward, so two bindings declaring the same values are indistinguishable to every layer above by construction. What differs is only how bytes reach the medium. Outside the explicit exception in 8.2.5, where any profile value differs, the bindings are different bearers and 8.4 applies. Within that exception, Layer 3 distinguishes the binding instances and consumes their individual profiles.

Note 1a to entry: The earlier list named four criteria, and two of them were members of the third. Reach is B7 and wire tier is B3 — both profile values — so naming them beside the same profile read as four independent tests where there were two.

Note 1b to entry: The fourth, the same delivery properties, was named nowhere in this document and could not have discriminated anything. Clause 4.4 makes delivery behaviour uniform across every conformant bearer: none is required to deliver (4.4.1), none acknowledges or retransmits at this layer (4.4.2), and one whose medium does either is forbidden to expose it upward (4.4.3). So two conformant bindings cannot differ in delivery properties above Layer 1 — 4.4.3 forbids the difference being visible, which is the property 4.4's own Note 1 says Layer 3 exists to remove. A criterion that every conformant bearer satisfies identically is not a test.

(PROVISIONAL(standard))

8.2.5 A UDP binding may select compact mode version 1 instead of the registry's default extended mode. A binding selecting this mode shall satisfy all of the following:

a) use ordinal 6 and bit 0x40, declare B3 compact, and fix that selection for the binding instance's lifetime; changing mode ends that instance and creates a new one;

b) require explicit matching endpoint configuration before exchanging frames; use a separate UDP endpoint for each simultaneous mode; never choose or change mode from received bytes, successful parsing, authentication, or application contents;

c) carry exactly one opaque frame in each UDP datagram, preceded by the eight octets ff 52 32 49 50 01 00 00, in that order: reserved L4 type marker, ASCII R2IP, version 1, compact mode 0, reserved 0; the envelope is medium framing, outside the L4 frame;

d) accept only datagrams of 9 through 1472 octets inclusive with all eight envelope octets matching; remove exactly that envelope before handing the frame upward; reject an empty body, any other discriminator, a truncated or oversized datagram, and a receive buffer that cannot hold the complete body, without delivering a prefix;

e) declare B2 as the complete inner-frame capacity, at most 1464 octets, accounting for the configured path's transport-unit limit and eight envelope octets; refuse an offer above that capacity, without fragmenting an R2 frame; the envelope does not enlarge the capacity of another bearer on the route;

f) leave ordinary extended UDP framing unchanged, and provide no parsing fallback between modes; in compact mode the envelope also precedes GROUP_MGMT frames, whose existing Layer 4, 9.1.2 parsing rule remains applicable;

g) preserve the inner frame bytes during envelope addition and removal; perform any relay changes only under Layer 4, 4.4, retaining the tier-crossing refusals of Layer 4, 9.2, including refusal to cross an integrity-tagged frame to an extended-only next hop;

h) treat the envelope and endpoint configuration as neither peer authentication nor membership, discovery, routing or receipt authority; retain the remaining obligations of this layer and the existing Layer 2 discovery and Layer 5 admission rules.

Note 1 to entry: This mode permits a protected compact frame to remain compact over a radio-to-IP route. It does not transcode that frame. The compact identifier widths and security margin remain those of Layer 4, including 13.4. TCP remains extended; this clause defines no compact TCP mode, no port assignment, and no automatic negotiation.

Note 2 to entry: This is a mode contract, not a complete UDP binding document under 11.1. Discovery carriage, peer association and other UDP binding details still require their own specifications and evidence. Matching the envelope alone does not demonstrate complete bearer or hive conformance. Byte-exact framing vectors are in TEST-VECTORS-COMPACT-UDP.md.

8.3 Reserved ordinals

8.3.1 Ordinal 1 and bit 0x02 are reserved. They shall not be assigned to a bearer.

Note 1 to entry: An ordinal is reserved rather than freed because the bit positions above it would otherwise move, and every implementation that had stored a bearer set would be holding a wrong one. Reservation costs one bit and reassignment costs correctness.

Note 2 to entry: What ordinal 1 was is part of the registry's record: infrastructure WiFi, retired as a bearer (ruled 2026-07-23). An AP-associated link is not a bearer in this registry. The host's IP stack presents tcp (3) and udp (6), and Reality2 runs over them as an application — a tenant of the host's networking rather than the owner of a radio — so the WiFi-ness of the link underneath a socket is invisible above Layer 1, exactly as the ethernet-ness of a wired one is. An implementation carrying a legacy assignment of ordinal 1 is carrying a retired bearer, and a future bearer never takes the ordinal: it takes the lowest unassigned one (8.4.1).

8.4 Adding a bearer

8.4.1 A new bearer shall be given the lowest unassigned ordinal.

8.4.2 A new bearer shall satisfy Clause 4 in full before it is assigned an ordinal.

8.4.3 Where more than seven bearers are needed, the width of the bearer set shall be extended at Layer 4, and ordinals shall not be reused to avoid doing so.

Note 1 to entry: 8.4.3 states the one thing that cannot be allowed when the registry fills. Reusing an ordinal makes every stored bearer set silently wrong rather than merely full, and nothing distinguishes an old value from a new one.

9 Regulated media

9.1 A bearer whose medium is subject to a transmission duty cycle or airtime budget shall declare so in its profile (B8).

9.2 Such a bearer shall report unavailable when its budget is exhausted, and shall not accept frames it cannot lawfully transmit.

9.3 Such a bearer shall not exceed its budget in order to send a frame, whatever the frame is.

9.4 A regulated bearer shall make available to the Layer 2 scheduler that owns its output: a) an indication that a new legal budget window has begun; b) the airtime that remains legal in the current window; and c) the exact prospective airtime cost of an offered frame after all binding framing has been applied. That interface shall not expose the binding's budget, framing, medium-access control or radio lifecycle.

9.5 All output offers to one regulated bearer of one hive shall pass through one serial scheduler. That scheduler shall be the only party that combines 9.4's information with Layer 2's beacon reservation in 6.2.2a.

Note 3 to entry: 9.4 is an information boundary, not an invitation for Layer 2 to reimplement a radio driver. A quote has no side effect; only the binding may wrap a frame, account airtime, perform medium access or change radio state. The scheduler exists because an ordinary output path that can bypass the reservation makes it a comment rather than a control: it can spend the very airtime the later beacon is supposed to have preserved.

Note 4 to entry: the new-window indication is an edge, consumed by its scheduler. A level that remains true throughout a window permits the scheduler to reset its reservation more than once; no indication at all carries a spent reservation into the next window. Both defeat the "one beacon" bound in 6.2.2a.

Note 1 to entry: 9.3 has no exception for urgency, and the omission is deliberate. A budget with an exception for important traffic is a budget that is exceeded whenever traffic is important, which on a shared medium is exactly when everyone else needs it too. The obligation is also a legal one on the operator in most jurisdictions, and this standard cannot grant relief from it.

Note 2 to entry: 9.2 makes exhaustion visible as a state rather than as unexplained loss. The layers above can then choose another bearer or wait, which they cannot do if the frame was simply dropped.

10 Bearers that hold connections

10.1 A bearer whose medium requires a connection before frames can be carried shall maintain that connection, and shall not require the layers above to know it exists.

10.2 Such a bearer shall treat a peer as unreachable after a period of silence, and shall declare that period in its profile.

10.3 The silence period shall be at least three times the interval at which the bearer expects to hear from a peer.

10.4 Such a bearer should reattempt a lost connection with an interval that increases up to a stated maximum, and should continue reattempting for as long as the bearer is in service.

Note 1 to entry: The factor of three in 10.3 is a floor, not a recommendation. One missed exchange is normal on every medium in this registry; two is common on the regulated ones. A silence period shorter than the factor allows declares live peers dead during ordinary operation, and each such declaration costs a reconnection.

Note 2 to entry: 10.4's continuing indefinitely is deliberate. A hive that gives up on a peer permanently is a hive that has to be restarted by a person when the peer returns, and the peers in this system are expected to come and go.

Note 3 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(standard).

11 What a binding document specifies

11.1 A document specifying an individual binding shall state:

a) the ordinal it uses, from the registry in 8.2, and where it shares that ordinal with another binding (8.2.4), which properties make the two indistinguishable above Layer 1;

b) how a frame is placed in a transport unit, and how transport units are delimited where the medium does not delimit them;

c) the discovery marker required by 4.7, exactly, as bytes and as their position in the medium framing — or, where the bearer does not participate in discovery (4.6.2a), that it carries none;

d) how a medium address is associated with a canonical hive identifier, satisfying 4.5;

e) the maximum transport unit payload, and what varies it if it is not fixed;

f) how the quality value of 5.3.1 is derived from what the medium measures;

g) the conditions producing each of the four states in 5.2 — or, for a state no condition of the medium can produce, that the medium cannot reach it and what prevents it;

h) its duty cycle or airtime budget, where 9.1 applies; and

i) its connection lifecycle and silence period, where Clause 10 applies — and, where Clause 10 does not apply, any precondition the medium places on carrying a frame to an individual peer, the limit on how many such preconditions may be held at once, and how such a precondition comes to be met; and

j) the build-mode declaration required by Layer 2, 5.4a.1, exactly, as bytes and as their position in the medium framing — or, where the medium's announcement cannot carry it, that it cannot; and

k) how the medium is shared: whether a bearer determines the medium to be idle before transmitting, what it does when it is not, and how a bearer that has just received a frame it is required to relay decides when to transmit it — or, where the medium arbitrates access itself, that it does and by what mechanism.

Note 0a to entry: k) IS THE ONLY ITEM IN THIS LIST THAT TWO CONFORMANT IMPLEMENTATIONS CANNOT SUPPLY SEPARATELY. Every other item is a fact one binding states and both sides then read the same way; a contention discipline is a bargain, and half a bargain is worse than none — a bearer that waits politely while its peer transmits immediately has simply given the medium away. That is why it belongs in the binding rather than in an implementation note: it is exactly the kind of thing each side would otherwise choose sensibly and differently.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

11.2 A binding document shall not restate a requirement of this document.

Note 1 to entry: 11.2 is what keeps the bindings interchangeable. A requirement restated in seven binding documents is seven requirements, and they will not stay identical — the first one edited becomes the one that differs, and nothing indicates which of the seven was meant to govern.

Note 2 to entry: 11.1 c) says exactly because a discovery marker is compared byte for byte. A marker described in prose is a marker two implementers will spell differently, and two hives whose markers differ do not fail to connect — they never see each other at all.

12 Conformance

Every requirement of this document is demonstrated by conformance evidence of one of the three classes of 01-terminology.md, 3.10 — behavioural, by construction, or by declaration. A requirement whose evidence is by construction is not thereby optional: the demonstration is an inspection a third party can perform without the implementer's cooperation, and its absence is non-conformance exactly as a failed behavioural test is. Behavioural evidence for any requirement of this document carries the control 01-terminology.md 4.2 to 4.5 requires — the same procedure run against a case the requirement excludes, giving a distinguishable non-satisfying result attributable to the requirement under test; a demonstration that could not have come out any other way is not evidence. (PROVISIONAL(supervisor))

12.1 A bearer conforms to this document where it:

a) satisfies every requirement of Clause 4;

b) reports everything required by Clause 5, keeping each current;

c) accepts what Clause 6 requires and rejects what Clause 6 forbids;

d) declares a complete profile under Clause 7;

e) uses an ordinal from the registry in 8.2;

f) meets Clause 9 where its medium is regulated, and Clause 10 where it holds connections; and

g) is specified by a binding document meeting Clause 11.

12.2 An implementation of Layer 1 conforms where every bearer it provides conforms, and where 4.8.1 holds of it.

12.3 Conformance shall be demonstrable with two implementations of one bearer built independently from its binding document.

12.4 Conformance to 11.1a shall be demonstrated by a role-asymmetric pairing test: two devices of the same implementation, both attempting to reach the other, complete a connection.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 1 to entry: 12.3 is the only test that reaches what this layer is for. A single implementation tested against itself will agree with itself about anything it left unstated — including the discovery marker, which is where two independent implementations most often discover they were never going to meet.

13 What is not decided at this layer

This clause states gaps and contains no requirements.

13.1 What a hive does with a bearer that reports failed. 5.2 requires the state to be reported and distinguishes it from unavailable by whether waiting helps. What constitutes the intervention, whether it can be automatic, and whether a hive may retire a bearer that keeps failing, are unspecified.

13.2 Medium framing is used normatively and defined nowhere — and it decides B2. 4.7.1 places the discovery marker in its medium framing, 4.7.2 forbids it inside the frame, and 11.1 c) and j) require a binding document to state a position in the medium framing. The term appears in this document three times, in requirements, and Clause 3 does not define it. Two readings survive the text and they differ by exactly the advertised maximum transport unit payload:

  • the medium's own framing, outside the payload it carries — the marker costs no payload bytes and 3.7's largest frame in one transport unit is unaffected; or
  • anything a bearer adds for its medium, including the 4.2.2 wrapping that sits inside the transport unit — the marker consumes payload and B2 falls by its length.

The first is the better reading on two structural grounds, neither conclusive. 11.1 lists b) how a frame is placed in a transport unit and c) the marker's position as separate items, so the marker's position is not the frame's placement; and 11.1 j)'s escape — where the medium's announcement cannot carry it, that it cannot — is meaningless under the second reading, because wrapping a bearer adds itself always has room. A medium that cannot carry a field is a fact about the medium, which is what makes that branch honest.

Consequence, and it is live: a bearer whose medium exposes no framing outside the payload is required by 4.7.1 to carry a marker and forbidden by 4.7.2 from the only place it could put one. 4.7 offers no cannot carry it branch, which is what 11.1 c) inherits. (PROVISIONAL(standard))
