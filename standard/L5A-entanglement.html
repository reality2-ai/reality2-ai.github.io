Reality2 Standard — Layer 5 companion: Entanglement

Status: Draft — first pass Date: 2026-08-05

Verbal forms. shall = requirement · should = recommendation · may = permission · can = possibility and capability.

Introduction

Layer 5 decided what an entanglement is: an explicit agreement between two trust groups — a scope, a level, and conditions — standing exactly as long as its conditions are met, answered on breach by severance or demotion (Layer 5, Clause 10). This document supplies what that frame deliberately left open: how an entanglement is established, what the levels are, how conditions are expressed and evaluated, how the relationship lives, lapses, and ends, and what liveness across it can and cannot mean.

Two design commitments run through every clause:

The conditions are the agreement, and the agreement is one signed artefact. There is no ceremony whose memory fades and no terms that live in two places. Both groups sign one statement of terms, each holds it, and everything the entanglement later does — gate crossings, judge conduct, demote, expire — is done against that artefact.

Each side judges alone. No arbiter exists, no shared evaluator, no consensus. Each group evaluates the conditions on its own evidence, by its own lights — which means the two sides can disagree about whether the entanglement still stands, and the design treats that as ordinary rather than as an error.

1 Scope

This document specifies the establishment of an entanglement between two trust groups: the agreement artefact, the authority to bind a group, the key establishment, the grades of the level of authentication, and the completing trial.

It specifies the vocabulary of conditions, their two failure classes, and how each side evaluates them.

It specifies the lifecycle: standing, lapse, completion, demotion, severance, and re-establishment, and what liveness across an entanglement establishes.

It establishes what conformance to this document consists of, and identifies what is not decided.

It does not specify byte formats, which belong to the formats companion. It does not specify the enrolment ceremony or the management wallet, which are a separate companion document. It does not specify bearer-specific evidence of proximity, which belongs to each bearer's binding document.

3 Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the terms and definitions given in the documents listed in Clause 2 apply, together with the following.

3.1 agreement artefact

statement of an entanglement's scope, grade, conditions and intended lifetime, signed by an authorised member of each participating group

3.2 grade

named strength of the verification binding an entanglement's keys to the counterpart group's identity

Note 1 to entry: The grade instantiates the level of authentication of Layer 5, 10.2.1 b). Grades are ordered; demotion (Layer 5, 10.3) moves down them.

3.3 condition

stated predicate of an agreement artefact that a participating group evaluates locally against its own evidence

3.4 lapse

end of an entanglement because a condition is no longer met, without fault

3.5 introducer

group holding standing entanglements with two other groups, whose attestation carries one group's identity to the other

4 The agreement artefact

4.1 One artefact, both signatures

4.1.1 An entanglement shall be recorded in a single agreement artefact stating its scope, its grade, its conditions, and its intended lifetime.

4.1.2 The artefact shall carry member-attributable evidence (Layer 5, 7.4) from each participating group, computed over the identical terms.

4.1.3 Each group shall hold its own copy of the signed artefact, and every judgement this document requires — gating a crossing, classing a failure, demoting, expiring — shall be made against it.

Note 1 to entry: 4.1.2 binds the conditions into the signed thing. Terms that travelled beside a signature rather than under it could drift apart from it, and a dispute would then be about which terms were agreed rather than whether they were met. One artefact, two signatures, zero ambiguity about what was promised.

Note 2 to entry: The evidence of 4.1.2 names the member key that signed for each group. That is deliberate: when conduct is later judged, the claim "your side agreed to this" is checkable against a signature, not against a recollection of a ceremony.

4.2 Authority to bind

4.2.1 The member signing an artefact for its group shall hold that group's authority to entangle (Layer 6, 4.1).

4.2.2 A counterpart shall verify the signer's membership by the evidence of 4.1.2, and is not required to verify the signer's internal authority.

Note 1 to entry: 4.2.2 states where the burden falls, and it falls inward. A group is bound to its counterparts by any member signature its counterpart accepted in good faith — whether that member held the internal authority is the group's own governance, enforced by its own enumeration (Layer 6, 4.1) and answered by its own eviction and rotation machinery. A counterpart cannot audit another group's constitution, and this document does not pretend otherwise.

5 Establishment

5.1 The sequence

5.1.1 An entanglement shall be established by the following sequence, in order:

a) proposal — one group presents terms: scope, grade, conditions, intended lifetime;

b) agreement — the groups exchange revisions until the terms are identical, and each side's authorised member signs the identical terms (4.1);

c) verification — the counterpart group's identity is verified at the agreed grade (5.3);

d) key establishment — the entanglement's keys are derived (5.2); and

e) trial — one crossing in each contracted direction passes the counterpart's delivery gate (5.4).

5.1.2 Until the trial completes, the entanglement shall be provisional: crossings under it shall not be delivered beyond what the trial itself requires.

Note 1 to entry: Proposal and agreement ride the unauthenticated level of the ladder (Layer 5, 10.1) — that level exists precisely so that first contact is possible. Nothing is granted by the exchange itself; everything is granted by what is signed and then verified.

5.2 Key establishment

5.2.1 The entanglement's keys shall be derived bilaterally from the two groups' key material and the agreement artefact, under the one-derivation rule of Layer 5, 5.2, such that both sides derive identical keys and neither transmits one.

5.2.2 The derivation shall produce at least a payload-protection key and an integrity key, distinct from each other and from every key of either group.

5.2.3 The derivation shall order the two groups' identities canonically, so that the keys are independent of which group initiated.

5.2.4 The keys shall be re-derivable from the artefact and the groups' retained key material.

Note 1 to entry: 5.2.4 is the durability rule, and it is why an entanglement survives a crash without a resumption protocol. The relationship's keys are a deterministic consequence of one bilateral invariant — who agreed, to what — so a device that lost its session state and still holds its group material and the artefact holds the entanglement. Nothing needs to be re-negotiated; the agreement is the state.

Note 2 to entry: The exact agreement scheme, derivation labels and key set are the formats companion's. What binds here is the shape: bilateral, canonical ordering, one derivation, re-derivable, nothing transmitted.

5.3 The grades

5.3.1 The grade shall be one of the following, ordered from weakest to strongest, and the artefact shall name it.

Grade Name What binds the keys to the counterpart Defeats
0 opportunistic nothing beyond the in-band exchange passive observation only
1 confirmed a short verification string, derived from both group identities and the establishment exchange, compared over a channel the establishment did not use — a screen read aloud, a display matched, a physical tap an interposer at establishment
2 introduced an introducer's member-attributable attestation (Layer 5, 7.4) of the counterpart's group identity, under an entanglement each side already holds with the introducer at grade 1 or above an interposer, without physical co-presence
3 ceremonial grade 1 or 2, and additionally a person on each side confirming at a person-authentication level the artefact states an interposer, and an unattended endpoint

5.3.2 The verification string of grade 1 shall be derived from both groups' identities and the establishment exchange, such that an interposed establishment yields visibly different strings; it shall bind the group identities and shall not be a reusable code.

5.3.3 An introducer's attestation (grade 2) shall carry the counterpart's group identity and shall be believed per the conditions of the entanglement it arrives under.

5.3.4 A receiver shall treat the grade as a ceiling on what its own policies may accept over the entanglement, and demotion (Layer 5, 10.3) shall lower that ceiling to the named lower grade at once.

Note 1 to entry: Grade 0 is stated honestly rather than dressed up: its keys encrypt against the world but authenticate nothing about the counterpart — an interposer present at establishment holds the channel. It exists because the payment example's counterpart — the conference-room screen — needs exactly this and no more, and because refusing to name the weak grade would not remove it, only leave it unlabelled.

Note 2 to entry: Grade 2 is how the hub topology works at scale: the insights store introduces a community to nothing — it attests identity, and each pair still agrees its own terms. An introduction is evidence about who the counterpart is, never a grant of anything.

Note 3 to entry: Grade 3 is the floor for management-bearing scope (Clause 9). The artefact states the required level; how a group's devices reach that level is its own vocabulary. The scale itself — ruled as 0–7, probability-anchored, level-only across boundaries — has its normative home in the person-authentication companion, not yet written; until it lands, the level is a number the two artefact signers agree the meaning of. (Settled by ruling; the ruling record is informative and outside this standard.)

5.4 The trial

5.4.1 The trial shall consist of at least one frame in each contracted direction, carrying no application effect, crossing the counterpart's delivery gate under the entanglement's keys.

5.4.2 An entanglement whose trial does not complete within the artefact's stated establishment window shall be discarded: keys destroyed, artefact void.

Note 1 to entry: The trial is the will-it-actually-work check made structural. An entanglement that was agreed, verified and keyed — and whose frames then cannot cross, because a tier boundary intervenes or a scope was mis-stated — is a standing lie. Two frames retire it.

6 Conditions

6.1 What a condition is

6.1.1 Each condition of an artefact shall state:

a) the predicate — what is claimed to hold;

b) the evidence — what each side evaluates it against; and

c) the failure class — lapse (6.2) or breach (Layer 5, 10.3), and for breach, whether the stated response is severance or demotion and to which grade.

6.1.2 Each group shall evaluate every condition locally, against its own evidence. No condition shall require a shared evaluator, an arbiter, or the counterpart's agreement to fail.

6.1.3 A group shall not adopt a condition whose evidence requires a facility its platforms have not declared (Layer 0, Clause 8).

Note 1 to entry: 6.1.3 is why there is no absolute-time trap here. A condition anchored to calendar time is adoptable by the venue with a clock and not by the sensor without one — the condition vocabulary bends to the platform declaration, never the reverse. The party that holds the ruler makes the measurement.

6.2 The two failure classes

6.2.1 A lapse shall end the entanglement, or suspend it where the artefact stands and its terms permit resumption; in either case the derived keys are destroyed as 8.2 requires, and a suspended entanglement resumes only by re-derivation under 5.2.4. A lapse is without fault: no standing is affected, and re-establishment is ordinary.

Note 1 to entry: Suspension and key destruction are not in conflict, because the keys are not the entanglement. 5.2.4 requires them to be re-derivable from the artefact and the groups' retained key material, and 8.5 already relies on exactly that to survive a restart — so a side holding the artefact holds the relationship, whatever it does with the derived keys. What ends an entanglement is the artefact going void (5.4.2), a terminal lapse such as completion or lifetime end, severance, or dissolution (8.1). (Reworded 2026-08-02: the earlier text left "suspend" apparently contradicting 8.2. No choice was made here — 5.2.4 and 8.5 already decided it, and this states what they decide.)

6.2.2 A breach shall be answered as Layer 5, 10.3 provides — severance or demotion, as the artefact states — and is evidence about the breaching group's conduct.

6.2.3 A condition whose failure can occur innocently — presence fading, a lifetime ending, a scope completing — shall be classed as lapse.

6.2.4 No condition shall class silence as breach. Unreachability shall only ever lapse.

Note 1 to entry: 6.2.4 is the core premise applied to fault. Silence is ambiguous, always — asleep, moved, dead, and budget-exhausted are indistinguishable from outside — so a group can never be at fault for being unheard. A counterpart that needs standing liveness contracts for it (Clause 7) and treats the contract's failure as lapse: the door closes; nobody is blamed for the weather.

Note 2 to entry: The distinction is what the earthgrid example turns on. The presenter walking out of the room is a lapse — the condition failed, the keys died, no one's standing moved. The insurer redistributing licensed insights is a breach — the artefact named it one, and the response and the reputational evidence follow. Conflating the two would make every sleeping sensor a defaulter.

6.3 The condition vocabulary

6.3.1 An artefact's conditions shall be drawn from the following classes; a class an implementation does not support is a condition it shall not agree to.

Class Predicate over Evidence Typical failure class
presence the counterpart hearable on named bearers sightings and fade behaviour (Layer 2, 6.3) lapse
temporal a window or lifetime the ruler of the side that holds one (6.1.3) lapse
transactional requirements per crossing — a required person-authentication level per operation or amount, required member-attributable evidence per event class the crossing itself (Layer 5, 7.4; attested levels) neither — an unmet transactional condition refuses that crossing and nothing else, unless the artefact states that repetition is breach
conduct obligations on use of what crossed — licence, redistribution, retention member-attributable claims and the group's own observation, weighed as all claims are weighed breach
disclosure grants of visibility — the member-key set (Layer 5, 7.4.4), class and capability queries, catalogue listing the grant's presence in the artefact breach if exceeded
reciprocity contracted liveness — an expected keepalive under Clause 7 the contracted signal's arrival lapse (6.2.4)

6.3.2 A disclosure grant shall be explicit in the artefact; no disclosure shall be inferred from any other condition or from the entanglement's existence.

Note 1 to entry: The transactional row is where the payment ladder lives: under 10 at level 4, under 100 at level 5, over 100 at level 6 is three transactional conditions over one entanglement — evaluated per crossing, refusing individually, breaching nothing. The amount ladder never tears the relationship down; it prices each act.

Note 2 to entry: Conduct conditions are evaluated on claims — and a claim of breach is itself evidence carrying its issuer's signature (Layer 5, 7.4), weighed by the receiving group with everything it knows, corroborated or not. The artefact makes conduct judgeable; it does not make judgement automatic.

6.4 Disagreement between the sides

6.4.1 The sides may reach different conclusions about a condition, and an implementation shall tolerate the asymmetry: an entanglement one side holds lapsed and the other holds standing fails crossings in one direction and not the other.

6.4.2 A side whose crossings repeatedly fail should re-evaluate the entanglement's standing rather than retry indefinitely.

Note 1 to entry: There is no protocol here to force convergence, deliberately — a convergence protocol would be a shared evaluator wearing a different hat. The mesh's ordinary signals — crossings failing, liveness fading — carry the disagreement to whichever side has not yet noticed, and 6.4.2 is the nudge to listen to them.

7 Liveness across an entanglement

7.1 Traffic verifying under the entanglement's integrity key shall establish only that some holder of the entanglement's keys was reachable: it is advisory, it shall not establish which group — still less which member — was alive, and it shall not satisfy any life-safety or standing-liveness contract.

7.2 Authoritative liveness of the counterpart shall require member-attributable evidence (Layer 5, 7.4) over a challenge or fresh token — the entanglement's channel keys shall not substitute for it.

Note 1 to entry: The channel keys are symmetric and bilateral: either side — including the asker — can produce traffic that verifies under them, and a replayed frame verifies too. 7.1 keeps that honest. 7.2 is the strong form, and it exists in this standard already: the certificate-carrying member signature over a nonce is exactly the evidence of Layer 5, 7.4.3 a). The standing keepalive contract itself — rates, windows, scopes — is not specified here and remains open; what is decided here is the two strengths and the rule that one never impersonates the other.

8 Lifecycle

8.1 An entanglement shall end in exactly one of the following ways, and an implementation shall distinguish them:

a) completion — the scope is exhausted or the lifetime ends: a lapse, planned;

b) lapse — a lapse-class condition is no longer met (6.2);

c) severance — by breach response, or unilaterally at either side's discretion (Layer 5, 10.3.3); or

d) dissolution — both sides agree to end it.

8.2 On completion, lapse, severance or dissolution, a side shall destroy the entanglement's derived keys it holds; crossings under them fail the gate from that moment.

8.3 Demotion (Layer 5, 10.3) shall not end the entanglement: the keys stand, the grade ceiling lowers (5.3.4), and the artefact's stated demotion terms govern what remains in scope.

8.4 A severing or dissolving side may send notice to the counterpart; notice shall be a courtesy, and the end of the entanglement shall never depend on its delivery.

8.5 An entanglement shall survive restart: a side holding its group material and the artefact holds the entanglement (5.2.4), and conditions are re-evaluated on return.

8.6 Re-establishment after any ending shall be a fresh establishment (Clause 5) with fresh keys; the prior relationship's record is evidence for each side's own judgement, never an entitlement and never a bar.

Note 1 to entry: 8.4 is the no-promises rule applied to goodbye. A severance that required notice to take effect would hand the severed party a veto by unreachability. The gate simply starts refusing; the courtesy event, where it arrives, spares the counterpart some failed crossings.

Note 2 to entry: 8.6 is where standing does its work without becoming authority. The insurer whose entanglement was severed for breach can ask again — and meets each community's updated belief, priced into the new terms or into silence. Nothing forbids the second chance; nothing subsidises it either.

9 Management-bearing entanglements

9.1 An artefact whose scope includes management operations (Layer 6) shall be at grade 3, and shall name the managing group, the managed surface, and the operations granted, explicitly and exhaustively.

9.2 A management crossing shall carry member-attributable evidence (Layer 5, 7.4) regardless of the entanglement's other terms; the entanglement's channel keys alone shall never authorise a management operation.

9.3 A management grant shall be revocable by the granting side as a demotion or severance (Layer 5, 10.3), at any time.

Note 1 to entry: This clause is the narrow neck through which cross-group management passes, and it is deliberately narrow: top grade, exhaustive enumeration, member attribution on every act, revocable at will. A group that manages another's devices does so as a named member of a named group under a named grant — never as a key that opened a channel once.

10 Conformance

Every requirement of this document is demonstrated by conformance evidence of one of the three classes of 01-terminology.md, 3.10 — behavioural, by construction, or by declaration. A requirement whose evidence is by construction is not thereby optional: the demonstration is an inspection a third party can perform without the implementer's cooperation, and its absence is non-conformance exactly as a failed behavioural test is. Behavioural evidence for any requirement of this document carries the control 01-terminology.md 4.2 to 4.5 requires — the same procedure run against a case the requirement excludes, giving a distinguishable non-satisfying result attributable to the requirement under test; a demonstration that could not have come out any other way is not evidence. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor); the control sentence added the same day, finding SS183.)

10.1 An implementation conforms to this document where it:

a) establishes entanglements only by the sequence of 5.1, over one dually-signed artefact (Clause 4);

b) derives keys bilaterally, canonically ordered, re-derivably (5.2);

c) implements the grades of 5.3 it offers, treats the grade as a ceiling, and lowers it on demotion at once;

d) states, for every condition it agrees to, the predicate, the evidence and the failure class, evaluates them locally, and never classes silence as breach (Clause 6);

e) treats channel-key traffic as advisory liveness only (Clause 7);

f) distinguishes the endings of 8.1, destroys keys on ending, and survives restart (Clause 8); and

g) gates management scope as Clause 9 requires.

10.2 Conformance to 6.2.4 shall be demonstrated by the negative case: a counterpart made unreachable mid-entanglement lapses and is not recorded as in breach.

11 Security considerations

11.1 Grade 0 authenticates nothing about the counterpart. An interposer present at establishment holds the channel thereafter. The grade exists for stakes where that is acceptable, and 5.3.1 requires the artefact to say so in its own text.

11.2 The entanglement's keys are symmetric: either side can fabricate traffic that verifies as the channel — which is why a crossing establishes the entanglement and never the member (Layer 5, 10.4.1), why liveness under channel keys is advisory only (7.1), and why management requires member attribution per act (9.2).

11.3 A disclosure grant, once exercised, cannot be unexercised: a member-key set handed over under 7.4.4 is knowledge the counterpart keeps even after severance. Rotation retires the keys' validity, not the counterpart's memory of the roster's size and shape.

11.4 Breach claims are an attack surface: a false claim, credibly signed, damages a group's standing with third parties. The defence is the standard's ordinary epistemology — claims are weighed, not obeyed; corroboration is priced in; and a claimant caught fabricating has spent its own standing to no effect.

11.5 Grade 2 concentrates trust in introducers. An introducer that lies about an identity poisons every entanglement built on its word — bounded by 5.3.3: its attestations are believed per each side's own entanglement conditions with it, and an introducer's standing is as revisable as anyone's.

12 What is not decided at this layer

This clause states gaps and contains no requirements.

12.1 Byte formats. The artefact, the evidence it carries, the derivation labels, the verification-string length and alphabet, the grade encoding — all owed to the formats companion.

12.2 A machine-readable condition grammar. Clause 6 fixes the semantics — predicate, evidence, failure class — and the vocabulary classes, and does not fix an encoding in which two implementations express arbitrary terms interoperably. Hand-configured conditions interoperate today because both sides configure them; a marketplace where a policy sentant reads a stranger's terms needs the grammar. This is the largest open item in this document.

12.3 Per-bearer proximity evidence. What evidences presence, how continuously, and how spoofable-at-range it may be, per bearer — owed to the bearer binding documents, priced by grade.

12.4 The standing keepalive contract. Rates, windows, scopes and its relation to life-safety silence detection — register item Q36; Clause 7 decides only the two strengths.

12.5 The enrolment ceremony and the management wallet. Deliberately not here — one document per concern. They remain owed as their own companion (Layer 5, 13.1 and 13.7).

12.6 Introducer protocol detail. Grade 2 states what an introduction establishes; the exchange by which one is requested and delivered is not specified.
