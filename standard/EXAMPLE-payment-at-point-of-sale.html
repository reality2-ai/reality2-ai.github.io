Reality2 Standard — worked example: payment at a point of sale

Informative example

Status: Informative — worked example Date: 2026-08-12

⚠ INFORMATIVE, and honest about its edges. This example exercises the whole stack end-to-end against a scenario a user would recognise. Where it runs on decided text it cites the clause; where it runs on register questions it says so in place — an example that hid its open edges would teach an implementer confidence nobody has earned. Verified against the documents it cites on 2026-07-31.

Cast

  • Person trust group P — the user's phone and watch. The user is P's key holder: that is what personal sovereignty means at Layer 5. The wallet is a sentant on the phone; the tokens are signed artefacts in its custody.
  • Merchant trust group M — the PoS terminal, holding delegated authority within M, and a back-office hive elsewhere.
  • A shared token system — stipulated: both sides understand the same signed-token scheme. Payment is the transfer of tokens from P's wallet to M.

And the key structural fact (Roy, 2026-07-31): the tokens live in the wallet, on the devices the person carries. There is no central authority in this transaction — no bank queried, no ledger consulted, no server reachable or needed. The whole exchange is two sovereign groups, physically adjacent, transacting over their own radio link with value one of them carries — which is why it works in a market with no signal exactly as it works in a city. This is the payment expression of the stack's own shape: no authentication server (identity is membership), no routing infrastructure (the mesh is the peers), no truth oracle (Thurisaz), and now no mint in the loop at transaction time. The token system's issuer exists somewhere — issuance and settlement are its affair — but the transaction needs nobody but the two parties present. The cost of that sovereignty is named at the end: offline value can be double-spent offline, and the token design carries that burden, not the network.

The three authentication axes, kept apart

The scenario runs on three different kinds of "who is this?", and confusing them is the main way to mis-build it:

Axis Question Who answers, how
Group (L5, Clause 7) is this frame M's / P's? the delivery gate — binary, cryptographic
Entanglement level (L5, 10.1) how far do these two groups trust each other? the entanglement's agreed level and conditions
Person-authentication level how sure is P that its human is present right now? P's own devices, as a decaying confidence — see below

The person-authentication level

P's devices continuously estimate the probability that the person wearing and holding them is the user: heart-rate signature, gait analysis, wear-continuity, unlock history. The estimate is expressed on an agreed scale — level 0, no idea, to level 7, above 95 % certain — and it behaves exactly as this standard's epistemology says a belief behaves:

  • It is a standing level maintained by passive evidence. Continuously worn, regularly moving devices might sustain level 5 without the user doing anything.
  • It decays. Take the watch off and the level collapses toward 0; leave the phone on a table and it sags.
  • Active evidence gives a momentary spike above the standing level. A fingerprint read is strong evidence with a fast decay — level 6 for a moment, sliding back to 5.
  • A confirm affordance is an act that raises it, and affordances are graded: passive presence < an acknowledgement tap < a biometric. (This gives operational shape to a term the terminology deliberately leaves undefined; the definition is register work.)

The human–PoS interaction is ZKP-inspired: prove the predicate, never the evidence (Roy, 2026-07-31). M's question is only ever "is the person present at level ≥ N?" — and what crosses the entanglement is a signed attestation of that predicate and nothing else. The gait signature, the heart-rate model, the fingerprint template, the unlock history — none of it leaves P, ever; it is not that M does not receive the biometrics, it is that the protocol has no place to put them. The user proves enough, and only enough: one graded fact per requirement.

The level is P's self-assessment, and M can never verify the gait data behind it. What M receives is a signed attestation of the level — member-attributable evidence from P — and M believes it per the entanglement's conditions, the way a payment terminal trusts a card scheme's cardholder verification within scheme rules. A lying wallet is a breach: severance, demotion, and the token system's dispute layer — the liability lives in the relationship, priced by the amount ladder below.

The signal trace

Phase 0 — the mesh, before anyone means anything

Both devices are already meshing; no decision is involved.

# Signal Layer facts
0.1 PoS beacons on BLE: marker, beacon identifier, class hash (commerce.pos.terminal), production build mode L2 5.1; broadcast, addressed to no one
0.2 Phone scans: filter 1 (is it R2), filter 2 (class hash match), build-mode check — production/production, else cooperation is refused L2 7.1, 5.4a.5
0.3 Both form neighbour entries — link axis only; nothing is verified, nothing is trusted L3 4.2

Phase 1 — first contact, on the unauthenticated rung

No shared group, no entanglement: strangers — and strangers are legal (L5 10.1).

# Signal Layer facts
1.1 PoS sentant → public event commerce.pos.invite: an invitation and a transaction reference only cleartext, claims nothing; the sentant firewall is the only gate (L7 9.1)

⚠ The basket and the amount do not belong in this phase. The unauthenticated rung is cleartext and forgeable. A naive implementation putting the total in the invitation broadcasts the user's shopping to the street. Amounts move only inside Phase 3's encryption.

Phase 2 — a short-lived entanglement ⚠ register territory (Q25)

The two groups establish an entanglement on the ruled frame (L5 10.2): scope = this transaction's events, both directions · entanglement level = payment-grade · lifetime = minutes, a condition · conditions including the timeout, both sides remaining in production mode, and the amount ladder:

Purchase amount Required person-authentication level Typical way P reaches it
under $10 level 4 standing passive confidence
under $100 level 5 an acknowledgement tap (4 → 5 is just an acknowledgement)
over $100 level 6 a fingerprint — a momentary spike above the standing level

Establishment requires the human on P's side — the user is the key holder, and what they confirm has to be bound to this terminal: merchant identity and conditions shown on both screens, so a ghost terminal a metre away cannot relay itself into the confirmation.

⚠ The establishment ceremony's exact signals are Q25 — design-stage. This example runs on the ruled frame (conditions, level, lifetime, severance/demotion); the companion document owes the bytes.

Phase 3 — the payment, inside the entanglement

Every frame now: entanglement-tagged (gate path L5 7.1.2 c), payload encrypted under the entanglement's key (L5 7.3) — no key negotiation; the classification names the key.

# Signal What it carries Layer facts
3.1 M→P commerce.pay.invoice amount, token type, transaction id, required person-authentication level for this amount encrypted; idempotent by transaction id
3.2 (phone-local) wallet → screen → the user the confirm affordance the amount demands: tap at $50, fingerprint at $150 never framed, never on air (L7 5.1.2)
3.3 P→M commerce.pay.transfer the signed token — P's wallet key over "token T to group M, transaction X" — plus the signed attestation of the current person-authentication level the entanglement authenticates the channel; the token signature authorises the payment (L5 7.2.3: the gate is a floor, never the authorisation)
3.4 M checks token signature · attested level ≥ required level · transaction id fresh to M duplicate deliveries harmless — applied once by transaction id
3.5 M→P commerce.pay.receipt M's signed receipt receipt-with-release: on it, the wallet marks tokens spent; the merchant releases the goods. Until it arrives, the phone retransmits — fire-and-forget beneath, certainty at the endpoints

Where the security actually lives, stated plainly: the delivery gate proved the frames are M's and P's — and deliberately not which member sent them, nor that they are fresh (L5 7.2.2). Payment is member-attributable, non-idempotent and freshness-critical — the exact class L5 7.2.3 assigns evidence-beyond-the-gate. The token system supplies that evidence (the wallet's signature, the transaction id), and the level attestation rides the same signature. The network carries a payment protocol; it does not do payments.

Phase 4 — teardown

Conditions met → the entanglement expires (lifetime was a condition); keys destroyed. Timeout or mismatch → severed mid-transaction — and the transaction id makes the abort clean: a transfer without a receipt is tokens never marked spent.

What an observer saw

Single-hop BLE: nearly nothing. If relayed: the envelope only — that traffic happened, when, how much of it, and the event hashes (commerce.pay.* is recognisable in flight — the accepted metadata bound of L5 7.3.4). Amounts, tokens, levels: never.

Where the tokens come from, and where they go

The lifecycle is cash, deliberately (Roy, 2026-07-31). The user converted dollars into tokens beforehand — the wallet's equivalent of withdrawing cash — and the merchant will reconvert them into dollars afterwards, the equivalent of banking the till. Three phases, and only the outer two touch any rail beyond the mesh:

Phase Parties Where it runs
Issuance — dollars → tokens into P's wallet wallet ↔︎ the token issuer / bank online, at the edge of the R2 world; not a mesh concern
Circulation — the transaction of this example P ↔︎ M, and nobody else the sovereign middle: offline, adjacent, authority-free
Redemption — tokens → dollars M ↔︎ the issuer / bank online, at the other edge

An issuer exists — and is never in the loop, exactly as the mint that printed a banknote is not a party to handing it over. Both sides' "shared understanding of the token system" is, concretely, trust in the issuer's signature on the tokens; what the transaction itself needs is only the two groups present.

Or the token is a true cryptocurrency — and only the edges change. The "issuer" becomes the currency's own protocol; issuance is acquiring coin; redemption is broadcasting settlement to the chain when connectivity allows; the offline transfer hands over a signed, not-yet-settled instrument, and the double-spend rules are whatever that currency's design provides (channel constructions, bounded-float instruments, settle-on-reconnect). Not one frame of the trace above changes — the stipulated "shared understanding of the token system" was the only coupling, and it is deliberately pluggable: fiat-backed issuer tokens and a cryptocurrency ride the identical mesh transaction.

And the scope ruling that closes the subject (Roy, 2026-07-31): the agreement is purely between the two entities — it is not this standard's problem. What the tokens are, what they are worth, how they settle, and what happens when the parties fall out are the parties' agreement, end to end. The standard's whole contribution to payment is what this example has been using it for: discovery, a conditional private channel between two sovereign groups, attested evidence at an agreed level, and frames that arrive. Value is an application. It stays one.

Redemption is also where double-spend settles. A duplicated offline token is caught when the copies meet at the issuer — like counterfeit surfacing at the bank deposit — and the merchant's exposure between transaction and redemption is float risk, priced exactly where this example already prices risk: the amount ladder. Small amounts ride the standing level and the float; large amounts demand a fingerprint today and, at the token system's discretion, an online check — a choice the token design makes, not the mesh.
