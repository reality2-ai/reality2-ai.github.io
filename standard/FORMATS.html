Reality2 Standard — Formats companion

Status: DRAFT for ratification, authorized 2026-08-01 (D-023). Date: 2026-08-05.

Verbal forms. shall = requirement · should = recommendation · may = permission · can = possibility and capability.

Provisional discipline. This draft settles seven register items (SS23, SS26, SS35, SS36, SS37, SS38, Q32). Where the parts left a choice unmade, this document makes it openly and provisionally: every invented value is marked PROVISIONAL(SS-n) and is open to replacement until ratified — implementations code against it and swap on ruling, exactly as the Layer 4 vector set was built. Where this document conflicts with a ratified part, the part is right.

1 Scope

This document pins the byte-level formats the layer parts reference but do not state: identity material and its wire derivations (Layer 4, 6.1.3 and 7.1.4); the payload cipher and its envelope (Layer 5, 7.3); the update package (Layer 6, Clause 4); the capability-contract registry and its first three contracts (Layer 7); and the definition and score formats (Layer 7, 10.2). The Layer 4 conformance vector set is a separate normative companion, TEST-VECTORS-L4.md, cited in Clause 2 — ratified in substance, and published with this document rather than contained in it.

2 Normative references

01-terminology.md, L4-wire.md, L5-trust-and-identity.md, L6-management.md, L7-application.md, TEST-VECTORS-L4.md.

3 Identity material and wire identity (settles SS23, SS26)

3.1 The identity material of a member, a hive, or a group shall be an Ed25519 key pair; the identity is the 32-byte public key. PROVISIONAL(SS23) — algorithm choice; Ed25519 fits no_std targets and L5's signature uses.

3.1a A verifier shall reject a small-order or non-canonical Ed25519 public key.

3.1b A verifier shall reject a non-canonical Ed25519 signature encoding. PROVISIONAL(SS114).

Note 1 to entry: The reason is that this standard treats a signature as an identity for a decision, in at least two places — Layer 6, 5.2.3 reasons about the package that was signed, and Layer 5, 7.4.3a advances a high-water mark on a verified signature. Under permissive verification one message has several valid signatures, so one authorisation can appear as two: the replay this standard refuses everywhere else, arriving through the verifier instead of through the wire.

Note 2 to entry: This clause is a reason, not yet evidence. The implementation that proposed it measured its own guard and found it unproven — downgrading strict verification to permissive left its entire suite green. A property no test distinguishes is one four platforms will each decide differently, and interop will look fine until it does not, so strictness belongs here with a vector rather than in any one implementation's judgement. The vector is owed. (Recorded this way at the reporting lane's insistence, which is the counterweight working: a reason offered as a reason.)

3.2 Identity material shall be generated from the platform's cryptographically secure random number generator (Layer 0, 5.5.1) at first provisioning, and never from a name, a serial number, or any operator-supplied string (Layer 4, 7.1.4).

3.3 The extended wire identity of a hive (Layer 4, 6.1.3) shall be derived as:

  • hive half = bytes 0–3 of SHA-256 of the hive's identity public key;
  • group half = bytes 0–3 of SHA-256 of the group's identity public key;

each taken big-endian. PROVISIONAL(SS23) — hash and truncation choice.

3.4 The compact wire identity of a hive shall be its hive half alone — the 4-byte projection of 3.3, as adopted provisionally by the reference implementation and endorsed here.

3.5 Where a derived half equals 0x00000000 or 0xFFFFFFFF (reserved: Layer 4, 6.1.4 and 7.1.3), the derivation shall be repeated with the byte 0x01 appended to the hashed input, iterating until a non-reserved value results. PROVISIONAL(SS23).

3.6 Uniqueness is not guaranteed and shall not be assumed; collisions of wire identity are survived as Layer 4, 7.2 survives all hash collisions. The identity itself (the public key) is what is unique with overwhelming probability, and every consequential act verifies against it above the trust boundary.

4 Payload cipher and envelope (settles SS35)

4.1 The payload cipher for every encrypted level of Layer 5, 7.3 shall be XChaCha20-Poly1305 (256-bit key, 24-byte nonce, 16-byte tag). PROVISIONAL(SS35) — chosen over ChaCha20-Poly1305 because the 24-byte random nonce removes the nonce-uniqueness bookkeeping a 16-bit compact message identifier cannot provide, at the cost of frame bytes; and over AES-GCM for constant-time software implementation on targets without AES hardware.

4.2 An encrypted payload shall be the envelope: nonce (24 bytes) || ciphertext || tag (16 bytes), nonce freshly drawn from the CSPRNG per frame. The 40-byte overhead is accepted; deployments for which it dominates use public-by-design events (L5, 7.3.1) or the extended tier.

4.3 The associated data of the AEAD shall be the frame's authenticated span as Layer 4, 10.2.1 defines it, excluding the payload itself. PROVISIONAL(SS35) — binds ciphertext to origin, identifier, and target without double-covering the payload.

4.4 Per Layer 5, 7.3.3, no cipher identifier is carried: the Layer 4 version bits are the algorithm epoch (L4, 4.1.3 Note 1), and this suite is the suite of version 0.

4.5 Key derivation, rotation, and distribution remain Layer 5's (5.2.2 and Clause 6); this document names only the cipher those keys drive, and the purpose strings that separate them (Clause 4a).

4a Key-derivation purpose strings (settles SS39)

4a.1 Layer 5, 5.2.1 fixes the derivation (HKDF-SHA256, 32-byte output, secret as input keying material, public binding as salt, purpose string as info) and 5.2.3 forbids two derivations sharing a purpose string — but states no values, so two conformant implementations derive different keys and get silence. The values are fixed here. PROVISIONAL(SS39) — the scheme, not merely the strings, is open to replacement until ratified.

4a.2 A purpose string shall be the exact ASCII byte sequence given below, with no terminator and no padding:

Purpose String
group payload protection r2/v0/group/payload
group integrity (Layer 4 tag key) r2/v0/group/integrity
entanglement payload protection r2/v0/entanglement/payload
entanglement integrity r2/v0/entanglement/integrity
enrolment ceremony session r2/v0/ceremony/session
sealed storage of derived keys r2/v0/storage/seal

4a.3 The v0 field is the Layer 4 version — the algorithm epoch (L4, 4.1.3 Note 1) — and a new epoch takes a new set of strings, never a reused one.

4a.4 A purpose string not in this table shall not be used with the derivation of Layer 5, 5.2.1. A new derivation is added by adding a row here, under a name that states its use.

Note 1 to entry: The strings read as paths on purpose. A purpose string is a namespace, and the failure it prevents is silent: two derivations sharing one string yield the same key for different jobs, which no test detects and no party observes until the compromise of one exposes the other.

4b The CBOR profile (settles a gap found implementing L4 Clause 11)

4b.1 Layer 4, 11.1 requires definite-length CBOR and 11.2 fixes the type set. This clause pins what those leave open, because two of the gaps are signature-affecting and one is a remote crash on a constrained device. PROVISIONAL(SS95).

4b.2 Deterministic encoding. Where any clause of this standard requires a canonical encoding — notably 5.2, whose signature is computed over one — the encoding shall be:

a) definite lengths everywhere, for every string, array and map (already 11.1);

b) integers and lengths in the shortest form that represents the value;

c) map keys sorted by their encoded bytes, compared lexicographically as byte strings, shortest-first where one is a prefix of the other;

d) floats in the shortest width that represents the value exactly, and never narrowed where narrowing would change it;

e) no tags (4b.4), and no duplicate keys (4b.3).

4b.2a Deterministic decoding. Where a clause requires the deterministic encoding, a decoder shall reject input that is not in it — an integer or length not in shortest form, a map whose keys are not in the order 4b.2 c) requires, or any indefinite length.

4b.2b A decoder shall not accept such input and canonicalise it.

Note 0 to entry: 4b.2 binds the encoder; without 4b.2a nothing binds the decoder, and a canonical form enforced in one direction only leaves the other free to disagree. The consequence is not cosmetic where a signature is involved (5.2): if a verifier accepts a non-canonical encoding, then one logical map has two byte forms, and either the signature fails on a form nobody intended to reject, or the verifier canonicalises first and verifies over bytes that were never signed. Rejecting is the only reading that keeps one content, one encoding, one signature true in both directions. (Added 2026-08-02 after an implementation found it had enforced its canonical rule on encode and never on decode; PROVISIONAL(supervisor).)

4b.3 Duplicate map keys. A decoder shall reject a map containing two entries with the same key.

4b.3a A decoder shall not resolve a duplicate-key conflict by taking the first entry, the last entry, or either.

4b.4 Tags. A decoder shall reject a payload containing a tag (CBOR major type 6). Layer 4, 11.2's list is exhaustive for this standard, not a floor.

4b.5 Nesting depth. A decoder shall reject a payload nested more deeply than its stated limit.

4b.5a A decoder shall state its nesting limit.

4b.5b That limit shall be at least 8. PROVISIONAL(SS95) — the floor, not the ceiling.

Note 1 to entry: "Canonical CBOR" names at least three incompatible things in the wild — RFC 7049 canonical, RFC 8949 deterministic, and CTAP2 all differ — so a clause requiring "the canonical encoding" without saying which pins nothing at all. 4b.2 is RFC 8949's deterministic profile stated in full rather than by reference, so an implementer needs no other document. The failure it prevents is SS39's and SS91's: two conformant implementations sign different bytes for the same content, reject each other's packages, and nothing observable says why — here meaning a device cannot be updated by half the fleet.

Note 2 to entry: 4b.3 rejects rather than resolving because every resolution is exploitable. Two entries for one key, a signer that reads the first and a verifier that reads the last, and one signed document means two different things to the two parties — the classic CBOR attack. Rejection is the only rule with no exploitable side, which is the reasoning of 01-terminology.md 5.1 applied to a parser.

Note 3 to entry: 4b.5 exists because this standard's own target devices make it a security matter rather than a robustness one: a recursive-descent decoder on a microcontroller with a few hundred kilobytes of memory is a remote crash without a depth limit, and the attacker's cost is a few bytes of nesting. A limit belongs in the profile rather than in each implementer's judgement, because an implementer who omits it has written something that looks correct.

5 Update package (settles SS37)

5.1 An update package shall be one definite-length CBOR map with integer keys:

Key Type Field
1 uint package format version; this document specifies 0
2 tstr target class, exactly as the platform declaration states it
3 uint per-target monotonic sequence (L6, 5.2.3)
4 bstr image bytes
5 bstr SHA-256 of the image bytes
6 map self-test specification (L6, 3.4): keys PROVISIONAL(SS37)
7 array signatures
8 uint content class (5.1a)

PROVISIONAL(SS37) — the envelope; keys 1–5, 7 and 8 are structural, key 6's interior awaits the health-check contract.

5.1a Key 8 shall state the content class the package delivers, from the classes Layer 6, 5.1.1 requires: 1 the stack, 2 modules alongside it, 3 declarative definitions, 4 parameters, and 0 the whole image, where the platform collapses the classes into one image (Layer 6, 5.1.2). PROVISIONAL(supervisor) — the key number and the enumeration; the classes themselves are the part's.

5.1b A package stating no content class shall be rejected, never treated as carrying the whole image.

Note 1 to entry: Layer 6, 5.1.1 makes the four classes independently deliverable, and their costs differ — a parameter change touches a running value, a stack change reboots the device (5.1.1 Note 1). Before 5.1a the map could not say which one it carried, so a receiver could not decide what an update would cost it, and a hive that separates the classes had no field to separate them by. Found by an implementer re-examining a register row rather than implementing it: defining the class in the crate would have satisfied the row and produced a classification that never crosses a wire. (CORE-11, SS257.)

Note 2 to entry: The default in 5.1b is restrictive for the same reason as 5.2a and 5.2b: 0 is the widest class in the enumeration, so reading an absent key as 0 would let a package that names nothing be treated as authorised to replace everything. The polarity rule of 01-terminology.md 5.1 decides it, and an absent key is exactly where a fold selects the permissive branch by accident.

5.2 Each entry of key 7 shall be [signer identity (32-byte public key), Ed25519 signature] over the deterministic CBOR encoding (4b.2) of the map containing every key except key 7.

5.2c A package shall be accepted only where a signature verifies under an update authority the receiving hive recognises (L6, 4.1.2).

5.2a A package whose key 7 is absent or empty shall be rejected.

5.2d A package none of whose signatures verifies shall be rejected. An empty signature list is not a satisfied requirement.

5.2b A self-test specification (key 6) that is absent or states no check shall be treated as a self-test that did not pass, never as one that passed vacuously.

Note 0 to entry: 5.2 said keys 1–6 only until 2026-08-02, which was correct while the map ended at key 6 and silently wrong the moment a key was added: key 8 selects what a package is permitted to replace, and a field that selects behaviour outside the signature is a field an attacker edits in flight. Stated as a rule about the map rather than a list of numbers, so the next key added is covered by construction rather than by somebody remembering. (PROVISIONAL(supervisor).)

Note 1 to entry: 5.2a and 5.2b state the same trap twice because it wears different clothes each time. A verifier written as every signature verifies returns true for no signatures; a health gate written as every check passed returns true for no checks. In both cases the code is natural, the test suite is green, and the result is that the least verified package in existence is the one that passes most easily. The polarity rule of 01-terminology.md 5.1 decides it — absent evidence selects the restrictive branch — but a fold over an empty collection quietly selects the permissive one, so the clauses say it outright. (Added 2026-08-02 after the same shape was found in a deployed flashing path; PROVISIONAL(supervisor).)

5.3 A package is carried as chunks under the Layer 6, 5.3.5 chunked-transfer model; the package format is independent of its carriage.

5.4 The sequence record a hive keeps per target (Layer 6, 5.2.3a) shall be:

Offset Size Field
0 4 magic 0x52 0x32 0x53 0x51 (R2SQ)
4 1 record format version; this document specifies 0
5 1 target identifier length n
6 n target identifier, as the package's key 2 states it
6+n 8 sequence, unsigned, big-endian
14+n 4 CRC-32 of bytes 0 to 13+n inclusive

PROVISIONAL(SS53) — widths and the CRC choice.

5.4a The record shall carry two sequences, not one: the sequence of the image the hive is running, and the sequence of an image staged and not yet committed, each with an explicit presence flag. The floor of Layer 6, 5.2.3 is the running sequence alone; a pending sequence is never a floor. Record format version 1. PROVISIONAL(SS109) — the shape, from an implementation that reached it by building the full cycle.

Note 1 to entry: One number cannot distinguish staged n from running n, and the difference is the brick of L6 5.2.3c Note 1 — a device that reverts beneath its own floor. Absence is not zero in either field: a record that has only ever staged reads as no running sequence, not as floor zero, which is 5.5's three-way distinction applied within the record rather than to it.

5.5 A record whose magic, version or CRC does not verify is unreadable, and Layer 6, 5.2.3b applies: no value from it is a sequence floor. A hive shall be able to report the distinction between no record, unreadable record, and sequence n — the three are different facts and the middle one is the dangerous one to lose.

5a Revocation entry (settles a gap found implementing L5 6.2)

5a.1 The bytes a revocation entry's signature (Layer 5, 6.2.2) covers shall be, in order: subject (32 bytes, the revoked certificate's member public key) || epoch (8 bytes, unsigned, big-endian) || sequence (8 bytes, unsigned, big-endian) || reason (1 byte: 0 compromise, 1 eviction, 2 retirement, 3 superseded). PROVISIONAL(SS91).

5a.2 The signature itself is not covered. The advisory absolute time of Layer 5, 6.2.2 is not covered.

Note 1 to entry: Excluding the advisory time is deliberate twice over: signing an operator's clock would let that clock change an entry's identity, and 6.2.2 already bars any decision from reading it — a field nothing may act on has no business inside a signature. Covering the reason is equally deliberate: Layer 5, 8.2 grades the grace period by reason, so a substitutable reason would let a compromise eviction — which carries zero grace — be downgraded to one that does not.

Note 2 to entry: Without this clause every implementation invents the byte order, which is SS39's failure mode with a worse consequence: two conformant implementations reject each other's revocations, so an evicted member stays a member on half the mesh, permanently, and 6.2.1's never-pruned set means nothing ever corrects it. (Layout proposed by the core lane while fixing the 6.2.2b defect, and offered as a starting point rather than a claim.)

5b Persona disposition record (a conforming construction for L6 5.5.3a)

5b.1 This clause is informative. Layer 6, 5.5.3a states the properties a persona disposition must have; a platform whose durable storage offers an atomic transition of its own may satisfy them by other means. Nothing in 5b is a conformance requirement, and an implementation is not less conformant for choosing a different construction.

5b.2 The construction is two records of identical layout, each holding: a generation (unsigned, monotonically increasing), the disposition it records, and a validating field over everything before it. A reader takes the complete record of highest generation as selected; a record whose validating field does not check is not complete and is not a candidate. Where neither record is complete, Layer 6, 5.5.4 applies and the device is nobody's.

5b.3 A writer targets whichever record is not currently selected, writes it with a generation one above the selected one, and writes its validating field last. The selected disposition therefore changes at the instant the validating field completes — the transition is the completion of the checksum, not the start of the write — so there is no instant at which a partial state is the selected one, which is the property Layer 6, 5.5.3 requires.

Note 1 to entry: The construction is testable by walking every prefix of the write — truncate the write at each byte offset in turn and assert that a complete disposition is still selected at each. That control is the reason this construction is offered rather than merely described: it distinguishes a layout that is crash-safe from one whose author intended it to be. (Contributed and tested by the core lane; recorded here as a starting point, not a claim, on the same footing as 5a.)

5b.4 An implementation of this construction should state its acceptance conditions in full — each condition, in order, at the point where the record is parsed — and should state which fields the validating field covers and which conditions are checked separately on top of it. Layer 6, 5.5.1 guarantees a second reader written independently (5.5.3a Note 3), and 01-terminology.md 5.4b is what makes the two comparable.

Note 2 to entry: Nothing here says where the records live, and deliberately so — erase granularity, wear levelling and partition layout are platform facts, and Layer 6, 5.5.1 requires the recovery layer and the stack to share no code, not to share no storage. What they must agree on is this record's layout, which is why it is written down.

5c Firmware identity (settles SS133 / CORE-1)

5c.1 The firmware identity of Layer 6, 6.1.3 shall be one definite-length CBOR map with integer keys:

Key Type Field
1 tstr target class, as key 2 of the running image's package (5.1)
2 uint per-target sequence, as key 3 of that package
3 bstr SHA-256 of the image bytes, as key 5 of that package

PROVISIONAL(SS133).

5c.2 A key whose value the hive cannot determine shall be absent from the map.

5c.2a An absent key means this hive cannot state this value, and shall not be encoded as zero, as an empty string, or as any other placeholder.

Note 1 to entry: The three fields are the package's own identifying keys, so the answer is directly comparable with the package an operator holds — which is the only question a firmware identity is asked. A free-text version banner answers a different question and answers it differently on every platform.

Note 2 to entry: 5c.2 is 01-terminology.md 5.3 at a public reporting surface, and the harm of the alternative is concrete: a hive whose image position is unreadable (Layer 6, 5.4.2 Note 0) cannot state a sequence, and a substituted 0 reads as the first package ever — an operator comparing it against their own record concludes the device is catastrophically behind, and the repair they attempt is the wrong one. Absent is a fact; zero is a lie with a plausible interpretation.

6 Capability-contract registry (settles SS36, SS38; frames Q32's home)

6.1 A capability contract names, for one capability identifier: the descriptor a provider publishes, the events it consumes and emits with their parameter schemas (CBOR, L4 Clause 11), and the semantics an implementer cannot infer from the schema. A contract shall state all three; a capability identifier without a contract in this registry is deployment-local and carries no interop claim.

6.1b Each entry of this registry shall state all three parts of 6.1 — descriptor, events with their schemas, and the semantics not inferable from the schema — or state explicitly that a part is deployment-local and why.

Note 1 to entry: 6.1b is a check on this registry rather than on an implementation, because the registry failed it: 6.3 as first drafted stated events and semantics well and named its descriptor only obliquely, while 6.2 next door named a full core set. The gap was found by an implementer binding the contract, not by anyone reading it — which is the argument for stating the check rather than trusting the pattern. (Added 2026-08-02 on composer-lane evidence.)

6.1a Registering a contract claims the name. A contract's entry claims its identifier and the meaning stated for it, and two contracts shall not claim semantically distinct meanings under one term. Where two uses of a term differ in meaning, they take different terms, however natural the shared word.

Note 1 to entry: This is a naming rule in a document otherwise about bytes, and it earns its place because the failure it prevents is invisible to every mechanism the standard has. Classes at both scales share one 32-bit FNV space with no central registry (R2-CAP.md 3.1a — superseded corpus, cited for provenance; see Note 0 to 7.2), so a near-miss spelling or a cross-scale collision fails silently — and a semantic collision does not fail at all: it delivers, matches, and means the wrong thing. The live instance that prompted the rule: one deployed catalogue carries a class named for presence meaning a node's network announcement, and another named for presence meaning a human detected by radar. They hash distinctly, so nothing breaks today; an operator or an agent searching the term finds both and cannot tell which is which. Cheap to state now, unfixable in the field later. (Added 2026-08-02 on composer-lane evidence.)

6.2 Display — ai.reality2.cap.display. PROVISIONAL(SS36), corrected to the composer-lane catalogue truth (review 2026-08-01): the descriptor core set is {kind, width, height, colour, text_rows} — width/height are pixel dimensions and text_rows is a separate capacity axis (a 160×80 colour panel has pixel dims and a text-row capacity a sentant sizes against); there is no addressing field. The command set is CMD_RENDER_FIELDS, CMD_CLEAR, CMD_RENDER_TEXT, CMD_RENDER_RAW, plus an optional descriptor-gated backlight operation (allowed only where descriptor.backlight != 0; power-cut stays driver-local). The load-bearing semantic the contract states: the severity adaptation rule — semantic severity in, presentation out: colour on colour panels, glyph/prefix on mono, top-N on row-limited displays. Reference instance: DFR1195 = ST7735S (inferred), 160×80, RGB565.

6.3 Presence/occupancy — ai.reality2.cap.presence. PROVISIONAL(SS38). Descriptor core set: {range_max, decay} — range_max the sensing envelope in metres, decay in seconds (below); optionally field_of_view in degrees, and multi_subject stating whether the provider can distinguish more than one subject. Events: events presence.enter, presence.leave, and periodic presence.standing, each carrying {distance: float (metres), confidence?: float 0–1} — confidence is optional and provider-derived (a plugin may synthesize it, and a mandatory field would force the first implementation to invent a number). Vendor unit conversion is the plugin's job. Absence is declared by presence.leave or by decay — a consumer shall treat a standing state older than the declared decay interval (descriptor field, seconds) as unknown, never as absence.

Note 1 to entry: range_max is what makes presence.leave interpretable — an argument that survives its grounding, and is reasoned rather than measured (Note 3), having been labelled as bench evidence when it was not. On a six-metre sensor, leave means a subject left a small room; on a twelve-metre one the same event may mean they walked to the far side of a space they are still in. A consumer asking "is the room empty" cannot answer it from the event alone — distance says where a subject is, never where the sensor stops seeing — so the envelope belongs in the descriptor, published once, rather than being guessed per event. multi_subject exists for the same reason: a consumer counting occupants against a provider that cannot separate them is wrong in a way no payload reveals. (Added 2026-08-02 on composer-lane evidence from binding this contract.)

Note 2 to entry: decay is a declared interval, not a measured sensor property. A provider derives it from its configured reporting cadence and says so, rather than asserting a figure about hardware it cannot support.

Note 3 to entry: This contract has no bench grounding, and its first draft claimed one. It was written against an ensemble built for a part identified as an 80 GHz presence radar; the part is a liquid-level sensor (DFRobot SEN0676, distance to a surface in millimetres), so every sentence here that rested on it — the grounding claim, and a note reasoning about what is or is not a measured register fact of that part — was reasoning about the wrong device. The contract's own arguments stand on their own: range_max makes leave interpretable, multi_subject prevents a consumer counting occupants a provider cannot separate, decay is declared rather than measured. What has been withdrawn is the evidence, not the design — and until a presence part is actually bound, this contract is drafted and ungrounded, which is a weaker and truer status than it carried before.

One part of the design does not survive, and the lane that supplied the evidence found it: a distance reading has no absent state to decay into. The decay-to-unknown rule and the presence.leave event were reasoned from a part that returns a number for a surface that is always there, so they were never modelled on the behaviour they describe. They remain right for a presence sensor on the argument above and on no observation, and the first implementer to bind a real presence part should expect to correct them. (Corrected 2026-08-02 on the supervisor's hardware brief and the composer lane's own retraction; findings SS159, SS164.)

6.4 Scalar environment — ai.reality2.cap.env.scalar is the existing catalogue form for single-value sensors and is registered as-is: {value: float, unit: tstr}. PROVISIONAL(SS38).

Note 1 to entry: The mandatory unit is load-bearing and there is now a measured instance of what it prevents. The bench part's own register map states one register in centimetres and two in millimetres — a configuration value and the readings derived from it — with nothing in any reading announcing which. A contract carrying the value without the unit cannot be wrong loudly: a consumer combining a centimetre configuration with a millimetre reading produces an answer that is off by ten, plausible, and silent. Vendor register maps are not internally consistent about units, so the unit travels with the value.

Note 2 to entry: This is the contract the bench part actually needs. It reports distance to a surface, not occupancy — see 6.3 Note 3 — so a level or distance ensemble is env.scalar, and the presence contract's binding is still owed by whichever part first provides one.

7 Definition and score formats (settles Q32)

7.1 The definition format (a sentant's behaviour as data, Layer 7) and the score format (an ensemble's parts and their conversation, Layer 7, 7.2) are specified in this clause. PROVISIONAL(supervisor) throughout: distilled 2026-08-02 from the imported corpus of 7.2, with every departure from that corpus named at the clause that departs.

Note 0 to 7.2: Every R2-* document cited anywhere in this companion is SUPERSEDED CORPUS, never canon. Stated here as a test rather than as a list, because a test disposes of the citations nobody enumerated: per d041 — Roy, standard is the canon — a sentence you can find ONLY in an old-corpus document is not a requirement and never was one. Cite them for provenance; do not build from them. (Written after a peer lane found that a ruling phrased about DIRECTORIES made it search directories and miss the sibling class entirely: a ruling's noun shapes the search that applies it.)

7.2 The substance is imported rather than reinvented: corpus R2-DEF.md at spec DEF 0.14 is held verbatim with the editing record, and is identified by its provenance rather than by where it is kept — r2-specifications commit 3932727, 1811 lines, sha256 prefix 8ecb5df83b24864f, verified 2026-08-01 and recomputed on every run of the requirements gate since 2026-08-02. R2-CAP.md 0.9 is available from the same commit on ask — as corpus, under Note 0, not as a document anything may be built from.

Note 1 to entry: What this clause is not. The imported corpus is a working document with examples, deprecations, rationale and open questions. This clause carries the obligations; the examples stay in the import. Where the import states a behaviour without stating who is obliged, this clause names the party — and where it states two things in one sentence, they are two requirements here.

7.3 Two rules the deployed history proves load-bearing

(composer lane, d013 evidence.)

7.3.1 A definition or score validator shall reject a document carrying a field no schema defines.

7.3.2 A consumer meeting an unresolvable or unrecognised versioned reference (name@ver) shall fail with an error.

7.3.3 Such a consumer shall not substitute an absent value.

Note 1 to entry: Of ten deployed scores exactly one drifted, carrying fields no schema defines. A validator without rejection accumulates deployment-local dialect silently — and the drifted file becomes the model the next author copies. Note 2 to entry: a pre-migration consumer once silently emitted empty values for every migrated entry, caught only by a negative control; every ref-shape migration pairs with a nothing-emitted-empty regression gate (01-terminology.md 4.2).

7.4 The definition document

7.4.1 A sentant definition shall carry a name and a description, each a non-empty string, and at least one automation.

7.4.2 A sentant definition shall carry a class: a non-empty UTF-8 string declaring the sentant's type convention, in the reverse-DNS form Layer 2, 5.5 hashes.

Note 1 to entry: This was drafted as a should and the part it serves says shall. Layer 7, 4.2.1 requires a definition to carry automations, class, public events, plugin bindings and initial vars; the imported corpus marks class RECOMMENDED and keeps class-less definitions valid, and this clause followed the import over the part it is a companion to. Where a companion and a part disagree, the part is right — and the practical consequence is not cosmetic: Layer 2's discovery and 5.5's class hash have nothing to name for a class-less sentant. (Corrected 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), finding SS209, raised by an independent review.)

7.4.2a A sentant definition shall carry a public_events array, each entry the name of an event the sentant declares as its accepted external interface.

7.4.2b A sentant definition shall carry a plugins array, each entry the name of a plugin the definition binds.

Note 2 to entry: 7.4.2a and 7.4.2b close an omission that made a Layer 7 requirement unwritable. Layer 7, 4.2.1 obliges a definition to carry automations, class, public events, plugin bindings and initial vars; this clause set provided no obligation and no key for either, and 7.3.1 rejects a field no schema defines — so an author who wrote one was refused, and one who did not was conformant with a document Layer 7 forbids. Layer 7, 5.2.1 — which makes a sentant's public events its declared external contract and requires its hive to advertise exactly those — had no source document at all, and Layer 2's advertisement had nothing to carry. An empty array is a declaration of no public events and is not the same as the key being absent (01-terminology.md 5.1). The sharpest part of the finding: Note 1 above quotes that list of five and acts on one of them. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor); findings F2 and F3 of the Clause 7 pass, D-137.)

7.4.3 A definition's data map, where present, shall have string keys.

7.4.3a The values of that map shall be strings, numbers, booleans, arrays, or maps of the same.

7.4.3b A hive shall initialise a sentant's variables from its data map at instantiation.

Note 1a to entry: 7.4.3b names what data IS. Layer 7, 4.2.1 obliges a definition to carry initial vars and Layer 7, 3.2 defines vars; this clause set carried a data map and never said the two were the same thing, so Layer 7's requirement had no carrier and 7.7.1a resolved templates against the sentant's variables — a term bound to no field in this document. The imported corpus states the binding twice; the field came across and the sentence that made it usable did not, which is the distillation mechanism registered as SS233. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor); finding F4, D-137.)

7.4.4 A definition shall declare its storage disposition as exactly one of volatile (neither definition nor state survives a restart), durable (the definition survives a restart; the sentant starts fresh), or durable-state (both survive a restart; the sentant resumes from its last snapshot), and a definition stating none is volatile.

7.4.4a A hive shall not retain a definition or state declared volatile across a restart, whatever the platform's storage makes possible.

7.4.5 A hive performing a sentant declared durable-state shall tolerate the loss of state written since its last snapshot.

Note 0 to entry: This clause said power cycle where Layer 7, 4.2.2 says restart, and the difference is not a synonym. A warm restart is a restart and not a power cycle, so the earlier wording permitted a volatile definition to survive one — by omission, not by statement. The part carries no warm-or-cold qualifier and the companion's silence cannot widen it (this document's own front matter: where it conflicts with a ratified part, the part is right). 7.4.4a states the consequence as an obligation rather than leaving it here: where a platform makes warm-restart survival physically possible — a non-volatile store that is not cleared — that is a platform property to discard, not to expose, because otherwise two conformant hives disagree about whether a volatile sentant is still there. (This note carried that as an obligation until the gate refused it — a requirement in a note is invisible to every register, SS222 — and the word for it is avoided here because the denominator counts the token wherever it appears, including in a sentence about the token.) (Corrected 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), on the first of the sixteen findings held at D-127; ruled by the supervisor against L7 4.2.2 read byte-exact.)

Note 1 to entry: Storage disposition and eviction are different questions and the words sound alike. Storage is about surviving a restart; eviction is about surviving inactivity (Layer 7's lifecycle). The combination durable plus evictable is the one constrained devices want: many definitions on flash, instantiated only when an event arrives for them, with the hive acting as a lazy loader. Note 2 to entry: 7.4.5 is stated because snapshots are periodic and a hard power cut lands between them — an author needing exact recovery checkpoints explicitly, by event, and a clause that implied otherwise would be promising what no platform delivers (01-terminology.md 5.4).

7.5 Automations

7.5.1 An automation shall carry a name unique within its sentant, and an array of transitions.

7.5.2 A transition shall name the event it matches.

7.5.2a The wildcard shall be the string *.

7.5.2b The initial marker shall be the string start.

7.5.3 A loader shall take an automation's set of states to be the union of the from and to values of its transitions, excluding the wildcard and the initial marker.

7.5.3a A from or to value of * shall have the same effect as omitting it.

7.5.4 A loader shall take the initial state to be the to value of a transition whose from is the initial marker, and where no such transition exists, the first state named in definition order.

7.5.5 A transition stating no from shall match in any state.

7.5.5a A transition stating no to shall leave the automation in the state it was in.

7.5.6 A hive receiving an event for which the current state has no matching transition shall discard it, without error and without queueing, whatever the event's source.

7.5.7 A hive shall hold at most one pending delayed event per event name per sentant.

7.5.7a A later delayed send of that event name shall replace the pending one.

7.5.8 A hive shall not carry a pending delayed event across a restart.

Note 0 to entry: 7.5.2a and 7.5.3a pin two values this clause set named and never stated. 7.5.3 excluded the wildcard and the initial marker from a state set, and 7.5.4 took the initial state from a transition whose from is the initial marker — and no clause said what either of them looks like, so two implementations could pick different strings and both satisfy every obligation here. The imported corpus pins both, * and start, and the values did not come across with the rules that use them (SS233). 7.5.3a is the second half of the same defect: 7.5.5 and 7.5.5a covered a transition stating no from or to, while the corpus's own examples write * explicitly — under 7.5.3 alone that is a state named *. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor); finding F13, D-138.)

Note 1 to entry: 7.5.6 is a decision, not an omission. Silent discard means an event arriving in the wrong state is indistinguishable from one nobody sent — which is what makes the four transition patterns composable, and which is also why an author who needs to know sets up a transition to observe it. Stated here because an implementer meeting an unmatched event and no rule would reasonably invent a dead-letter queue, and two hives would then disagree about whether an event was delivered. Note 2 to entry: 7.5.7 gives cancellation without a cancel verb — replace the timer, or move to a state that discards the event when it fires. Note 3 to entry: 7.5.8 is what Layer 0, 5.2 already implies for any interval spanning a power cycle; it is repeated here because the alternative reading — a timer restored at boot — fires an event whose originating state no longer exists.

7.6 Actions

7.6.1 An action shall name exactly one of a command from the set send, set, debug, test, or a plugin with the command to invoke on it.

7.6.2 A hive shall execute a transition's actions in the order written, passing to each action the accumulated parameter map, so that a value set by an earlier action is visible to a later one.

7.6.3 A send action shall name the event to send, and where it names no target the event goes to the sending sentant.

7.6.4 A set action shall name the key it writes.

7.6.4a A set action stating no value shall clear that variable.

7.6.5 A test action shall name the condition it evaluates.

7.6.5a A test action shall execute its true branch where the condition holds and its false branch where it does not.

7.6.6 A plugin invocation shall be delivered to the plugin as an event.

7.6.6a A plugin invocation shall not appear on the wire.

7.6.6b A plugin invocation shall not be observable by another sentant.

7.6.7 A plugin invocation shall return no value to the action pipeline.

7.6.7a A plugin reporting a result shall do so by sending an event to the invoking sentant, handled by a transition of its own.

7.6.8 A definition shall not state where a plugin runs or how it is implemented.

7.6.9 A hive shall not terminate a sentant because an action failed at run time.

7.6.9a A hive shall skip a failed action and perform the state change the transition states.

Note 1 to entry: 7.6.6 was drafted the wrong way round and Layer 7 decides it. Layer 7, 6.1.1 requires a plugin to interact with its hive and with sentants through events alone; the imported corpus says a plugin invocation is not an event, and this clause followed the import over the part — the second time in one clause set (7.4.2 was the first). What the corpus was actually asserting is invisibility, not a different mechanism: an invocation does not reach the wire and no other sentant observes it, which is 7.6.6a and 7.6.6b, stated separately and unchanged. The interaction itself is an event in both directions, and the only thing that crosses back is an ordinary event. This is what makes a definition portable — the same definition runs where a capability is a local driver and where it is a remote service, which is 7.6.8. Note 2 to entry: fire-and-forget is the load-bearing half of 7.6.7. The action chain does not wait, so an author expresses I expect a result by writing the transition that consumes it, and expresses and if it never comes with a delayed event (7.5.7). A clause promising a return value would oblige every hive to block a state machine on a remote service. Note 3 to entry: 7.6.9 keeps a definition error from taking a device down; it is deliberately not silence — Layer 7's reporting obligations still apply, and 01-terminology.md 5.3 governs the case where the hive cannot tell whether the action ran.

7.7 Parameter templating

7.7.1 A template expression shall be written {{ … }}.

7.7.1a A template expression shall name its source as one of params (the incoming event's parameters), vars (the sentant's variables), event (the triggering event's name), or sender (the sender's name).

7.7.2 A template expression shall resolve nested values by dot notation.

7.7.3 An implementation of this standard shall not accept the legacy __name__ substitution form.

Note 0 to entry: 7.7.1a listed four sources and required none of them to be named, so a bare {{x}} was permitted to resolve by searching them in some order — which is exactly the behaviour 7.7.3 forbids the legacy form for, in the note below, under a different syntax. The prohibition's own reason was not implemented by the clause it accompanies. The imported corpus avoids it by namespacing every root; the roots did not come across with the rule that needs them (SS233). (Retexted 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor); finding F14, D-138.)

Note 1 to entry: 7.7.3 is a departure from the imported corpus, and it is deliberate. That corpus deprecates the legacy form and keeps it for compatibility. The justification as first written — this standard has no deployed base to be compatible with — was false, and it contradicted this document two pages earlier, where 7.3.1's note counts ten deployed scores. The deployed base is real; what this standard has is no compatibility obligation to it, because an implementation built from this text is a new target and the corpus's own transition record keeps the old form working for the implementations that already read it. Carrying two resolution orders — parameters, then variables, then data — into a new implementation would make the first bug in this area undiagnosable. And the claim that an importer rewrites the old form mechanically was false, twice stated and wrong both times: the legacy form resolves dynamically at run time — a bare name means the event parameter where that key is present and the variable otherwise — so no single static replacement preserves it, and the new syntax requires the author to name a source. The migration is semantic, not mechanical: each occurrence needs a decision about which source it meant, and a converter can find them but cannot resolve them. Stated because a departure that under-states its cost is a departure nobody budgets for. (PROVISIONAL(supervisor); a ruling that reverses this restores a rule nobody has yet implemented, which is the cheap direction.)

7.8 The score document

7.8.1 A score shall carry a name, a description, a version naming the version of its content, a schema version naming the version of this clause it targets, and at least one part.

7.8.1b A score shall carry a class, in the same reverse-DNS form as 7.4.2.

Note 1 to entry: Layer 7, 7.1.3 requires an ensemble to have a name, a class and a version, and this clause carried name and version and not class. The score is the document by which an ensemble is authored and distributed, so a score that cannot state the ensemble's class cannot express what Layer 7 requires the ensemble to have. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), finding SS209.)

7.8.1a The keys of a score shall be spelled exactly: name, description, class, version, schema_version, parts, and — where present — plugins, registrations, capabilities, trust_group, required_transports, compile_target, signatures, unsigned.

7.8.1e The keys of a part shall be spelled exactly: name, description, class, storage, data, plugins, public_events, automations, include, runtime_executable.

Note 1 to entry: 7.3.1 rejects a field no schema defines, so an unpinned spelling is not a stylistic question — it is an interoperability failure with a refusal at the end of it. Two implementations that agree on every rule and disagree on one key name reject each other's documents, and each is conformant. Note 2 to entry: This clause departs from the imported corpus in three names and the mapping is stated rather than left to be discovered: the corpus writes ensemble where this clause writes score, sentants where this clause writes parts, and ensemble_version where this clause writes schema_version. The renamings follow Layer 7's vocabulary — ensemble is the running thing and score is the document describing it — and schema_version says what the field holds, which ensemble_version does not. An importer maps the three names mechanically; a document carrying the old spellings is refused by 7.3.1 rather than silently accepted, which is the behaviour that makes the departure visible instead of ambiguous. Note 2a to entry: mechanically is true of one document and understates a tree. Measured 2026-08-04 in a catalogue holding seventeen scores: all seventeen carry all three old spellings, and the change reaches the manifest index and anything targeting a catalogue path as well as the documents themselves — so it is a migration rather than an edit, and one that lands on entries lanes may be flashing against. The renaming is not reopened by this; what is recorded is that the cost sits with the holder of the catalogue rather than with the importer. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), finding SS208, raised by an independent review of this clause.)

7.8.2 A score's part shall be either an inline definition or a reference to a definition document.

7.8.2a A part given by reference shall be validated as though written inline.

7.8.2b A part given by reference shall carry the key include, whose value is a versioned reference of the form name@version.

Note 0 to entry: include was admitted as a part key and defined nowhere. 7.8.2 said a part is either an inline definition or a reference to a definition document without naming the key or its syntax, while 7.3.1 rejects a field no schema defines — so the schema named a field it did not define. The name@version form is the one 7.3.2 already refuses to resolve loosely. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor); finding F16, D-138.)

7.8.3 A score shall declare each part's runtime executable disposition, and a part stating none is runtime-executable.

7.8.4 A hive shall not load a part declared not runtime-executable.

7.8.5 Plugin names shall be unique within a score.

7.8.5a A hive shall namespace an ensemble-owned plugin by its score's name.

7.8.6 A score's registrations map shall key each entry by the name of the hive-shared plugin it registers with.

7.8.6a The value of such an entry shall be that plugin's own registration payload.

7.8.7 A hive loading a score shall register each entry of 7.8.6 with the named plugin for as long as the score is present.

7.8.7a A hive shall unregister those entries when the score is unloaded.

7.8.8 A hive shall refuse a registration that conflicts with one already held by another score.

7.8.9 A score's capabilities map, where present, shall state the events its parts emit and consume.

7.8.9a A hive shall aggregate those events into what Layer 2, Clause 8 advertises.

7.8.10 A score's trust group constraints, where present, shall be satisfied by the hive's trust-group context before the score is loaded.

7.8.11 A score's required transports, where present, shall name transports that are to be in service before the score is performed.

7.8.12 An implementation shall not depend on the order in which a score's fields are written.

Note 1 to entry: 7.8.3 and 7.8.4 exist because a score may govern a build. A part that is a contract for a builder to realise and a part that is runtime-loadable are different things, and before this the grammar could not say which one it was holding. The default is runtime-executable because that is what the great majority are; the non-default is the one declared, which is the direction that fails safe — a build contract loaded by mistake is a worse outcome than a loadable part left unbuilt. Note 2 to entry: 7.8.8's conflicts are the measured ones — two scores claiming the same route prefix, or declaring types that collide in a stitched schema. Note 3 to entry: 7.8.9 is the score's half of Layer 2's advertisement; a score that lies here does not gain reach, because Layer 2, 5.5.3 already refuses a class string learned from a hive that is not its own.

7.9 Signing a score

7.8.1c A score shall be written in YAML 1.2, JSON, or TOML.

7.8.1d A loader shall map a score's scalars to the CBOR types of Clause 4b before any signature is computed or verified.

7.9.1 A score distributed across a trust group shall carry at least one signature by a key-holding member of that group.

7.9.2 A signature shall be computed over the canonical serialisation of the score with the signature field and its value excluded.

7.9.3 The canonical serialisation of 7.9.2 shall be the deterministic encoding of Clause 4b over the score's parsed content.

7.9.4 A hive shall not load an unsigned score unless that score carries an explicit declaration that it is unsigned.

7.9.4a A hive shall reject a score whose signature field is merely absent.

7.9.4b The declaration of 7.9.4 shall be the key unsigned with the value true.

7.9.1a Each entry of signatures shall carry exactly the keys signer, algorithm, signature, and signed_at.

Note 0a to entry: Without 7.8.1c and 7.9.1a, one semantic signature was a claim two implementations could not both keep. Signing the parsed content means the parse decides the bytes: three source syntaxes are permitted and each admits scalars the others type differently — an unquoted 01, a bare yes, a timestamp — so two conformant readers of one file produce different canonical encodings and neither can verify the other's signature. And the signature entry itself had no schema, so 7.3.1 would have refused whatever an author wrote. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), finding SS219.)

Note 0 to entry: Without 7.9.4b the exemption of 7.9.4 could not be used. 7.3.1 refuses any field no schema defines, so an explicit declaration that the score is unsigned named no field an author could write: declaring it made the document invalid, and not declaring it made the score unloadable. The clause required something the format could not express — the defect 01-terminology.md 4.5 exists to prevent, arriving in a clause written the same day by the lane that wrote 4.5. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor), finding SS208, raised by an independent review.)

7.9.5 A hive shall not redistribute an unsigned score over a trust-group channel.

7.9.5a A hive shall treat an unsigned score received from a remote peer as a protocol error.

Note 1 to entry: 7.9.3 is the second deliberate departure from the imported corpus. That corpus signs a hash over the YAML source text with the signature key elided. A signature over a serialisation is a signature over whitespace, key order, quoting style and line endings — every one of which a well-meaning editor changes, and none of which is semantic. The corpus anticipates the move and requires that a publishing toolchain be able to re-sign; this clause makes the destination normative now rather than leaving two methods in the field. The cost is real and stated: an implementation must parse before it can verify, so a malformed document is rejected by the parser rather than by the verifier — which 01-terminology.md 5.1 already requires of it.

Note 2 to entry: 7.9.4 is a correction to the imported corpus and the most important rule in this clause. That corpus permits a local development score to be loaded unsigned — with no way for a hive to know a score is local-development except that it has no signature. A stripped, truncated or corrupt score is then indistinguishable from a local-development one and loads: the gate fails open on precisely the input class it should be hardest on. Under 7.9.4 the exemption is earned by a declaration, never by an absence — which is 01-terminology.md 5.1 (an absent value selects the more restrictive behaviour) reaching a signature check. Falsifier, per 01-terminology.md 4.2: strip the signature field from a valid score and it must be rejected, not loaded as local-dev; add the declaration and it must load; and a properly signed score must still load, or the falsifier proves only that loading is broken.

Note 3 to entry: 7.9.5 bounds the blast radius of 7.9.4 and is worth reading beside it — the mesh path was already closed, so the fail-open the correction closes was confined to locally-loaded scores.

7.10 Validating a document

7.10.1 A loader shall reject a definition or score that fails any obligation of 7.4 to 7.9, naming the obligation it failed.

7.10.2 A loader shall reject an action naming a plugin the document does not declare.

7.10.3 A builder realising a score shall verify that what it built matches what the score declared.

7.10.4 A converter producing a score from a swarm document shall carry each sentant across as one part, in the order the swarm wrote them.

7.10.5 Such a converter shall supply values for class, version and schema_version.

7.10.6 Such a converter shall mark the result unsigned where it does not sign it.

Note 1 to entry: The load-time and run-time halves are different in kind and this clause only governs the first. Template resolution, target resolution, plugin availability and expression typing cannot be decided from the document — they are the run-time errors of 7.6.9. What can be decided from the document alone must be, because the alternative is a device that accepts a definition and fails in the field at a moment nobody chose. Note 2 to entry: 7.10.3 is the build-time form of 01-terminology.md 5.4 — the record agrees with the world — for the case where the score is the record and the built artefact is the world.

Note 3 to entry: What this clause does not carry forward from the import, stated so the silence is legible. The swarm document — a bare grouping of definitions with no registrations, capabilities or signatures — is subsumed by the score of 7.8. The mapping is total and stated here rather than left to an importer's judgement, because a partial mapping is how a converted document acquires values nobody chose: a swarm's name and description become the score's; each sentant becomes one part, in the order written, and that order is preserved, because the corpus instantiates a swarm's sentants in declaration order and nothing in 7.8 overrides it; registrations, capabilities, plugins and trust_group are absent, not empty; version and schema_version have no source in a swarm (7.10.5 places that obligation on the converter) — a converter that invents them silently is the defect 7.3.2 refuses one level up; and the result is unsigned: true unless it is signed at conversion, because a converted document has no signature and 7.9.4a refuses a merely absent signature field. The corpus's error identifiers are not carried: 7.10.1 obliges an implementation to name the obligation, and a shared identifier vocabulary is a separate question, not settled here. (Both PROVISIONAL(supervisor), 2026-08-02.)

8 Conformance

8.1 The vector discipline of Layer 4, 12.2 applies to every format here: each section, when ratified, carries byte-exact vectors including required-to-fail cases. The identity derivation (Clause 3) and cipher envelope (Clause 4) vectors land with ratification of their provisional choices, and a generator accompanies them — the vectors are generated, never hand-written.

8.2 Until ratification, an implementation building against a PROVISIONAL choice shall mirror the mark, typically in a comment beside the vector, so the swap on ruling is one site, not an archaeology.

9 What is not decided

The self-test specification interior (5.1 key 6); the definition/score content (Clause 7, pending import); signature scheme agility across version epochs; and the compact projection's collision posture beyond 3.6.

9.1 A score cannot state the capabilities it requires. 7.8.1a pins the score's keys exactly, and the list carries capabilities — which 7.8.9 defines as the events its parts emit and consume, aggregated into what Layer 2 advertises — and required_transports, which 7.8.11 defines as what must be in service before the score is performed. So the requires pattern exists for transports and exists for nothing else. An ensemble that needs a hardware capability present on the hive has no pinned key in which to say so, and by 7.3.1 any key it invents is a field no schema defines, which a conforming validator refuses. The asymmetry has no stated reason, and it is the reason invented spellings appear in practice rather than one agreed key. They appear at BOTH levels and therefore fail different clauses: a score-level spelling fails 7.8.1a, a part-level spelling fails 7.8.1e, and a fix aimed at one clause leaves the other level untouched. (Measured by composer, 2026-08-04, in a catalogue of seventeen: required_capabilities as a SCORE key in one, and capabilities_required as a PART key in eight — reported first as a single split, which would have pointed the fix at one clause and missed the eight.) Adding the key would not by itself settle it: required_transports already shows the shape of the residue, carrying a pinned key and no defined value set (SS261), and a capability requirement drawn from a registry that has no home (Q38) would inherit exactly that. (Added 2026-08-04, PROVISIONAL(standard), finding SS273.)
