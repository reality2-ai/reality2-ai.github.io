Reality2 Standard — Formats companion

Normative document

About this reading edition

The numbered requirements and their provisional markers are retained from the cited source revision. Selected non-normative source notes, editorial attributions and internal working details are omitted. Clause numbers are unchanged; references to omitted notes may remain. The overview and worked examples are informative. Navigation and reading aids are not requirements.

Version: 0.9.0 · Date: 2026-09-18 · Status: DRAFT for ratification, authorized 2026-08-01 (D-023)

Verbal forms. shall = requirement · should = recommendation · may = permission · can = possibility and capability.

Provisional discipline. This draft settles seven register items (SS23, SS26, SS35, SS36, SS37, SS38, Q32). Where the parts left a choice unmade, this document makes it openly and provisionally: every invented value is marked PROVISIONAL(SS-n) and is open to replacement until ratified — implementations code against it and swap on ruling, exactly as the Layer 4 vector set was built. Where this document conflicts with a ratified part, the part is right.

1 Scope

This document pins the byte-level formats the layer parts reference but do not state: identity material and its wire derivations (Layer 4, 6.1.3 and 7.1.4); the payload cipher and its envelope (Layer 5, 7.3); the update package (Layer 6, Clause 4); the capability-contract registry and its first three contracts (Layer 7); and the definition and score formats (Layer 7, 10.2). The Layer 4 conformance vector set is a separate normative companion, TEST-VECTORS-L4.md, cited in Clause 2 — ratified in substance, and published with this document rather than contained in it.

2 Normative references

01-terminology.md, L4-wire.md, L5-trust-and-identity.md, L6-management.md, L7-application.md, TEST-VECTORS-L4.md.

3 Identity material and wire identity (settles SS23, SS26)

3.1 The identity material of a member, a hive, or a group shall be an Ed25519 key pair; the identity is the 32-byte public key. PROVISIONAL(SS23) — algorithm choice; Ed25519 fits no_std targets and L5's signature uses.

3.1a A verifier shall reject a small-order or non-canonical Ed25519 public key.

3.1b A verifier shall reject a non-canonical Ed25519 signature encoding. PROVISIONAL(SS114).

Note 1 to entry: The reason is that this standard treats a signature as an identity for a decision, in at least two places — Layer 6, 5.2.3 reasons about the package that was signed, and Layer 5, 7.4.3a advances a high-water mark on a verified signature. Under permissive verification one message has several valid signatures, so one authorisation can appear as two: the replay this standard refuses everywhere else, arriving through the verifier instead of through the wire.

3.2 Identity material shall be generated from the platform's cryptographically secure random number generator (Layer 0, 5.5.1) at first provisioning, and never from a name, a serial number, or any operator-supplied string (Layer 4, 7.1.4).

3.3 The extended wire identity of a hive (Layer 4, 6.1.3) shall be derived as:

  • hive half = bytes 0–3 of SHA-256 of the hive's identity public key;
  • group half = bytes 0–3 of SHA-256 of the group's identity public key;

each taken big-endian. PROVISIONAL(SS23) — hash and truncation choice.

3.4 The compact wire identity of a hive shall be its hive half alone — the 4-byte projection of 3.3, as adopted provisionally by the reference implementation and endorsed here.

3.5 Where a derived half equals 0x00000000 or 0xFFFFFFFF (reserved: Layer 4, 6.1.4 and 7.1.3), the derivation shall be repeated with the byte 0x01 appended to the hashed input, iterating until a non-reserved value results. PROVISIONAL(SS23).

3.6 Uniqueness is not guaranteed and shall not be assumed; collisions of wire identity are survived as Layer 4, 7.2 survives all hash collisions. The identity itself (the public key) is what is unique with overwhelming probability, and every consequential act verifies against it above the trust boundary.

4 Payload cipher and envelope (settles SS35)

4.1 The payload cipher for every encrypted level of Layer 5, 7.3 shall be XChaCha20-Poly1305 (256-bit key, 24-byte nonce, 16-byte tag). PROVISIONAL(SS35) — chosen over ChaCha20-Poly1305 because the 24-byte random nonce removes the nonce-uniqueness bookkeeping a 16-bit compact message identifier cannot provide, at the cost of frame bytes; and over AES-GCM for constant-time software implementation on targets without AES hardware.

4.2 An encrypted payload shall be the envelope: nonce (24 bytes) || ciphertext || tag (16 bytes), nonce freshly drawn from the CSPRNG per frame. The 40-byte overhead is accepted; deployments for which it dominates use public-by-design events (L5, 7.3.1) or the extended tier.

4.3 The associated data of the AEAD shall be the frame's authenticated span as Layer 4, 10.2.1 defines it, excluding the payload itself. PROVISIONAL(SS35) — binds ciphertext to origin, identifier, and target without double-covering the payload.

4.4 Per Layer 5, 7.3.3, no cipher identifier is carried: the Layer 4 version bits are the algorithm epoch (L4, 4.1.3 Note 1), and this suite is the suite of version 0.

4.5 Key derivation, rotation, and distribution remain Layer 5's (5.2.2 and Clause 6); this document names only the cipher those keys drive, and the purpose strings that separate them (Clause 4a).

4a Key-derivation purpose strings (settles SS39)

4a.1 Layer 5, 5.2.1 fixes the derivation (HKDF-SHA256, 32-byte output, secret as input keying material, public binding as salt, purpose string as info) and 5.2.3 forbids two derivations sharing a purpose string — but states no values, so two conformant implementations derive different keys and get silence. The values are fixed here. PROVISIONAL(SS39) — the scheme, not merely the strings, is open to replacement until ratified.

4a.2 A purpose string shall be the exact ASCII byte sequence given below, with no terminator and no padding:

Purpose String
group payload protection r2/v0/group/payload
group integrity (Layer 4 tag key) r2/v0/group/integrity
entanglement payload protection r2/v0/entanglement/payload
entanglement integrity r2/v0/entanglement/integrity
enrolment ceremony session r2/v0/ceremony/session
sealed storage of derived keys r2/v0/storage/seal
bulk session, initiator to responder r2/v0/bulk/initiator-to-responder
bulk session, responder to initiator r2/v0/bulk/responder-to-initiator

4a.3 The v0 field is the Layer 4 version — the algorithm epoch (L4, 4.1.3 Note 1) — and a new epoch takes a new set of strings, never a reused one.

4a.4 A purpose string not in this table shall not be used with the derivation of Layer 5, 5.2.1. A new derivation is added by adding a row here, under a name that states its use.

Note 1 to entry: The strings read as paths on purpose. A purpose string is a namespace, and the failure it prevents is silent: two derivations sharing one string yield the same key for different jobs, which no test detects and no party observes until the compromise of one exposes the other.

4b The CBOR profile (settles a gap found implementing L4 Clause 11)

4b.1 Layer 4, 11.1 requires definite-length CBOR and 11.2 fixes the type set. This clause pins what those leave open, because two of the gaps are signature-affecting and one is a remote crash on a constrained device. PROVISIONAL(SS95).

4b.2 Deterministic encoding. Where any clause of this standard requires a canonical encoding — notably 5.2, whose signature is computed over one — the encoding shall be:

a) definite lengths everywhere, for every string, array and map (already 11.1);

b) integers and lengths in the shortest form that represents the value;

c) map keys sorted by their encoded bytes, compared lexicographically as byte strings, shortest-first where one is a prefix of the other;

d) floats in the shortest width that represents the value exactly, and never narrowed where narrowing would change it;

e) no tags (4b.4), and no duplicate keys (4b.3).

4b.2a Deterministic decoding. Where a clause requires the deterministic encoding, a decoder shall reject input that is not in it — an integer or length not in shortest form, a float not in the shortest width that represents its value exactly, a map whose keys are not in the order 4b.2 c) requires, or any indefinite length.

4b.2b A decoder shall not accept such input and canonicalise it.

Note 0a to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(standard).

Note 0 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

4b.3 Duplicate map keys. A decoder shall reject a map containing two entries with the same key.

4b.3a A decoder shall not resolve a duplicate-key conflict by taking the first entry, the last entry, or either.

4b.4 Tags. A decoder shall reject a payload containing a tag (CBOR major type 6). Layer 4, 11.2's list is exhaustive for this standard, not a floor.

4b.5 Nesting depth. A decoder shall reject a payload nested more deeply than its stated limit.

4b.5a A decoder shall state its nesting limit.

4b.5b That limit shall be at least 8. PROVISIONAL(SS95) — the floor, not the ceiling.

Note 2 to entry: 4b.3 rejects rather than resolving because every resolution is exploitable. Two entries for one key, a signer that reads the first and a verifier that reads the last, and one signed document means two different things to the two parties — the classic CBOR attack. Rejection is the only rule with no exploitable side, which is the reasoning of 01-terminology.md 5.1 applied to a parser.

Note 3 to entry: 4b.5 exists because this standard's own target devices make it a security matter rather than a robustness one: a recursive-descent decoder on a microcontroller with a few hundred kilobytes of memory is a remote crash without a depth limit, and the attacker's cost is a few bytes of nesting. A limit belongs in the profile rather than in each implementer's judgement, because an implementer who omits it has written something that looks correct.

5 Update package (settles SS37)

5.1 An update package shall be one definite-length CBOR map with integer keys:

Key Type Field
1 uint package format version; this document specifies 0
2 tstr target class, exactly as the platform declaration states it
3 uint per-target monotonic sequence (L6, 5.2.3)
4 bstr image bytes
5 bstr SHA-256 of the image bytes
6 map self-test specification (L6, 3.4): keys PROVISIONAL(SS37)
7 array signatures
8 uint content class (5.1a)

PROVISIONAL(SS37) — the envelope; keys 1–5, 7 and 8 are structural, key 6's interior awaits the health-check contract.

5.1a Key 8 shall state the content class the package delivers, from the classes Layer 6, 5.1.1 requires: 1 the stack, 2 modules alongside it, 3 declarative definitions, 4 parameters, and 0 the whole image, where the platform collapses the classes into one image (Layer 6, 5.1.2). PROVISIONAL(supervisor) — the key number and the enumeration; the classes themselves are the part's.

Note 3 to entry: Change levels in Layer 6, 5.6 are admission policy, not these wire values: a level 1 parameter package uses content class 4, for example. A claimed content class does not by itself establish the permitted change level. An executable installation bundle, including its required sentant definitions and parameter installation policy, is bound inside the authenticated payload; no extra unsigned manifest or change-level key is introduced here. Human-readable score formats and their canonicalization remain governed by 7.8.1c/d and 7.9.

5.1b A package stating no content class shall be rejected, never treated as carrying the whole image.

Note 2 to entry: The default in 5.1b is restrictive for the same reason as 5.2a and 5.2b: 0 is the widest class in the enumeration, so reading an absent key as 0 would let a package that names nothing be treated as authorised to replace everything. The polarity rule of 01-terminology.md 5.1 decides it, and an absent key is exactly where a fold selects the permissive branch by accident.

5.2 Each entry of key 7 shall be [signer identity (32-byte public key), Ed25519 signature] over the deterministic CBOR encoding (4b.2) of the map containing every key except key 4 and key 7; key 4, the image bytes, is bound through key 5 — its SHA-256 — which Layer 6, 5.2.2 obliges the receiver to verify against the image before applying.

Note 0c to entry: WHAT THE EXCLUSION COSTS, MEASURED RATHER THAN ASSERTED AS HARMLESS. Because key 7 is outside the signed map, a signature does not commit to the signature COUNT nor to the identities of the other signers — an ordinary property of a multi-signature envelope, and one a reader should be told rather than left to derive. No hazard was found and the reasoning is short enough to check: removing entries gains an attacker nothing — 5.2a rejects a package whose key 7 is absent or empty, and 5.2c admits one only where a signature verifies under an update authority the receiving hive recognises — and adding an invalid entry gains nothing either, since 5.2d rejects a package none of whose signatures verifies. What an attacker can do is strip a co-signer from a package that still carries one valid signature, so key 7 records who signed and does not attest that they are all still listed. A deployment that requires two named authorities cannot get that from key 7 alone; it is a policy above this format, and it is named here rather than left as a gap somebody meets while building one.

5.2c A package shall be accepted only where a signature verifies under an update authority the receiving hive recognises (L6, 4.1.2).

5.2a A package whose key 7 is absent or empty shall be rejected.

5.2d A package none of whose signatures verifies shall be rejected. An empty signature list is not a satisfied requirement.

5.2b A self-test specification (key 6) that is absent or states no check shall be treated as a self-test that did not pass, never as one that passed vacuously.

Note 0 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

5.3 Ordinary-frame package carriage uses the Layer 6, 5.3.5 chunked-transfer model. An explicitly selected optional bulk service may carry the same package under Layer 6, 5.3.1 and Layer 7, 6.4. The package format and signed bytes are independent of carriage; neither service framing nor a transport acknowledgment changes what the recipient verifies before applying the package.

5.4 The sequence record a hive keeps per target (Layer 6, 5.2.3a) shall be:

Offset Size Field
0 4 magic 0x52 0x32 0x53 0x51 (R2SQ)
4 1 record format version; this document specifies 0
5 1 target identifier length n
6 n target identifier, as the package's key 2 states it
6+n 8 sequence, unsigned, big-endian
14+n 4 CRC-32 of bytes 0 to 13+n inclusive

PROVISIONAL(SS53) — widths and the CRC choice.

5.4a The record shall carry two sequences, not one: the sequence of the image the hive is running, and the sequence of an image staged and not yet committed, each with an explicit presence flag. The floor of Layer 6, 5.2.3 is the running sequence alone; a pending sequence is never a floor. Record format version 1. PROVISIONAL(SS109) — the shape, from an implementation that reached it by building the full cycle.

Note 1 to entry: One number cannot distinguish staged n from running n, and the difference is the brick of L6 5.2.3c Note 1 — a device that reverts beneath its own floor. Absence is not zero in either field: a record that has only ever staged reads as no running sequence, not as floor zero, which is 5.5's three-way distinction applied within the record rather than to it.

5.5 A record whose magic, version or CRC does not verify is unreadable, and Layer 6, 5.2.3b applies: no value from it is a sequence floor. A hive shall be able to report the distinction between no record, unreadable record, and sequence n — the three are different facts and the middle one is the dangerous one to lose.

5a Revocation entry (settles a gap found implementing L5 6.2)

5a.1 The bytes a revocation entry's signature (Layer 5, 6.2.2) covers shall be, in order: subject (32 bytes, the revoked certificate's member public key) || epoch (8 bytes, unsigned, big-endian) || sequence (8 bytes, unsigned, big-endian) || reason (1 byte: 0 compromise, 1 eviction, 2 retirement, 3 superseded). PROVISIONAL(SS91).

5a.2 The signature itself is not covered. The advisory absolute time of Layer 5, 6.2.2 is not covered.

Note 1 to entry: Excluding the advisory time is deliberate twice over: signing an operator's clock would let that clock change an entry's identity, and 6.2.2 already bars any decision from reading it — a field nothing may act on has no business inside a signature. Covering the reason is equally deliberate: Layer 5, 8.2 grades the grace period by reason, so a substitutable reason would let a compromise eviction — which carries zero grace — be downgraded to one that does not.

5b Persona disposition record (a conforming construction for L6 5.5.3a)

5b.1 This clause is informative. Layer 6, 5.5.3a states the properties a persona disposition must have; a platform whose durable storage offers an atomic transition of its own may satisfy them by other means. Nothing in 5b is a conformance requirement, and an implementation is not less conformant for choosing a different construction.

5b.2 The construction is two records of identical layout, each holding: a generation (unsigned, monotonically increasing), the disposition it records, and a validating field over everything before it. A reader takes the complete record of highest generation as selected; a record whose validating field does not check is not complete and is not a candidate. Where neither record is complete, Layer 6, 5.5.4 applies and the device is nobody's.

5b.3 A writer targets whichever record is not currently selected, writes it with a generation one above the selected one, and writes its validating field last. The selected disposition therefore changes at the instant the validating field completes — the transition is the completion of the checksum, not the start of the write — so there is no instant at which a partial state is the selected one, which is the property Layer 6, 5.5.3 requires.

5b.4 An implementation of this construction should state its acceptance conditions in full — each condition, in order, at the point where the record is parsed — and should state which fields the validating field covers and which conditions are checked separately on top of it. Layer 6, 5.5.1 guarantees a second reader written independently (5.5.3a Note 3), and 01-terminology.md 5.4b is what makes the two comparable.

Note 2 to entry: Nothing here says where the records live, and deliberately so — erase granularity, wear levelling and partition layout are platform facts, and Layer 6, 5.5.1 requires the recovery layer and the stack to share no code, not to share no storage. What they must agree on is this record's layout, which is why it is written down.

5c Firmware identity (settles SS133 / CORE-1)

5c.1 The firmware identity of Layer 6, 6.1.3 shall be one definite-length CBOR map with integer keys:

Key Type Field
1 tstr target class, as key 2 of the running image's package (5.1)
2 uint per-target sequence, as key 3 of that package
3 bstr SHA-256 of the image bytes, as key 5 of that package

PROVISIONAL(SS133).

5c.2 A key whose value the hive cannot determine shall be absent from the map.

5c.2a An absent key means this hive cannot state this value, and shall not be encoded as zero, as an empty string, or as any other placeholder.

Note 1 to entry: The three fields are the package's own identifying keys, so the answer is directly comparable with the package an operator holds — which is the only question a firmware identity is asked. A free-text version banner answers a different question and answers it differently on every platform.

Note 2 to entry: 5c.2 is 01-terminology.md 5.3 at a public reporting surface, and the harm of the alternative is concrete: a hive whose image position is unreadable (Layer 6, 5.4.2 Note 0) cannot state a sequence, and a substituted 0 reads as the first package ever — an operator comparing it against their own record concludes the device is catastrophically behind, and the repair they attempt is the wrong one. Absent is a fact; zero is a lie with a plausible interpretation.

5d Membership certificate (settles SS489)

5d.1 A membership certificate of Layer 5, 6.1.1 shall use exactly the following 136-byte representation, without padding, terminator or optional fields:

Byte offset Width in bytes Field
0 32 Subject member public key
32 32 Group public key
64 8 Issued-at key epoch, unsigned 64-bit integer, big-endian
72 64 Ed25519 signature by the group key over exactly bytes 0 through 71

Note 1 to entry: The enclosing protocol identifies this object as a membership certificate and selects its algorithm version. This representation introduces no negotiation, expiry or role field. Signature and public-key validation follow Clause 3; group, subject, currency and revocation checks follow Layer 5, Clause 6. Decoding the representation alone establishes none of those facts. Membership alone grants neither update authority nor authority to acknowledge a particular source's durable storage.

6 Capability-contract registry (settles SS36, SS38; frames Q32's home)

6.1 A capability contract names, for one capability identifier: the descriptor a provider publishes, the events it consumes and emits with their parameter schemas (CBOR, L4 Clause 11), and the semantics an implementer cannot infer from the schema. A contract shall state all three; a capability identifier without a contract in this registry is deployment-local and carries no interop claim.

6.1b Each entry of this registry shall state all three parts of 6.1 — descriptor, events with their schemas, and the semantics not inferable from the schema — or state explicitly that a part is deployment-local and why.

6.1a Registering a contract claims the name. A contract's entry claims its identifier and the meaning stated for it, and two contracts shall not claim semantically distinct meanings under one term. Where two uses of a term differ in meaning, they take different terms, however natural the shared word.

6.1c A contract of this registry may be keyed by a sentant class instead of by a capability identifier. Such a contract names the events that class emits, with their parameter schemas (CBOR, Layer 4, Clause 11), and the semantics an implementer cannot infer from the schema; it shall state all three. A sentant class whose emitted events are stated in no contract of this registry is deployment-local and carries no interop claim.

6.1d A contract keyed by a sentant class shall be keyed by the class and never by an instance of it.

6.1e Where a sentant emits an event that a contract of this registry names, that event's payload shall conform to the schema the contract states for it.

Note 2 to entry: What this changes is what a definition is, and that is a real shift rather than an editorial one. A definition states what a sentant is and accepts; what it produces is now stated outside it, and a reader holding only a definition cannot tell what that sentant emits. The gain is that nothing was added: no new key, no schema change, no validator moved, and 7.3.1 already refuses an emits field because no schema defines one.

Note 3 to entry: The class and never an instance, because an instance could not be checked by anyone. Layer 7, 5.1.4 keeps sentant instance identifiers out of every frame and 5.1.3 delivers a class-addressed event to every sentant of that class on the receiving hive — so an instance is neither addressable nor namable from outside the hive that runs it, and a contract naming one would state a party no reader could identify. Twenty instances of one class emitting the same events is the ordinary case, not an edge, and one contract covers them because the class is what the wire carries.

Note 4 to entry: What checks a wrong or absent contract, stated because the answer is uncomfortable and the cost was accepted knowingly. No validator an implementation runs sees a contract at all: 7.3.1 binds a definition or score validator, and a contract is in neither document. So the checks are 6.1b, which is a check on this registry, and a reader — and 6.1a, which stops a second contract claiming the same name for a different meaning. The option chosen avoids moving every validator, and the validator it avoids is exactly the one that would have caught a contract that is wrong rather than missing. That is the cost of adding no mechanism, and it is named here rather than left for somebody to find.

6.2 Display — ai.reality2.cap.display. PROVISIONAL(SS36). The descriptor core set is {kind, width, height, colour, text_rows} — width/height are pixel dimensions and text_rows is a separate capacity axis (a 160×80 colour panel has pixel dims and a text-row capacity a sentant sizes against); there is no addressing field. The command set is CMD_RENDER_FIELDS, CMD_CLEAR, CMD_RENDER_TEXT, CMD_RENDER_RAW, plus an optional descriptor-gated backlight operation (allowed only where descriptor.backlight != 0; power-cut stays driver-local). The load-bearing semantic the contract states: the severity adaptation rule — semantic severity in, presentation out: colour on colour panels, glyph/prefix on mono, top-N on row-limited displays.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(standard).

6.3 Presence/occupancy — ai.reality2.cap.presence. PROVISIONAL(SS38). Descriptor core set: {range_max, decay} — range_max the sensing envelope in metres, decay in seconds (below); optionally field_of_view in degrees, and multi_subject stating whether the provider can distinguish more than one subject. Events: events presence.enter, presence.leave, and periodic presence.standing, each carrying {distance: float (metres), confidence?: float 0–1} — confidence is optional and provider-derived (a plugin may synthesize it, and a mandatory field would force the first implementation to invent a number). Vendor unit conversion is the plugin's job. Absence is declared by presence.leave or by decay — a consumer shall treat a standing state older than the declared decay interval (descriptor field, seconds) as unknown, never as absence.

Note 2 to entry: decay is a declared interval, not a measured sensor property. A provider derives it from its configured reporting cadence and says so, rather than asserting a figure about hardware it cannot support.

6.4 Scalar environment — ai.reality2.cap.env.scalar is the existing catalogue form for single-value sensors and is registered as-is: {value: float, unit: tstr}. PROVISIONAL(SS38).

7 Definition and score formats (settles Q32)

7.1 The definition format (a sentant's behaviour as data, Layer 7) and the score format (an ensemble's parts and their conversation, Layer 7, 7.2) are specified in this clause. PROVISIONAL(supervisor) throughout: distilled 2026-08-02 from the imported corpus of 7.2, with every departure from that corpus named at the clause that departs.

7.2 The substance is imported rather than reinvented: corpus R2-DEF.md at spec DEF 0.14 is held verbatim with the editing record, and is identified by its provenance rather than by where it is kept — r2-specifications commit 3932727, 1811 lines, sha256 prefix 8ecb5df83b24864f, verified 2026-08-01 and recomputed on every run of the requirements gate since 2026-08-02. R2-CAP.md 0.9 is available from the same commit on ask — as corpus, under Note 0, not as a document anything may be built from.

7.3 Two rules the deployed history proves load-bearing

7.3.1 A definition or score validator shall reject a document carrying a field no schema defines.

7.3.2 A consumer meeting an unresolvable or unrecognised versioned reference (name@ver) shall fail with an error.

7.3.3 Such a consumer shall not substitute an absent value.

7.4 The definition document

7.4.1 A sentant definition shall carry a name and a description, each a non-empty string, and at least one automation.

7.4.2 A sentant definition shall carry a class: a non-empty UTF-8 string declaring the sentant's type convention, in the reverse-DNS form Layer 2, 5.5 hashes.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

7.4.2a A sentant definition shall carry a public_events array, each entry the name of an event the sentant declares as its accepted external interface.

7.4.2b A sentant definition shall carry a plugins array, each entry the name of a plugin the definition binds.

7.4.2c A sentant definition may carry a requires array, each entry the class of a capability the sentant needs its hive to provide for it to run.

7.4.2d Each entry of requires shall be a capability class.

7.4.2e An entry of requires shall not be the name of a plugin or of a binding.

7.4.2f A hive that cannot provide every capability a definition's requires names shall not perform that part.

7.4.2g A hive providing capabilities beyond those a definition's requires names shall perform that part.

Note 3 to entry: The permission at 7.4.2g is stated because the opposite reading is available and somebody will take it. A board binding more than one sentant needs is the ordinary case — a reference board binds a display and an indicator while a part needs one of them — and a rule read as exactly these would make every multi-capability board non-conformant for every part that does not use all of it. 6.2.2b's test is whether the hive can provide what the part requires, never whether the two sets are equal, and 6.2.1's hive-shared plugins exist precisely so one provided resource serves many parts.

Note 2 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

7.4.2c An event name should be in the reverse-DNS form 7.4.2 requires of a class. A name not in that form is conformant, and the Notes below state what is given up by it, so that departing from this recommendation is an informed act rather than a default.

Note 1 to entry: What is given up is not a rejected frame — it is an accepted one. The event name is the unit of meaning across the wire: Layer 7, 5.1.4 makes it what does the addressing once a frame reaches a hive, and the class it is delivered to is resolved by the receiving hive rather than carried to it. So an unnamespaced name can be claimed by an unrelated deployment by construction rather than by accident — two parties both writing level.reading agree on the wire, both verify, both deliver, both are conformant, and each has acted on a different meaning. Every counter reads correct, and no instrument in this standard sees it: the capability summary of Layer 2, Clause 8 answers whether an event will land — 8.1 admits false positives and not false negatives in a summary that is carried, so a hash absent from a carried summary is a definitive no, while a beacon carrying no summary is no answer rather than a negative (Layer 2, 5.2 makes it optional and 8.4 bars it where nothing is public) — and never whether it means the same.

Note 2 to entry: Layer 4, 7.2 does not cover this and is not the answer to it. 7.2.1 declines to detect collisions and 7.2.2 requires whatever acts on a delivered event to tolerate parameters that do not fit, on a hash its own Note describes as colliding rarely and eventually — wrong delivery is harmless delivery because the handler gets bytes it rejects. A shared name is the other failure and the bytes fit: the right-looking handler accepts them. One is rare and announces itself at the handler; the other is systematic and silent. Layer 4, 7.1.2 closes the neighbouring half — it makes the obvious variations of one name hash alike — and states its own limit: it cannot make two different names hash alike, and does not try. This clause is about two different meanings sharing one name, which is the case normalisation cannot reach.

7.4.3b A hive shall initialise a sentant's variables from its data map at instantiation.

Note 1 to entry: Storage disposition and eviction are different questions and the words sound alike. 7.5 Automations

7.5.1 An automation shall carry a name unique within its sentant, and an array of transitions.

7.5.1a The keys of an automation shall be spelled exactly: name, description, transitions.

7.5.1b The keys of a transition shall be spelled exactly: from, event, to, actions.

Note 0 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

7.5.2 A transition shall name the event it matches.

7.5.2c Where a hive compares an event that arrived in a frame with an event name a definition carries — matching a transition (7.5.2), or building a capability summary from those names (7.8.9a) — it shall compute the identifier hash of the name it holds as Layer 4, 7.1.1 and 7.1.2 require, and compare that with the frame's event identifier hash.

Note 1 to entry: A definition carries names; a frame carries a hash; the hive holds both, and the conversion runs in one direction only. The pattern is not new here — Layer 7, 10.6 already states it for a different question: the event identifier hash is in clear on every frame and unalterable by a relay, so a receiver holding the definition resolves the question with nothing added to the wire. The reverse is barred at Layer 2, 5.5.2 for the class hash and holds for the same reason: a 32-bit hash is not a name and cannot be turned back into one.

7.5.2a The wildcard shall be the string *.

7.5.2b The initial marker shall be the string start.

7.5.3 A loader shall take an automation's set of states to be the union of the from and to values of its transitions, excluding the wildcard and the initial marker.

7.5.3a A from or to value of * shall have the same effect as omitting it.

7.5.4 A loader shall take the initial state to be the to value of a transition whose from is the initial marker, and where no such transition exists, the first state named in definition order.

7.5.5 A transition stating no from shall match in any state.

7.5.5a A transition stating no to shall leave the automation in the state it was in.

7.5.5b Where more than one transition of an automation matches an arriving event in the current state, a hive shall take the first in the order the transitions are written, and no other.

Note 0 to entry: This is not a preference between two reasonable answers; it is what keeps a definition portable. Layer 7, 4.2.1 Note 1 makes the definition the unit of portability — carried to another hive and run there — and a document that resolves differently on two conformant hives is not portable in the sense that clause means. So a tie-break cannot be deployment-local, however local the mechanism resolving it looks.

Note 1 to entry: The order is the one already used twice in these clauses, so nothing new is introduced: 7.5.1 makes an automation's transitions an array, which is ordered; 7.5.4 falls back to the first state named in definition order; and 7.6.2 executes a transition's actions in the order written. An author who has already been told that action order is significant has no reason to expect transition order not to be.

Note 2 to entry: Why the alternative was rejected. A loader could instead refuse a definition in which two transitions can both match — decidable, since overlap is a matter of one event name and two from sets. It would forbid the idiom the wildcard exists for: a specific transition followed by a * fallback is two matching transitions by design (7.5.2a, 7.5.5), and it is the ordinary way to write a default. Ordering makes that idiom work; refusal would delete it. (PROVISIONAL(standard))

7.5.6 A hive receiving an event for which the current state has no matching transition shall discard it, without error and without queueing, whatever the event's source.

7.5.7 A hive shall hold at most one pending delayed event per event name per sentant.

7.5.7a A later delayed send of that event name shall replace the pending one.

7.5.8 A hive shall not carry a pending delayed event across a restart.

Note 1 to entry: 7.5.6 is a decision, not an omission. Silent discard means an event arriving in the wrong state is indistinguishable from one nobody sent — which is what makes the four transition patterns composable, and which is also why an author who needs to know sets up a transition to observe it. Stated here because an implementer meeting an unmatched event and no rule would reasonably invent a dead-letter queue, and two hives would then disagree about whether an event was delivered.

Note 2 to entry: 7.5.7 gives cancellation without a cancel verb — replace the timer, or move to a state that discards the event when it fires.

Note 3 to entry: 7.5.8 is what Layer 0, 5.2 already implies for any interval spanning a power cycle; it is repeated here because the alternative reading — a timer restored at boot — fires an event whose originating state no longer exists.

Note 0 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

7.5.8a Suspending a sentant shall not discard its pending delayed events.

7.5.8b A pending delayed event's deadline shall not be recomputed on suspension or on resumption.

7.5.8c A pending delayed event whose deadline elapses while its sentant is not visible shall be delivered when that sentant next becomes visible.

Note 4 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(standard).

Note 5 to entry: What makes holding safe is 7.5.7 and not a new bound. At most one pending delayed event exists per event name per sentant, so a suspension of any length accumulates nothing — the pending set is bounded by the sentant's own event names whether it is suspended or not. No capacity rule is needed here, and one added on this account would bound something that cannot grow.

Note 6 to entry: The deadline is held, not deferred, and the nearest wrong implementation looks identical from outside. Skipping a suspended sentant's timer and letting it fire on the first opportunity after resumption evaluates the deadline late, which is the re-counting 7.6.3a forbids arriving by another route: 7.6.3a makes the delay a whole number of milliseconds counted from the moment the action executes, and that instant is unaffected by anything the lifecycle does. 7.5.8c delivers late where it must; 7.5.8b is what keeps late from becoming longer.

Note 7 to entry: A test that only checks nothing arrived during the suspension is not a control. 7.5.7 Note 2 makes move to a state that discards the event when it fires the sanctioned way to cancel a timer, there being no cancel verb — so a timer consumed and dropped at a suspended sentant is byte-for-byte the pattern this document recommends, and no observable separates it from a correct hold. The demonstration is therefore positive: the event arrives after resumption, and the same procedure run against a hive that fires and drops must fail. (01-terminology.md 4.2 to 4.4.)

Note 8 to entry: Unload is not decided here. These clauses bind suspension and resumption. Layer 7, 4.3.2 covers only a durable sentant's persisted definition, and a pending delayed event is not a definition — so what unloading does to one remains unstated, and an implementation that discards them is neither obeying nor breaching a clause. Recorded rather than closed, because the absence is easy to read as settled once its neighbours are not.

7.5.9a The instantiation marker shall be the string init.

7.5.9b The instantiation event shall carry internal origin (Layer 7, 5.2.3) and no parameters.

7.5.9c A hive shall treat each reload of a durable or durable-state sentant at boot (Layer 7, 4.2.3) as an instantiation of 7.5.9.

7.5.9d A hive shall not deliver an instantiation event on resumption from suspension.

7.5.9e A transition whose from is the initial marker shall match only the instantiation event of 7.5.9.

7.5.9f A hive shall perform the instantiation of 7.5.9 as a lifecycle-caused delivery of its own, consumed once per instantiation.

7.5.9g A hive shall not treat any event a sentant or a peer sends as the instantiation event, whatever its name, origin or parameters.

7.5.9h A transition whose from is the initial marker shall match the instantiation delivery of 7.5.9f and no sent event; the event it names is still matched (7.5.2).

Note 2 to entry: 7.5.9e is what keeps 7.5.4 true rather than competing with it. The initial state a loader computes is the to of the start-marker transition, so when that transition fires at the instantiation event its to names the state the automation is already in — the loader's static answer and the runtime's behavioural one agree by construction, and what the firing adds is the actions, which are the boot hook the antecedent's examples put there. A definition wanting boot actions without a start-marker transition writes an ordinary transition on init from its initial state.

8 Conformance

8.1 The vector discipline of Layer 4, 12.2 applies to every format here: each section, when ratified, carries byte-exact vectors including required-to-fail cases. The identity derivation (Clause 3) and cipher envelope (Clause 4) vectors land with ratification of their provisional choices, and a generator accompanies them — the vectors are generated, never hand-written.

8.2 Until ratification, an implementation building against a PROVISIONAL choice shall mirror the mark, typically in a comment beside the vector, so the swap on ruling is one site, not an archaeology.

9 What is not decided

The self-test specification interior (5.1 key 6); the definition/score content (Clause 7, pending import); signature scheme agility across version epochs; and the compact projection's collision posture beyond 3.6.

9.1 A score cannot state the capabilities it requires. 7.8.1a pins the score's keys exactly, and the list carries capabilities — which 7.8.9 defines as the events its parts emit and consume, aggregated into what Layer 2 advertises — and required_transports, which 7.8.11 defines as what must be in service before the score is performed. So the requires pattern exists for transports and exists for nothing else. An ensemble that needs a hardware capability present on the hive has no pinned key in which to say so, and by 7.3.1 any key it invents is a field no schema defines, which a conforming validator refuses. The asymmetry has no stated reason, and it is the reason invented spellings appear in practice rather than one agreed key. They appear at BOTH levels and therefore fail different clauses: a score-level spelling fails 7.8.1a, a part-level spelling fails 7.8.1e, and a fix aimed at one clause leaves the other level untouched. Adding the key would not by itself settle it: required_transports already shows the shape of the residue, carrying a pinned key and no defined value set (SS261), and a capability requirement drawn from a registry that has no home (Q38) would inherit exactly that. (PROVISIONAL(standard))

9.2 Reference resolution is terminating but not bounded. 7.8.2c requires the include graph to be acyclic and 7.8.2d requires a consumer meeting a reference it is already resolving to fail — so resolution ends. Nothing says how much it may cost to get there. A deep but perfectly legal chain still consumes a consumer's resolution state, and on a class-1 platform — 64 kB, Layer 0, 8.3.2 — that is a real limit reached without any clause being violated. The corpus already holds the idiom that would close it, one clause family over and pointed at a different object: 4b.5 has a decoder reject a payload nested beyond its stated limit, 4b.5a obliges it to state that limit, and 4b.5b floors the limit at 8. The same three-part shape applied to resolution depth would do it; whether it should be applied is not decided here, and neither is whether a bound belongs to this document or to the platform declaring what it can afford. This is the family Layer 7, 10.4 records for sentants — nothing bounds how many a hive accepts … and the bound is deployment policy for now — arriving at a second construct.

The gap this entry recorded is closed by 7.6.3h/7.6.3i (the send carries the accumulated map; action-stated entries are defaults the map overrides) and 7.6.6c (the plugin invocation, same model), landed together with 7.6.2a's key-position ruling as the one model SS387/SS448 were held for. The entry keeps its number and this note so a reader following an old citation lands on the resolution rather than on silence (01-terminology.md's withdrawal rule, applied to a gap).
