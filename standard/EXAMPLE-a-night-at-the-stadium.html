Reality2 Standard — worked example: a night at the stadium

Status: Informative — worked example Date: 2026-08-05

⚠ INFORMATIVE. The third worked example, and the first at scale: tens of thousands of R2-enabled devices in one bowl of concrete. Payment showed the high-stakes dials, the room showed the low-stakes dials; this one walks the whole stack, layer by layer, under load — and adds one new shape: presence as attested history, the virtual sticker you can only buy because you were there. Open edges are flagged in place.

Cast

  • Person trust group P — one fan among thousands: a phone, and a watch that sleeps.
  • Venue trust group V — the stadium operator: snack kiosks, the kitchen behind them, fixed relay hives bolted to the concourse, big screens, and an attestation sentant.
  • The crowd — everyone else's person groups. They are not bystanders; below the trust boundary they are the infrastructure.

The premise of this example

A stadium is the shared mesh's home fixture: thousands of strangers, packed close, none of them entangled with each other, every one of them relaying (L3: relaying is the default, not conditional on knowing anybody). The venue does not build a network for the crowd — the crowd is the network, and the venue's fixed hives are just the members of it that never leave and never sleep.

Layer by layer

Layer 0 — what is actually in the bowl

Every platform in the stadium published its declaration long before tonight (L0 8.2):

Who Platform character Availability (P8)
Fan's phone large class, duty-cycled by its host OS duty-cycled, short intervals
Fan's watch small class, aggressive sleeper duty-cycled, long intervals
Kiosk, kitchen, fixed relays mains-powered always-on

Nothing above this layer asks what the hardware is — only what it declared. The watch's long sleep intervals are about to matter three layers up.

Layer 1 — the bearers in service

Phones and kiosks share a short-range high-rate bearer; the venue's fixed hives add a long-range regulated bearer as a backbone for their own telemetry. Each bearer is in service or it is nothing (L1 3.5a): a fan whose phone has its short-range radio on is on that bearer's network, full stop. The regulated backbone matters for what it forbids: its airtime budget is a hard ceiling, and everything the venue does tonight is planned so that nothing time-critical depends on it.

One tier discipline, decided here and honoured everywhere above: signed traffic is planned end-to-end within one tier (L4 9.2.3) — so everything person-to-venue tonight lives on the crowd's own tier, and the backbone never carries a frame that would have to cross to reach a fan.

Layer 2 — discovery in a beacon storm

Forty thousand beacons in one bowl, and the layer is built for exactly this:

  • Step 1 of the progressive filter is free (L2 7.1): almost everything a fan's scanner hears is another fan, and the class hash — checked before any connection, before any cost — discards them in one comparison. The scanner is looking for ai.reality2.vendor.kiosk, and the crowd is noise it never spends a query on.
  • Cadence is an outcome, not a setting (L2 6.2, 6.3): on anything regulated, beacons are the lowest-priority traffic, so the busier the air the rarer the announcements — and every fade period was set against the loaded interval, not the bench one, which is why peers are not declared dead at half-time when the air is thickest.
  • The watch declares its duty class in its beacon (L2 5.4b): receptive only at intervals, with a coarse longest-interval value. Every stranger's phone within range — none of them entangled with the fan, none of them able to verify anything — now knows to hold rather than transmit at a silent radio. This value could not have arrived any other way: the crowd relays for groups it does not belong to, and a verified message is exactly what a stranger cannot get.

⚠ Open edge, flagged: nothing bounds how many peers a scanner tracks or which sighting it discards when full (Q15, L2 12.2). Tonight is the case that question exists for: two conformant phones in row 40 will know about different subsets of the crowd, and neither is wrong.

Layer 3 — routing through forty thousand relays

  • Every fan's phone forwards frames for strangers, ciphertext it cannot read, for groups it cannot join. The bounds that make this survivable are the ones that need no identity: hop limit, replication budget, payload size (L3 10.4 Note 1) — deliberately crude, and the reason a hive that believes nothing it hears is still safe in a crowd.
  • Duplicate suppression is doing more work here than anywhere else (L3 5.3): in a dense mesh every frame arrives many times by many paths, and the cache is what keeps density from becoming amplification.
  • The fan moves and nothing breaks. Seat, concourse, queue, seat again — the physical substrate under P changes completely in minutes, and the logical destination does not change at all. Neighbour tables converge on wherever P is hearable now; frames for P flow toward the current truth, not the remembered one.
  • The watch's frames wait. Its neighbours — strangers included — hold rather than flood (L3 7.3.3), on the duty class its beacon declared, with retention patience sized by the declared interval (L3 7.4.3).

Layer 4 — the frames themselves

Everything tonight is frames, indistinguishable to the network (frame principle, L4 5.1 Note 4): a beacon, an order, a payment, a picked-up notification, a sticker attestation — the type field says how to treat, never what it means. Signed frames stay on the tier they were born on (L4 9.2.3); the venue planned for that at Layer 1.

Layer 5 — three kinds of relationship, all live at once

The whole ladder is on display in one place (L5 10.1):

Relationship Rung What flows
Fan ↔︎ the crowd the mesh — not a rung at all carriage: relayed ciphertext, beacons, nothing readable
P ↔︎ V, all evening low-level entanglement presence, order events, the attestation
P ↔︎ kiosk, for one order payment-grade entanglement tokens, per the payment example
  • P ↔︎ V is established on arrival with near-zero ceremony, conditioned on proximity through the event window — the conference-room entanglement, outdoors. Walking out at full-time is the teardown; nobody logs out of a stadium.
  • P ↔︎ kiosk is the PoS example verbatim: amount ladder, level-4 acknowledgement for a snack-sized purchase, tokens from the wallet P carries, no central authority in the loop. Its lifetime is the order: scope is this order's events, and the standing condition is an order exists that is not yet fulfilled.
  • The delivery gate never sleeps: every event under either entanglement is classified, and the classification names the key (L5 7.3). An observer in row 41 — in radio range of everything — sees envelopes.

Layer 6 — the venue's fleet, invisible tonight

Two hundred venue hives get their firmware as chunked transfers (L6 5.3.5) — overnight, on the quiet air, never during a match, because the update model tolerates taking days and the airtime budget tonight is spoken for. Diagnostics stay silent toward strangers (L6 6.2.2): forty thousand devices probe nothing, learn nothing.

Layer 7 — the sentants, and the snack

The order, as events between sentants, each at the assurance grade its purpose warrants (L7 5.3):

# Event Grade Why
1 P → kiosk: order.place + payment receipt-with-release money moved; the payment example's contract
2 kiosk → P: order.accepted + order reference receipt the fan walks away on the strength of it
3 kitchen → P: order.ready receipt, re-sent until acknowledged P has moved; the frame chases them through the crowd — repeated at intervals, each copy suppressed as a duplicate wherever paths converge, until P's phone says got it
4 P arrives; hand over; order.fulfilled fire-and-forget the snack in the hand is the real receipt

Event 4 fulfils the entanglement's condition, and the P ↔︎ kiosk keys die with it. The network never knew any of this was food.

The sticker: presence as attested history

The new shape. Weeks later, the fan can buy a virtual sticker — I was at that match — and only someone who was there can.

During the event, V's attestation sentant issues, under the P ↔︎ V entanglement, a signed claim into P's wallet: this persona's entanglement with V stood through event E's window. The evidence behind it is exactly the entanglement's own condition — proximity, continuously, on the venue's bearers — which V was already maintaining for its own purposes. The attestation costs one event.

At purchase time, P presents the claim. This is the ZKP-inspired posture from the payment example, applied to history instead of identity: prove the predicate — was at event E — never the evidence. No movement trace, no entry time, no seat, no companions; the claim carries the predicate and V's signature, nothing else.

And the sovereignty keystone, third appearance: the venue keeps no attendance register. The person carries their own proof of where they have been, discloses it when they choose, to whom they choose — presence history is wallet contents, not somebody's database. The venue could not sell, leak, or be subpoenaed for a record it never held.

Two honest costs, named:

  • Re-persona forfeits the sticker rights. The claim names the persona, and re-persona is, observably, a different hive (L5 5.1.4) — deliberately unlinkable to the old one. The fresh start is genuinely fresh, in both directions.
  • The claim is only as good as its issuer's signature discipline. V's group signature authenticates an insider of V — which insider, and whether kiosk firmware could mint attendance claims, is exactly the member-attributable evidence question (Q24, since ruled: the attestation is a certificate-carrying member signature, L5 7.4, so a claim now names its issuing member key), and a venue that cares will want its attestation authority narrower than any member — a policy it can enforce on evidence that exists.

⚠ Open edges, flagged: proximity as a condition — its evidence, continuity, and spoof-tolerance at stadium scale (someone parked outside the gates all evening) — is Q25's conditions vocabulary; whose signature covers an attestation is Q24; what a presence claim carries and what it deliberately omits follows the ruled Q34 disclosure floor — the predicate only (C6); its wire format is companion work.
