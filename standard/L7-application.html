Reality2 Standard — Layer 7: Application

Status: Draft — this names the editorial maturity of the text, and nothing else. It is not a licence to deviate: every requirement here is normative now and the implementing lanes build from it (d041, Roy: standard is the canon). The only text open to replacement is a value marked PROVISIONAL(...) at its own clause. (Subject stated because a status field is scoped to a question the reader may not be asking, and a claim with an unstated subject will be given the subject the reader brought — composer, 2026-08-03.) Date: 2026-08-04

Verbal forms. shall = requirement · should = recommendation · may = permission · can = possibility and capability.

Introduction

Everything below this layer moves, protects and delivers frames. Layer 7 is what the frames were for. The unit of computation is the sentant: an autonomous agent built from finite state machines, living on a hive, receiving events, mutating its own state, emitting events. Applications are not installed and do not run anywhere in particular — they are ensembles: described by a score, performed by whichever hives currently host their parts, growing and shrinking as hives come and go.

Three positions define this layer, and each is the top of a column that runs down the whole standard.

A sentant is sovereign over its own behaviour. Nothing outside a sentant can read its state, force a transition, or discover its internal structure. The only thing the world can do to a sentant is send it an event, and the sentant's own state machine decides what — if anything — that event does. This is the top of the trust column: the delivery gate decides what arrives; the sentant decides what matters.

The network below is lossy, moving, and promises nothing — and this layer is written for that, not in spite of it. Events are fire-and-forget by default; effects are idempotent; where a purpose needs more assurance, the endpoints contract for it in events, at whatever grade the purpose warrants. An application that would only work on a reliable network is not an application of this standard.

One carriage for everything. A sentant's event and a plugin's chunk of data are the same artefact — a frame — indistinguishable to every layer below. This layer adds no second path to the network, and nothing here is entitled to know how a frame travelled.

1 Scope

This document specifies the sentant: its defining properties, its definition, its lifecycle, and its event model.

It specifies the plugin: its contract with the hive, its two ownership flavours, and its place in the event model.

It specifies the ensemble: its composition, its score, and its distributed performance.

It establishes what conformance at this layer consists of, and identifies what is not decided.

It does not specify the byte-level definition format or score format, which belong to a companion part. It does not specify any particular user interface, runtime language or host framework.

3 Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the terms and definitions given in the documents listed in Clause 2 apply — in particular sentant, plugin, ensemble, apiary and class (01-terminology.md 3.4–3.7, 3.9) — together with the following.

3.1 automation

finite state machine within a sentant: states, transitions, and the actions a transition performs

3.2 vars

sentant's mutable named values, alterable only by its own transitions

3.3 event

named message, optionally carrying data, that is the sole means of acting on a sentant, a plugin, or a hive

3.4 public event

event name a sentant declares as its accepted external interface

3.5 origin tag

marking the hive runtime places on every event: internal, meaning it arose on this hive, or external, meaning it arrived over the network

3.6 score

declarative description of an ensemble: its sentants, its plugin requirements, and its identity

4 The sentant

4.1 The six properties

4.1.1 Every sentant shall satisfy all of the following, and an implementation shall enforce each.

Property Requirement
Immutable The definition — automations, class, public events, plugin bindings — is fixed at load. A running sentant's definition shall not be modified; only vars and current states change.
Persistent A loaded sentant exists until explicitly unloaded. It shall not be evicted for inactivity, and a crashed sentant shall be restarted by its hive.
Unique Each sentant instance has an identifier unique on its hive, never reused, and never transmitted on the wire.
Consistent A sentant is atomically visible: fully loaded and routable, or invisible. No partially initialised sentant shall be discoverable or receive events.
Opaque Internal structure — states, current state, transitions, private events, vars — shall not be observable by anything outside the sentant. There is no state query, and none shall be added.
Deterministic Given the same vars, states and event, a sentant produces the same actions. Anything non-deterministic — time, randomness — enters as an observable input via an event or a plugin, never as hidden state.

Note 1 to entry: Opacity is a design position, not a missing feature. A sentant's state is observable only through what it chooses to emit — if it does not emit, the observer knows nothing, and that is the contract. The development-mode diagnostics of Layer 6 observe the hive's infrastructure, never a sentant's interior; even there, no state query exists.

Note 2 to entry: Determinism is what makes a sentant auditable from its definition and a state snapshot alone — behaviour is provable, not observed. The cost is honest: anything that varies has to arrive as input, which is why time and randomness are plugin-provided and visible in the event record.

Note 3 to entry: Immutability is what makes advertising trustworthy: a class and public event list inspected once hold until the sentant is unloaded. Changing a sentant is unload-and-load, never mutation — the same shape as build modes and firmware: identity changes by replacement.

4.2 Definition and storage

4.2.1 A sentant shall be created from a declarative definition carrying everything required to run it: automations, class, public events, plugin bindings, and initial vars. A definition shall have no dependency not named within it.

4.2.2 A definition shall declare its storage class: volatile (gone at restart), durable (definition survives restart, state starts fresh), or durable-state (definition and last-snapshotted state survive). The default is volatile.

4.2.3 A hive shall reload durable and durable-state sentants at boot, and a durable-state sentant shall tolerate resuming from a snapshot older than its last activity.

Note 1 to entry: 4.2.1's self-containment is what makes a sentant movable — it can be carried to another hive, or another group, as a frame's payload, and run there without hidden dependencies. The definition is the unit of portability, which is also why the definition, not the code, is what an ensemble's score names.

Note 2 to entry: 4.2.3's tolerance clause is the premise applied to storage: a hard power cut loses everything since the last snapshot, and a sentant that cannot survive that is mis-declared — it needed explicit checkpointing through events, not a stronger promise from the platform.

4.3 Lifecycle

4.3.1 Load, unload, suspend and resume shall be explicit operations, subject to the management authority of Layer 6.

4.3.2 Unloading a durable sentant shall remove its persisted definition; suspension shall not.

5 Events

5.1 One model, two scopes

5.1.1 An event shall be the only way anything acts on a sentant.

5.1.2 Events between sentants on one hive shall be dispatched locally and never framed; events to other hives travel as frames of Layer 4, and the sending of one is fire-and-forget.

5.1.3 An event addressed to a class shall be delivered to every sentant of that class on the receiving hive.

5.1.4 Sentant instance identifiers shall never appear in a frame; across the wire, addressing ends at the hive and the event name and class do the rest.

Note 1 to entry: 5.1.4 is Layer 4's the wire stops at the hive seen from above. What handles an event is the receiving hive's own affair, invisible and unaddressable from outside — which is what lets a hive host one sentant or a thousand behind the same four-byte address.

5.2 Public, private, and the origin tag

5.2.1 A sentant's public events (3.4) are its declared external contract, and shall be what its hive advertises for it.

5.2.2 An event not in the public list is private. A private event arriving from outside shall still be processed — secrecy of names is not access control.

5.2.3 The hive runtime shall tag every event with its origin (3.5), shall overwrite any origin a sender supplied, and a transition may filter on the tag.

Note 1 to entry: 5.2.2 and 5.2.3 together are the honest version of a boundary that is usually faked. Hiding private event names is opacity, not protection — a guessed name arrives. What protects is the tag: a transition gated on internal origin cannot be fired from the network at all, however good the guess, because the runtime — not the sender — decides what is internal. One mechanism, applied where the trust actually is.

Note 2 to entry: The origin tag is binary — internal or external — and says only who injected the event: the runtime, or the delivery path. It does not say what the delivery gate established. An external event arrives with its delivery classification (Layer 5, 10.1.1: same-group, entangled by grade, or unauthenticated) as a separate fact, and a transition that cares filters on both. A sentant gating on external origin alone has decided what all of the outside may do — including its unauthenticated part, which 10.1.1 delivers with Layer 7 filtering as the only gate; deciding what the verified outside may do is a filter on the classification, not on the tag. (Reworded 2026-08-01 — the earlier text wrongly excluded unauthenticated delivery.)

5.3 Delivery semantics

5.3.1 Event delivery shall be fire-and-forget: no acknowledgement exists at this layer or below, and a sender shall not be able to observe delivery.

5.3.2 A sentant's externally visible effects shall be idempotent with respect to duplicate delivery of the same event.

5.3.3 Where a purpose requires assurance, the participating endpoints shall contract for it in events — a receipt, a release, a completion — at the grade the purpose warrants, and such contracts are application traffic carried like any other.

Note 1 to entry: 5.3.2 is the last line of a defence stated at three layers: transit duplicates are suppressed (Layer 3), destination duplicates are bounded (Layer 4), and what makes the residue harmless is that acting twice equals acting once. Replication is how delivery is achieved at all on this network; idempotency is what makes replication free to use.

Note 2 to entry: 5.3.3 is the UDP-to-TCP spectrum with both ends on one substrate. The purpose of a message decides its grade, and only the endpoints know the purpose — a superseded sensor reading wants nothing, a firmware piece wants a receipt, a bounded store wants receipt-with-release. The network never learns that grades exist.

6 Plugins

6.1 The contract is events

6.1.1 A plugin shall interact with its hive, and with sentants, through events alone; its implementation, language and process boundary are invisible outside the hive.

6.1.2 A plugin's data shall travel the network as ordinary frames. This layer defines no other carriage, and a plugin needing bulk movement uses the chunked-transfer contract of Layer 6's model at the assurance grade its purpose warrants.

6.1.3 A plugin shall be the declared source of any non-determinism a sentant consumes (4.1.1), so that time, randomness and the physical world enter the record as events.

6.2 Two ownership flavours

6.2.1 A plugin wrapping a resource the platform exposes once — a network server port, an audio device, a display, a filesystem — shall be hive-shared: one instance per hive, with a registration mechanism by which ensembles attach their use of it.

6.2.2 A plugin embodying ensemble-specific logic shall be ensemble-owned: loaded from the ensemble's score, one instance per ensemble per hive.

6.2.3 A user interface — web, native, voice, physical actuation — is a plugin, in either flavour as its resource dictates, and shall have no standing this standard distinguishes from any other plugin.

6.2.2a "Loaded from the ensemble's score" (6.2.2) is an instancing rule, not a loading mechanism: the score names the plugin, and a hive provides it by whatever means its platform allows — loading it at runtime where the platform declares that facility, or having it built into the image where the platform does not. One instance per ensemble per hive is the rule in either case.

6.2.2b A hive that cannot provide a part — for want of a load facility, a bearer, or any other platform capability the part requires — does not perform that part, and is not thereby non-conformant. The ensemble's behaviour at that subset of its parts is governed by 7.2.2.

Note 3 to entry: 6.2.2a and 6.2.2b state what 7.1.2 and 7.2.2 already imply, because read alone 6.2.2 appeared to mandate a runtime loader — which a constrained platform does not have and cannot get: native code cannot enter a flashed image at runtime without an interpreter this standard neither requires nor describes. An ensemble is performed by whichever hives currently host its parts (7.1.2); a hive that cannot host a part is simply not one of them, and an ensemble is required to define its behaviour at any subset of its parts down to one (7.2.2). Nothing further is needed — but the absence of the statement read as a requirement no small device could meet. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor).)

6.3 Reach

6.3.1 A plugin's reach shall be declared as either local — it acts only within its hive and the Reality2 network — or egress — it exchanges data with endpoints outside the trust group, over a bearer carrying non-Reality2 traffic.

6.3.2 An egress plugin's traffic does not pass the delivery gate of Layer 5 and is not gated by it: it is not a frame, and no clause of Layer 5 applies to it. The trust decision is made when an ensemble's score naming that plugin is authored and admitted to the group — once, at admission, and not per message.

6.3.3 A plugin requiring a bearer the platform does not provide is unavailable on that platform, and 6.2.2b applies.

Note 1 to entry: The reach axis is orthogonal to the ownership axis of 6.2: a plugin is hive-shared or ensemble-owned, and independently local or egress. A plugin addressing a remote API is the case that makes the axis necessary — it is exactly the non-determinism 6.1.3 requires to enter as a plugin-sourced event, which is the model working as intended, and it is simultaneously a path by which data leaves the trust group without any frame being sent. The whole of Layer 5 gates frames. This path is ungated by construction, and 6.3.2 says so rather than leaving it to be discovered: an unstated absence reads as a permission, and this one is a permission — but a declared one, whose cost is visible in the score before the ensemble is admitted.

Note 2 to entry: 6.3.3 is the ordinary consequence of Layer 1's registry and Layer 1's 4.9.4: an egress plugin generally needs an IP-carrying bearer, so a hive whose only bearer is a local radio cannot host one. That is a platform fact, not a defect, and it is why 6.2.2b exists. (Clause 6.3 added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor).)

Note 1 to entry: 6.2.1 exists because the failure is concrete: three ensembles each declaring "a web interface" cannot be three web servers fighting for one port. The singleton is the hive's; the ensembles register into it — routes, bundles, channels — and it outlives any one of them.

Note 2 to entry: 6.2.3 is a real collapse, not a labelling choice. With interfaces as plugins, the ensemble model has exactly two part types, and everything this standard says about plugin lifecycle, advertisement and delivery applies to a voice interface exactly as to a sensor driver. The interface to a human is an interface like any other.

7 Ensembles

7.1 What an ensemble is

7.1.1 An ensemble shall consist of one or more sentants and zero or more plugins, described by a score (3.6).

7.1.2 An ensemble is its score. It shall have no binary, no installation, and no home hive: it is performed by whichever hives currently host its parts.

7.1.3 An ensemble shall have a name, a class, and a version, and different versions may coexist in a group during transition.

7.2 Performance

7.2.1 An ensemble's parts shall find each other by class and event, through the discovery of Layer 2 and the delivery of the layers below — never by host address.

7.2.2 A hive joining a group and hosting parts of an ensemble extends that ensemble's performance; a hive leaving contracts it. Neither event is an error, and an ensemble shall define its behaviour at any subset of its parts, down to one.

7.2.3 An ensemble's score shall travel within its trust group as content, deliverable to a joining hive by the ordinary delivery of this standard.

Note 1 to entry: 7.2.2 is the premise as an application obligation. On a network where peers come and go, an application is a degradation gradient, not a binary — the question a score answers is not "what do I need to run" but "what do I do with what is present". A part that is absent is a part that is absent right now.

7.3 Apiary

7.3.1 An apiary is a suite of ensembles composed for a common purpose. It shall be a grouping with an identity, and shall carry no mechanism of its own — membership, delivery and trust are the group's, performance is each ensemble's.

Note 1 to entry: The apiary is deliberately mechanism-free — a name for what these ensembles are together. A functional grouping and a security boundary are different things; the trust group is the boundary, and an apiary never is.

8 Conformance

Every requirement of this document is demonstrated by conformance evidence of one of the three classes of 01-terminology.md, 3.10 — behavioural, by construction, or by declaration. A requirement whose evidence is by construction is not thereby optional: the demonstration is an inspection a third party can perform without the implementer's cooperation, and its absence is non-conformance exactly as a failed behavioural test is. Behavioural evidence for any requirement of this document carries the control 01-terminology.md 4.2 to 4.5 requires — the same procedure run against a case the requirement excludes, giving a distinguishable non-satisfying result attributable to the requirement under test; a demonstration that could not have come out any other way is not evidence. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor); the control sentence added the same day, finding SS183.)

8.1 An implementation conforms to this document where it:

a) enforces all six sentant properties (4.1), including restart of crashed sentants and atomic visibility;

b) honours the storage classes of 4.2 across restart;

c) tags every event's origin, overwrites sender-supplied origins, and supports origin filtering (5.2.3);

d) delivers class-addressed events to all matching sentants (5.1.3) and keeps instance identifiers off the wire (5.1.4);

e) runs plugins on the event contract with no second network carriage (6.1); and

f) performs ensembles from scores, at any subset of parts (7.2.2).

8.2 Conformance to sentant opacity shall be demonstrated negatively: every external query surface of the implementation is enumerated, and none returns a sentant's current state, transitions or vars.

9 Security considerations

9.1 The sentant model is the last firewall: an event that matches no handler does nothing, silently. A hive processes what its sentants declare and shrugs off the rest — there is no registry of valid events to probe and no error to learn from.

9.2 Origin tagging is trustworthy exactly because the runtime owns it (5.2.3). A compromised peer cannot forge internal origin; a compromised hive runtime can, and nothing at this layer defends a sentant from its own host.

9.3 Determinism plus event-visible inputs make a sentant's behaviour auditable and replayable — and make the event record sensitive, since it is sufficient to reconstruct behaviour. The record is internal state and is protected by opacity like everything else.

9.4 Idempotency is a security property here, not only a delivery convenience: replayed frames verify (Layer 4) and deliver (Layer 5), and 5.3.2 is what makes that harmless at the effect.

10 What is not decided at this layer

This clause states gaps and contains no requirements.

10.1 The execution model. Declared a gap at Layer 0 and owed here: whether an action may block, what time budget an invocation has, whether a plugin invocation may be re-entered, and what happens to other sentants while one executes. The determinism property constrains the answer — single-threaded per sentant is implied by 4.1.1 — but blocking, budgets and re-entrancy are unstated, and a component that has to wait for the physical world has no stated-safe way to.

10.2 The definition and score formats. 4.2.1 and 7.1.1 state what they carry; the byte-level formats, their schema, and their versioning are the companion part's, with test vectors.

10.3 The term capability. Still deliberately undefined (01-terminology.md Clause 5). This layer speaks of classes and public events; whether capability names the event, the class, or a declared set of either is unresolved, and the beacon's capability summary (Layer 2) currently summarises public events.

10.4 Resource bounds. Persistence forbids eviction (4.1.1) and nothing bounds how many sentants a hive accepts, from whom, or what happens at the platform's memory limit — the denial-of-service this invites is real and the bound is deployment policy for now.

10.5 Cross-group ensembles. Scores travel within a group (7.2.3), and entanglement delivers events across groups (Layer 5). Whether an ensemble may span groups — parts performed in two groups, joined by entanglement — is neither enabled nor forbidden, and the identity and authority questions it raises are unexamined.

10.6 Declaring an event public by design. Layer 5, 7.3.1 exempts an event that is public by design from intra-group payload encryption and states no criterion and no declaration mechanism. The exemption cannot be specified at Layer 5: 7.3.3 forbids a frame carrying any statement of which key protects it, and no key protects this is such a statement, so a receiver cannot distinguish an exempt frame from an encrypted one from the frame. The discriminator must therefore be a property of the event's definition, held by both sides before the frame exists — which makes it this layer's to declare, and 4.2.1's definition format carries no such field. The resolution path exists and is unbuilt: the event identifier hash is in clear on every frame (Layer 4, 4.2.1, 7.1.1) and unalterable by a relay (Layer 4, 4.4.2), so a receiver holding the definition resolves the question with nothing added to the wire. (Added 2026-08-04, PROVISIONAL(standard), finding SS44, whose register entry is promoted and before Roy. Until it is decided, the fail-closed reading stands: encrypt every intra-group payload and take no exemption, because the exemption is the undefined branch.)
