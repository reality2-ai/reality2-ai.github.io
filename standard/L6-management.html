Reality2 Standard — Layer 6: Management

Normative document

Introduction

Layers 0 to 5 keep a hive running and keep it belonging. Layer 6 is acting on a hive from outside it — updating what it runs, adjusting what it is configured to do, asking it what state it is in. Everything here is somebody reaching into a device, which is why this layer is shaped almost entirely by one question: who may, and how would the device know?

The answers are inherited, not invented. The delivery gate is the floor (Layer 5, 7.2), and management is precisely the traffic for which the floor is not enough — updating firmware and changing configuration are member-attributable, consequential operations, the class Layer 5 already says needs evidence beyond the gate. So this layer's mechanisms are largely arrangements of Layer 5 material: authority is a certificate role, delivery rides ordinary frames, and the one authority that is not cryptographic at all is physical possession, which outranks everything.

Three commitments organise the clauses.

A remote authority can change what a hive runs; only hands can change what it belongs to. An update arrives over the network, signed by an authority the group appointed, and replaces code. It never touches the persona. Membership moves only under physical possession — the same guard as the claim state — so the worst a fully compromised update authority achieves is pushing code the group's own process authorised, never stealing the device into another group.

A hive survives its own updates, including the failed ones. An update that boots badly rolls back by itself; an update that cannot even boot is recoverable by a resident layer beneath the stack that needs nothing from the stack to run. And because that recovery path bypasses every network authority, using it erases membership — un-bricking the hardware never hands anyone the persona.

Observation is tiered by build mode. Any device answers what are you and are you alive. Only a development device can answer show me your insides — not by policy but by construction, because the production image does not contain the code that would answer.

1 Scope

This document specifies the authority model for management operations: who may update, configure and inspect a hive, and what evidence each operation requires.

It specifies the update model: the classes of updatable content, the package and its verification, delivery over the mesh, application, health checking and rollback, and the recovery layer beneath.

It specifies the boundary between remote authority over code and physical authority over membership.

It specifies the diagnostic surfaces: what every device answers, what only a development device answers, and how both are gated.

It establishes what conformance at this layer consists of, and identifies what is not decided.

It does not specify the applications being managed, which are Layer 7, or any particular management user interface, protocol binding or tool.

3 Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the terms and definitions given in the documents listed in Clause 2 apply, together with the following.

3.1 update authority

member appointed by its trust group to sign update packages for that group

3.2 update package

signed unit of updatable content: a header naming what it is for and what it contains, a payload, and the authority's signature

3.3 sequence

monotonically increasing number carried by an update package, per target, by which older packages are refused

3.4 health check

test an updated hive applies to itself before committing to the update

3.5 recovery layer

resident facility beneath the stack, requiring nothing from the stack, by which physical possession restores a device to a working image

3.6 management surface

any interface by which a party outside a hive reads its state or changes its behaviour

4 Authority

4.1 Who may manage

4.1.1 A management operation shall be accepted only from a party presenting evidence the operation's class requires, and the delivery gate alone shall not authorise any management operation.

4.1.1a The evidence each operation class requires shall be:

Operation class Required evidence
public inspection — the identity-and-health summary of 6.1.1 none; any asker, read-only
internal inspection — routing tables, neighbour data, telemetry (6.2) verified member of the hive's own group, on a development-mode device only
configure — parameters and declarative definitions member-attributable evidence (Layer 5, 7.4)
update — any content class of 5.1 an update authority's signature (4.1.2) and the sequence rule of 5.2.3
authority appointment or revocation a group act, recorded as certificate role and revocation at Layer 5 (4.1.4)
membership or claim-state mutation physical possession (4.2.3)
cross-group management entanglement at grade 3, with member-attributable evidence per act (L5A-entanglement.md, 9.1 and 9.2)

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

4.1.2 Update packages shall be accepted only under a valid signature of an update authority (3.1) of the receiving hive's group, or of a group whose entanglement with the receiving group carries management scope at the required grade.

Note 1 to entry: The required grade is grade 3 — an entanglement whose scope includes management operations is at that grade, names the managing group, the managed surface and the operations granted explicitly and exhaustively, and every management crossing carries member-attributable evidence regardless of the entanglement's other terms (L5A-entanglement.md, 9.1 and 9.2). The channel keys alone never authorise a management operation.

4.1.3 A group shall be able to enumerate its current update authorities on demand, from a signed, versioned artefact, without network access to any issuer — and a holder list without its version shall be rejected as indistinguishable from an arbitrarily old one.

4.1.4 Appointing and revoking an update authority shall be group acts, recorded as certificate role and revocation at Layer 5.

Note 1 to entry: 4.1.1 is Layer 5's floor rule applied to the traffic that most needs it. Every operation in this document is member-attributable and consequential — the class for which passage of the gate was explicitly declared insufficient.

Note 2 to entry: 4.1.3 exists because a deny-list alone cannot be managed. Revocation says who was removed; nothing that cannot list who currently holds authority can notice that the list is wrong — and a permission model that has to phone home to find out is not one a mesh that is mostly offline can use.

4.2 The two authorities, and the line between them

4.2.1 A remote management operation may change what a hive runs and how it is configured. It shall not alter the hive's persona, its membership, or its claim state.

4.2.2 An update shall not write the persona's storage, and an implementation's update path shall be constructed so that it cannot — not merely instructed that it may not.

4.2.3 Membership mutation shall require physical possession, as Layer 5, 4.4 requires for the claim state.

Note 1 to entry: The reason this is a security rule rather than tidiness: compromising the update authority already lets an attacker push code the group authorised — that is the accepted blast radius of appointing one. What has to remain impossible remotely is moving the device between groups: an update path that could write persona would let a compromised authority steal devices into a group nobody's hands ever touched. 4.2.2's by-construction demand is the difference between a rule and a property.

5 The update model

5.1 What is updatable

5.1.1 A hive's updatable content shall be classed as: the stack (the runtime itself), modules alongside it, declarative definitions, and parameters — each independently deliverable where the platform separates them.

5.1.2 On a platform where these classes collapse into one image, an update of any class is an update of the image, and the model is unchanged.

Note 1 to entry: The classes exist because their costs differ — a parameter change touches a running value, a stack change reboots the device — and a platform that cannot separate them pays the largest cost for every change. That is a platform property, declared at Layer 0, not a protocol difference.

5.2 The package

5.2.1 A package shall carry: what it targets (a class of devices, a group, or one hive), its content class (5.1), its sequence (3.3), a digest of its payload, and the authority's signature over all of it.

5.2.2 A hive shall verify the signature and the digest before applying, and equally before advertising or serving the package to anyone else.

5.2.3 A hive shall refuse a package whose sequence is not strictly greater than the sequence it currently runs for that target — for application, for advertisement, and for serving alike.

5.2.3a The record a hive keeps of that sequence shall carry a validity discriminator — a magic value, a checksum, or another proof that the record was written by this mechanism — and a hive shall verify it before reading the sequence.

5.2.3b A hive that cannot read a valid sequence record for a target shall not treat any value as that target's sequence floor, and shall report the record as unreadable rather than refusing packages against it.

5.2.3c A hive shall set a target's recorded sequence only to the sequence of a package it has verified under 5.2.2 and applied. It shall not set that record to a chosen value, and shall provide no means by which a caller may.

Note 1 to entry: The record follows the running image, and never leads it. Applied is the load-bearing word of 5.2.3c and the gap between verified and applied is a whole power cycle: stage sequence n, raise the floor to n, boot, fail the health checks, revert. The device now runs n−1 while its record claims n, so the one package that could repair it — a corrected re-issue of n — is no longer strictly greater and is refused for ever; only n+1 is acceptable, and n+1 may not exist. The device has reverted itself out of reach of its own fix.

The same hazard reaches the two halves of committing, which are separately durable and not order-symmetric: release the prior image first and lose power, and the floor stays low — the package is simply re-accepted, costing airtime. Raise the floor first and lose power, and the floor is n while the slot is still pending, so a later health failure reverts to n−1 beneath a floor of n: the same soft brick, by power cut rather than by choice. Hence: leave the floor behind the running image, never ahead of it.

Note 0 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 1 to entry: 5.2.3a and 5.2.3b exist because the alternative was observed on metal: two devices refused every update, permanently and silently. Their anti-rollback record validated nothing, and stale bytes decoded as an enormous sequence floor — against which no genuine package could ever be strictly greater. The failure is unusually dangerous for two reasons. A refusal against a corrupt floor is indistinguishable, from outside, from having no update available; and the obvious defence does not work, because a flash driver that read-modify-writes a whole sector leaves a real record and foreign bytes coexisting, so record presence cannot be inferred from erased-sector signatures. Only a discriminator written by the mechanism itself distinguishes my record from bytes that happen to be here.

5.2.4 A hive shall not reveal, to a party whose package's signature has not been verified, whether the package would have been accepted on any other ground — not by an error, and not by a distinguishable behaviour.

Note 1 to entry: 5.2.2's second half is what bounds an epidemic to authentic packages: nothing an honest hive could not verify is ever re-advertised, so a forged or corrupt image cannot recruit honest hives as its distributors.

Note 2 to entry: 5.2.3 is this standard's second monotonic refusal (the first guards the claim state) and it is the anti-replay for updates: yesterday's genuinely-signed package is this morning's downgrade attack, and refusing on sequence needs no clock — which matters on platforms that have none.

Note 3 to entry: 5.2.4 closes a quiet oracle. A reject that says wrong target class or arrives observably faster than a signature check has told an unauthenticated party something about the device. Silence toward the unverified is uniform: no reason, no differential timing.

5.3 Delivery — ordinary frames or an optional bulk service, pulled by the needy

5.3.1 Subject to the installation-path restrictions of 5.6, update delivery shall use ordinary frames and the ordinary mesh, except for payload carried by an explicitly selected optional bulk service meeting Layer 7, 6.4; no delivery mechanism of this clause shall require a new capability at Layers 1 to 4.

Note 0 to entry: The exception changes payload carriage, not update authority or the image lifecycle. Advertisement, recipient pull, session authorization and application results remain ordinary events. A bulk service is an additional declared platform facility, not a mandatory extension to a Reality2 bearer. Its absence does not imply permission to fall back silently to another service or route. The signed package, target and sequence checks of 5.2 and the staging/health/rollback rules of 5.4 apply regardless of the selected payload carriage. A service transport acknowledgment is not an image-verification or boot-health result.

5.3.2 A hive holding a verified package should advertise its availability — a small frame naming the package, its target, its sequence and its size — and the advertisement shall never carry the payload.

5.3.3 A hive that needs an advertised package shall pull it; a package shall not be pushed unrequested into the mesh.

5.3.4 A hive that does not need a package it has verified should re-advertise it, and may serve it, so that the package spreads to needing devices beyond the origin's reach.

Note 0 to entry: 5.2.2 and 5.3.5 together require a hive to read a held image back out of its own storage — verification before serving, and any holder answering a pull, cannot both hold for a multi-megabyte image on a device with a few hundred kilobytes of memory unless the image can be read back in pieces. Neither clause says so and both depend on it, so the platform capability is assumed silently. An implementer should expect to need it; the platform declaration of Layer 0 is where it would be stated. The seam has since been built: Layer 0, 8.2.1 now carries P11 — whether a stored image can be read back in pieces bounded by available memory — so the capability is no longer assumed silently. This note is retained for the reasoning that produced the parameter, and no longer states a live gap. Amended 2026-08-06: a gap statement outlives its own closure, and a note naming a filled hole is a false signal pointing at solved ground.)

5.3.5 A pulling hive may retrieve a package in pieces, from any holder, over any interval its bearers' budgets dictate; it shall reassemble, verify the whole as 5.2.2 requires, and only then apply. It may request specifically the pieces it lacks, and a holder receiving such a request should resend those pieces.

Note 3 to entry: Package distribution and caching do not establish installation permission. For levels 3 and 4, 5.6 restricts delivery into the receiving hive's executable staging area to direct non-LoRa proximity or local USB. A holder can relay or cache a package for a maintainer without becoming an installation path.

Note 1 to entry: For remotely admissible content under 5.6, 5.3.5 is the delivery model for the device every other mechanism cannot reach: the sensor whose only bearer is slow, regulated, and asleep most of the day. Pieces arrive as the airtime budget permits — days are conformant, not degraded — the assembler holds its own partial state across sleeps, asks for what it lacks when awake, and any holder may answer. Reliability is end-to-end repair by the party that noticed: the missing set only shrinks, and a lossy path costs rounds, never correctness. No acknowledgement exists anywhere beneath it.

Note 2 to entry: The push prohibition in 5.3.3 is the mesh protecting itself. A package is the largest thing this standard moves; the decision to spend that bandwidth belongs to the device that needs it, which alone knows its version, its storage and its budget.

5.4 Applying, and surviving the application

5.4.1 A platform able to stage an update alongside the running image shall apply by staging, switching, and keeping the prior image until the update is committed.

5.4.1c A hive shall determine the image it is executing from the platform's report of the image it booted, never from a record of the image that was configured to boot. A hive shall not erase or overwrite the image it is executing.

Note 1 to entry: The two answers differ exactly after a fallback — which is to say after a revert, which is when this protection matters most. A bootloader that cannot start the configured image starts another, and a record naming the configured one is then correct about the configuration and wrong about the world (01-terminology.md 5.4). A guard keyed on the configured image therefore protects the wrong slot precisely in the situation 5.4.3 exists to survive, and permits erasing the executing image — a damage path, not a diagnostic one.

Note 2 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

5.4.1a A platform declaring the capability of 5.4.1 shall verify at boot that the storage slots that capability requires are present, and shall declare itself update-incapable where they are not.

Note 0 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 1 to entry: A platform may require metadata inside the image itself for its own slot management — an ESP32-S3 bootloader, for instance, refuses to boot an OTA slot whose image carries no application descriptor. The staging model of this clause is implementable on such platforms, but a package's payload is not portable across them: an image is built for a platform, and the platform declaration (Layer 0, Clause 8) is where that requirement is visible. Nothing in this standard assumes a raw binary.

5.4.2 An updated hive shall run its health checks (3.4) within a bounded window, and shall commit the update only when they pass.

5.4.1b At most one target shall hold a pending update across any one reboot. A hive holding a pending update for one target shall refuse to stage an update for another until that one is committed or reverted. A hive refusing on this ground should report the refusal, distinguishably from any other reason it might not have taken the update.

Note 1 to entry: Without this bound, a health window that passed for the running image can promote a different target's pending sequence — one staged and never health-checked. The floor for that target then rises to a package whose health was never established, and 5.2.3 refuses every earlier package for it thereafter: the brick of 5.2.3c reached through a third route, neither a chosen value nor a mis-timed write but a misdirected authorisation. The permission is real, it gates that a commit happens, and it does not gate which.

Note 2 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 3 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 4 to entry: The release condition is load-bearing and is not decoration. Committed or reverted names the two ways a pending update ends; a hive that held the bound without releasing on both would take exactly one update for ever — a brick reached by over-enforcing a rule written to prevent one. An implementation should have a control that fails on that alone. (An implementation of this clause reported exactly such a test: refusal from staging, staged and health-window states; permission from idle, unknown position, committed and reverted.)

5.4.2a A hive whose image position is unreadable shall accept and stage an update, and shall not commit one.

Note -1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 0 to entry: Where the platform holds no readable record of the running image's position, no health window is opened at all — the unreadable case of 5.2.3b has a distinct correct action, not merely a distinct name. Opening a window for an image the platform cannot account for would check nothing against nothing, and the window's own deadline would then revert a perfectly good image for failing to report on itself. Folding that case into pending is therefore actively harmful rather than merely imprecise. Verified on the reference platform, where a plain flash write leaves no valid record at all — so this is the first state a freshly provisioned board exercises, not a rare edge, and a conformance suite starting only from a clean confirmed image never reaches it.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 2 to entry: Erasing the update record is not a way to reach the unprovisioned state, on such a platform, and an implementer or operator reaching for it will appear to succeed: the erase works, and the next boot destroys the state it produced. This is recorded here because it is a procedure people invent independently — it reads as the obvious way to get a clean device — and it silently produces the opposite of what it claims.

5.4.2b A hive shall verify at boot that its recorded image position agrees with the slots it names — that a slot recorded as holding a pending image holds one — and shall treat a disagreement as a position of its own: opening no health window, committing nothing, and reporting the disagreement. Such a hive shall accept and stage an update.

Note 1 to entry: This is 5.4.1a's principle applied to the update record instead of to the partition table: a platform's own assertion about its configuration is not evidence of that configuration. 5.4.1a covers slot presence and 5.4.2 Note 0 covers an unreadable position; a readable position that is false was covered by neither, and it is reachable — an interrupted copy leaves the staged slot erased while the record still names it as pending.

Note 2 to entry: The failure this prevents is a health check that tests the wrong image and cannot tell. Where the named slot cannot boot, the bootloader falls back to the prior image; the record still says pending, so a health window opens against an image that is perfectly healthy and the checks pass. The update commits, and the floor advances to a sequence whose image was never applied — the device runs n−1 beneath a floor of n and refuses package n, the one that would repair it, for ever. A fourth route to 5.2.3c's brick, after a chosen value (Note 0 there), a mis-timed write (Note 1 there) and a misdirected authorisation (5.4.1b Note 1).

Note 3 to entry: The structural point generalises beyond this clause and is the reason it is stated at all. A validity discriminator — 5.2.3b's magic value, a checksum — protects a record against corruption, not against being false. It establishes that the mechanism that owns the record wrote these bytes; it establishes nothing about whether the bytes still describe the world. No checksum catches a true-looking lie. 01-terminology.md 5.4 states the general obligation this clause instances.

Note 4 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

5.4.3 An updated hive whose health checks fail, or that fails to boot the new image, shall revert to the prior image without external help.

5.4.4 A hive that reverted should report the reversion and its reason when next able, and the reason should be distinguishable from the adjacent ones — in particular, the health checks did not pass and the health checks could not be run are different facts.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

5.4.5 Applying an update shall preserve the persona (4.2) and the device's claim state, across success, failure and reversion alike.

Note 1 to entry: 5.4.3's without external help is the load-bearing phrase. The devices this standard cares most about are exactly the ones nobody can reach when an update goes wrong — a rollback that needs a hand on the device is, for them, a brick with paperwork.

Note 2 to entry: Permission to stage under 5.4.2a or 5.4.2b does not waive management evidence or the installation-path restrictions of 5.6. Unreadable image position is distinct from an unauthorized update source or path.

5.5 The recovery layer

5.5.1 A device shall have a recovery layer (3.5): resident, independent of the stack, by which physical possession restores a working image when nothing else can.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

5.5.2 The recovery layer shall not be reachable by any remote path, and shall not be able to authorise or apply a mesh-delivered update.

5.5.3 A recovery re-image shall erase the device's persona as part of the recovery write, returning the device to a group of itself — and the erasure shall be crash-safe: interrupted recovery leaves either the complete prior persona or a complete fresh state, never a partial one.

Note 1 to entry: A recovery re-image does not, by itself, make the device claimable. The recovered device holds a fresh, closed group of itself; becoming OPEN still requires the distinct local reset action of Layer 5, 4.4. Un-bricking and un-claiming are two physical acts, deliberately not one.

Note 2 to entry: 5.5.3 is what stops the un-brick path being a sovereignty bypass. A recovery write bypasses every authority this layer defines — that is its purpose — so a recovered device comes back as nobody's: flashing a stolen device rescues the hardware and erases exactly the membership the thief wanted. The physical path may un-brick the device; it may never hand over the persona.

5.5.3a A device's persona disposition shall be determinable at boot from durable storage alone, and shall be self-validating — that is, a complete record is distinguishable from an incomplete one by reading that storage and nothing else. A transition from one disposition to another shall be effected such that at every instant one complete disposition is selected — the prior one before the transition, the new one after it.

Note 1 to entry: 5.5.3's crash safety is not achievable by intent, and this clause is what makes it achievable. No writer can promise power will not be lost mid-write; it can only arrange that no partial state is ever the selected one. The usual construction — two generation-numbered self-validating records, the writer targeting whichever is not currently selected — has that property because the transition is the completion of the validating field, not the start of the write. A platform whose durable storage offers an atomic transition of its own may satisfy this clause without two records; the construction is given in full, informatively, at FORMATS.md 5b.

Note 2 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 3 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

5.5.4 Where no complete persona disposition can be read — for any cause, including damage arising outside recovery — the device shall treat itself as holding a fresh persona: a closed group of itself, as after a recovery re-image. The device should report that condition distinguishably from a persona it was never given.

Note 1 to entry: 5.5.3 covers interrupted recovery and nothing else. A persona region can also be damaged by flash wear, by age, or by a write from something else, and a reader still has to decide. The two readings are not symmetric. Read as present, a partial persona acts as a complete membership — the sovereignty bypass 5.5.3 Note 2 exists to close. Read as fresh, the device loses its group: a denial of service against its own owner, but an attacker gains nothing, since a recovered device is nobody's already. One reading loses a membership; the other hands one over, and that is the whole of the argument.

Note 2 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 3 to entry: The behavioural fold here is deliberate and the diagnostic one is not. A device that never held a persona is nobody's as a matter of fact, not as a fallback — a definite answer reached by a different route — and although the stack cannot distinguish the two, whoever investigates a device can and must. That is 01-terminology.md 5.3's test applied correctly: indistinguishable to the machinery and indistinguishable to whoever acts on it are different questions, and only the second one forbids the fold.

5.6 Change levels and ensemble update policy

A change level describes what an operation changes and how it can reach the receiving hive. It is not a trust grade, certificate role, storage class or additional content-class code in the package of 5.2. The content classes of 5.1 remain unchanged.

Level Changed content Permitted installation path
1 — Parameters Values exposed by the installed application through supported configuration operations; no definition or executable replacement Remote TN management, direct proximity or local USB
2 — Sentants Separately replaceable declarative sentant definitions within an admitted ensemble, using compatible plugins already installed on the receiving hive Remote TN management, direct proximity or local USB
3 — Firmware ensembles Firmware-managed ensembles and application plugins, including a newly required plugin Verified executable release through direct proximity or local USB
4 — Core firmware TN stack, runtime, security enforcement, update verifier or rollback machinery Verified executable release through direct proximity or local USB

Here firmware includes the equivalent installed executable on a hosted platform. A platform that separates modules can update the affected executable module; a platform that combines the content classes follows 5.1.2. A combined image that changes core code, or whose application/core change boundary the receiving hive cannot establish, is level 4. The physical recovery layer of 5.5 remains separate and is never made remotely reachable by this table. Local USB installation here is an ordinary authorized update, not the persona-erasing recovery re-image of 5.5.3.

5.6.1 A receiving hive shall classify a requested change by its actual content and effects at the highest applicable level in the table, independently of the sender's claimed level, file extension or package label.

5.6.2 A hive shall determine which ensemble definitions and parameter operations are separately changeable from its locally held admission policy, with an unclassified definition treated as firmware managed and no candidate able to promote its own permissions.

Note 1 to entry: The two ensemble policies are remotely replaceable (eligible for level 2) and firmware managed (replacement requires level 3 or, where core content is involved, level 4). Both can expose level 1 parameter operations. Changing this policy is part of the installed enforcement machinery, not an ordinary parameter operation. An ensemble name or a score signature alone does not establish permission to invoke a protected service.

5.6.3 A hive shall refuse a level 1 operation that is not an admitted parameter operation or that changes a definition, plugin binding, executable or change-admission policy.

5.6.4 A hive shall refuse a requested level 2 replacement if it affects a firmware-managed definition, introduces executable code, or requires a plugin, capability or command contract not already installed and compatible on that hive.

Note 2 to entry: A newly required plugin needs level 3 or level 4 installation before a definition-only replacement can use it. This is admission of a requested replacement, not a requirement that every part of an ensemble run on every hive. Layer 7, 6.2.2b and 7.2.2 continue to govern performance at a subset of parts; an unsupported replacement does not remove already working parts. A missing optional part does not become a required plugin merely because it exists in the score.

5.6.5 A hive shall receive a level 3 or level 4 payload into its executable update staging area only through an authorized direct proximity session or a local USB connection, rather than through an intermediary hive or a remote IP management path.

5.6.6 A hive admitting a direct proximity session shall establish its current direct peer association from authenticated endpoint binding and actual platform service observations, without accepting a TN hop limit, claimed peer address or sender assertion as proof, and cease accepting payload when that association is lost.

Note 3 to entry: Direct proximity means a direct local non-LoRa radio bearer or platform service over that radio between the updating and receiving hives, without forwarding by another hive. Components of one complex hive do not thereby become intermediary hives. This is not a cryptographic proof of distance or immunity to physical-layer relays. The selected service profile still determines additional radio, rate and route restrictions. An IP/TCP/UDP path, a shared LAN or an IP address alone does not satisfy this condition; IP-specific installation admission is not specified by this clause and supplies no exception. Package distribution to a maintainer or cache can use 5.3's ordinary mesh; it does not authorize that cache or recipient to move bytes into an executable slot remotely. A held package can be installed later when the direct association is available. Incomplete transfer does not discard an otherwise valid prior image or move a sequence floor.

5.6.7 A hive shall apply the management evidence required by 4.1.1a and, where the operation is an update, all target, signature and sequence checks of 5.2, regardless of its change level or use of proximity or USB.

Note 4 to entry: Levels 1 and 2 do not confer anonymous or merely group-gated configuration authority. A configure operation and an update package retain their respective evidence requirements; scores also retain FORMATS 7.9 signing requirements. Levels 3 and 4 are executable updates, not configure operations. No level permits remote membership or claim-state mutation.

5.6.8 Before disturbing a working definition, a hive shall validate the complete proposed level 2 replacement against its admitted schema, capability and command contracts, resource bounds, storage requirements and change policy.

5.6.9 A hive providing level 2 replacement shall stage the candidate separately, retain the prior working definition and recoverable state until commit, and supervise a bounded trial whose required health checks include continued operation of the authorized maintenance/update path.

5.6.10 A hive whose level 2 trial fails, times out or is interrupted before commit shall restore the prior working definition and recoverable state without external help, with power loss leaving a complete recoverable selection rather than a partially selected definition.

Note 5 to entry: This is a definition transaction, not a second pending firmware image; 5.4.1b's restriction on pending image targets remains. A platform unable to stage, supervise and recover a separate definition treats it as firmware managed. A successful plugin invocation or data receipt alone is not trial health. Levels 3 and 4 retain 5.4's image health, rollback and persona protection.

5.6.11 A hive shall prevent level 1 and level 2 changes, including indirect plugin invocations and resource consumption, from replacing, disabling or taking control of its admission verifier, trial supervisor, rollback mechanism or essential maintenance entry, with recovery independent of candidate sentant cooperation.

5.6.12 A hive replacing sentant definitions shall use the explicit lifecycle of Layer 7, 4.3 with a declared state restart or compatible state migration that preserves the declared storage-class obligations, prevents stale completions from reaching replacement instances and leaves running definitions immutable.

Note 6 to entry: A retained rollback package is not a second live persisted sentant definition; unloading still removes the latter under Layer 7, 4.3.2. Recovery can load the retained version as a new instance. State migration does not create an external state-query interface or relax sentant opacity. Firmware identity and ensemble/definition versions are distinct; a management interface should distinguish proposed, staged, trial, committed and reverted versions without reporting a prepared package as installed.

5.6.13 A level 3 or level 4 release shall include one authenticated, versioned installation bundle containing its required executable components, plugins and sentant definitions together with a complete parameter installation policy specifying supplied values or defaults and retention or migration of existing device-specific values.

5.6.14 A hive applying such a release shall stage and select the compatible bundle as one transaction, run health checks against that selected combination, and restore the prior compatible combination on rollback without requiring follow-up configuration messages to make either combination operable.

Note 7 to entry: Physical writes can be sequential; the selected release is coherent at every recoverable activation boundary. A bundle's parameter policy can retain each device's existing name, location and reading interval, so the same release can serve multiple device identities. Persona and claim-state preservation remains mandatory under 5.4.5. Referenced pre-existing components need authenticated version/digest binding and locally verified presence before the bundle can be considered complete. Platform-specific packaging remains inside the authenticated payload of FORMATS Clause 5; change levels do not add an unsigned manifest alongside it. Separately replaceable level 2 definitions remain eligible for later admitted changes after the firmware bundle commits.

5.6.15 Both endpoints of a level 3 or level 4 proximity transfer shall observe and authenticate their current direct radio peer, bind the observations to the intended hive, and exclude LoRa from the payload transfer regardless of its apparent reach or directness.

Note 8 to entry: A LoRa wake or discovery event can precede the update. It does not qualify the transfer path; the updater subsequently establishes the direct non-LoRa association. Neither a repeater that hears the target nor a relayed radio report substitutes for the updater observing the target's own radio. The USB alternative in 5.6.5 remains independent of radio availability.

5.6.16 A hive applying or reverting an update shall preserve its device configuration, including existing application JSON fields such as name, location and timing, through a declared lossless migration or retention policy, with a recoverable prior configuration for rollback and no implicit reset to release defaults.

Note 9 to entry: An explicitly authorized parameter change or reset is a separate intent. New parameters can receive declared defaults; existing values and unrelated metadata survive a normal update. Keeping configuration in a separate region is one implementation; snapshot and restore is another. Neither permits partial configuration to become active after interrupted writes. Persona keys and claim state retain their separate protected storage and authority rules.

5.6.17 A hive shall represent all parameters declared by its hosted ensembles in its device JSON configuration document, with declared types, bounds and defaults, and use the admitted values as the authoritative runtime parameter set.

Note 10 to entry: The logical JSON document can have a platform-specific durable encoding and bounded capacity. Distinct ensembles need unambiguous parameter names or namespaces. The document is separate from the score schema; a JSON key is not permission to replace an automation, bind a plugin or change admission policy. Runtime implementation constants and protected identity material are not ensemble parameters. This document exposes declared configuration, not a sentant's private vars or current state; sentants consume changes through events under Layer 7, 5.1.1, without a new state-query interface. A platform unable to represent a required parameter does not silently run that ensemble with a hidden substitute.

5.6.18 A hive shall apply an authorized and validated parameter-document change at the ensemble's declared safe application boundary without requiring firmware replacement, preserving the prior effective values until that boundary.

5.6.19 A hive reporting a parameter change to an authorized manager shall distinguish durable acceptance from runtime application, and distinguish either from refusal, deferred application or failure.

Note 11 to entry: A reading period can take effect after the active acquisition; a name can take effect without that delay. A stored revision alone proves neither outcome. Admission and parameter schemas, not arbitrary JSON mutation, define which changes are accepted. A failed update cannot be presented as an applied parameter set merely because the portal retained its proposed JSON.

Note 12 to entry: Before updating, an authorized maintainer can export the confirmed device JSON together with hive identity, configuration schema and revision, and active firmware/ensemble versions. This is a configuration backup, not an export of protected identity keys or a license to replay stale management proofs. Restoration is a newly authorized, validated operation. A pending portal proposal is not the device's confirmed configuration.

6 Diagnostics

6.1 The public tier — every device

6.1.1 Every hive shall answer, to any asker, its identity-and-health summary: what it is (its class), its build mode as Layer 2 declares it, whether it is alive, and its firmware identity.

6.1.2 The public tier shall contain no relationship data: nothing about who the hive talks to, relays for, or has seen.

6.1.3 The firmware identity of 6.1.1 shall be the identity of the running image's update package — its target class, its per-target sequence, and the digest of its image bytes — in the shape FORMATS.md 5c gives. A hive that cannot determine one of the three shall report that it cannot, and shall not report a substitute value.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

6.1a The member tier — self-health on any device

6.1a.1 A hive shall answer, only to a verified member of its own group, its self-health summary: its stored-energy state where it runs on stored energy, the time since it last restarted, the state of its own storage, and the number of peers it currently holds links to, as a count and never as a list.

6.1a.2 The member tier shall contain no relationship data: nothing about who the hive talks to, relays for, or has seen.

6.1a.3 An asker that is not a verified member of the hive's own group shall receive nothing — no response, no error.

6.1a.4 The code answering a member-tier query may be present in a production image.

6.1a.5 A hive that cannot determine a field of 6.1a.1 shall report that it cannot, and shall not report a substitute value.

Note 0 to entry: ‼ THIS TIER EXISTS BECAUSE THE HARM THIS STANDARD NAMES IS NOT THE ONE THAT WAS BEING PREVENTED. 6.2.3 Note 2 states it exactly — the aggregation of who-talks-to-whom is the harm — and a battery level is not that. Telemetry was answerable only from a development device not because a device's own energy state is a topology fact, but because there was nowhere else to put it: the public tier answers any asker (6.1.1) and the internal tier is development-only (6.2.1), so a fact that is neither safe for strangers nor dangerous to members had no home.

Note 1 to entry: The gap this closes is an operator's, and it had only two exits, both bad. Managing a production fleet — knowing which device is about to run out of power, which has filled its store — required either flashing development images, which Layer 2, 5.4a.1 makes every device announce in every beacon on every bearer for the rest of its life, or having no health data at all. The choice between lying about what a fleet is and being unable to see it is not a choice a standard should force.

Note 1a to entry: ‼ A COUNT IS A FACT ABOUT YOURSELF; A LIST IS AN EDGE IN A GRAPH. I can hear four devices is self-health and aggregates into a fleet view that shows which devices are isolated and which are well connected. I can hear A, B, C and D reconstructs the network, and it is the asset that grows more valuable the more of it an attacker holds — which is why 6.2.3 Note 2 names aggregation, not observation, as the harm. So the degree is admitted here and the edges are not, and that boundary is the reason 6.1a.2 can be stated at all.

⚠ AND THIS IS WHY APPROXIMATE LOCATION DOES NOT FOLLOW. Position derived from which peers a device hears is the edge list, arrived at by another name: a member tier carrying it would ship edge-answering code in every production image behind a membership check, so one compromised member, or one stolen device key, maps the whole fleet. A deployment that wants position at this tier states it as a configured fact about the device — where it was installed — which is not relationship data and is answerable without any peer being named.

Note 2 to entry: ‼ 6.1a.2 IS LOAD-BEARING AND ITS ABSENCE WOULD UNDO 6.2.1. The internal tier's protection is that the answering code is absent from a production image, not disabled in it (6.2.1). A relationship fact admitted to this tier would ship that code in production behind nothing but a membership check — which is exactly the runtime check 6.2.1 Note 0 refuses. The bound is the tier, and widening it later would silently convert a membership check into the topology oracle 6.2.2's silence exists to prevent.

Source note omitted from this reading edition.

6.2 The internal tier — development devices only

6.2.1 Internal state — routing tables, neighbour data, event flow, telemetry — shall be answerable only by a development-mode device, and the code that answers shall be absent from a production image, not disabled in it.

Note 0 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

6.2.2 An internal-tier query shall be answered only for a verified member of the hive's own group; any other asker shall receive nothing — no response, no error.

6.2.3 Internal-tier data shall not be forwarded to any unauthenticated party, and shall not be aggregated across devices except under the same operator authority that may query it.

Note 1 to entry: 6.2.2's silence is deliberate on both counts. Dev-ness itself is public (the beacon declares it), so silence is not hiding that a development device exists — it is refusing to be a topology oracle or an amplification surface: an unauthenticated asker gets zero bytes, not even an error to time.

Note 2 to entry: 6.1.2 and 6.2.3 are the same rule at two tiers — the aggregation of who-talks-to-whom is the harm this standard's privacy position names, and no diagnostic convenience overrides it.

7 Management surfaces

7.1 A hive may expose a management surface (3.6) — an API for query and configuration — and every such surface shall enforce the authority model of Clause 4: no unauthenticated remote surface shall exist in a production image, with exactly one carve-out: the public tier of 6.1, which is read-only, answers only the identity-and-health summary of 6.1.1, carries no relationship data (6.1.2), and accepts no operation.

Note 0 to entry: The carve-out is not a loophole but the boundary drawn exactly: the public tier answers any asker by design (6.1.1) and changes nothing on the device, so it is a diagnostics answer, not a management surface — anything that reads more, or writes anything, is under Clause 4 authority without exception.

7.2 A management surface reachable beyond the device itself shall authenticate its callers as members, and shall scope what each caller may do by the same evidence classes as any other management operation.

7.3 A bench or commissioning tool holding local, physical access to devices is an operator-authority surface; it shall not be reachable as a remote service.

Note 1 to entry: The protocol binding of a surface — query language, transport, encoding — is deliberately unspecified. What is specified is who gets an answer, and that no image ships a surface whose default is anyone.

7.4 Where a caller presenting the evidence Clause 4 requires names an operation the hive does not recognise, the surface shall return an answer distinguishable from no answer.

7.5 Where such a caller names an operation the hive recognises and its platform cannot perform, the surface shall return an answer distinguishable from the answer 7.4 requires.

7.6 A surface shall not satisfy 7.5 by treating the operation as one of another class.

Note 2 to entry: 7.4 and 7.5 are about the AUTHORISED caller and change nothing for anybody else. 6.2.2 stands exactly as written — an internal-tier query from any other asker receives nothing, no response and no error — and 9.4's posture is untouched: an unauthenticated party still learns nothing from a hive's refusals. The reasons 6.2.2 gives are refusing to be a topology oracle and refusing to be an amplification surface, and a verified member of the hive's own group is neither of those things.

Note 3 to entry: This does not reach Layer 7, 9.1, and the two must not be collapsed. An event that matches no handler does nothing, silently, because the sender may be probing and there is no registry of valid events to probe — that reason is about an unauthenticated sender on a mesh. It does not reach a caller who has already presented the evidence Clause 4 requires. Two silences, different grounds, and only one of them travels.

Note 4 to entry: What 7.6 forbids is the repair an implementer reaches for first, and it is the one that makes a test agree with the defect. A platform that cannot perform an operation is tempted to record it as an operation of some other class — a malformed request, an unknown verb, a diagnostic — and a fixture written against that relabelling then passes, because the surface and the fixture agree about a thing neither of them did. The gated case is discharged by saying it is unsupported, and by nothing else.

8 Conformance

Every requirement of this document is demonstrated by conformance evidence of one of the three classes of 01-terminology.md, 3.10 — behavioural, by construction, or by declaration. A requirement whose evidence is by construction is not thereby optional: the demonstration is an inspection a third party can perform without the implementer's cooperation, and its absence is non-conformance exactly as a failed behavioural test is. Behavioural evidence for any requirement of this document carries the control 01-terminology.md 4.2 to 4.5 requires — the same procedure run against a case the requirement excludes, giving a distinguishable non-satisfying result attributable to the requirement under test; a demonstration that could not have come out any other way is not evidence. (PROVISIONAL(supervisor))

8.1 An implementation conforms to this document where it:

a) accepts management operations only with the evidence Clause 4 requires, and can enumerate its authorities as 4.1.3 requires;

b) preserves persona and claim state across every update outcome (4.2, 5.4.5);

c) verifies before applying, advertising and serving; refuses non-increasing sequences; and reveals nothing to the unverified (5.2);

d) delivers as Clause 5.3 permits and never pushes unrequested packages;

e) applies with self-reversion (5.4) and carries a conforming recovery layer (5.5); and

f) tiers its diagnostics as Clause 6 requires, with the internal tier structurally absent from production images.

8.2 Conformance to 5.4.3 shall be demonstrated by deliberately applying an update whose health checks fail: the device reverts unaided, and its persona is intact after reversion.

9 Security considerations

9.1 The update authority's compromise is bounded by design: code the group's process authorised can be pushed; the device cannot be re-homed (4.2). The residual is real — authorised-but-malicious code — and is bounded by the group's own appointment and revocation of authorities, which is why 4.1.3's enumeration exists.

9.2 Sequence monotonicity (5.2.3) prevents downgrade by replay of genuinely signed packages, without requiring any clock.

9.3 The recovery layer is a deliberate hole in every remote authority, guarded physically (5.5.2) and stripped of membership value (5.5.3).

9.4 The confirmation-oracle discipline (5.2.4) and diagnostic silence (6.2.2) are one posture: an unauthenticated party learns nothing from a hive's refusals — not existence, not eligibility, not timing.

10 What is not decided at this layer

This clause states gaps and contains no requirements.

10.1 The chunked-transfer profile. 5.3.5 states the model; the piece naming, the missing-pieces request encoding, piece size relative to bearer budgets, and whether the mechanism is one generic facility shared with application bulk transfer (a sensor's image or sound recording travelling the same way, in the other direction) are owed to a companion part. The ordinary-frame facility is direction-agnostic, with firmware as one profile of it; its bytes remain unresolved here. Optional bulk-service profiles under Layer 7, 6.4 declare their own carriage without changing the package format or making such a service a prerequisite for ordinary-frame delivery.

10.2 The authority-enumeration artefact. 4.1.3 states the required properties (signed, versioned, offline-verifiable, staleness-detectable); its encoding and distribution shape are undecided. The silence is inherited, as 10.12's is. Three antecedent management surfaces were read for this clause and not one enumerates an authority: the first has no authentication at all and says so with its reason — physical proximity is the control, and a web convenience layer grants nothing a nearby radio did not already have; the second carries trust-group fields deliberately unenforced, as placeholders against a later authority model; the third gates at connect and states its own gap in its own text — the realised check is browser-enforced and therefore does not bind a caller that is not a browser. So the artefact 4.1.3 requires has no antecedent to draw on, and three prior designs each deferred it rather than solving it differently. One rule from that reading is already satisfied here and is recorded so it is not re-imported as new: their rule that the existence of a management transport is no authorisation of any particular exposure — 7.1 states it more strongly, as no unauthenticated remote surface in a production image at all. (PROVISIONAL(standard))

10.3 Health-check content and window. 5.4.2 bounds neither what a health check tests nor how long the window is, and a check that tests too little commits bad updates while a window too short reverts good ones on slow platforms. The antecedents supply numbers for a different question, and the resemblance is the hazard. What they carry is observer-side liveness — a device unseen for thirty seconds is stale, for five minutes set aside, for an hour dropped; three consecutive unanswered queries at a minute's spacing mark it offline — and every one of those figures answers can the manager still see it, where 5.4.2 asks did the update work. The two come apart exactly where it matters: the device decides 5.4.2's question about itself and reverts unaided (5.4.3), with no manager watching and possibly none reachable, while a liveness window measures a path that a good update and a bad one can both interrupt. So a reader who finds those numbers has found a window for someone else's decision, and 10.3's is still owed. (PROVISIONAL(standard))

10.4 Reversion reporting. 5.4.4 is a should with no format: what a reversion report carries, and to whom it goes, is unspecified.

10.5 Configuration and parameters as managed state. This document specifies parameter delivery as an update class and says nothing about reading configuration back, drift between declared and actual configuration, or who may see a hive's current settings beyond the public tier.

10.6 Management across entanglement in detail. 4.1.2 admits management-scoped entanglement at a required grade; the levels themselves, and what operations each level licenses, are the entanglement companion's (Layer 5, Q25).

10.7 The certificate role 4.1.4 requires does not exist. 4.1.4 makes appointing and revoking an update authority group acts recorded as certificate role and revocation at Layer 5. Revocation has a home — Layer 5, 6.2 — and the role does not. Layer 5, 6.1.1 binds the member keypair's public half to the group and 6.1.3 adds the key epoch; there is no role field on a certificate anywhere in this standard, and the term update authority appears in no Layer 5 document nor in the formats companion. The nearest thing is a different axis: the enrolment companion's invitation carries a role, and its own 10.3 records that the only decided distinction there is key holder or not. So this clause requires a field, a vocabulary and a value, and none of the three exists — while the half that resolves makes it read as satisfied. And the requirement is stated twice in this document — 4.1.4 and the authority-appointment row of 4.1.1a's table — so a repair at one leaves the other standing. (PROVISIONAL(standard))

10.8 Ordering verification against apply rather than against the commit. 5.3.5 requires a pulling hive to reassemble, verify the whole, and only then apply; 5.4.1 requires a platform to apply by staging, so staging is the first step of applying rather than a step before it. On the reference platform of Layer 0, Clause 8 — two update slots of 1600 kB on a device with a few hundred kilobytes of memory — an image of that size can be reassembled nowhere but the staging slot, and writing it there is already applying. The stated order therefore names a sequence the reference platform cannot perform. The mechanism is sound and only the ordering is wrong: stage, verify from the slot by reading it back, and commit on success — and Note 0 to 5.3 already assumes exactly that read-back capability, having found the same memory limit from the serving side and stopped one consequence short of this one. What is owed is an ordering stated against the commit rather than against apply. (PROVISIONAL(standard))

10.9 No precondition on stored energy before a firmware write. Nothing in this document, the formats companion or Layer 0 requires a device to hold any charge before it erases or writes a staging slot: battery and charge appear in none of the three. 5.4.1's keep-the-prior-image rule protects against a bad image, not against loss of power mid-write, and a device that browns out part-way through erasing a slot is not saved by an image it can no longer reach a bootloader to select. The standard's own device model makes this the expected case: Layer 0's duty-cycled platform is intermittent by definition, and the formats companion's power-source enum carries battery / intermittent as a declared value. So the corpus knows a device may be running on stored energy and never asks how much remains before it writes flash. (PROVISIONAL(standard))

10.10 The cost of the recovery tier, and the scope of what it erases. The recovery layer of 5.5 requires physical possession, and a deployment that reaches its devices only over the air still has that tier — by retrieval rather than by cable. Nothing here bounds how often it is invoked, and an operator's expectation that retrieval be rare is a requirement no clause makes true; 10.9 is the clause that would, and its denominator is retrievals caused solely by a firmware write, since a hardware failure and a redeployment already put hands on the device. Nor does 5.5.1's when nothing else can describe how the tier is actually used: a redeployment to another trust group is a deliberate recovery of a working device, not a failure, so the tier is exercised routinely by design while this document frames it as a last resort. 5.5.3 Note 1 already holds un-bricking and un-claiming apart, so this is a mismatch of framing rather than a contradiction — but it decides what any rate figure is a rate of.

10.11 What a recovery re-image erases, and over what storage. 5.5.3 requires a recovery re-image to erase the device's persona, and bounds nothing else. Whether that is a wipe or a crypto-erase is not stated, and the difference is the whole guarantee: where application state at rest is encrypted under something the persona roots, destroying the persona destroys access to it and the ciphertext survives a re-image; where it is not, the persona's destruction protects nothing, and this document, Layer 5 and Layer 7 place no confidentiality obligation on stored application state at all — L5 5.3.2 seals key material and L7 4.2.2 defines a durable-state class with no such requirement attached. 5.5.3 Note 2's argument that a recovered device comes back as nobody's is about membership, and reads as though it were also about data. Compounding it, this standard has no vocabulary for storage outside the main processor: L0 P1 states memory and persistent storage as one undifferentiated figure, P9 and P11 describe the image, and P10 draws its boundary around the update path rather than around the processor — so a medium that neither an update nor a recovery reaches cannot be declared, and the scope of 5.5.3 over such a medium is undefined rather than narrow. What is owed is a decision on vocabulary before a clause can be written. (PROVISIONAL(standard))

10.12 How a device gets its first image. This document specifies the update model in detail and the recovery layer beside it, and neither describes how an image reaches a board that has none. What a blank board must do is specified — Layer 5 requires it to mint a persona and form a trust group of itself at first boot, and 5.4.2a Note 0 records that its first state is one with no readable image position — but the act of placing the first image is nowhere addressed, and no clause requires that image to be signed, verified or structured. 5.2.2's verify-before-applying binds a package delivered by the update model, which a cable flash is not; and 5.5.1, the only physical image-write path this standard defines, restores a working image, which presupposes there was one, while 5.5.3 has it erase a persona a blank board does not hold. So the operation every device undergoes exactly once is the one operation this standard does not name. What is owed is a sentence of scope rather than a mechanism: either that placing a first image lies outside this standard, or what such an image must satisfy. Either answer cures it; the silence is what cannot stand, because a reader cannot tell today whether an unsigned cable flash conforms or is simply not addressed. (PROVISIONAL(standard))
