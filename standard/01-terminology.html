Reality2 Standard — Terminology and core definitions

Normative document

About this reading edition

The numbered requirements and their provisional markers are retained from the cited source revision. Selected non-normative source notes, editorial attributions and internal working details are omitted. Clause numbers are unchanged; references to omitted notes may remain. The overview and worked examples are informative. Navigation and reading aids are not requirements.

Version: 0.9.0 · Date: 2026-09-03 · Status: Draft

What "Draft" names here is the editorial maturity of the text, and nothing else. It is not a licence to deviate: every requirement here is normative now and the implementing lanes build from it. The only text open to replacement is a value marked PROVISIONAL(...) at its own clause.

Verbal forms. This document uses the ISO verbal forms — shall for a requirement, should for a recommendation, may for a permission, can for possibility and capability.

Clause numbers are retired, never reissued — and this is infrastructure rather than an editorial preference. A clause number is the only part of this standard that has no presentation layer: prose is re-wrapped, emphasised and re-cased freely and correctly, while 5.3.5 cannot be reformatted into something else. That is what makes a citation of this standard checkable at all, and every citation of it — inside these documents, in conformance evidence, in an implementer's tests — resolves through it. A number that moves does not produce an error. It matches something else, or nothing, and the check that relied on it goes on passing.

So: a withdrawn clause keeps its number, and the number is never given to a different obligation. Where a requirement is withdrawn, the number stays with a note in its place recording the withdrawal, its date and its authority — Layer 2, 5.4a.5 is the worked example. A reader who follows an old citation lands on the withdrawal rather than on silence or on somebody else's clause. Renumbering is a change to every citation of this standard at once, and belongs to whoever governs the standard rather than to an editor tidying a clause.

⚠ And the case this rule does NOT cover, stated because it has already happened here once. Splitting one clause into two moves an obligation to a new number without renumbering anything — the old number survives, still holding part of what it held, so nothing looks withdrawn and nothing looks reused. A citation of the old number stays resolvable and stops being true.

Notes to entry are informative and do not bind. They may explain a numbered requirement, but they do not add an obligation, permission or prohibition absent from numbered text.

Introduction

This document defines the terms used throughout the Reality2 Standard.

The Reality2 Standard is self-contained. Everything an implementer is bound by is inside this folder, and nothing in a normative clause depends on a document an implementer cannot obtain.

A term appears here only where the meaning decides which rule applies. Vocabulary that merely helps a reader along belongs in the clause using it. Clause 6 lists the terms Reality2 uses without yet defining them, so that their absence reads as a decision rather than an oversight — an undefined term doing load-bearing work cannot be tested against, because each reader supplies their own boundary for it.

1 Scope

This document defines terms used in the Reality2 Standard for transient networking and trust groups.

It specifies the requirement of Clause 4, which arises from the definition of complex hive and cannot be stated inside a terminological entry, and the cross-layer resolution rules of Clause 5, which apply wherever a value or an ordering decides between a permissive and a restrictive behaviour.

It applies to all other documents of the Reality2 Standard.

It does not specify wire formats, protocol behaviour, or conformance criteria for any mechanism.

2 Normative references

There are no normative references in this document.

(This clause is stated even when empty. No normative clause in this document depends on any other document. Background reading is listed in the Bibliography, which is informative.)

3 Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the following terms and definitions apply.

Each definition is a single phrase substitutable for the term wherever it is used, and contains no requirement or recommendation.

3.1 device

hardware that receives frames of the Reality2 wire protocol, validates them, and can originate or relay them

Note 1 to entry: The wire protocol is specified at Layer 4. Equipment that only transmits raw data without processing it is not a device; it connects to one as a peripheral.

Note 2 to entry: Device names the hardware. Hive (3.2) names what the network addresses. The two terms are not interchangeable.

3.2 hive

externally-visible R2 entity having one identity, one beacon, one capability set and one trust-group binding

Note 1 to entry: In the normal case, a hive is a device (3.1).

Note 2 to entry: Where a document requires the addressable entity — hive identifier, beacon identity, trust-group membership, capability advertisement — it means hive, not device.

3.3 complex hive

hive (3.2) implemented by two or more cooperating processors and indistinguishable from a single-processor hive to every other hive

Note 1 to entry: A complex hive can span two physical devices; the canonical instance is a phone and a microcontroller joined over USB.

Note 2 to entry: Clause 4 states the condition under which physically separated processors constitute one hive.

3.4 sentant

autonomous finite-state-machine-based agent residing on a hive (3.2) that receives events, mutates internal state, and emits events

Note 1 to entry: The sentant is the fundamental unit of computation.

Note 2 to entry: A sentant's finite state machines are termed automations; its mutable state is termed vars.

3.5 plugin

component running on a hive (3.2) that provides capabilities, whose lifecycle the hive manages and with which the hive communicates by events

Note 1 to entry: How a plugin is implemented is not visible outside the hive.

3.6 ensemble

composite distributed unit of functionality comprising one or more sentants (3.4) and zero or more plugins (3.5), described by a score

Note 1 to entry: An ensemble has no binary and is not installed. It is performed by whichever hives currently host its parts.

Note 2 to entry: An ensemble is its score — the declarative description of its parts — rather than the parts themselves.

Note 3 to entry: An ensemble with zero plugins is valid and common.

3.7 apiary

suite of ensembles (3.6) composed for a common purpose

Note 1 to entry: An apiary is a functional grouping. A trust group (3.8) is a security and membership boundary. The two are distinct.

3.8 trust group

flat group of hives (3.2) sharing a cryptographic identity

Note 1 to entry: There is no hierarchy within a trust group.

3.9 class

UTF-8 string implying by convention a minimum set of events handled by its bearer

Note 1 to entry: Reverse-DNS format is recommended for global uniqueness. Any non-empty string is valid. There is no central registry; class conventions are established by agreement between implementers.

Note 2 to entry: A class applies at two scales. A sentant (3.4) has a class. A hive's (3.2) class is an overall descriptor of the summation of the classes of the sentants it hosts. These are one concept at two scales.

Note 3 to entry: The hive-level summation can be authored by hand; it is not required to be derived mechanically.

Note 4 to entry: Both scales share one 32-bit FNV-1a hash space with no central registry. A collision or a near-miss spelling between scales produces no match and no error.

3.10 conformance evidence

what an implementer offers to show that a requirement is met, of one of three classes

Note 1 to entry: behavioural — an input produces an observable result a third party can decide. This is the default and covers most requirements.

Note 2 to entry: by construction — the requirement is negative and structural, and the evidence is a stated demonstration that the capability is absent: a named module or crate boundary across which the thing cannot be referred to, an enumerated interface surface, a build artefact lacking the symbol.

Note 2a to entry: The criterion, and it is what keeps this class honest. A claim qualifies as demonstrable by construction only if a third party can verify it by reading a bounded and identified piece of the implementation, without the implementer's cooperation. Bounded and identified are both load-bearing: "the code enforces it" names nothing a reviewer can open. "Trust me, it holds" is not evidence of this class — it is an unproven claim wearing the class as a costume, and by-construction evidence and an unproven claim look identical from outside. The difference is whether the absence is reviewable. This class exists because negative structural requirements genuinely have no behavioural test; it does not exist as somewhere to put what an implementer cannot demonstrate.

Note 2b to entry: Some properties are unobservable in-language, and saying so is better than a weak test that implies otherwise. Whether a comparison runs in constant time is not visible to a unit test — a test showing it agrees with an ordinary comparison would pass for an ordinary comparison too — and whether memory was erased on release cannot be read back in a language that forbids reading freed memory. For these the evidence is the code itself, and Note 2a's criterion is what makes that acceptable: a named, bounded fragment a reviewer can read. A weak test left standing as though it were a control is worse than an acknowledged limit, because it answers a question nobody asked and implies the real one was settled.

Note 3 to entry: by declaration — the requirement obliges the implementer to state something, and the statement is the evidence.

Note 4 to entry: The classes exist because a negative structural requirement has no passing behavioural test, and never will. Layer 0's requirement that code implementing Layers 1 to 4 have no access to trust-group private keys is met by a boundary across which those keys cannot be named; there is no input that makes an implementation demonstrate that absence by behaving. Calling such a requirement untestable is wrong — it is testable by inspection, and this clause says so rather than leaving each implementer to decide whether an unverifiable-by-input requirement is binding.

Note 5 to entry: A requirement obliging no implementation at all — one whose subject is another document, or this standard itself — has no conformance evidence of any class, and belongs in a note rather than in a requirement.

3.11 layer contract

defined portable set of operations, inputs, outputs, state, failure outcomes and ownership that one Reality2 layer makes available to another

Note 1 to entry: A layer contract defines the meaning of a boundary, not its programming language, process model or call syntax. It can be realised by an in-process interface, a message boundary or a separate processor without changing the contract above it.

Note 2 to entry: A contract carries portable facts. A board name, pin, radio chip, vendor driver, operating-system object or runtime representation belongs below the contract that uses it. The architecture map in 00-overview.md, 5.0 identifies the contract each layer owns; the owning layer part gives its individual operations their exact meaning.

Note 3 to entry: An optional facility is a separately named contract capability, not a new meaning for an existing operation. A caller that does not select it sees the existing contract unchanged; a caller that does selects it explicitly and can report it absent or unsupported. This is what permits a small image to omit a facility without making a different claim for the facilities it retains.

4 Requirements arising from Clause 3

4.1 A bridge between the cooperating processors of a complex hive (3.3) shall be cryptographically keyed where it can be observed, or interposed on, without disassembling the hive.

4.1a A bridge reachable only by disassembling the hive should be cryptographically keyed.

4.1b Cooperating processors shall constitute one hive only where exactly one identity, one beacon, one capability set and one trust-group binding (3.2) is presented to other hives, and no part presents any of its own. Where more than one of any of these is presented, the parts are devices (3.1), each requiring its own.

Note 1 to entry: 4.1b, not the key, is what makes the parts one hive, and the reason is that it is the only one of the two the party it serves can check. 3.3 requires a complex hive to be indistinguishable from a single-processor hive to every other hive — and no other hive can see a bridge key. A criterion resting on the key asked an outside observer to depend on something invisible to it; 4.1b restates the test in exactly the terms 3.2 already uses, so it is decidable from outside by the parties the definition is written for.

Note 2 to entry: The requirement is stated as a clause because a definition cannot contain one. Complex hive (3.3) says what the term means; 4.1 says what has to be true for the term to apply.

4.2 Behavioural conformance evidence (3.10) shall include, for each demonstration that a requirement is met, the same procedure applied to a case the requirement excludes.

4.3 The excluded case of 4.2 shall produce a result that does not satisfy the requirement and is distinguishable from the satisfying one.

4.4 The non-satisfying result of 4.3 shall be shown to follow from the requirement under test rather than from another cause.

4.5 Where no case the requirement excludes can be constructed, the evidence shall state that, and name what prevents it.

Note 3 to entry: A demonstration that could not have come out any other way is not evidence. It is a procedure producing a confident output while being incapable of the outcome that would matter — structurally the same object as a check that never fails, and indistinguishable from a real demonstration in any report. 4.2 is the smallest thing that separates them: the check refused the bad input is worth nothing without and it accepted the good one, and the converse holds equally.

Note 4a to entry: The operational form of 4.3 and 4.4 is one word: a denominator. Reported by an implementer that had run both a sound control and a hollow one and could say which was which only afterwards:

Predict the exact set of results the excluded case produces, and name it before running. Not that something fails — which of them, and how many.

A prediction with a denominator is falsifiable in both directions. Too few failures means the excluded case never took effect (4.3); too many means something other than the requirement under test also broke — a wrong-reason result wearing a right-reason appearance (4.4). Something failed distinguishes neither. Collateral failures are not noise to be tolerated: they are the attribution evidence. The sound control disabled one mechanism and predicted exactly two named failures; it produced exactly two and no others. The hollow ones asserted only that something refused.

And a denominator over outcomes is bounded by the values it assumes the observable can take, which is the failure this Note did not previously cover. Where the result is a measured quantity rather than a set of discrete events, naming the outcomes silently fixes the set of values that quantity may take — and a value between the named ones is not a missing row: it is invisible, because it is read as the nearest row that exists. The instance is exact. A four-outcome table for a switched power rail named both pulse, the enable pin pulses with the rail high, the pin never moves, and the rail never rises — every one of them assuming the rail is at its supply or at ground. Measured with a meter, the rail sat steady at about one volt with three point three volts at the converter input, which is neither, and a report of it drops would have been recorded as the gating working. The intermediate value was the whole diagnosis: below the part's operating minimum and well above ground, so its power-on reset never re-armed and every commanded power cycle was a brown-out. The operational addition is one question asked before running rather than after: what reading would fit none of these rows? It is answerable in advance, it costs a sentence, and where the answer is none, the space is genuinely discrete and the denominator above is sufficient as written. The general form, of which the continuous case is the commonest instance: what result would this instrument be unable to represent? (PROVISIONAL(standard))

Note 4b to entry: Where the failing set cannot be enumerated in advance, 4.5 is not the only recourse. The substitute is weaker than a count and still falsifiable: name in advance one outcome that would refute attribution, and check for it — no compiler error may appear in the log is the instance, and it is what caught a build gate that had been credited with a refusal the compiler made. Constructible where a count is not, which is what keeps 4.4 affordable on the paths that most need it.

Note 5 to entry: The same defect outside code, which is why this is stated generally. A pin voltage was credited as evidence of a wiring fault and carried two independent diagnoses, until an unmodified board of the same type, connected to nothing, produced the same reading. Every step of the reasoning was locally sound; the unasked question was what else produces this result, and it was answered in thirty seconds by measuring a unit known to be well. An implementer can satisfy a clause with a measurement of that kind, and the evidence is indistinguishable from a real one — so the standard asks for the excluded case rather than trusting the question to be asked.

Note 6 to entry: Stated once here after being stated for one document. Layer 4, 12.2 already requires both directions of its wire vectors — a vector required to fail for each covered field and a byte-identical pair for each field outside the span — and its Note 0 records why one direction alone is insufficient: an implementation authenticating a mutable field passes every required-to-fail vector and cannot relay. That is 4.2 and 4.3 for one clause set, written before anyone saw it was general.

Note 7 to entry: Scope. 4.2 binds behavioural evidence only. By construction evidence is an inspection with no procedure to run (3.10, Note 2a), and by declaration evidence is a statement. Where a by-construction property is enforced by a build-time mechanism, the demonstration that the mechanism refuses is itself behavioural evidence about that mechanism, and 4.2 to 4.4 apply to it — which is 5.2, Note 3's compile-failure test, and the reason it is a control rather than a claim.

Note 8 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 9 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(standard).

4.6 A verification procedure stated in a document of this standard shall name the clause or clauses it verifies, and shall name all of them where it verifies more than one.

Note 0 to entry: The occasion, measured 2026-08-09 across all fourteen documents: 29 verification blocks, and THREE OF THE FOUR CARRYING A CONFORMANCE VERDICT SAT UNDER A CLAUSE OTHER THAN THE ONE THEY TESTED. One tested a beaconing obligation and sat under the anti-gaming rule beside it; one asserted a capacity verdict under a clause about processors; one carried a requirement's oracle under a recommendation. All three were reachable by reading the clause they sat under and invisible from the clause they tested. A verdict that cites its clause moves when the clause moves.

Note 1 to entry: This does not stop a block restating its clause's consequence — it makes the restatement traceable to what it restates. The failure it addresses is the one Layer 1, 11.2 names across documents arriving inside one: a requirement restated is a second requirement, and the first one edited becomes the one that differs. A block phrased in its own terms — observed traffic rather than the obligation — survives its clause's amendment intact and becomes a correctly-worded contradiction, and an amendment then has nowhere to look.

Note 2 to entry: 4.6 binds every verification procedure and not only one that states a verdict, and the reason is a measurement that could not be completed. Of the 29 blocks, 25 assert no verdict, and requiring a citation there looks like ceremony until you try to check them: an attempt to distinguish a block that TESTS a distant clause from one that merely MENTIONS it, by comparing each block's nearest clause with the numbers it names, produced four candidates and could not resolve one of them without reading the surrounding argument. The ambiguity the rule removes is exactly the ambiguity that prevents the rule's necessity from being measured, so the cheap version of this rule cannot be shown to be sufficient. Naming more than one clause is permitted because three blocks already reference more than one, and forcing a single citation would make one of them false.

Verification of 4.6: take every verification procedure in a document and read its first sentence. One that names no clause fails 4.6. One that names a clause it does not verify is not caught here and is outside what this rule claims. (PROVISIONAL(standard))

4.7 Where every input to a demonstration was produced by the implementation under test, the evidence shall be recorded as closed, and shall name what has not yet supplied it a real input.

Note 1 to entry: ⚠ CLOSED EVIDENCE IS NOT WEAK EVIDENCE, AND THAT IS WHY IT NEEDS A WORD OF ITS OWN. It can be well constructed: the excluded case of 4.2 can be built inside a closed loop, the control can be sound, and the tests can pass for the right reasons. What a closed demonstration establishes is conformance to the author's model of the input, not to the input — every value it was given came from the same understanding that produced the code, so the two agree by construction and no disagreement is available to be found. 4.2's Note 3 names the general failure and this is its provenance form: not a demonstration that could not come out another way, but a demonstration whose inputs could not have surprised it.

Note 2 to entry: The discriminator is one question and it is answerable without judgement: did any input to the demonstration come from OUTSIDE the thing under test? A fixture the implementation constructs and passes to itself is a closed loop; a value produced by another component, another implementation, or a device is not. Where the producing side does not exist yet, that is the honest and common case — and 4.7 asks for it to be named rather than inferred from silence.

Note 3 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(standard).

4.8.1 An implementation shall cross a boundary between Reality2 layers only through the layer contract (3.11) that the applicable part defines.

4.8.2 A layer contract shall not expose a board, pin assignment, radio chip, vendor driver, operating-system object, source-language type or runtime representation to the layer that consumes it.

4.8.3 A change shall preserve the defined meaning of every existing layer-contract operation.

4.8.4 A change needing a different meaning shall define a separately named optional capability or a versioned contract.

Note 1 to entry: 4.8.1 does not require layers to be adjacent. Layer 6 may request the image-application facility Layer 0 defines, and a Layer 7 plugin may consume a Layer 0 peripheral capability, because both are named contracts. Neither exception permits an upper layer to reach through the contract and manipulate a board-specific implementation.

Note 2 to entry: 4.8.2 does not prohibit a binding document from naming a medium's physical requirements. It prohibits exporting those requirements to a consuming layer. A LoRa binding can require a sync word and a board adaptation can configure an RF switch, while L2 and L3 continue to see only the L1 bearer contract.

Note 3 to entry: 4.8.3 and 4.8.4 are the compatibility rule. Adding an optional capability can make a new image more capable; changing what an existing request or report means makes old callers silently wrong. The latter is a new contract, even if a particular implementation can make it look like a small code edit.

5 Resolution of absent, unreadable, unrecognised and false values

5.1 Where a value selects between a more permissive and a more restrictive behaviour, a value that is absent, unreadable, or not recognised shall select the more restrictive behaviour.

5.2 Where a check and a state change are both required, and performing the state change before the check would admit a behaviour the check exists to prevent, the check shall precede the state change.

5.3 Where a platform is asked a question about its own state, an answer meaning I cannot tell is a distinct and conformant answer, and a clause posing such a question shall name it alongside the others.

Note 0 to entry: Written after the same omission appeared four times in four subsystems: claim state, the update sequence record, boot position, and the length of an image held in a slot. Each time an implementer faced a question with three real answers and a clause offering two, and each time folding the third into a definite one was the dangerous move — a faulted device read as claimable, a corrupt record read as a floor, an unaccounted image read as pending, and free space read where a live rollback target sits. That last is the sharpest: a platform storing raw images genuinely cannot always report a length, and folding unknown into empty invites a caller to erase the fallback that 5.4.3 depends on. Four instances is a pattern; the fifth will be someone folding it.

Note 0a to entry: The fifth arrived within the hour, and it changes how this clause must be read. A health verdict held two answers where there are three — a self-test needing a radio that is down cannot report passed and cannot report failed. What makes it different from the first four is that no behaviour diverges: both non-passing answers revert, fail-closed, identically. Every functional test passes either way. The harm is in what the device says, not in what it does — reporting the checks did not pass when the checks never ran sends whoever reads it hunting a defect in the new image while the real fault is that the check could not execute.

So the test is not whether folding changes the code's behaviour. An implementer who asks that will find it does not, and fold anyway. The test is:

Is the third answer distinguishable to whoever acts on it?

Note 1 to entry: 5.1 is written because it was discovered four times independently, by four implementers, none of them prompted by a clause saying it — a damaged claim state must not read as claimable; an unreadable update-sequence record must not read as a floor; an unreadable build class must not buy a development image's exemptions; an unrecognised algorithm identifier must not be acted on. Each was found after the permissive reading had already been written somewhere, and in two cases after hardware had failed in the field.

Note 2 to entry: 5.2 is the same rule in time rather than in value, and its failure mode is worse: the wrong order is invisible to conformance testing by construction. Every legitimate exchange behaves identically under both orderings, so only an adversary distinguishes them — as at Layer 5, 7.4.3a, where advancing an anti-replay mark before verifying a signature converts the mechanism into a lockout that anyone holding a public certificate can trigger.

A check reachable only from tests is indistinguishable, in the shipped artifact, from a check never written — and worse than never writing it, because the tests pass and a reviewer grepping for the clause name finds a hit.

A value that cannot be constructed without its check makes check exists but is not called impossible to express, and covers bulk import, deserialisation, restore-from-storage and test helpers at once. Such a guard still needs a control, and it is a compile-failure test rather than a runtime one — otherwise "the type prevents it" is exactly the unproven claim Note 2a warns of, and nobody has shown the compiler actually refuses. This is the by construction evidence class of 3.10 doing security work rather than paperwork.

Note 4 to entry: Refusing on an anomalous precondition preserves the diagnosis; proceeding destroys it. This is the second argument for 5.1, and it is independent of safety. A bring-up sequence that stopped on an unexpected reading produced a cheap, decisive follow-up test and a one-line physical instruction; had it proceeded instead, the observable result would have been silence — indistinguishable from a dead part, a wrong rate, a wrong protocol or a wrong address, and the team would have been debugging a phantom. The same shape runs through this standard's refusal rules — Layer 1, 5.2.2 (refuse rather than accept and discard), Layer 6, 5.2.4 (refuse without leaking why), the unreadable-record rule of Layer 6, 5.2.3b. A system that proceeds on a bad precondition does not merely risk harm: it converts a diagnosable fault into an ambiguous symptom.

Note 5 to entry: Two severities, which look alike in a report and are not alike. A guard that production goes around means the shipped artifact contains an unchecked path. A guard that is merely not reached yet is a reprieve, not an acquittal — the protection is worth nothing until someone writes the path, and worth nothing then either if they reach for a lower-level interface instead of the guarded one. The remedy that survives both is the same: an unconstructible value keeps its worth while unreached, because whoever finally writes the path cannot express the unchecked call. A remembered check that is currently unreached is worth nothing later; a type that cannot be built without its check is worth exactly as much later as today.

Note 6 to entry: This class of defect clusters by authorship proximity, not by call graph — which decides what a fix should trigger. Instances found while these clauses were being written sat two variants along in one enumeration, one method either side of a corrected one, one level up in the containing structure, and inside a type whose own defect had just been fixed. In every case the neighbours were written by the same author in the same sitting under the same unstated assumption, and in none was the neighbour adjacent in the call graph. A reviewer reading the diff of a fix sees none of them, because the neighbours are unchanged lines. The practical consequence: when a defect of this class is fixed, the unit to re-read is the whole structure and its immediate relations — siblings, container, contents — not the change. Two of this standard's own clauses were found that way.

Note 7 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 8 to entry: 5.1 does not decide when the two behaviours are restrictive toward different parties, and until this note it offered no tie-break. Layer 6, 5.5.4 is the instance: an unreadable persona read as present keeps a device locked to its group and refuses any new claim, which looks like the restrictive answer; read as fresh the device refuses to exercise a membership it cannot prove, which also looks like the restrictive answer. Two axes, and 5.1 alone chooses neither. The rule that decides it:

Ask which reading grants an authority, not which looks more locked down. The restrictive answer is the one under which an unauthorised holder gains nothing.

Under that test the persona case is not close — fresh costs an owner a membership, present hands one to whoever holds the hardware. (PROVISIONAL(supervisor))

5.4 A record's integrity check establishes that the mechanism owning the record wrote its bytes; it does not establish that those bytes still describe the world. Where a decision is irreversible, or raises a floor that later refuses what it once accepted, the recorded description shall be confirmed against the state it describes, wherever the platform can observe that state.

Note 0 to entry: No checksum catches a true-looking lie. A magic value, a CRC or a signature answers did the owning mechanism write this? — a question about the record. Every clause that leans on such a discriminator inherits the limit, and until this clause none of them said so. The instance that produced it: an interrupted copy left a staged slot erased while the update record still named it as pending, so the record parsed cleanly, validated cleanly, and was false. The platform then health-checked the wrong image, passed, and raised a floor above an image that had never been applied (Layer 6, 5.4.2b).

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 1b to entry: The writer of a false record need not be adversarial, interrupted, or faulty. The strongest instance measured so far was written by a platform's own boot path, in good faith, exactly as documented: a bootloader on a layout with no factory image found no valid update record, and — as designed — constructed and wrote a fresh one marked valid, with a correct checksum. Nothing malfunctioned. But valid there means there was nothing here so I wrote something, not an update was applied and confirmed, and no reader of the record alone can tell the two apart. A record's provenance being trustworthy says nothing about whether it describes the world, which is why 5.4 is stated about the record rather than about who wrote it.

Note 1c to entry: A discriminator that covers part of a record leaves the rest unvalidated, and the split is rarely where a reader assumes. In the same platform the checksum covers the record's sequence and not its state, so a corrupted state that lands on a defined value passes validation intact — the record is valid and its most consequential field is unchecked. Ask what the discriminator covers, not whether one is present; the answer is usually narrower than the word checksum suggests.

Note 2 to entry: The test that follows from 5.4 is one question, and it is not the question a validator answers. Not is this artefact well-formed but:

Does anything check this artefact against the thing it claims?

The artefact and the world it describes can disagree, and only something that reads both can notice — which is why the disagreement that produced this clause existed between an update record and a storage slot, where no checksum over either could ever have seen it.

Note 3 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

5.4a Where a durable record is read by more than one party whose actions depend on it, an implementation shall accept that record only under the strictest validity predicate applied by any of those parties.

Note 0 to entry: Two correct readers of one record can disagree, and nothing in the record can reveal it. Measured on the reference platform: the boot chooser requires three conditions of an image-position record — a sequence that is not the empty marker, a matching checksum, and a state that is neither invalid nor aborted — while the library the application reads it through writes a checksum and never verifies one on read, comparing sequences alone. So a record with a good sequence and a corrupt checksum is invalid to the boot chooser and valid to the application: the device boots one image and reports another, silently, with every integrity check the application can see passing.

Note 1 to entry: Neither party is corrupt and neither is wrong. Each is a correct implementation of its own contract; the disagreement is entirely in the predicates, and a checksum cannot tell a reader that another reader ignored it. Only reading both definitions finds it — which is why this is stated as an obligation rather than left to verification, since verifying the artefact is not enough when someone else has already verified it differently.

Note 2 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

5.4b An implementation that reads such a record shall state the conditions under which it accepts it, in a form the other parties reading that record can read.

Note 0 to entry: 5.4a can only be satisfied by comparing definitions, so an unstated predicate does not merely risk divergence — it guarantees the other party is guessing. Publishing the conditions is the minimum an implementation owes the other readers of a record it shares, and it costs nothing: the conditions already exist, in a function body where no one else can reach them.

Note 1 to entry: The statement is worth little unless it says what any integrity check covers. A checksum over a subset of a record is the divergence of 5.4a Note 0 in miniature — a reader that sees checksummed and assumes all of it has been told the truth and drawn a false conclusion. Naming the covered fields, and any condition checked separately on top, is what makes the predicate comparable rather than merely present.

Note 2 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

5A One origin, many surfaces

5A.1 Where an implementation asserts the same fact about its own state on more than one surface, every surface shall derive that assertion from one origin.

Note 1 to entry: This is the opposite of the rule that governs a check, and the difference is real rather than a contradiction. For a check, two independent origins are required, because agreement is evidence only where the sides could have disagreed. For an assertion about your own state, disagreement is the failure: if two surfaces ever disagree the device is lying on one of them, and which one is not discoverable from either. Same-source is a defect in a comparison and a requirement in a broadcast.

5A.2 Where such an assertion controls admission, membership or trust, and the implementation cannot determine the value, it shall assert the value that grants least.

5A.3 Where 5A.2 applies, a surface answering what this implementation knows about itself shall report both the value asserted and whether that value was determined.

Note 2a to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 3 to entry: The failure directions are asymmetric, which is why 5A.2 states a direction rather than leaving it to 5.1. A development device that fails to advertise development is admitted to a production network — a relaxed-trust device inside a production boundary, which is precisely what the rule exists to prevent. The reverse costs the device its admission and is loud. Absence of certainty is not absence of the property. 5.1 already selects the more restrictive branch for an absent value; 5A.2 says the same for a value the device cannot determine about itself, where the temptation is to report the pleasant answer.

Note 3a to entry: The origin is not always a runtime value, and where the standard says absent, not disabled, it must not be. This clause was first written as one runtime value and that wording was wrong within the hour: Layer 6, 6.2.1 requires development-only code to be absent from a production image, not disabled in it, and its Note 0 states that a runtime check on build mode IS the disabled the clause refuses — the answering code ships, the branch is one flipped byte from taken, and the binary still contains the answers. So for build mode the origin is the build configuration: one configuration, two surfaces, and neither surface exists in a production image at all. A single runtime flag driving an indicator and a beacon satisfies the letter of 5A.1 and destroys what Layer 6 was protecting.

Note 4 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

6 Terms not defined in this document

Listed so their absence is legible as a decision rather than an oversight. No term below may be inferred from context by an implementer. Where a later document of this standard needs one, it names the gap rather than assuming a reading.

Term What is undecided
capability Whether the word names an event, a class, or a declared set of either. What a hive advertises is now decided — the capability summary is over public events (Layer 2, Clause 8; Layer 7, 5.2.1) — so the residue is purely which thing the word should name (Layer 7, 10.3).
confirm affordance Which means of confirming an action count. The term decides how strongly an action is authorised, so an implementer needs its boundary and not a list of examples that happen to be on one side of it.
bridge PARTLY RULED, 2026-08-07 (Roy): a bridge is NOT a transport in this standard's sense. Of the three jobs the word was doing, that one is struck: a bridge is the link between parts of one hive, and it carries no Layer 1 obligations, no bearer binding and no frames in Layer 1's sense — so its absence from Layer 1 is correct by definition rather than a gap, which is how a sweep first mis-filed it. Still open: whether bridge also names a kind of device, which the phone-plus-dongle family still forces, since one dongle does both at once.
swarm Whether it names anything distinct from ensemble (3.6).
