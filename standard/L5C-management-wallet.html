Reality2 Standard — Layer 5 companion: The management wallet

Status: Draft — first pass Date: 2026-08-03

Verbal forms. shall = requirement · should = recommendation · may = permission · can = possibility and capability.

Introduction

A person who stewards more than one trust group — a household's groups, a community's earthgrid, a fleet of deployments — holds something Layer 5 names but does not specify: custody of keys for provisioning others, distinct from membership in anything (Layer 5, 4.3 Note 2). This document specifies the instrument of that custody: the management wallet.

Two commitments shape it:

Custody is not membership, and the wallet keeps them apart. The wallet's host hive has exactly one persona, in exactly one group, like every hive (Layer 5, 4.3.2). The group secrets the wallet holds are cargo, never identity — held for the moments custody exists for, and exercised as nothing else.

The wallet stores while the owner is absent; it acts only while the owner is present. Holding is passive and unattended. Every exercise of custody is an act of the owner, evidenced at a stated person-authentication level — the wallet is a person's instrument, never an autonomous authority.

One disambiguation, before anything else: this document is not about value tokens or attestation claims. The worked examples speak informally of a person's "wallet" of payment tokens and presence claims — those are application data, stored however applications store things. The management wallet holds key custody. Same word in the street; different thing in this standard.

1 Scope

This document specifies the management wallet: what it may hold, the separation of custody from membership, the conditions under which held material may be exercised, how custody is granted and transferred, what its loss costs, and when a custody relationship should be an entanglement grant instead.

It establishes what conformance to this document consists of, and identifies what is not decided.

It does not specify byte formats, which belong to the formats companion. It does not specify storage of application data — value tokens, attestations, claims — which is not custody and not this document's subject.

3 Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the terms and definitions given in the documents listed in Clause 2 apply, together with the following.

3.1 management wallet

facility on a hive holding custody of key material for trust groups its owner manages

3.2 custody

holding of a group's secret key material for the purpose of managing that group, as distinct from membership in it

Note 1 to entry: Layer 3 uses custody for a different thing at a different scale — a relay holding a frame for later forwarding (Layer 3, 3.8). In this document custody is always of key material; where the two could be confused, say key custody and frame custody.

3.3 owner

person whose authority every exercise of a wallet's custody evidences

3.4 custodian

wallet, or the person owning it, holding custody of a given group's secret

4 Custody is not membership

4.1 A wallet's host hive shall have exactly one persona, in exactly one group (Layer 5, 4.3.2); the groups whose material the wallet holds shall not be among its memberships by virtue of that holding.

4.2 A wallet shall not use held material to participate in a held group: not to beacon as it, answer as it, originate or tag its frames, or pass its delivery gate as a member.

4.3 Held material shall be exercised only for the management acts custody exists for: issuing certificates at enrolment (L5B-enrolment.md), rotation, recovery, the issuing of invitations, and the activation of dormant personas (5.1 e). Activation is not the wallet participating: the sealed hive that wakes acts as itself (5.1a, 6.5), and 4.2 binds the wallet, not it.

4.4 The custody rules of Layer 5, 5.3 apply to every key a wallet holds: most protected facility, no implicit copies, erased when released, redacted from every diagnostic.

Note 1 to entry: 4.2 is the clause that keeps the wallet from becoming what Layer 5 4.3 Note 2 warns against — a bridge no group agreed to. A wallet that answered as a member of five groups would be one device inside five boundaries: its compromise is five compromises, and its ordinary traffic links five groups to one radio. Custody held but never worn keeps the exposure at rest, where Clause 8 can price it.

Note 2 to entry: The distinction is enforceable because the acts differ in kind. Membership is exercised on the mesh — beacons, frames, gate passages — continuously and observably. Custody is exercised in ceremonies — rare, local, owner-present. An implementation that finds itself using wallet material on the data path has crossed the line 4.2 draws.

5 What a wallet may hold

5.1 A wallet may hold:

a) group secret key material, for each group whose custodian its owner is;

b) invitations — issued and not yet consumed, and received and not yet exercised (L5B-enrolment.md, Clause 5);

c) recovery material — shares or equivalents by which a group's secret can be reconstituted, where a group has split its custody (8.3); and

d) the records custody requires: agreement artefacts the owner signed (L5A-entanglement.md), certificates issued, rotation history for the groups held; and

e) dormant personas — complete hive personas, each sealed in its own isolated environment, held for activation as hives on the wallet's host or elsewhere.

5.1a A dormant persona shall stay sealed: the wallet holds and activates the sealed environment, and shall not extract, read or exercise the persona's key material itself. An activated persona runs as an ordinary hive — one persona, one group, its own beacon — under Layer 5, 4.3.5 where co-hosted, and its member secret never leaves its sandbox (Layer 5, 5.3.3 is satisfied because the sandbox is the hive).

5.1b On a platform capable of one hive at a time, activation of a dormant persona shall deactivate the running one first: personas swap; they do not stack.

5.2 A wallet shall enumerate what it holds to its owner, and to no one else.

5.3 A wallet shall hold nothing whose exercise it cannot gate under Clause 6.

Note 1 to entry: 5.2's second half is a privacy commitment with teeth: the list of groups a person manages is a map of their responsibilities — which communities, which deployments, which households. No protocol surface, no diagnostic tier, no entangled peer is owed that map. The wallet's inventory is between the wallet and its owner.

6 Holding and acting

6.1 A wallet shall associate every operation class it supports with a required person-authentication level of its owner, and shall refuse the operation when the owner's current attested level is below it. (The scale is ruled 0–7 and its normative home is the person-authentication companion — 12.4; the ruling record is informative and outside this standard.)

6.2 Unattended — the owner absent or below every stated level — a wallet shall store, and shall exercise nothing.

6.3 Custody transfer (Clause 7) and custodian revocation shall require the highest level the wallet supports, including a fresh momentary confirmation; they shall not proceed on standing presence alone.

6.4 An enrolment the wallet provisions shall additionally satisfy the ceremony's own requirements (L5B-enrolment.md, 6.3, 6.4) — the wallet's owner gate does not substitute for the ceremony's person.

6.5 Activation and deactivation of a dormant persona (5.1 e) are operation classes under 6.1. Once activated, the running hive is not the wallet's: it acts as itself, and nothing it does is gated here.

Note 1 to entry: This clause is where the wallet's answer to "what may it do without the owner present?" is stated, and the answer is nothing that exercises custody. The graded levels do the useful softening: standing presence — the owner nearby, passively attested, decaying — can suffice for the routine low end (reading the inventory, issuing a pre-authorised invitation), while the irreversible high end demands the fresh explicit act. The levels are stated per operation class by the wallet's configuration; this document deliberately pins none, for the same reason C6 left the anchors to the companion.

Note 2 to entry: The asymmetry of 6.2 is the design: a stolen, powered, unattended wallet is a vault, not an agent. What its thief holds is Clause 8's problem — real, priced there — but nothing about possession alone makes the wallet do anything.

7 Granting and transferring custody

7.1 Custody shall be granted only by a ceremony at the key-custody grade (L5B-enrolment.md, 6.3.1): anchored, with a person confirming at the invitation's stated level.

7.2 Custody, once held, cannot be taken back — it is outlived: removing a custodian shall be effected by rotating the group's keys without them (Layer 5, Clause 8), after which what they hold opens nothing current.

7.3 A transfer of custody shall be a grant to the new custodian (7.1) followed by a rotation past the old (7.2), where the old custodian is to lose custody; and a grant alone, where custody is to be shared.

7.4 A backup of a wallet is a custodian: it shall be granted custody as 7.1 requires, counted as a custodian thereafter, and rotated past like any other when retired.

Note 1 to entry: 7.2 is the honest mechanics of symmetric material: a secret shown cannot be unshown. A protocol that claimed to "revoke custody" by deleting a copy it does not control would be a phantom gate. Rotation is the only real revocation this architecture has, and it already exists — so custody removal costs one rotation, which is exactly what eviction of a member key-holder already costs (Layer 5, 8.1).

Note 2 to entry: 7.4 closes the quiet hole. A "backup" that were exempt from custody accounting would be an unenumerated custodian — the exposure of Clause 8 with none of the bookkeeping. Naming it a custodian makes the copy visible to the one party entitled to the map (5.2): the owner deciding how exposed their groups are.

8 Loss, compromise, and splitting

8.1 A wallet's capture shall be treated as the simultaneous exposure of every group whose secret it holds: the response is rotation of every held group (Layer 5, Clause 8), at the zero-grace severity of a compromise.

8.2 A wallet's owner shall be able to enumerate, from outside the lost wallet, which groups require that rotation — the inventory of 5.2 shall be recoverable by the owner without the wallet.

8.3 A group may split its custody so that no single wallet holds material sufficient to exercise it alone; the threshold and scheme are the group's choice, and the formats companion's to encode.

Note 1 to entry: The wallet is, by construction, the highest-value capture in this architecture — a single object whose loss is many groups' compromise at once. This document does not soften that; it prices it. 8.2 makes the recovery actionable (an owner who cannot list what was lost cannot rotate what was lost); 8.3 offers the one real mitigation, which is not holding the whole secret anywhere; and Layer 0's protection-at-rest gap (L0 10.4) is nowhere more load-bearing than here.

9 Custody or grant — which relationship to use

9.1 Where another party is to manage a group's devices in a bounded way, the relationship should be a management-bearing entanglement (L5A-entanglement.md, Clause 9) — enumerated operations, member-attributed acts, revocable at will — and not a grant of custody.

9.2 Custody should be held only where stewardship is intended: the holder stands in for the group's own continuity, not for a bounded task.

Note 1 to entry: The two mechanisms bracket delegation honestly. A grant is bounded power, revocable by a policy change; custody is total power, revocable only by rotation. A community that hands its earthgrid's secret to a contractor has made them a steward it can only rotate away from; the same contractor under a management-bearing entanglement is a named group, doing named things, attributable per act, severable in one decision. The default belongs at the bounded end.

10 Conformance

Every requirement of this document is demonstrated by conformance evidence of one of the three classes of 01-terminology.md, 3.10 — behavioural, by construction, or by declaration. A requirement whose evidence is by construction is not thereby optional: the demonstration is an inspection a third party can perform without the implementer's cooperation, and its absence is non-conformance exactly as a failed behavioural test is. Behavioural evidence for any requirement of this document carries the control 01-terminology.md 4.2 to 4.5 requires — the same procedure run against a case the requirement excludes, giving a distinguishable non-satisfying result attributable to the requirement under test; a demonstration that could not have come out any other way is not evidence. (Added 2026-08-02, PROVISIONAL(supervisor); the control sentence added the same day, finding SS183.)

10.1 An implementation conforms to this document where it:

a) keeps custody out of membership: one persona, no participation as any held group (Clause 4);

b) holds only what Clause 5 permits and enumerates it to the owner alone;

c) gates every exercise on the owner's attested level, exercises nothing unattended, and demands the fresh high-level confirmation for transfer and revocation (Clause 6);

d) grants custody only by the key-custody ceremony, treats removal as rotation, and counts backups as custodians (Clause 7); and

e) supports the capture response of Clause 8, including the owner's out-of-wallet inventory.

10.2 Conformance to 6.2 shall be demonstrated by the negative case: a wallet with its owner absent, presented with each supported operation, exercises none — and the vacuity guard is the positive case, the same operations succeeding with the owner attested at level.

10.3 Conformance to 4.2 shall be demonstrated by observing the wallet's host on the mesh: it presents exactly one persona, and no traffic of any held group originates from it outside a ceremony.

11 Security considerations

11.1 The wallet concentrates what the architecture elsewhere distributes. Clause 8 prices the capture; 8.3 is the only structural reduction. A deployment that cannot tolerate single-point custody splits it or does not create it.

11.2 The owner is the gate, so the owner is the target: coercion of a present owner defeats every level the wallet can state. That residual is social, not protocol — named here so nobody believes a level solves it. Duress-shaped mitigations are application territory.

11.3 The inventory is surveillance-grade metadata (5.2 Note 1). Implementations carry the redaction discipline of Layer 5, 5.3.4 to the inventory as much as to the keys.

11.4 A wallet's owner-binding rides the person-authentication scale, and inherits its honesty: standing presence is probabilistic. 6.3's fresh-confirmation floor for the irreversible acts exists because a decaying standing level is exactly what a thief walks in behind.

12 What is not decided at this layer

This clause states gaps and contains no requirements.

12.1 Byte formats. The wallet's records, the inventory, recovery shares, the out-of-wallet inventory copy — owed to the formats companion.

12.2 The splitting scheme. 8.3 permits split custody and fixes nothing about the threshold scheme — owed with the formats companion, and the corpus's share-based recovery drafts are input.

12.3 Succession. A steward dies; the community's groups have to outlive them. Split custody (8.3) is the structural answer available today; whether the standard owes a named succession mechanism — dormant shares, time-locked recovery, social recovery — is undecided, and it is the most human-shaped gap in this document.

12.4 Per-operation level anchors. 6.1 requires stated levels and pins none; the anchors land with the person-authentication companion (C6 residuals).

12.5 Wallet-to-wallet protocol. 7.3's transfer decomposes into existing ceremonies; whether a dedicated wallet-to-wallet transfer exchange exists — one act instead of grant-plus-rotation — is undecided.
