Reality2 — Layer 1 binding: ESP-NOW (wifi-mesh, ordinal 5)

Normative document

About this reading edition

The numbered requirements and their provisional markers are retained from the cited source revision. Selected non-normative source notes, editorial attributions and internal working details are omitted. Clause numbers are unchanged; references to omitted notes may remain. The overview and worked examples are informative. Navigation and reading aids are not requirements.

Version: 0.9.0 · Date: 2026-08-24 · Status: PROVISIONAL(standard) · Scope: this bearer only

⚠ This is a binding document under Layer 1, Clause 11. It states what that clause defers to a binding and restates no requirement of Layer 1 (11.2). Where it appears to repeat a rule, it is citing the clause that governs and saying what this medium does about it.

Verbal forms. shall = requirement · should = recommendation · may = permission · can = possibility and capability.

The source-only implementation preface is omitted from this reading edition. The binding begins at clause 1.

1 Scope, and what is deliberately not here

1.1 This document binds ESP-NOW to bearer ordinal 5 (wifi-mesh), whose registry row Layer 1, 8.2.1 fixes.

1.2 It states items b), c), d), e) and j) of 11.1, and the announcement layout those require. Item a) is fixed by the registry row (Layer 1, 8.2.1), which also carries this bearer's reach, wire tier and largest payload.

1.2 (as amended 2026-08-14) It also states f) in 6a, g) in 6b, h) in 6c and i) in 6d. All ten items of 11.1 are now stated, and none of them by a pointer.

1.2a ⚠ Items h) and i) are owed and are stated nowhere; f) and g) were, and 6a and 6b now state them. This clause read that they were addressed in the prior draft this replaces. That citation cannot be followed: no binding document for this bearer exists in the corpus this one replaces, and this document's own first revision states none of them either (SS359, 2026-08-14). So all four — the quality derivation (f), the conditions producing each of the four states (g), the duty cycle or airtime budget (h) and the connection lifecycle and silence period (i) — were, for the only bearer this corpus binds, undocumented. Each is a measurement of a medium rather than a choice, and the debt was stated here rather than discharged by a pointer, because a pointer a reader cannot follow disposes of an obligation without meeting it.

⚠ All four were measured the same day and are now stated — 6a, 6b, 6c and 6d, the last gap at 6b.4 closed the same afternoon. The debt is left written above rather than deleted: the four items were discharged by a citation to nothing for the whole of this document's first revision, and a reader arriving at 6a should be able to see what it replaced. And two of the four found a declaration where they expected a measurement — the quality bounds at 6a and the connection nil at 6d Note 2 — which is what the obligation to state a fact is for.

1.3 Out of scope, named rather than left silent:

  • every other bearer. TCP (3), USB (4) and UDP (6) have no binding document in this corpus, and nothing here supplies one. A binding is per-bearer by construction and this one holds over ESP-NOW alone. ⚠ BLE (0) was in that list until 2026-08-17 and now has L1-BINDING-BLE.md — the sentence was true when written and was falsified by a change in another file, which is the class this corpus keeps finding; it is corrected here rather than left for a reader to trip over.
  • whether the Layer 2 capability summary is carried in the announcement. 5.2 makes it a may; this document defines the fields Layer 2, 5.1 makes mandatory and reserves the rest.
  • the class-hash algorithm. See 5.4 — it is a stated dependency, not a choice made here.

2 b) How a frame is placed in a transport unit

2.1 One Reality2 frame shall occupy exactly one ESP-NOW transmission, beginning at the first octet of the ESP-NOW payload.

2.2 No length prefix, terminator or padding shall be added. The medium delimits the unit: an ESP-NOW transmission carries its own length, which is the length Layer 4, 4.2.4 relies on a bearer to provide for the compact tier.

2.3 A transmission whose payload is shorter than the compact tier's fixed fields shall be discarded without error.

Note 1 to entry: 2.2 is why the compact tier is the right one for this ordinal. Layer 4, 4.2.4 requires the bearer to supply frame boundaries and states that carrying a length again would spend two bytes of every frame confirming what the medium already said. ESP-NOW supplies exactly that, so nothing is added and 8.2.1's tier assignment is met rather than worked around.

3 c) The discovery marker — and the one thing this medium may not be able to do

3.1 The marker required by Layer 1, 4.7.1 shall be the three-octet organisationally unique identifier of the ESP-NOW vendor-specific element carrying the transmission, in the medium framing and outside the Reality2 frame.

3.2 A receiver shall distinguish Reality2 traffic by that element alone, before parsing any frame.

3.3 ⚠ This item carries an unmeasured platform dependency and it is stated rather than assumed. Layer 1, 4.7.2 requires that the marker not be placed inside the frame, and Note 2 explains why: a marker inside the frame would be bytes the bearer added, and 4.2.1 says there are none. On ESP-NOW an application is ordinarily given the payload and nothing else — and the payload is the frame. So a marker written by the sending application at the head of the payload would breach 4.7.2, however convenient it is.

3.4 Whether an implementation on a given platform can set or observe the vendor-specific element of 3.1 is a property of that platform's interface, and this document does not assert it. An implementer who finds it unavailable has met 4.5's case: the requirement cannot be satisfied on that platform, which is a fact to state and not a licence to move the marker inside the frame.

Source note omitted from this reading edition.

4 d) Medium address to canonical hive identifier

4.1 The medium address is the six-octet ESP-NOW peer address.

4.2 A bearer shall associate a medium address with the canonical hive identifier Layer 1, 3.6 defines, and shall report that identifier upward, as 4.5.1 requires.

4.3 Until a canonical identifier is known for a medium address, a bearer may report one derived from that address, and shall replace it as soon as one is known — which is 4.5.3 applied, and the association is learned from received traffic rather than computed from the address.

4.4 A derived identifier shall not persist once a canonical one is known for that address (4.5.2).

Note 1 to entry: 4.5's Note 1 records this as the rule in this document most likely to be implemented wrongly, and its failure is silent — a hive reachable on three bearers becoming three neighbours. On this bearer the temptation is concrete: the six-octet address is stable, unique and free, and reporting it upward costs nothing and looks correct.

5 The announcement, and e) the payload it must fit in

5.1 Maximum transport unit payload

5.1.1 The maximum payload shall be 250 octets, fixed, and nothing varies it.

5.1.2 This is the value Layer 1, 8.2.1 records for ordinal 5, and it is the value measured on the boards this binding was drafted against.

5.2 The announcement is a frame

5.2.1 A beacon on this bearer shall be carried as a Layer 4 frame of type HEARTBEAT, whose payload is the announcement body of 5.3.

5.3 The announcement body

5.3.1 The body shall be a sequence of elements, each type (1 octet) || length (1 octet) || value (length octets), in ascending type order, with no element repeated.

5.3.2 The elements shall be:

Type Length Value Required by
0x01 4 beacon identifier L2 5.1 b), 5.4.1
0x02 4 class hash L2 5.1 c) — content owed, see 5.4
0x03 1 build-mode declaration L2 5.4a.1 — see Clause 6

5.3.3 An element whose type is not in 5.3.2 shall be skipped and its length honoured.

5.3.4 A body whose final element is truncated shall be treated as unreadable from that element onward, and shall not be treated as though the element were absent.

5.3.4a The body's extent shall be the payload length of the frame carrying it (5.2.1, 2.2), and a body shorter than that length shall be treated as unreadable throughout — not as a shorter body that is well-formed to its end. PROVISIONAL(r2, SS390).

Note 0 to entry: 5.3.4 protects a cut INSIDE an element and 5.3.4a protects a cut AT a boundary, and without the second the first reads as covering both. A body cut at an element boundary is byte-for-byte a shorter, complete, well-formed body: nothing in it is truncated, so 5.3.4 has no subject, and 6.3's well-formed to its end, element 0x03 absent applies in terms — the verdict is production, which 6.4 forbids a scanner to reach from unknown. ‼ And the element at risk is the one that matters by construction rather than by bad luck: 5.3.1 fixes ascending type order and the build-mode declaration is 0x03, the last of the three required elements, so it is the first thing any truncation removes. Measured 2026-08-18 on a development body of fifteen octets: truncated to twelve — the boundary after the class hash — a decoder reports no truncation and concludes production.

5.3.5 The beacon identifier shall be the value Layer 2, 5.4.1 requires — generated randomly by the hive or installed at manufacture, and stable — and shall not be derived from any other identifier the hive holds.

Note 1 to entry: 5.3.4 is what makes three outcomes distinguishable, and without it two of them collapse. Layer 2 needs absent, declared and unreadable to be different: 5.4a.2 forbids a production beacon to carry any development-related bytes, so absence must be encodable as the absence of bytes rather than as a value meaning production; and 5.4a.4 requires a scanner that cannot read the field to reach unknown. A fixed-offset layout gives two outcomes and a scanner cannot tell a field that was never sent from one that was cut off. The type-length form gives the third for one octet per element.

Note 2 to entry: 5.3.1 and 5.3.3 keep this minimal by Roy's instruction rather than by taste — the goal is to create the least complicated set of rules and leave the rest to convention, and the reason for relying on convention is that it minimises the amount of data that has to be sent. The mandatory body is fifteen octets of a 250-octet payload. Nothing that a convention could carry instead is carried here.

Note 3 to entry: Nothing in this body is derived from the hive's trust group. Layer 2, 5.3 a) bars a beacon from carrying any identifier of, or value derived from, the hive's trust group, and 5.3 b) bars any identifier of the hive other than the beacon identifier. That constrains the encoding and not only the choice of what to advertise — a value that merely happened to be a function of group material would breach it.

5.4 ⚠ The class hash: width defined, content owed, and the dependency named

5.4.1 The element at 0x02 shall be four octets wide, big-endian.

5.4.2 The algorithm producing that value is not fixed by this document, and this document does not resolve the contradiction that prevents it. The corpus states three incompatible things about the class hash:

  • Layer 4, 7.1.1 is stated as a requirement: every identifier hash is FNV-1a, 32-bit, with the offset basis and prime given — and 7.1.4 places class strings behind an identifier hash by name;
  • 01-terminology.md 3.9 Note 4 states as fact that both scales share one 32-bit FNV-1a space;
  • Layer 2, 5.5 Note 0 states that the algorithm is still unspecified, register finding SS1, and reasons from that absence that an unspecified algorithm selects its own default, and the default is the weak one.

5.4.3 A conforming implementation of this binding shall emit four octets in this element and shall not treat their content as interoperable until SS1 and SS310 are settled.

6 j) The build-mode declaration

6.1 A hive running a development image shall include element 0x03, with length 1 and value 0x01.

6.2 A hive running a production image shall not include element 0x03, and its announcement shall carry no other development-related octet.

6.3 A scanner shall read the three outcomes as:

Observed Read as
element 0x03 present with value 0x01 development
body well-formed to its end, element 0x03 absent production
body truncated, unparseable, or the element's value not 0x01 unknown

6.4 A scanner shall not read unknown as production.

6.5 An indicator, lamp or other surface visible to a person shall not be reported as the declaration 6.1 requires.

Note 3 to entry: A lamp is not this. 5.4a.1 requires the declaration in a beacon, which is a wire artefact; a pulsing indicator is visible to a person and invisible to a peer, and is no part of 5.4a. An implementation may light one, and 6.5 forbids reporting it as the declaration.

6a f) How the quality value is derived

6a.1 The quality value of Layer 1, 5.3.1 shall be derived from the received signal strength the medium reports for each reception, mapped linearly onto the closed interval from 0 to 1 between an implementer-chosen floor and ceiling in dBm.

6a.2 It shall be derived from receptions only — never from a transmission or its outcome — and held per medium address, because quality is a property of a link rather than of a hive.

6a.3 The floor and ceiling shall be stated by the implementation. This document fixes neither.

Note 2 to entry: ⚠ A HAZARD OF THIS MEDIUM'S SDK, RECORDED BECAUSE IT COSTS A BINDING AUTHOR NOTHING TO AVOID AND IS INVISIBLE ONCE MADE. The signal strength is documented in dBm — a signed quantity — and at least one vendor accessor returns it in an unsigned byte, so -39 arrives as 217. Mapped without sign extension, every reception exceeds the ceiling and quality is a constant 1.0 at every signal level the radio has ever seen. That implementation satisfied Layer 1, 12.1 b) throughout: the value was reported, and it was current. Sign-extended, the same captures gave 0.20, 0.52, 0.85 and 1.00. This is why 5.3.1 carries a verification requiring the value to be shown to vary, and why 6a.3 asks for the bounds to be stated rather than trusted.

6b g) The conditions producing each state

6b.1 The states of Layer 1, 5.2 shall be produced on this bearer by the following conditions.

State Condition on this medium
available the radio is up and the peer table has room
restricted the peer table is full — see 6b.2
unavailable a send cannot be accepted now and the cause is expected to clear: the driver's queue is full, or the radio is momentarily down
failed the radio stack is not running, the interface is wrong for the peer, or the channel does not match — see 6b.4

6b.2 The peer table of this medium is bounded at 20 peers in total and 6 encrypted peers. Adding a peer beyond either bound is refused by the medium, and the count in use is readable, so the condition is observable before it is reached.

6b.3 A bearer shall not report available while the peer table is full, and shall not signal a full peer table only on the path that adds a peer.

6b.4 failed shall be reported where the send path reports a condition that elapsed time does not cure: the radio stack is not running, an internal fault of the medium layer, the interface is not the one this peer is reachable on, or the channel does not match the peer's. (SDK identifiers, given as the observable rather than as the condition: ESP_ERR_ESPNOW_NOT_INIT, ESP_ERR_ESPNOW_INTERNAL, ESP_ERR_ESPNOW_IF, ESP_ERR_ESPNOW_CHAN.)

6b.5 unavailable shall be reported where the send path reports transient queue or heap pressure, which is the only condition on this medium that a later attempt is expected to clear. (ESP_ERR_ESPNOW_NO_MEM.)

6b.6 A failure attributable to the request rather than to the bearer shall not change the bearer's state: an unrecognised argument, a peer that is not registered, and an attempt to register a peer already registered are refusals of that call. A full peer table is the exception and is not a request fault — it is 5.2's restricted (6b.3).

Note 1 to entry: 6b.2's two figures are the SDK's own header constants and are cited as such, with a control: the same search of the same header returns the address length and the key length, so a nil would have meant the wrong file was being read. They are not a reading taken from a radio driven to its limit, and are recorded that way deliberately — a constant a header states and a constant a device honours are different claims, and this document makes the weaker one.

6b.7 Where the send path reports a condition this document does not classify, the bearer shall not report available. It shall report unavailable unless it already holds failed or restricted, which it keeps.

Note 0c to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(standard).

Note 1a to entry: The scope of 6b.4 and 6b.5, stated because the two halves rest on different evidence. The error set and its values are measured from the medium's own bindings, with a present-control — the same search returns 27 neighbouring constants, so the set is not a partial read. The classification of each into expects to clear and needs intervention is a reading of what each condition means, not a measurement of a radio driven into it — and which subset the send call can actually produce is an interface-contract fact that is not measured either. Recorded this way because a classification and a capture are different objects, and 5.2's whole distinction is whether waiting helps.

Note 1b to entry: 6b.6 is the case a bearer will get wrong by being conscientious. An implementation that maps every send failure onto a state will set one because a caller named a peer it had not registered — a fact about that call, true of no other peer and of no later moment. 5.2's states describe what the bearer can carry to the peers it has, so a refusal attributable to the request is outside them; and the cost of collapsing the two is a bearer reporting itself unable while it is carrying frames to everyone else. The measured instance had the opposite fault and the same root — ten distinct conditions discarded at one site by a pattern that binds nothing, so failed was unreachable and every send failure read as transient.

6c h) Duty cycle and airtime budget

6c.1 This binding states no duty cycle and no airtime budget, and a bearer conforming to it declares B8 = not regulated in its profile.

6c.2 ⚠ 6c.1 is a statement about this document and this implementation, and not a finding about the law. Whether the medium is subject to a transmission duty cycle or airtime budget — which is what Layer 1, 9.1 turns on — is a regulatory fact about the band and the jurisdiction of operation, not a property of any code. This document does not determine it, and an operator whose jurisdiction imposes one is bound by 9.2 and 9.3 whatever this clause says.

6d i) Establishment, and the absence of a lifecycle

6d.1 Layer 1, Clause 10 does not apply to this bearer, on 10.1 read with its Note 3: a peer registration here is satisfied unilaterally and locally, the far end neither knowing of it nor participating in it, so it cannot be lost by silence and cannot be reattempted.

6d.2 The medium nonetheless places a precondition on unicast, which 11.1 i) requires to be stated: a frame addressed to an individual peer shall not be carried until that peer has been registered with the medium, and a send to an unregistered peer is refused rather than transmitted.

6d.3 At most 20 peers may be registered at once, of which at most 6 may be encrypted (6b.2). Exhausting that limit is 5.2's restricted and is not a connection failure.

6d.4 A registration shall persist until it is explicitly removed or the device restarts. This binding states no silence period and no idle timeout, because nothing on this medium becomes unreachable by an elapse: a peer becomes unreachable by an act.

6d.5 A bearer shall register a peer with the medium on first reception from its medium address, under the transitional identifier of 4.3.

6d.6 Registration under 6d.5 shall not be treated as evidence about who the peer is.

6d.7 ⚠ Registering the canonical identifier of 4.2 is not discharged by this document, and no format in this corpus supplies the means. See SS375.

Note 0d to entry: 6d.5 registers what the bearer already knows and asserts nothing. The transitional identifier is derived from the medium address (4.3), so binding that identifier to that address is self-consistent by construction — it says only that these bytes came from here, which is what was observed. An outsider registering its own address under its own derived identifier gains nothing it did not have: it could already transmit. That is the test 6d.6 records — a mechanism is safe here where it grants an attacker nothing they did not already hold, not where it is hard to abuse.

Note 0f to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(standard).

Note 2 to entry: Both of this bearer's nils were fields somebody had already filled in, and the obligation to state them is what made anybody ask whether they were true. regulated: false and no connection were declarations before they were measurements; measuring found one of them unsupported. That is 11.1 doing to h) and i) what it did to f): the requirement to state a fact found that the fact had been assumed.

7 What this closes, what it narrows, and what it leaves open

7.1 SS304 is NARROWED, not closed. That entry records that the development-mode declaration is obliged on every bearer while its byte layout is deferred to a binding document that exists for none. Clause 6 supplies the layout for ESP-NOW, so an image on this bearer can now announce conformantly. It remains open for every other bearer discussed in that source finding, and 5.4a.1's on every bearer is not satisfied by one binding.

7.2 SS264 is NARROWED, not closed. One ordinal now has a binding; six do not.

7.3 SS1 and SS310 are untouched and are load-bearing here. 5.4.2 states the dependency and 5.4.3 bounds what may be claimed while it stands.

7.4 Layer 2, 7.1.1 step 1 becomes answerable on this bearer for the first time, subject to 3.3's platform dependency — which is the largest consequence of this document and is not what prompted it.

7.5 Not covered: the capability summary; the duty-class declaration of 5.4b; every other bearer; and whether the announcement should carry anything a convention could carry instead — which Roy's instruction answers no by default.
