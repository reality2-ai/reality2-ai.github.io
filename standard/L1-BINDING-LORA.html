Reality2 — Layer 1 binding: LoRa (lora, ordinal 2)

Normative document

About this reading edition

The numbered requirements and their provisional markers are retained from the cited source revision. Selected non-normative source notes, editorial attributions and internal working details are omitted. Clause numbers are unchanged; references to omitted notes may remain. The overview and worked examples are informative. Navigation and reading aids are not requirements.

Version: 0.9.0 · Date: 2026-09-03 · Status: PROVISIONAL(standard) · Scope: this bearer only

⚠ This is a binding document under Layer 1, Clause 11. It states what that clause defers to a binding and restates no requirement of Layer 1 (11.2). Where it appears to repeat a rule, it is citing the clause that governs and saying what this medium does about it.

Verbal forms. shall = requirement · should = recommendation · may = permission · can = possibility and capability.

1 Scope, and what is deliberately not here

1.1 This document binds LoRa — the chirp-spread-spectrum modulation used peer-to-peer, without LoRaWAN — to bearer ordinal 2 (lora), whose registry row Layer 1, 8.2.1 fixes: reach long-range, wire tier compact, largest payload 222 B, relative cost 5.

1.2 It states items b) in Clause 2, c) in Clause 3, d) in Clause 4, e) in 5.1, j) in Clause 6, f) in 6a, g) in 6b, h) in 6c and i) in 6d. Item a) is fixed by the registry row. All ten items are stated, and none by a pointer. Layer 1, 11.1a has no subject on this medium (2a).

1.3 ⚠ This is the third binding, and it inverts the second on nearly every axis. BLE holds connections and is unregulated; LoRa holds none and is regulated, so Clause 9 engages here for the first time in this corpus (6c) and Clause 10 does not (6d). The two documents are worth reading together: between them they exercise both conditional clauses of Layer 1, and neither could have done it alone.

1.4 ⚠ And this medium has no address of any kind, which is the hard part of the document. A LoRa transmission is a preamble, a sync word, an optional header, a payload and a checksum. There is no source field, no destination field and no address anywhere in it — every transmission is heard by every radio in range whose sync word and modulation match. Clause 4 is where that is dealt with, and it is why this binding wraps a frame where the other two do not.

1.5 Out of scope, named rather than left silent:

  • LoRaWAN. This binds LoRa the modulation, not the network stack above it. LoRaWAN has its own addressing, its own join procedure and its own duty-cycle machinery, and a hive using it would be carrying Reality2 frames over a network rather than over a medium.
  • The channel plan and the region. 6c.3 obliges a deployment to declare them and this document fixes neither. The corpus contains no region ruling, and a document that pinned one would be legislating for jurisdictions it has not read.
  • The class-hash algorithm (SS1), inherited and not introduced here.
  • Which spreading factor a deployment uses. It is declared (6c.3) because it determines airtime and therefore the budget, not fixed, because it trades range against airtime and that trade is a deployment's to make.

2 b) How a frame is placed in a transport unit

2.1 One frame shall occupy exactly one LoRa transmission.

2.2 The transmission payload shall be the wrapping of 4.3 followed by the frame, and a bearer shall remove that wrapping before delivering the frame upward (Layer 1, 4.2.2).

2.3 A bearer shall use the explicit header mode, so that the transmission carries its own payload length.

2.4 Transport units are delimited by the medium: a LoRa transmission is a discrete event with a length in its header, so the second half of 11.1 b) — how transport units are delimited where the medium does not delimit them — has no subject here.

2.5 A bearer shall enable the payload checksum, and shall discard a transmission whose checksum fails without delivering anything upward.

2.6 Layer 1, 4.4.3 has no subject on this medium: raw LoRa performs no acknowledgement and no retransmission of its own, so there is nothing for a bearer to refuse to expose upward. A completed transmission is the radio having transmitted, which is all it ever claimed.

Note 1a to entry: ⚠ WHY THE WRAPPING IS NOT 4.7.2's PROHIBITION WEARING ANOTHER HAT, AND NOT 4.2.3 EITHER — BOTH OBJECTIONS ARE REACHABLE AND BOTH FAIL. 4.7.2 forbids the marker being placed inside the frame; the wrapping of 4.2 is not the marker (the sync word is, 3.1) and it is not inside the frame — 4.2.1 is about the bytes delivered upward, and the wrapping is removed before they are (2.2). 4.2.3 forbids a bearer interpreting the contents of a frame; a bearer reading its own wrapping is reading bytes it wrote, and it reads nothing the frame contains. The distinction that matters: this bearer never inspects a frame to decide anything, which is the property 4.2.3 exists to protect and which the whole of Clause 4 here preserves.

Note 2 to entry: 2.5 is 4.2.1 applied to a medium that can deliver corrupted bytes. A frame delivered upward must be exactly the bytes handed down at the peer; a transmission that fails its checksum is not those bytes, and passing it up would make the bearer the source of a malformed frame. The alternative — delivering it and letting a higher layer notice — breaks 4.2.1 and moves a medium's defect into a layer that cannot attribute it.

2a Roles: 11.1a has no subject here

2a.1 LoRa has no roles a device adopts: there is no initiator and no responder, no central and no peripheral, and no state a device holds about another before transmitting. So Layer 1, 11.1a has no subject on this medium and this binding states no role-concurrency obligation.

Note 1 to entry: The medium is nonetheless half-duplex, and that is a different fact. A radio transmitting cannot receive, so a hive is deaf for the airtime of every frame it sends — which at the slowest spreading factors is a substantial fraction of a second. That is not a role and 11.1a does not reach it; it is a property the cadence rules of Layer 2, 6.2 and the budget of 6c are already written against, and it is named here so a reader does not mistake its absence from 2a.1 for an oversight.

2a.2 A direct LoRa bearer while its platform is receptive shall keep its radio receptive except while it performs one channel-activity detection or transmits one frame. An accepted frame waiting for either operation shall be held in bounded memory; it shall not cause the bearer to remain in a transmit-oriented state while it waits.

2a.3 Where a direct LoRa bearer defers a transmission, it shall return the radio to receive during that deferral and shall process arrivals before making its next transmission decision. This makes the deferral window available to 6e.5: an overheard relay can suppress the queued duplicate before this hive becomes deaf to send it.

2a.4 A direct LoRa bearer that is receptive only at intervals shall not use a common fixed listen-then-transmit phase as its medium-access regimen. It shall randomise each transmission opportunity from entropy that is local to the hive, retain work while it is not receptive, and state its rendezvous behaviour in its Layer 1 B9 profile value. For direct LoRa, that intermittent value has a nonzero receptive window and open local ranges for both its next-window delay and its transmission-opportunity delay. A shared phase makes every hive deaf at the same instant; no queue can recover a frame that no receiver was able to hear.

Note 2 to entry: Continuous reception is the direct LoRa default; transmission is a short exception, not the second half of a global cycle. A duty-cycled profile is a distinct deployment choice, not an optimisation that can be inferred from an empty queue. The random contention delay of 6e remains necessary in either profile: independent scheduling prevents a permanent common phase, while carrier sensing and relay suppression decide a particular transmission.

3 c) The discovery marker

3.1 The discovery marker Layer 1, 4.7.1 requires shall be the LoRa sync word, carried in the physical framing ahead of the payload, with the value 0x52 on parts taking an eight-bit sync word and 0x5424 on parts taking a sixteen-bit one.

3.2 A bearer shall not use the public-network sync word, and shall not use the library or vendor default for a private network.

3.3 The marker shall not be placed inside the frame (Layer 1, 4.7.2), and on this medium it is not placed inside the transport unit at all.

Note 1 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

Note 3 to entry: What the marker does NOT do, stated because the clause says distinguish Reality2 traffic from other traffic and this marker's discrimination is bounded. A sync word separates this deployment from public LoRaWAN and from any deployment using a different value. It does not separate it from another deployment that happens to choose 0x52, and nothing in the medium can. 3.2 exists to make that boundary as wide as the medium allows: the defaults are where collisions actually live, because they are what everything else uses.

4 d) Medium address to canonical hive identifier

4.1 ⚠ This medium has no address. A LoRa transmission carries no source field and no destination field. So this binding creates one, in the wrapping Layer 1, 4.2.2 permits, because 4.5.1 obliges a bearer to report the sender's canonical hive identifier and 4.2.3 forbids it to read the frame in order to find one.

4.2 The wrapping shall be four octets, carrying the sender's medium address, and shall precede the frame in the transmission payload.

4.3 A hive shall generate its medium address at random, shall change it whenever the persona changes (Layer 5, 5.1.4), and shall not derive it from any other identifier it holds.

4.4 A bearer shall associate a medium address with the canonical hive identifier Layer 1, 3.6 defines, shall report that identifier upward as 4.5.1 requires, and shall learn the association from received traffic rather than computing it from the address.

4.5 A bearer shall discard the association when a hive's medium address changes, and shall not treat two addresses as one peer on any evidence this layer holds.

4.6 A bearer shall not treat the medium address as evidence of who the sender is.

Note 1 to entry: ⚠ THE ADDRESS IS A LABEL AND NOT A CLAIM, WHICH IS WHY 4.6 IS A CLAUSE AND NOT A NOTE. It is four octets any transmitter may set to anything, on a medium with no authentication whatever. Its only job is to let a bearer say these two receptions came from the same radio, so that 4.5.3's derived identifier has something to be derived from and the neighbour table does not collapse every peer into one. Everything that depends on knowing who a peer is happens above Layer 4, which is why a forgeable label costs nothing here — and why treating it as evidence would be the one way to make it cost something.

Note 3 to entry: Four octets is a size chosen against the population, and it is stated so a later revision can argue with the reasoning rather than the number. A long-range bearer's neighbourhood is bounded by airtime long before it is bounded by address space, and four octets cost 1.8% of the largest payload. A collision costs two hives one shared neighbour entry until either rotates, which is a degradation and not a failure.

5 The announcement, and e) the payload it must fit in

5.1 Maximum transport unit payload

5.1.1 The maximum transport unit payload is 222 octets, fixed by the registry row (Layer 1, 8.2.1), and nothing on this medium varies it, so 11.1 e)'s second half has no subject.

5.1.2 The transmission carrying it is therefore 226 octets — the four-octet wrapping of 4.2 and the frame — which shall be within the maximum the modulation permits.

Note 1 to entry: The wrapping is charged to the medium and not to the frame, and the arithmetic is why that is honest rather than convenient. A LoRa transmission admits 255 octets; 222 of frame and 4 of wrapping is 226, leaving 29 unused. So the registry's 222 is carried whole, and the wrapping costs headroom that was never available to a frame. Had the sum exceeded 255 the registry row would be wrong and this document would have had to say so.

5.2 The announcement is a frame

5.2.1 The announcement shall be a frame, carried exactly as any other frame is, and addressed as Layer 2 requires.

5.2.2 A hive shall announce while any LoRa bearer is in service (Layer 1, 3.5a) and that bearer participates in discovery, subject to the cadence Layer 2, 6.2 sets for a regulated bearer and to the budget of 6c.

Note 1 to entry: This is the ESP-NOW arrangement rather than the BLE one, and the medium decides it. BLE has advertising — an unaddressed, unacknowledged, unrelayable transmission that exists to be overheard — so its announcement is not a frame. LoRa has no such facility: every transmission is the same kind of transmission. So the announcement is a frame, and the only thing distinguishing it is what it carries.

5.3 The announcement body

5.3.1 The body shall be a sequence of elements, each type (1 octet) || length (1 octet) || value (length octets), in ascending type order, with no element repeated.

5.3.2 The elements shall be:

Type Length Value Required by
0x01 4 beacon identifier L2 5.1 b), 5.4.1
0x02 4 class hash L2 5.1 c)
0x03 1 build-mode declaration L2 5.4a.1 — see Clause 6
0x04 2 duty-class declaration L2 5.4b — see 5.4

5.3.3 An element whose type is not in 5.3.2 shall be skipped and its length honoured.

5.3.4 A body whose final element is truncated shall be treated as unreadable from that element onward, and shall not be treated as though the element were absent.

5.3.4a The body's extent shall be the payload length of the frame carrying it (5.2.1), which the explicit header of 2.3 states, and a body shorter than that length shall be treated as unreadable throughout — not as a shorter body that is well-formed to its end. PROVISIONAL(r2, SS390).

Note 0 to entry: 5.3.4 protects a cut INSIDE an element and 5.3.4a protects a cut AT a boundary, and without the second the first reads as covering both. A body cut at an element boundary is byte-for-byte a shorter, complete, well-formed body: nothing in it is truncated, so 5.3.4 has no subject, and 6.3's well-formed to its end, element 0x03 absent applies in terms — the verdict is production, which 6.4 forbids a scanner to reach from unknown. ‼ And the element at risk is the one that matters by construction rather than by bad luck: 5.3.1 fixes ascending type order and the build-mode declaration is 0x03, the last of the three required elements, so it is the first thing any truncation removes. Measured 2026-08-18 on a development body of fifteen octets: truncated to twelve — the boundary after the class hash — a decoder reports no truncation and concludes production.

5.3.5 The beacon identifier shall be the value Layer 2, 5.4.1 requires, and shall not be derived from any other identifier the hive holds.

5.4 ⚠ The duty-class element, and why it appears here first

5.4.1 A hive receptive only at intervals shall carry element 0x04, whose value is two octets: the duty class Layer 2, 5.4b defines, then the longest interval between its receptive windows, coarsely stated as 5.4.3 encodes it.

5.4.2 A hive continuously receptive shall not carry element 0x04.

Note 0 to entry: ⚠ THE TABLE AT 5.3.2 SAID LENGTH 1 AND 5.4.1 SAYS TWO OCTETS, AND THE TABLE WAS WRONG — CORRECTED 2026-08-18. 5.4.1 names two fields, the class and the interval, so one octet could not hold both. Found by writing the encoder: a codec has to emit a length, and the two statements gave different ones. A summary table and the clause it summarises disagreeing is the shape a reader resolves in whichever direction they read first — and an encoder written from the table would have produced a body a decoder written from the clause reads as truncated, which 5.3.4 makes unreadable rather than absent.

5.4.3 PROVISIONAL(supervisor), and it is a proposed encoding for a field the corpus says has none. The interval octet shall be read as 2^n seconds, where n is the octet's value, rounded up to the next representable value — so 0x00 is one second, 0x08 is about four minutes, 0x10 is about eighteen hours, and 0xFF is reserved and means the interval is not stated.

Note 1 to entry: ⚠ THE OTHER TWO BINDINGS CARRY NO SUCH ELEMENT AND BOTH ARE THEREBY UNABLE TO ANNOUNCE A DUTY-CYCLED HIVE. Layer 2, 5.2 obliges a beacon emitted by a hive receptive only at intervals to carry the declaration, and neither the ESP-NOW body nor the BLE one has a type for it. It is added here rather than there because this is the bearer where the case is ordinary: a long-range radio is what a battery-powered, intermittently-receptive hive uses, and Layer 2, 5.4b's own reason for existing is that an unentangled stranger who can verify nothing still needs to know to hold rather than transmit at a sleeping radio.

Source note omitted from this reading edition.

This document proposes bytes, and marks them provisional, because this is the bearer where the omission costs something. A long-range radio is where duty-cycled hives actually live; SS11 has been open since the first extraction with no instance forcing it; and a proposal Roy can reject in one line is more use than a third silent omission. A power-of-two ladder is chosen because the value is a bound rather than a measurement — a receiver uses it to decide whether to hold or to transmit, and doubling steps are the coarseness 5.4b.1 asks for in a form two implementations cannot read differently.

6 j) The build-mode declaration

6.1 The build-mode declaration Layer 2, 5.4a.1 requires shall be element type 0x03 of 5.3.2, one octet, with the values:

Value Meaning
0x01 development

6.2 A production hive shall not emit element 0x03 at all.

6.3 A receiver that finds no element 0x03 shall treat the declaration as absent; one that finds a value other than 0x01 shall treat it as unreadable; and neither shall be treated as production declared.

6a f) How the quality value is derived

6a.1 The quality value of Layer 1, 5.3.1 shall be derived from the signal-to-noise ratio the receiver reports for the transmission, and may be adjusted by the received signal strength where the implementation has both.

6a.2 The derivation shall map a stated floor to 0.0 and a stated ceiling to 1.0, shall clamp outside them, and shall be monotonic in signal-to-noise ratio.

6a.3 The floor and the ceiling shall be stated by the implementation. This document fixes neither.

6a.4 The value shall be held per medium address, and shall be discarded with the association when the address changes (4.5).

Note 1 to entry: Signal-to-noise ratio rather than signal strength, and this is the one place this medium's physics changes the answer. LoRa demodulates below the noise floor, so a usable link can have a received signal strength that looks like nothing at all; strength alone would report a working peer as unusable at exactly the ranges this bearer exists for. The other two bindings derive from strength because on their media strength is the whole story.

6a-bis B5, the fade rate, and why the BLE pattern does not transfer

6a.5 The bearer profile shall state B5 fade rate, and that value shall exceed the longest interval the airtime budget can produce under load (Layer 2, 6.3.2).

6a.6 The value shall not be derived from the interval observed while the bearer is idle.

Note 1 to entry: ⚠ THE BLE BINDING SETS B5 AGAINST A DECLARED RANGE OF ADVERTISING INTERVALS AND THAT PATTERN IS FORBIDDEN HERE. Layer 2, 6.2.3 forbids giving a regulated bearer a fixed beacon interval — on such a bearer the cadence is an outcome, not a setting, falling out of what the budget has left after everything else. So there is no declared range for B5 to exceed, and 6.3.2 substitutes the only thing there is: the longest interval the budget can produce under load.

6b g) The conditions producing each state

6b.1 available. The radio is initialised, the modulation and channel are configured, and the transmission budget of 6c is not exhausted.

6b.2 restricted. This bearer cannot reach restricted, and what prevents it is that the medium holds nothing per peer: there is no peer table, no registration, no connection and no resource that a further peer consumes (6d.1). Layer 1, 5.2's Note 1 gives the state's purpose as a hard limit on simultaneous peers; this medium has no such limit, so the condition that produces the state does not exist here.

6b.3 unavailable. The transmission budget is exhausted (6c.4, and Layer 1, 9.2 requires this state for it); or the radio is powered down or withdrawn by the platform.

6b.4 failed. The radio does not respond, its configuration is rejected, or the host-to-radio transport errors.

6c h) Duty cycle and airtime budget

6c.1 Layer 1, 9.1 applies to this bearer, and a bearer conforming to this document shall declare B8 regulated as true.

6c.2 A bearer shall compute the airtime of a transmission before sending it, and shall not send where doing so would exceed the budget (Layer 1, 9.3).

6c.3 A deployment shall declare the region it operates in, the channel plan, the spreading factor and bandwidth in use, the duty cycle or airtime budget those imply, and any medium-access parameter the region imposes (6e.7).

6c.3a Where a region imposes no duty cycle, a deployment shall declare that absence, and shall not state a budget in place of it.

Note 4 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

6c.4 A bearer whose budget is exhausted shall report unavailable (6b.3) and shall not accept a frame it cannot lawfully transmit.

Note 2 to entry: The spreading factor is in 6c.3's list because it changes the budget by more than an order of magnitude. The same frame at the slowest setting occupies the air for many times what it does at the fastest, so a budget stated without it is not a budget. This is also why 6c.2 obliges computing airtime per transmission rather than counting frames: on this medium frames are not comparable to one another.

Note 3 to entry: 9.3 has no exception and this binding adds none. A budget with an exception for important traffic is exceeded whenever traffic is important, which on a shared medium is when everyone else needs it too — and on this medium the obligation is also the operator's in law.

6d i) No connection, and what the medium requires instead

6d.1 Layer 1, Clause 10 does not apply to this bearer. LoRa requires nothing before a frame can be carried: a transmission is emitted and any radio in range with the matching sync word and modulation receives it.

6d.2 The medium places no precondition on carrying a frame to an individual peer, so there is no limit on how many such preconditions may be held at once and nothing that has to come to be met. This is the second and third parts of 11.1 i) answered in the negative, which is the whole of what they ask on a medium like this one.

6d.3 A bearer shall not require a peer to be known before transmitting.

Note 2 to entry: A consequence worth stating: every frame on this bearer is heard by every hive in range, whatever it is addressed to. Addressing is Layer 4's and is not a property of this medium — so a hive receives, and its bearer delivers upward, transmissions it is not the destination of. That is ordinary for a shared medium and is what Layer 3's relay rules and Layer 5's delivery gate are written against; it is named here because a reader coming from a connection-oriented bearer will expect the medium to have filtered already, and it has not.

6e k) How the medium is shared

6e.1 A bearer shall determine the medium to be idle immediately before transmitting, by the modulation-aware channel-activity detection the receiver provides, and shall not begin a transmission while a preamble or a transmission of the configured modulation is detected.

6e.2 Where the medium is not idle, a bearer shall defer, wait a randomly chosen whole number of slot periods, and test again; and shall treat a bounded number of consecutive deferrals as the medium being unavailable (6b.3) rather than continuing indefinitely.

6e.3 The slot period shall be at least the time one channel-activity detection occupies at the configured spreading factor and bandwidth. A slot shorter than the test that fills it cannot separate two transmitters.

6e.4 A bearer relaying a frame shall wait a randomly chosen delay before transmitting it, drawn from a window whose width decreases as the received quality value (6a) of the frame being relayed decreases.

6e.5 A bearer shall abandon a pending relay transmission where, before it begins, it receives the same frame — identified as Layer 3, 5.3.1 identifies it — from another hive.

6e.6 A bearer shall not transmit an originated frame ahead of a relay transmission already pending under 6e.4 solely because the originated frame is its own.

6e.7 Where the region a deployment declares (6c.3) imposes its own medium-access requirement — a carrier-sense threshold, a minimum sensing duration, a maximum dwell time, or a channel-hopping obligation — those parameters govern, and a bearer shall apply them in place of any less restrictive value this clause would otherwise permit.

6e.8 A bearer shall not transmit where the region has not been declared.

Note 0 to entry: ‼ THE REGIMES DIFFER IN KIND AND NOT ONLY IN NUMBER, WHICH IS WHY 6e.7 DELEGATES RATHER THAN TABULATES. Some jurisdictions impose a duty cycle and no sensing obligation; some impose mandatory carrier sense with a stated threshold and duration and no duty cycle; some impose a dwell time per channel and require hopping; and some impose a radiated-power limit and none of the three. A binding that fixed one shape would be wrong in most of the world — and, worse, wrong invisibly, because a bearer applying a foreign rule is conformant, silent, and less useful than the law requires. This document therefore states the discipline the medium needs (6e.1–6e.6) and gives the jurisdiction precedence over it (6e.7), which are two different questions that a single table would have merged.

Note 0a to entry: 6e.8 is the same refusal as 6c.4 pointed at a missing declaration rather than a spent budget. A bearer that transmitted under no declared region would be choosing a regime by omission, and the one it would choose is whatever its author's country happens to be — which is a defect that travels with the firmware and shows itself only in somebody else's jurisdiction. A refusal is recoverable by declaring the region; a transmission is not recoverable at all.

Note 1 to entry: 6e.4 IS INVERTED ON PURPOSE AND THE INVERSION IS THE WHOLE MECHANISM. A hive that heard the frame weakly is far from the sender, so its relay carries the frame furthest; giving it the shortest delay makes the most useful transmission happen first. The near hives, still waiting, then hear that relay and abandon their own under 6e.5. A uniform random delay spaces the transmissions and is indifferent to which of them was worth making; this one spends the airtime on distance. The two clauses are a pair and neither works alone: without 6e.5 the inversion merely reorders a scramble, and without 6e.4 the suppression cancels whichever hive happened to draw the short straw, which is as likely to be the nearest.

Note 2 to entry: 6e.5 is suppression and NOT retransmission, and the distinction is where this medium differs from an ordinary packet network. The obvious reliability move — send it again a few times and let the duplicate filter sort it out — is sound on a medium where a frame costs microseconds. Here a frame costs a substantial fraction of a second and the medium is shared with every hive in range, so repeating a relayed broadcast multiplies the scarcest resource by the repeat count at every hop, and Layer 3, 5.3.1 makes the copies harmless without making them free. An overheard relay is already an acknowledgement that the frame is travelling; 6e.5 spends that information instead of spending airtime. Retransmission remains the right answer for a frame addressed to one hive whose delivery is being confirmed — a bounded matter between two parties — and nothing here forbids it.

Note 3 to entry: 6e.1 does not make the medium collision-free and no clause here claims it does. Detection is local: two hives out of range of each other and in range of a third both find the medium idle and both transmit, and the third hears neither. That is the hidden-terminal case and this binding does not solve it — no listen-before-transmit discipline on any medium does — and it is named so that a reader does not take 6e.1 for a guarantee. What 6e.1 removes is the collision between hives that can hear each other, which on a medium where every hive relays every frame is the common case rather than the exotic one.

Note 4 to entry: Source commentary omitted; provisional markers retained: PROVISIONAL(supervisor).

7 What this closes, what it narrows, and what it leaves open

7.1 Closes. Bearer ordinal 2 now has a binding document, so Layer 1, 12.1 g) can be met for it. SS264 counted six bindings owed; with the BLE binding written the same day, four remain — TCP (3), USB (4), UDP (6), and any second binding sharing an ordinal.

7.2 Closes for this bearer, and narrows the finding. SS379 — the marker with one forbidden exit and one that is not an exit. This bearer has a real exit and 3.1 takes it: the sync word is physical framing, matched before a payload exists, costing nothing. The finding was raised while sizing this document and is answered by the medium rather than by a ruling — so SS379 remains open for ESP-NOW and is no longer general.

7.3 ⚠ Narrows SS379 a second way, and this half is a question for Roy rather than a disposal. The finding says the obligation has one forbidden exit and one that is not an exit. Layer 1, 4.2.2 appears to be a third: it permits a bearer to wrap a frame for its medium — a length prefix, a header, a radio preamble — and obliges it to strip the wrapping before delivering upward, so wrapping bytes are not inside the frame and 4.2.1 is untouched. This document relies on exactly that permission for its address (4.2), so if wrapping is available for an address it is available for a marker, at a cost in payload. SS272 bears directly: under its second reading of medium framing — anything a bearer adds for its medium, including the 4.2.2 wrapping — a wrapped marker satisfies 4.7.1 outright. If that is right, SS379's remaining instance is a cost rather than an impossibility, and what ESP-NOW faces is a payload charge it declined rather than an obligation it cannot meet.

7.4 Leaves open.

  • The sync-word value is PROVISIONAL(supervisor) (3.1 Note 1), for the reason the BLE marker is.
  • SS384 is answered here as it is on BLE (4.3), and remains open for every medium whose address is fixed in hardware.
  • The duty-class element 0x04 is defined here and is absent from the other two bindings (5.4 Note 1), which cannot therefore announce a duty-cycled hive at all. One addition to each, not this document's to make.
  • ⚠ SS11 blocks 5.4.3 and 5.4.3 proposes a way past it. The duty-class interval is obliged coarsely stated and given no granularity and no encoding, so its register row is FLAGGED rather than TESTABLE; this document's row is flagged the same way. A proposal Roy can reject in one line is more use than a third silent omission, and this is the bearer where the omission costs something.
  • ⚠ SS379's remaining instance and its third exit are both open, and 7.2 and 7.3 pull in opposite directions. One says the finding is narrower than it looked; the other says it may be a cost rather than an impossibility. Both are for Roy, and this document takes neither as settled.
  • Clause 9 has a source-level DFR implementation, but no on-air proof. The binding charges accepted airtime and exposes the resulting budget state. Portable controls and target compilation establish those paths; neither establishes a legal transmission on an actual radio, which remains evidence-bound rather than inferred from a successful build.
