Reality2 Standard — Layer 5 companion: The enrolment ceremony

Normative document

Introduction

Layer 5 defines the two claim states and the guard between them: OPEN and OWNER, with the transition to OWNER atomic and the way back physical-only (Layer 5, 4.4). This document specifies the one thing that crosses that guard in the legitimate direction: the ceremony by which an OPEN device becomes a member of an owner's group.

Three commitments shape every clause:

Proximity is the root of trust. Every grade of the ceremony reduces to evidence that the claiming hand — or the claiming cable — was at the device. The standard's threat model consistently prices attack in physical presence, and enrolment is where that price is first charged.

The ceremony scales with the role. A sensor joining an earthgrid and a device that will hold the group's secret key do not warrant the same ceremony. One sequence, graded verification.

No secret ever crosses. The candidate generates its own member keypair; the ceremony carries a public key out and a certificate in. What the ceremony installs is membership, never key material that existed somewhere else first.

1 Scope

This document specifies the enrolment ceremony: the parties, the invitation, the exchange and its verification grades, the atomic install, what follows enrolment, and what an aborted ceremony leaves behind.

It specifies what re-claim after physical reset installs, and what no ceremony can restore.

It establishes what conformance to this document consists of, and identifies what is not decided.

It does not specify the management wallet, which is a separate companion. It does not specify byte formats, which belong to the formats companion, nor the per-bearer carriage of the ceremony, which belongs to the bearer binding documents.

3 Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the terms and definitions given in the documents listed in Clause 2 apply, together with the following.

3.1 candidate

device in claim state OPEN, offered for enrolment

3.2 provisioner

hive acting for the owner group in a ceremony, whose member holds that group's enrolment authority

3.3 invitation

signed, single-use offer of membership naming the group, the issuing member, the role offered, and a code

3.4 ceremony session

keys derived from the ceremony's own exchange, protecting the ceremony's traffic and nothing else

4 The parties and the precondition

4.1 A ceremony shall have exactly two protocol parties — the candidate and the provisioner — and, at the grades 6.4.1 names, a person.

4.2 The candidate shall be in claim state OPEN, verified as Layer 5, 4.4.2 requires: before its claim request is emitted, and again immediately before the install commits.

4.3 A device in claim state OWNER shall not present itself as claimable, on any surface.

4.4 The provisioner's member shall hold custody of the group secret — a key holder (Layer 5, 5.3.1). A member without that custody cannot enrol devices, and the certificate the ceremony issues shall be traceable to the enrolling member's member-attributable evidence (Layer 5, 7.4).

Note 1 to entry: The rule is enforced by construction, not by policy: the certificate the ceremony installs is signed by the group's secret key (Layer 5, Clause 6), so the capability to enrol is the custody — there is no separate enrolment permission to grant, forget, or steal. A group decides who may grow it exactly when it decides who holds its secret, and the invitation's role (5.1) is where that decision is made for each new member.

4.5 The ceremony shall proceed only between devices of the same build mode; the development group's published-credential admission (Layer 5, Clause 9) is the one path that replaces it, and admits only development devices.

Note 1 to entry: 4.1's person is Layer 6's second authority made procedural: remote authority changes code; hands change membership (Layer 6, 4.2). The ceremony is the hands' half. What the person contributes is not computation but witness — that this device, physically here, is the one being admitted.

Note 2 to entry: An OPEN candidate is discoverable by the ordinary machinery — it beacons, as every hive does, and answers the capability question (Layer 2, 7.1) with its claimability. No beacon field carries claim state: claimability is learned by asking, one step up the filter, where a lie costs the liar a connection.

5 The invitation

5.1 An invitation shall name: the group's identity; the issuing member; the role offered — key holder or not: whether membership includes custody of the group secret (Layer 5, 5.3.1), and with it the capability to enrol further devices (4.4); a single-use code; and a validity bound.

5.2 An invitation shall carry the issuing member's member-attributable evidence (Layer 5, 7.4) over all of 5.1.

5.3 An invitation shall be single-use:

a) consumed by the install it authorised;

b) voided by a verification mismatch or a person's decline (6.4) — a fresh ceremony after either shall require a fresh invitation; and

c) an exchange that fails before verification, for transport reasons, may retry the same invitation with a fresh exchange.

5.4 The validity bound shall be evaluated by the provisioner's side, on its own ruler; the candidate shall not be required to evaluate it.

Note 1 to entry: 5.4 keeps the clockless candidate honest rather than pretending it can check what it cannot. A fresh-from-box device has no idea what time it is — the party that issued the bound is the party that holds a ruler for it, and the candidate's protections come from the verification grade, not the calendar.

Note 2 to entry: 5.3 b) is deliberate asymmetry: a mismatch might be radio misfortune — or the one observable of an interposed exchange, and the two cannot be told apart. Voiding the invitation makes the attacker's failed attempt spend the owner's artefact, not merely the attacker's time; the owner who re-issues does so knowingly.

6 The ceremony

6.1 The sequence

6.1.1 A ceremony shall proceed in the following order:

a) discovery — the provisioner finds the candidate by class and confirms claimability by asking (4.3, Layer 2, 7.1);

b) exchange — candidate and provisioner derive a ceremony session (6.2);

c) verification — the session and the invitation are verified at the grade the offered role requires (6.3, 6.4);

d) request — the candidate, still OPEN, emits its claim request under the session: the invitation's code and the public half of a member keypair it generated itself;

e) install — the provisioner returns the persona bundle: the group identity, the candidate's member certificate over the key from d), and the group material the role carries. For an ordinary member (a non-key-holder), that material is the current epoch's derived payload-protection and integrity keys of Layer 5, 5.2.2, delivered together with their epoch under the verified session; it excludes the group issuer secret and prior epochs' traffic keys. Custody remains subject to Layer 5, 5.3.2. The candidate re-verifies OPEN and commits atomically — persona installed, claim state OWNER, in one commit (Layer 5, 4.4.2); and

f) announcement — the provisioner introduces the new member to the group by the group's ordinary machinery.

6.1.2 The candidate's member secret key shall be generated on the candidate and shall never leave it (Layer 5, 5.3.3). No step of the ceremony shall transmit any secret that existed before the ceremony, other than group material delivered to the new member under the verified session, as the role carries.

6.2 The exchange

6.2.1 The ceremony session shall be derived from an ephemeral exchange in which the candidate commits to its contribution before the provisioner reveals its own, and reveals only after.

6.2.2 An exchange in which either party's contribution is absent or degenerate — a result an observer could compute — shall be rejected, and the rejection shall state that reason.

Note 1 to entry: Commit-before-reveal forecloses the co-located attacker's one good move: grinding ephemeral values until the short verification string collides with the one the person is about to read. Committed first, the candidate's contribution is fixed before the attacker sees anything to grind against.

6.3 Verification, graded by role

6.3.1 Verification shall be at one of three grades, and the invitation's offered role shall set the floor:

Grade Co-presence evidence Floor for
glance-confirmed a person compares a short verification string rendered at both endpoints — or rendered at one and confirmed at the other — and confirms the match ordinary membership
anchored a physical cable between provisioner and candidate carries the ceremony; the wire is the co-presence ordinary membership, headless pairs
anchored + person the anchored grade, and a person confirms the enrolment at a person-authentication level the invitation states membership with custody of the group secret

6.3.2 The verification string shall be derived from the ceremony session and the invitation — the group identity, the issuing member, and the code — such that an interposed exchange, or a substituted invitation carrying a copied code, yields visibly different strings at the two endpoints. The string discipline of the entanglement companion applies (L5A-entanglement.md, 5.3.2): it binds identities and is never a reusable code.

6.3.3 The comparison shall be one-shot: a mismatch or a decline shall abort the ceremony with the invitation voided (5.3 b), and shall not be retried within the same exchange.

Note 1 to entry: 6.3.2's second binding is the clause that stops the plausible attack. An attacker cannot forge the issuing member's evidence (5.2), but could try presenting its own group's invitation carrying a code copied from a legitimate one — the string binds which group and which issuer the candidate is being claimed into, so the substitution renders two different strings and the person aborts. The string authenticates the destination of the claim, not merely the absence of an interposer.

Note 2 to entry: The one-shot rule is arithmetic. A short string is guessable in volume — the attacker's strategy is retries — and a ceremony that loops on mismatch hands them exactly that. One mismatch, one dead invitation, one informed owner.

6.4 The person

6.4.1 At the glance-confirmed grade and at the anchored + person grade, a person shall perform the confirmation, and their decline shall abort the ceremony (6.3.3).

Note 0 to entry: The grades are not a ladder, and both clauses above previously read as though they were. 6.3.1's table has three rows and an ordering was never stated for them, so above had no defined meaning — while 4.1 put anchored cable at the bottom needing no person and this clause said glance-confirmed and above, which against the row order sweeps anchored in. Read together they named different floors for the same fact.

Note 0a to entry: The table already decides it, in its own middle column, and the enumeration above states that rather than inventing an ordering. Glance-confirmed's co-presence evidence is a person comparing a string; the anchored grade's is the cable — the wire is the co-presence, and there is nothing left for a person to do. Only anchored + person adds one by name. So the first two rows are alternatives at one floor — both read ordinary membership — and only the third is above either of them.

Note 0b to entry: The case this decides is the one the anchored row was written for. Its Floor for column reads ordinary membership, headless pairs: a pair of devices with no display and nobody present. Under the sweeping reading such a pair could not complete a ceremony at all, and the grade named for it would have been unusable by it.

(PROVISIONAL(standard))

6.4.2 Where the invitation states a person-authentication level for the confirmation (6.3.1, third grade), the confirming person's group shall attest the level and nothing else — never the method used or the evidence behind it.

6.5 After enrolment

6.5.1 The announcement (6.1.1 f) shall teach the group the new member's identity by ordinary verified traffic; no global roster push shall be required, and a member is a member from the commit of 6.1.1 e) regardless of who has yet learned of it.

6.5.2 The ceremony's sightings shall seed the new member's neighbour table.

Note 1 to entry: Two planes, deliberately distinct, and this clause keeps them so. Membership is a cryptographic fact from the instant of the commit; knowledge of the member is runtime state that spreads as frames do. A distant co-member that has never heard of the newcomer is not evidence of a failed enrolment — it is the designed shape of proximity-grounded knowledge, and the gate needs no roster to verify the newcomer's frames.

Note 2 to entry: 6.5.2 is enrolment kick-starting routing: a fresh hive's first task was already make noise and listen (Layer 2, Clause 4), and the ceremony hands it a warm neighbourhood — the provisioner and whatever the exchange overheard — instead of a cold table.

7 Abort, and what a ceremony never leaves

7.1 A ceremony that fails or aborts at any step shall leave the candidate exactly as it was: claim state OPEN, its group-of-one intact, no partial persona.

7.2 An interrupted install shall resolve to one of exactly two states: the prior state complete, or the new persona complete with claim state OWNER. No third state shall be observable, at any interruption point.

7.3 No ceremony shall restore a previous persona. Re-claim after a physical reset (Layer 5, 4.4.3) shall install a fresh persona by a fresh ceremony; the old persona is unreachable by any ceremony, for anyone, permanently.

7.4 Recovery (Layer 6, 5.5) shall not make a device claimable: it erases what it erases and leaves claim state untouched, and a claim after recovery still requires the physical reset first.

Note 1 to entry: 7.3 is forward-only enrolment, and it cuts both ways on purpose. The legitimate owner who reset their own device cannot restore its old persona either — they re-claim forward into a fresh one. What that buys is worth the cost: a stolen device's history is beyond every attacker with every credential, because it is beyond everyone.

8 Conformance

Every requirement of this document is demonstrated by conformance evidence of one of the three classes of 01-terminology.md, 3.10 — behavioural, by construction, or by declaration. A requirement whose evidence is by construction is not thereby optional: the demonstration is an inspection a third party can perform without the implementer's cooperation, and its absence is non-conformance exactly as a failed behavioural test is. Behavioural evidence for any requirement of this document carries the control 01-terminology.md 4.2 to 4.5 requires — the same procedure run against a case the requirement excludes, giving a distinguishable non-satisfying result attributable to the requirement under test; a demonstration that could not have come out any other way is not evidence. (PROVISIONAL(supervisor))

8.1 An implementation conforms to this document where it:

a) verifies OPEN at both check points and refuses claim on OWNER at both (4.2, 4.3);

b) issues and honours invitations as Clause 5 requires, including single-use and the void-on-mismatch rule;

c) runs the sequence of 6.1 with the exchange of 6.2, generating the member keypair on the candidate;

d) verifies at the grade the role requires, binding the string as 6.3.2 requires, one-shot;

e) leaves an aborted ceremony's candidate untouched, resolves interruption to exactly two states, and restores nothing (Clause 7).

8.2 Conformance to 6.3.2 shall be demonstrated with both negative cases: an interposed exchange, and a substituted invitation carrying a copied code — each yielding mismatched strings.

8.3 Conformance to 7.2 shall be demonstrated by interrupting the install at each step boundary and observing only the two permitted states.

9 Security considerations

9.1 The co-located rogue is the design's central adversary: present at the ceremony, able to speak both radio directions. The commit-then-reveal exchange denies it string grinding (6.2.1); the invitation binding denies it destination substitution (6.3.2); the one-shot rule denies it volume (6.3.3). What remains to it is denial — jamming a ceremony it cannot subvert.

9.2 The person is the residual surface. A person who confirms a mismatched string, or confirms without looking, admits whatever was interposed. The ceremony makes the check cheap and the strings visibly different; it cannot make anyone look. The key-custody grade adds the person-authentication requirement precisely because that role is where inattention is unaffordable.

9.3 Claimability answered by query (4.3) reveals that an unclaimed device is present. This is accepted: an unclaimed device is, by intention, findable — that is what being claimable is for — and it grants nothing but a conversation.

9.4 The development group's ceremony-free admission is bounded by its build-mode rule for MEMBERSHIP (Layer 5, 9.2–9.3a) — a bound on which group admits which device, not on which devices share a network (Layer 2, 5.4a.5a, d231): published credentials that admit only development devices, confer nothing on or from production, and are absent from a production image rather than inert within one (Layer 5, 9.3a).

10 What is not decided at this layer

This clause states gaps and contains no requirements.

10.1 Byte formats. The invitation, the persona bundle, the exchange messages, the string derivation and its length and alphabet — owed to the formats companion.

10.2 The management wallet. The provisioner side presumes custody — of invitations, of group material for delivery, of the authority evidence — and the wallet's own rules (what it may hold, what it may do without its owner present, how custody transfers) remain the separate companion Layer 5, 13.7 records.

10.3 The role vocabulary. 5.1 requires the invitation to name the role and 6.3.1 scales the ceremony by it. The one distinction is decided: key holder or not, chosen at enrolment, carrying both the group secret and the capability to enrol further devices (4.4). Whether any finer role vocabulary exists above that line is not specified.

10.4 Per-bearer carriage. Which bearers may carry a ceremony, and what each contributes as proximity evidence beyond the anchored cable, is the bearer binding documents' — including whether any radio-only path is acceptable for the key-custody grade.

10.5 Provisioning at scale. Two hundred venue hives enrolled one glance at a time is a real cost. Whether a batch form exists — one person, one confirmation, many anchored candidates — and what it may not shortcut, is undecided.

10.6 Ordinary-member material — semantic choice resolved, exchange still open. The install step in 6.1.1 e) now specifies the current epoch's derived payload and integrity keys as the ordinary member's material, excluding the issuer secret. Roy approved that choice on 2026-09-09 (D-348, SS271). The persona-bundle byte format, authenticated exchange and the re-obtainment path required by Layer 5, 5.3.2 remain unspecified. This resolution does not supply those contracts or qualify atomic installation and hardware-rooted custody. The original gap was raised on 2026-08-04 when an implementer found no clause delivering the keys.
