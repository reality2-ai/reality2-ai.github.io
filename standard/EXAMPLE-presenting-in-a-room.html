Reality2 Standard — worked example: presenting slides in a room

Status: Informative — worked example Date: 2026-08-04

⚠ INFORMATIVE. The deliberate counterpart to EXAMPLE-payment-at-point-of-sale.md: the same stack, the same mechanisms, at the other end of every ladder — minimal authentication, minimal ceremony, and one governing decision most protocols get wrong: where no mechanism is needed, none is imposed. Open edges are flagged in place.

Cast

  • Person trust group P — the presenter's phone, holding the slide deck.
  • Room trust group R — the conference-room screen: a hive whose display is a plugin, with a sentant handling presentation events (class ai.reality2.display.screen).
  • The room itself — full of people, and doing more governing than any protocol below.

The premise of this example

Nothing here is worth stealing. The stakes are a slide shown or not shown — so the requirement is proximity, the entanglement is low-level, and the ceremony is near zero. This is the assurance spectrum doing its job in the opposite direction from payment: the purpose selects the grade, and this purpose selects almost none.

The signal trace

Phase 0 — discovery

# Signal Layer facts
0.1 Screen beacons: marker, beacon identifier, class hash of ai.reality2.display.screen, production mode L2 5.1
0.2 Phone scans, matches the class — filter 2 exact, or filter 3 by asking for the class string and matching *.display.screen L2 7.1, 7.2
0.3 Phone asks what the screen can do — formats, resolution: the capability question L2 7.1 step 4, on the unauthenticated rung

Phase 1 — a low-level entanglement, conditioned on presence

P and R entangle on the ruled frame (L5 10.2), and every parameter sits at the bottom of its range:

  • level — display-grade: low. The screen needs to know almost nothing about who sends.
  • scope — presentation events and slide content, essentially one direction.
  • lifetime — while present: the governing condition is proximity, continuously, not a duration. The phone is here, hearably, on the room's own bearer.
  • ceremony — near zero: at most the screen shows a short code the phone displays back, binding the entanglement to this screen rather than the identical one next door.

When the presenter walks out, the condition fails and the entanglement expires — the sighting fades on the room's bearer (L2 6.3), the condition is no longer met (L5 10.2.3), and the keys die. Leaving the room is the teardown; nobody logs out.

⚠ Two open edges, flagged: the establishment ceremony's signals are Q25, and proximity as an entanglement condition — what evidences it, how continuously, how spoofable-at-range it is allowed to be — belongs to Q25's conditions vocabulary. For slides, coarse radio presence is plainly enough; the point is that the condition's strength is priced by the level, and this level is low.

Phase 2 — the deck, and the show

# Signal What it carries Layer facts
2.1 P→R: the deck, as chunked transfer slide images as manifest-verified pieces, encrypted under the entanglement's key the L6 5.3.5 model at the receipt grade — the phone wants to know the deck arrived before standing up
2.2 P→R display.show, display.next, display.prev slide index ordinary events, fire-and-forget — a lost next is repaired by pressing it again, which is the human doing end-to-end repair without knowing it
2.3 R renders — the display plugin is hive-shared (L7 6.2.1); the sentant's handler is the only gate that matters here

Even here the payload is encrypted — the deck is not broadcast to the street; an observer in radio range sees the envelope only (L2 11.4; L5 7.3.4 states the same boundary for a carrier, which an observer in range need not be). Confidentiality costs nothing extra: it fell out of the entanglement existing at all.

Phase 3 — who presents? The room decides, and that is a design position

Several people may be entangled with the screen at once, all legitimately. The standard supplies no floor-control protocol, no presenter token, no arbitration — the screen shows what it is sent, and who sends is governed by social cues. Someone stands up; someone defers; the chair nods. The mechanism for turn-taking in a meeting has been deployed for roughly as long as meetings, and a technical arbiter inserted here would be worse than none: it would move a social negotiation into a protocol nobody in the room can see or overrule.

(This is the standard's own discipline applied: an absence labelled as a decision. The absence of arbitration is not a gap — it is the correct allocation of a problem to the layer that actually solves it, which is the people.)

Phase 4 — the escalated variant: locking the screen to a speaker

The council meeting, the contentious topic, the protester with a phone. The same room now needs what the seminar did not: the slides locked to a specific person. Nothing new is built — the screen's group raises the requirements, using machinery the payment example already exercised:

  • R's policy for display.* events switches from any live entanglement to: only the clerk's entanglement, or only events carrying member-attributable evidence at a stated person-authentication level (the Q34 scale, consumed exactly as the PoS consumed it).
  • R may also refuse new entanglements while locked.

The protester's phone can still discover the screen, still speak, possibly still entangle — and its display.show matches no accepting policy. It is not blocked; it is unheeded (the strata synthesis: the agent can always speak; whether the far side acts is what is governed). No error educates the protester about the policy, either — silence toward the unentitled is the same posture the diagnostics tier takes (L6 6.2.2).

And the lock releases the way everything here releases: the clerk walks out, their proximity condition fails, their entanglement expires, and the screen's policy falls back to whatever the room configured for that case.

The pairing, stated once

Payment Presenting
Stakes money a slide
Entanglement level payment-grade display-grade
Governing condition the amount ladder proximity, continuously
Person-authentication attested, laddered, ZKP-inspired none — until the lock, when the same machinery returns
Assurance grade receipt-with-release receipt for the deck; fire-and-forget for the clicks
Contention not applicable the room, socially — by design
Teardown transaction completes walking out

One stack, no modes. Neither example uses a mechanism the other lacks — they set the same dials to different values, and the purpose chose the values. That is the claim the two examples exist to demonstrate.
