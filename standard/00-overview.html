Verbal forms. The documents of this standard use the ISO verbal forms — shall for a requirement, should for a recommendation, may for a permission, can for possibility and capability.

This document is informative. It states the premise the whole standard is built on, the structure of the document set, and how the parts fit — and it contains no requirements. Every requirement of this standard lives in one of the parts listed in Clause 5, and where this overview and a part appear to disagree, the part is right.

Introduction

Reality2 is a network of cooperating devices — from single-chip sensors to phones and servers — that form meshes over whatever communication media they have, host autonomous software agents, and organise trust cryptographically into sovereign groups. It is built for places where conventional networking assumptions do not hold: devices that sleep for hours, radios that reach sometimes, populations that walk in and out of range, and no infrastructure anywhere that everyone can rely on.

This standard is the document set from which an implementer who cannot ask its authors a question can build a conformant implementation of Reality2 transient networking and trust groups, and have it interoperate with every other conformant implementation.

1 Scope

This document describes the premise shared by every part of the Reality2 Standard, the layered structure of the document set, the trust boundary that divides it, the parties that communicate at each layer, and a reading order.

It contains no requirements, no recommendations and no permissions.

It applies to readers of every other part of the Reality2 Standard.

2 Normative references

There are no normative references in this document.

3 The core premise

Everything here is built on a highly lossy and movable network where nothing can be assumed. Every mechanism in this standard is shaped by that sentence, and it is the test any proposed mechanism has to pass: does this still work when frames are lost, peers have moved, and no assumption held?

The pattern it produces, visible at every layer:

  • Nothing below the endpoints promises anything. No acknowledgements, no delivery guarantee, no session — a bearer transmits, and only ever knows that it transmitted.
  • Loss is repaired by the party that noticed, end to end: retransmission, the missing-pieces pull, idempotent effects — never per-hop reliability.
  • Silence is ambiguous, always. Asleep, moved, dead and budget-exhausted are indistinguishable from outside, so nothing consequential is ever concluded from silence alone — links fade; membership never does.
  • State that matters lives at the endpoints — assembly state, membership, custody of frames for the absent — and survives arbitrary interruption.
  • Assurance, where needed, is a contract between the two endpoints, graded by the message's purpose. A sensor reading that the next reading supersedes is fire-and-forget; a firmware piece wants a receipt; the spectrum is the UDP-to-TCP spectrum, with one structural difference: both ends of it ride the same substrate. The network has exactly one delivery model — a receiver that says got that is sending application data, not a network signal, and the sender's "received, may now be replaced" tag is its own storage decision, made on that receipt.
  • Time is a local ruler, not a shared fact, below the trust boundary entirely.

4 The epistemology the premise demands

On a network where nothing can be assumed, nothing is ever proven — things are only un-refuted so far. The reasoning discipline this forces is called the Thurisaz technique: confidence is earned by surviving attempts to refute, updated sequentially as evidence arrives, decaying without fresh evidence, and held locally, with no consensus and no shared truth anywhere. The standard instantiates it at almost every layer:

Layer The claim The test The decay
L1 "this link works" every reception updates quality quality falls to zero unheard
L2 "something is there / it does X" filter step 5: perform the act and observe — the only authoritative step sightings fade per bearer
L3 "this path delivers" every frame is a refutation attempt — confidence reinforced by delivery, and the forwarding threshold is a belief threshold confidence decays; links fade; nothing is evicted from membership by belief
L3 "this neighbour is not a bad actor" behaviour observed; graduated and recoverable exclusion — never a verdict reputation recovers as behaviour does
L5 "this frame is the group's" the one binary gate in the stack — cryptography, not belief, at the boundary grace windows, then nothing
L6 "this image is healthy" the health check is a refutation attempt, and rollback is the refutation succeeding the prior image is retained until commit
L7 "this sentant does what it claims" determinism makes behaviour replayable — auditable refutation from the definition alone —

Two disciplines, one boundary — and keeping them apart is the design. Below and around the trust boundary, belief: probabilistic, local, decaying, never final. At the boundary itself, proof: the frame's integrity tag verifies under a held key or it does not — and where an act needs more than the gate, a member's signature likewise verifies or does not. Confidence never substitutes for the gate, and the gate never claims what only observation can — passing the gate is a floor, not a character reference (Layer 5, 7.2).

Two structural facts the layer numbering alone does not convey:

  • L1 and L2 instantiate per transport. A hive runs N parallel lower stacks, one per available transport, each with its own bearer binding, its own discovery and its own neighbour sensing. L3 is where they converge into one neighbour table, and L4 is one framing riding any transport.
  • The trust boundary is the L4/L5 seam. Everything L0–L4 is trustless shared mesh: any device participates, and relaying is the default — a hive forwards frames for hives it has never met, belonging to groups it cannot join, carrying ciphertext it cannot read. Everything L5 and above is authenticated, and every consequential act is gated there.

5.1 What the network sends is frames

A frame might be an event with a payload, or a chunk of a larger transfer — a firmware image assembling over days, a sound recording moving upstream. From the network's point of view there is no difference, only what to do with it at the other end: the type field says how to treat a frame, never what it means. There is no second carriage path; anything large is divided at its source by the endpoint that knows what the payload means, and no layer fragments (Layer 4, 6.2).

6 Who communicates with whom — a different answer at every layer

Most confusion about this system comes from asking "what talks to what?" without naming the layer. A sentant never talks to a bearer; a group never talks to a neighbour. The parties, layer by layer:

Layer Who talks to whom Reach
L0 nobody — the platform provides facilities to its own upper layers one device
L1 binding ↔︎ binding across one medium; the parties are adjacent devices on one bearer one hop, one bearer
L2 one hive → anyone in range — the beacon is addressed to no one, and a scanner listens to everything it can hear one hop, per bearer, party-less
L3 hive ↔︎ neighbour — the only relationship this layer has is heard directly; everything farther is neighbours-of-neighbours relaying, and the mesh is what emerges hop by hop
L4 origin ↔︎ destination — the frame's meaning is end to end even though its carriage is hop by hop; a relay touches three fields and reads nothing end to end
L5 member ↔︎ group (the gate); group ↔︎ group (entanglement, at a level of its ladder); person ↔︎ device (the claim, physically) trust-scoped
L6 appointed authority → its group's devices; operator ↔︎ device in hand; any asker ↔︎ the public tier only authority-scoped
L7 sentant ↔︎ sentant by events — same hive by dispatch, other hives by frames; sentant ↔︎ plugin on the same hive only; an ensemble is its parts conversing application

6.1 One message, three answers at once

Asked "is communication sentant-to-sentant, group-to-group, or hive-to-hive?", the answer is all three, because they answer different questions:

Sentants converse. Groups permit. Hives carry. A single message, vertically: the sentant composes → the hive addresses → the groups permit → the hive delivers → the sentant decides.

The user-facing metaphor is the top stratum only — intelligent agents interacting — and the strata below are why an agent's access is not free, without that ever being visible as mechanism. An agent can always speak (fire-and-forget, even unauthenticated); what its community's relationships set is whether the far side acts. Told as a story it is a social one: your agents can talk with theirs to the degree your communities trust each other — a human truth, not a protocol detail, and the reason the intelligent-agents metaphor survives the security model instead of fighting it.

6.2 Groups, and the ladder between them

A trust group is a flat group of hives sharing a cryptographic identity — sovereign, with no hierarchy inside it and no authority above it. Between groups there is a ladder: traffic between two hives is same-group, entangled — the groups hold a conditional agreement, scoped and levelled, standing exactly as long as its conditions are met — or unauthenticated, which still exists and still carries. The mesh is not a rung of this ladder: entanglement is a relationship between trust groups; the mesh is a relationship between devices, and every device is in it (Layer 5, Clause 10).

7 Reading order

Terminology first, then bottom-up. Each part states what it assumes of the part below, so the stack reads in layer order: L0 through L4 for the mesh, L5 through L7 for what trust adds. Every part carries its own conformance clause saying who conforms and how that is demonstrated, and every part ends with a what is not decided clause — an absence in this standard is labelled as an absence, never left to be read as a permission.

A minimal relaying device is an implementation of L0–L4: that floor is what makes every device a citizen of the shared mesh. Membership, delivery, management and applications build on it in order.
